Curb and Gutter

Part of: Typical Section is-subtopic-of
Curb and Gutter is a topic under Typical Section.
curb-and-guttercurbsmonolithic-curb-and-guttertied-curb-and-gutteruntied-curb-and-gutter
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

The curb‑and‑gutter assembly is a pavement‑edge treatment applied where a flexible or plain‑cement wearing course meets a flush curb, gutter, or similar structure. The wearing‑course mix is spread uniformly higher so that after compaction the surface slightly overhangs the curb, the exposed edge is immediately worked smooth, and a continuous strip at least 12 inches wide is sealed with hot (or approved emulsified) asphalt to fill all voids and form a watertight joint that prevents edge deterioration, water infiltration and provides proper drainage[1]. This construction also supplies structural support for the pavement margin and creates a smooth, safe transition for users[1].

Purpose and design objectives

The curb‑and‑gutter assembly is provided to prevent edge deterioration and water infiltration at the pavement margin. The specification requires that immediately after placement the outer edges be worked to eliminate sharp, ragged or open edges and any breakdowns be repaired, while the wearing course adjacent to a flush curb is placed uniformly higher so that after compaction it slightly overhangs the curb. A seal of hot (or approved emulsified) asphalt must then be applied a minimum width of 12 inches from the curb, with enough material to completely fill surface voids and provide a watertight joint along the curb. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

The curb‑and‑gutter treatment is placed uniformly higher than the adjoining flush curb so that after compaction the finished surface sits slightly above the edge, and the wearing‑course surface is then sealed with hot asphalt over a minimum width of 12 inches from the curb to form a watertight joint. Immediately after placement the exposed outer edges are worked to eliminate sharp, ragged or open edges, remove an unfinished appearance, and reduce edge breakdown, and any breakdown is repaired right away. These provisions provide structural support for the pavement edge, protect against water infiltration, maintain a smooth serviceable surface, and eliminate safety hazards. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

Scope and applicability

Under SECTION 413—SUPERPAVE MIXTURE DESIGN, curb and gutter treatment is appropriate on flexible‑pavement wearing courses where the edge abuts a flush curb, gutter, or similar structure; the wearing‑course mix is placed slightly higher so that after compaction the surface sits just above the adjoining element, the edge is cleaned and sealed, and an asphalt gutter is formed using a hot asphalt binder material of the class listed in Table A applied uniformly for at least 12 inches from the curb, or, if approved by the Inspector‑in‑Charge, emulsified asphalt of PennDOT Material Class TACK or NTT/CNTT may be used. Under SECTION 641—PLAIN CEMENT CONCRETE CURB GUTTER OR PLAIN CONCRETE MOUNTABLE CURB GUTTER, curb and gutter can be used in plain cement‑concrete or mountable concrete installations when the specified materials are employed; acceptable constituents include mortar, Class AA cement concrete, asphalt cement of class PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28, joint sealing material as described in Sections 705.4(b) or (c), caulking compound per Section 705.7, and appropriate curing and protection covers and curing compounds; joints for plain cement‑concrete curb gutter should be prepared and sealed or caulked according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, while joints for plain concrete mountable curb gutter follow the requirements of Section 633.3(e). [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 563)

Design inputs and requirements

The placement of curb‑and‑gutter when constructed as an asphalt wearing course is governed by the spreading and finishing provisions of Section 413.3(h) of the PennDOT Flexible Pavement specifications. Under those provisions the wearing‑course mixture must be spread uniformly higher adjacent to flush curbs, gutters or other abutting structures so that after compaction the finished surface sits slightly above the edge; the surface next to the curb must be cleaned, harmful material removed and sealed to form an asphalt gutter. Sealing shall be performed with a hot‑asphalt material of the class and type listed in Table A and applied evenly for a minimum width of 12 inches from the curb, although emulsified asphalt of PennDOT Material Class TACK or NTT/CNTT may be used if approved by the Inspector‑in‑Charge, and the application rate must fill all voids to provide a watertight joint. For plain cement concrete curb‑gutter systems the material requirements are set out in Section 641.2. The design shall incorporate mortar as required by Section 1001.2(d), Class AA cement concrete as specified in Section 704, and asphalt cement of Class PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28. Joint sealing material must follow the details of Sections 705.4(b) or (c) together with a caulking compound per Section 705.7, and appropriate curing covers and compounds shall be provided in accordance with Sections 711.1 and 711.2(a). Joint preparation must follow the manufacturer’s recommendations for plain cement concrete curb‑gutter and joints for plain concrete mountable curb‑gutter must be sealed as required by Section 633.3(e); form handling and surface correction are limited until the full curing period has elapsed. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 563)

Design methods and criteria

Procedures for curb‑and‑gutter work are defined by the specifications rather than by design equations, empirical models, or calculation tools. During spreading and finishing of the wearing course, the mixture is placed uniformly higher adjacent to flush curbs so that after compaction the finished surface sits slightly above the edge of the abutting structure; any harmful material is then removed, the area is cleaned, and a watertight joint is formed by sealing the surface with hot asphalt binder (or, if permitted by the Inspector‑in‑Charge, an emulsified asphalt consisting of PennDOT Material Class TACK or NTT/CNTT) applied in a strip at least 12 inches wide from the curb. For plain cement concrete curb‑gutter, construction follows the material list that includes mortar, Class AA cement concrete, asphalt cement (PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28), joint sealing material, caulking compound, curing covers and curing compound. Forms may not be rubbed to correct irregularities until they have been removed and the full curing period has elapsed; after curing, irregular surfaces are corrected by rubbing with a carborundum stone and minor defects are filled with mortar. Joints for plain cement concrete curb‑gutter are prepared and sealed or caulked in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations, while joints for plain concrete mountable curb‑gutter follow the specifications of Section 633.3(e). No design equations, empirical relationships, or calculation tools are prescribed for these components. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 563)

Curb and gutter must be constructed by placing the wearing‑course mixture uniformly higher than the curb so that after compaction the surface is slightly above the edge, then sealing a minimum width of 12 inches from the curb with hot asphalt of the class listed in Table A (or, if approved, emulsified asphalt such as PennDOT Material Class TACK or NTT/CNTT) to completely fill surface voids and create a watertight joint while removing any excess sealant; the exposed outer edges must be worked immediately after placement to eliminate sharp, ragged, or open edges and any edge breakdowns must be repaired right away, and a safety‑edge treatment is not required where a curb is encountered. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

Structural section and dimensions

For curb and gutter, the specification calls for sealing the wearing‑course surface adjacent to the curb by applying a hot‑asphalt sealant that extends at least twelve inches from the curb; no other pavement, slab, course, overlay, base, subbase, treated‑soil, shoulder, edge‑support, widening or reinforcement dimensions are prescribed in this section. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

When a pavement course adjacent to a curb or gutter would be more than six inches in compacted depth, it must be placed in two or more lifts of roughly equal thickness, each lift being no less than three inches and no greater than six inches; the safety edge at the outer edge shall be built with a minimum depth of 1.5 inches but not exceed five inches total and its final face angle must fall between 26 and 40 degrees measured from the pavement cross‑slope extended, while the wearing course next to flush curbs is set slightly higher than the curb and sealed for at least twelve inches using hot or emulsified asphalt to form a watertight joint. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

Materials and mixture design

In Section 413—Superpave Mixture Design, for curb and gutter work the wearing‑course asphalt mixture must be placed uniformly higher so that after compaction the finished surface is slightly above the edge of the curb; the adjacent surface then must be cleaned and sealed to form an asphalt gutter. The seal must be a hot asphalt material of the class and type listed in Table A applied evenly for at least 12 inches from the curb, with the application rate controlled so that all voids are filled and a watertight joint is achieved, and the contractor may substitute emulsified asphalt consisting of PennDOT Material Class TACK or NTT/CNTT if approved by the Inspector‑in‑Charge. In Section 641—Plain Cement Concrete Curb Gutter, the specification calls for mortar as referenced in Section 1001.2(d), Class AA cement concrete as defined in Section 704, and asphalt cement of either Class PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28; it also requires joint sealing material per Sections 705.4(b) or (c), a caulking compound according to Section 705.7, covers for curing and protection as outlined in Section 711.1, and a curing compound specified in Section 711.2(a). [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 563)

In accordance with Section 413.3(h) spreading and finishing, the curb‑and‑gutter edge must be built using the standard wearing‑course mixture but placed uniformly higher so that after compaction the surface sits slightly above the curb; the adjacent pavement is then cleaned of harmful material and sealed to create an asphalt gutter by applying a hot asphalt binder of the class listed in Table A over a minimum 12‑inch width from the curb, or, where permitted, an emulsified asphalt of PennDOT Material Class TACK or NTT/CNTT, with the application rate controlled to fill all surface voids and form a watertight joint. The mixture used for this treatment must be delivered and placed at the temperatures prescribed in Table A and must meet the asphalt‑content and gradation tolerances required for lot acceptance, ensuring structural integrity, durability, constructability, and performance of the curb‑and‑gutter assembly. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

Structural details and interfaces

When a pavement abuts a flush curb or gutter, the wearing‑course mixture must be placed uniformly higher so that after compaction the finished surface is slightly above the edge of the curb, and the exposed edge must be sealed to form an asphalt gutter by applying hot asphalt (or permitted emulsified asphalt) in a continuous band at least 12 inches wide from the curb to create a watertight joint; the Safety Edge treatment is not required where a curb or sidewalk is encountered. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

The design of the curb‑and‑gutter interface for plant‑mixed asphalt courses requires that the wearing‑course mixture be placed uniformly higher so that, after compaction, the finished surface sits slightly above the edge of the abutting structure; the adjacent surface is then sealed with a hot asphalt material of the class and type listed in Table A, or, if permitted by the Inspector‑in‑Charge, emulsified asphalt consisting of PennDOT Material Class TACK or NTT/CNTT. The sealant should be applied evenly for at least 12 inches from the curb, with the application rate controlled so that residual asphalt completely fills surface voids and provides a watertight joint. For plain cement‑concrete curb‑gutter (or plain concrete mountable curb‑gutter) the joints are prepared and sealed or caulked according to the manufacturer’s recommendations for plain cement concrete units, and as specified in Section 633.3(e) for mountable concrete units. Do not rub forms to correct irregularities until they have been removed and the full curing period has elapsed; any irregular surfaces are corrected by rubbing with a carborundum stone and minor defects are filled with mortar. Depressed curb‑gutter sections are placed where indicated, and an appropriate joint sealing material or caulking compound is used to achieve a durable connection. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 563)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

Curb and gutter should be built by placing the wearing‑course mix uniformly higher than a flush curb so that after compaction the surface sits slightly above the edge, then cleaning and sealing a strip at least 12 inches wide along the curb with hot (or approved emulsified) asphalt to fill all voids and create a watertight joint that forms an asphalt gutter for runoff, thereby preventing water infiltration and providing proper drainage. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

Curb and gutter sections must be finished so that the outer edges are free of sharp, ragged or open surfaces, providing a smooth transition for users; the wearing course adjacent to flush curbs and gutters shall be placed uniformly higher so that after compaction the surface sits slightly above the curb edge, then sealed with hot asphalt (or approved emulsified material) in a continuous band at least 12 inches wide to create a watertight joint, and a safety‑edge treatment is required on pavement edges except where a curb or sidewalk is present. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

Verification and expected performance

The finished curb‑and‑gutter assembly must be inspected right after the asphalt mat is placed; the outer edges are required to be worked smooth so that sharp, ragged or open edges are eliminated and any edge breakdown is repaired. The wearing course next to a flush curb or gutter shall be laid uniformly higher so that, after compaction, the surface sits slightly above the curb edge, and the adjacent surface must then be sealed with hot asphalt (or permitted emulsified asphalt) applied evenly for at least 12 inches from the curb, filling all voids to create a watertight joint. Verifying these actions confirms compliance with the spreading‑and‑finishing provisions and that the curb‑and‑gutter meets constructability, performance, and project requirements. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)

For curb and gutter the specification expects the pavement edge to be free of sharp, ragged or open edges and to exhibit minimal edge breakdown, while the adjacent wearing course must form a watertight joint that protects the curb from water infiltration. These outcomes are achieved by immediately working the exposed outer edges after placement, cleaning and sealing the surface next to the curb with hot asphalt applied at least 12 inches wide, and controlling the sealant rate so that all surface voids are completely filled; compliance is verified by visual inspection of the finished edge and confirmation that the seal fills the joint without gaps. [1]

[1] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 270–276)
