Pavement Widening

Part of: Pavement Design is-subtopic-of
Pavement Widening is a topic under Pavement Design.
fill-wideningpartial-lane-widening
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-28

Pavement widening, also called fill widening, is a rehabilitation treatment applied when a concrete pavement receives a bonded overlay that includes a widened lane of about 1–2 feet or asphalt shoulders, and it is used to retain the designed shoulder width while checking that bridge under‑clearances are not violated, thereby correcting inadequate shoulder dimensions and potential clearance conflicts[1]. The added fill must match the existing pavement structure—full lane additions of ten feet or more keep the original thickness unless heavier traffic loads require a redesign, partial widenings under ten feet replicate the existing material (plain cement concrete for JPCP or equivalent asphalt), transverse joints are aligned with those on the original pavement, drainage continuity is maintained, and construction details such as tied longitudinal joints and optional formwork for widths greater than six feet are prescribed[1][2].

Purpose and design objectives

Pavement widening, referred to as fill widening, is employed when concrete shoulders or a widened lane of 1 to 2 feet are provided with asphalt shoulders and the specification requires it so that the rebuilt section retains the designed shoulder width and permits checking that bridge under‑clearances are not violated; this treatment addresses inadequate shoulder dimensions and potential clearance conflicts, ensuring that the shoulder maintains its intended width for adequate support and safety. [1]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 67–70)

In accordance with the guidelines for concrete rehabilitation (section 3.3), a fill‑widening design shall retain proper shoulder widths and shall be checked to ensure adequate bridge under clearances, thereby satisfying functional requirements for lane and shoulder geometry and safety considerations related to vertical clearance. Section 4.8 specifies that full lane additions that are at least ten feet wide shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic loads require a design for actual traffic; in such cases the design shall reflect the actual loading. Partial lane widenings that are less than ten feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth, and the transverse joints on the widening or lane addition shall be aligned to match those on the original pavement. Widening of jointed plain concrete pavement (JPCP) shall be constructed with plain cement concrete. Section 5.11 requires that partial lane widenings of less than ten feet for asphalt pavements must match the existing pavement structure, and full‑width lane additions of at least ten feet should also match the existing pavement structure unless substantially different traffic loads dictate otherwise; when layer thicknesses are altered, it is important to check that drainage is adequately provided throughout the pavement cross section. [1]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 69–70), Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96), Ch. 5 (p. 115)

Scope and applicability

Pavement widening, expressed as fill widening, is appropriate when a concrete pavement is receiving a bonded overlay that includes a widened lane of about 1 to 2 feet and the design calls for asphalt shoulders, or whenever type 6 or type 7 paved shoulders are used to preserve the required shoulder width; in these situations the added fill ensures the shoulder retains its proper dimensions [1]. Pavement widening may also be applied in concrete rehabilitation projects that involve bonded or unbonded concrete overlays, unbonded crack‑and‑seat overlays, or reconstruction on rubblization, where the purpose is to retain the required shoulder width and verify bridge underclearances [1]. For rigid pavements, widening is appropriate for any uniform‑depth Portland cement concrete pavement built since 1945, whether or not it has been overlaid; full lane additions of ten feet or more shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic loadings require design for actual traffic, and partial lane widening of less than ten feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth [1]. Widening of Jointed Plain Concrete Pavement shall be constructed with plain cement concrete, while Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete, and designs shall use Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted [1]. For asphalt pavements, partial lane widening of less than ten feet must match the existing pavement structure, with asphalt layers matched by an equal depth of asphalt material and concrete layers matched by an equal thickness of concrete, and drainage layers should be kept consistent with those of the existing pavement [1]. Full‑width lane additions of ten feet or more should also match the existing pavement structure unless circumstances dictate heavier or lighter traffic loadings for the additional lane; in those cases design follows the actual traffic and it is important to check that drainage is adequately provided throughout the pavement cross section [1]. Pavement widening is appropriate for plain cement concrete base courses, and when the widening does not exceed six feet in width forms are not required, provided acceptable methods are used to contain the base course [2]. Where longitudinal joints are needed for construction convenience a tied joint may be formed and full‑width sawing is not required [2]. The surface should receive a rake or broom finish, and black liquid membrane forming curing compound may be used only if the base course will not be exposed to traffic before the surface course is placed, with discontinuation if it becomes unsatisfactory [2].

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 67–70), Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96), Ch. 5 (p. 115); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 205–206)

When the project involves a uniform‑depth rigid pavement built since 1945 but any of the specific criteria in Section 4.8 are not satisfied—such as a full lane addition that is at least ten feet wide requiring a thickness different from the original because of heavier traffic, a partial widening under ten feet that cannot match the existing pavement type or depth, a need to use reinforced concrete for a JPCP widening, or joint spacing that falls outside the prescribed limits—a designer may deem another design approach preferable and must seek an exemption with supporting documentation. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96)

Design inputs and requirements

Fill widening shall be provided as required to retain proper shoulder widths and shall verify that bridge clearances are not compromised. [1] Where widening is provided, concrete shoulders shall be used when the existing shoulders are concrete; otherwise the widened portion may match the existing shoulder type, and a widened lane of 1 to 2 feet may be combined with asphalt shoulders, with Type 6 or Type 7 paved shoulders applied where appropriate. [1] For rigid pavements, widening and lane additions are governed by the PCCP Guidelines which require use of Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted, and apply only to uniform‑depth rigid pavements built since 1945. [1] Full lane additions that are ≥ 10 feet shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic loads dictate design for actual traffic; partial lane widening that is < 10 feet must match the existing pavement type and depth. [1] For asphalt pavements, partial lane widening (< 10 feet) must match the existing pavement structure with an equal depth of asphalt material, and for concrete pavements the same matching requirement applies to thickness of concrete. [1] Drainage layers should be kept consistent wherever possible, and when layer thicknesses are changed drainage shall be adequately provided throughout the pavement cross‑section. [1] Transverse joints on the widening or lane addition shall align with those on the original pavement; when the original joint spacing is 20 feet or less the same slab length shall be used, and if it exceeds 20 feet the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet with intermediate joints as needed. [1] All lane additions and widenings shall be tied to the existing pavement and any deviation from these criteria requires an exemption request supported by documentation. [1] The widening of a cement‑concrete base course follows the provisions in Section 301.3 of the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications. [2] Forms are not required for base course widening six feet or less in width, provided that acceptable methods are used to contain the base course material. [2] Longitudinal joints placed for construction convenience shall be formed as tied joints as shown on the Standard Drawings, and sawing is not required in full‑width construction; transverse joint requirements follow Section 501.3(i)1. [2] The surface of the widened base course shall be finished with a rake or broom texture. [2] A black liquid‑membrane forming curing compound may be used only if the base course will not be accessible to traffic before the surface course is placed, and it must be discontinued if it becomes unsatisfactory, after which other curing methods shall be applied. [2]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 67–70), Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96), Ch. 5 (p. 115); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 205–206)

Design methods and criteria

For base‑course widening the specification limits form usage to projects wider than six feet; when the widening does not exceed six feet no forms are required and the contractor may use any acceptable method to contain the new material. Longitudinal joints that are placed for construction convenience must be tied joints, and if the widening is done in full width the joint need not be sawed. [2]

[2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 205–206)

For any widening or lane addition on a rigid pavement, the design shall use Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted, and this applies to all uniform‑depth rigid pavements built since 1945. When the added width is ten feet or more (full lane addition), the thickness of the new section shall be the same as that of the original pavement unless heavier traffic loads require design for the actual traffic; in such cases the design shall reflect the actual traffic conditions. For partial widenings that are less than ten feet, the new portion shall match the existing pavement type and depth. In jointed plain cement concrete (JPCP) pavements, transverse joints on the widening shall be aligned with those of the original pavement; if the original transverse joint spacing is twenty feet or less, slabs in the widened area shall have the same length as on the original pavement. If the original spacing exceeds twenty feet, the widening shall be constructed with JPCP and the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum length of ten feet and a maximum length of seventeen feet, incorporating intermediate joints so that slab lengths are approximately equal. For asphalt pavements, partial lane widenings less than ten feet must duplicate the existing pavement structure by providing an equal depth of asphalt material and, where concrete layers exist, an equal thickness of concrete, while keeping drainage layers as consistent as possible with those of the existing pavement. Full‑width additions of ten feet or more on asphalt pavements should also match the existing pavement structure unless traffic conditions dictate heavier or lighter loadings, in which case the design shall be for the actual traffic. Whenever layer thicknesses are altered, drainage must be checked to ensure adequate provision throughout the cross section. For a plain cement concrete base course, widening up to six feet in width does not require forms; any wider expansion must be contained by acceptable methods. Where longitudinal joints are placed for construction convenience, they shall be tied joints as shown on the standard drawings, and full‑width work does not require sawing. The finished surface shall receive a rake or broom finish. A black liquid membrane forming curing compound may be used only if the base course will not be exposed to traffic before the surface course is placed. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96), Ch. 5 (p. 115); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 205–206)

Structural section and dimensions

In section 3.3, fill widening specifies only a widened lane dimension of 1 to 2 feet, which may be provided as concrete shoulders or as a widened lane with asphalt shoulders; the design should also use Type 6 or Type 7 paved shoulders where applicable and employ fill widening as required to retain proper shoulder widths. In section 4.8, for rigid‑pavement widening a full lane addition that is at least 10 feet wide shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic requires a different design, while a partial widening of less than 10 feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth; transverse joint spacing in the widened section must follow the original layout, using slab lengths identical to the original when that spacing is 20 feet or less, but if the original spacing exceeds 20 feet the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet. [1]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 67–69), Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96)

For widening or lane additions on rigid pavements the design shall use Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted, and thicknesses shall follow the original pavement for full lane additions of ten feet or more unless heavier traffic requires a redesign; partial widenings of less than ten feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth. The transverse joint pattern must be aligned with the existing pavement; if the original joint spacing is twenty feet or less the new slabs shall have the same length, while for spacings greater than twenty feet the widening shall use plain cement concrete with joint spacing limited to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet, adding intermediate joints as needed to keep slab lengths roughly equal. All lane additions and widenings shall be tied into the existing pavement. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96)

Materials and mixture design

In section 4.8 WIDENING AND LANE ADDITIONS any widening or lane addition on a rigid pavement shall be designed using Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted. For partial widenings that are less than 10 feet the new section must use the same pavement type and depth as the existing surface; thus a jointed plain cement pavement (JPCP) shall be widened with plain cement concrete, while a jointed reinforced cement pavement (JRCP) may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete. Full‑width lane additions retain the original thickness of the existing rigid pavement unless heavier traffic conditions require otherwise. In section 5.11 WIDENING for partial lane widening that is less than 10 feet on an asphalt pavement the added pavement must use the same material type as the existing structure, matching asphalt layers with an equal depth of asphalt material and concrete layers with an equal thickness of concrete, and drainage layers shall be kept consistent with those already in place. For full‑width lane additions that are ten feet or wider the new pavement should also match the existing pavement structure unless traffic conditions dictate a heavier or lighter design, in which case the design must reflect the actual traffic loads while still ensuring adequate drainage throughout the cross section. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96), Ch. 5 (p. 115)

For widening or lane additions on rigid pavements, the material shall be Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted, and the mixture must correspond to the existing pavement type: full lane additions that are at least ten feet wide shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic requires a different design, while partial lane widenings that are less than ten feet wide shall match the existing pavement’s type and depth. Jointed plain cement concrete (JPCP) must be used for widening of JPCP sections, whereas jointed reinforced cement concrete (JRCP) may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete. Transverse joints in the new section shall align with those on the original pavement; if the original joint spacing is twenty feet or less, slab lengths should be the same as the original, and when it exceeds twenty feet the widening shall use JPCP with joint spacing adjusted to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet. All lane additions and widenings must be tied to the existing pavement, and any deviation from these criteria requires an exemption supported by documentation. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96)

Structural details and interfaces

In asphalt rehabilitation projects where pavement widening is used to retain the required shoulder width, the process requires cleaning and sealing of both longitudinal and transverse concrete joints before placement of the new overlay, and existing transverse or patch joints shall be saw‑cut and sealed prior to the overlay. The widened portion should receive paved shoulders using Type 6 or Type 7 shoulder pavement where applicable, and a continuous pavement base drain must be maintained; guide rail and drainage structures are adjusted or replaced as needed to accommodate the wider cross‑section. For concrete rehabilitation, fill widening shall be used to retain proper shoulder widths and verify bridge underclearances, but no specific joint configuration, reinforcement, load‑transfer device, transition detail, or connection is prescribed for the widened portion. In rigid‑pavement widening or lane additions, transverse joints on the widening or lane addition shall be aligned with those on the existing pavement. When the original transverse joint spacing is twenty feet or less, the new slabs are constructed to the same length as the original; when the original spacing exceeds twenty feet, the widened section uses plain cement concrete and joint spacing shall be limited to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet, adding intermediate joints as necessary. Jointed plain cement‑concrete pavements (JPCP) shall be widened with plain cement concrete, whereas jointed reinforced cement‑concrete pavements (JRCP) may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete. Full lane additions that are at least ten feet wide shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic loads require a different design, and all widening should be tied to the existing pavement. For base‑course widening where the added width is six feet or less, forms are not required, though acceptable methods must be used to contain the concrete. Reinforcement is omitted because references to reinforcement in both slip‑form paving and handling/placing reinforcement sections have been deleted. Where longitudinal joints are indicated or formed for construction convenience, they shall be formed as tied joints per the standard drawings, and sawing is not required for full‑width widening. Transverse joint requirements defer to Section 501.3(i)1. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 67–70), Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 205–206)

When a pavement is widened the treatment of the interfaces follows the procedures specified for rehabilitation in the applicable material category. For asphalt‑based projects any existing longitudinal and transverse joints are cleaned and sealed, heavy‑duty membranes may be placed where required, an asphalt tack coat is applied if necessary before the new layer is placed, and saw cuts are made and sealed over transverse and patch joints to secure proper bonding. For concrete‑based projects the design calls for cleaning and sealing all longitudinal joints and pavement/shoulder joints, provision of a continuous pavement base drain, adjustment or replacement of guide rail and drainage structures as necessary, and use of fill widening to retain proper shoulder widths and check bridge under clearances. Where rigid pavements are widened or lane additions are provided the new portion shall be constructed with Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted, and it must match the existing pavement type and depth. Full lane additions that are ten feet or wider shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic dictates a different design; partial widenings less than ten feet shall simply replicate the existing pavement. Transverse joints in the widened portion shall be aligned with those on the original pavement; if the original joint spacing is twenty feet or less, slabs of the same length are used, and when the original spacing exceeds twenty feet the widening shall use JPCP with joint lengths adjusted to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet, adding intermediate joints as needed so slab lengths remain approximately equal. For partial lane widening under ten feet the new asphalt must be built to the same structural composition as the existing pavement, using an equal depth of asphalt where the original is asphalt and an equal thickness of concrete where the original is concrete, and the drainage layers should be kept consistent with those already in place. For full‑width lane additions of ten feet or more the design should follow the existing pavement structure unless traffic conditions require a heavier or lighter design; in such cases layer thicknesses are sized for the actual traffic while still verifying that adequate drainage is provided throughout the cross section. When widening a plain cement concrete base course forms are omitted for widths of six feet or less but acceptable containment methods must still be used. Any longitudinal joints that are provided for construction convenience shall be tied joints as shown on the standard drawings, and full‑width sections do not require sawing. Transverse joints follow the provisions of Section 501.3(i)1. The surface is finished with a rake or broom texture, and curing may employ black liquid membrane forming compound only if the base course will not be opened to traffic before the overlying surface course is placed. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 67–70), Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96), Ch. 5 (p. 115); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 205–206)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

In the concrete rehabilitation and overlay specifications that include fill widening, the design calls for a continuous pavement base drain and requires any guide‑rail or drainage structures to be adjusted or replaced as necessary so that the widened shoulder maintains proper width and bridge clearances. [1]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 69–70)

The Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242, May 2015 Edition, Change No. 10) requires fill widening to retain proper shoulder widths and, where applicable, to use Type 6 or Type 7 paved‑shoulder classifications. It also mandates a leveling course of at least one inch, including cross‑slope correction, when performing fill widening. Guide rail and drainage structures shall be adjusted or replaced as necessary to maintain safety during the widening process. For concrete rehabilitation, the same manual requires verification that bridge under‑clearances are adequate in addition to retaining proper shoulder widths. Under section 4.8 of the same manual, any full lane addition that is ten feet or greater shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic loads require design for actual traffic, while a partial lane widening of less than ten feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth. The widened portion shall use Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption applies; JPCP widenings shall be constructed with plain cement concrete, and JRCP may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete. Transverse joints on the widening or lane addition shall be aligned to those on the original pavement, and when the original joint spacing is twenty feet or less the slab lengths shall equal the original; if it exceeds twenty feet the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet with intermediate joints added so that slab lengths are approximately equal. For asphalt pavements, section 5.11 specifies that partial lane widening of less than ten feet must match the existing pavement structure and, as much as possible, keep drainage layers consistent with those of the original pavement; when a full‑width lane addition of ten feet or more is provided it also should match the existing pavement structure unless circumstances dictate heavier or lighter traffic loadings for the additional lane, in which case the design must reflect those actual traffic loads. It is important when changing the pavement layer thicknesses to check that drainage is adequately provided throughout the pavement cross section. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION Publication 408/2026 SPECIFICATIONS states that for base‑course widening projects forms are not required when the expansion is six feet wide or less, provided an acceptable method is used to contain the concrete; expansions exceeding six feet shall employ formwork. Longitudinal joints placed for construction convenience shall be tied joints, and full‑width work does not require sawing. A white liquid membrane forming compound may not be used for curing, while a black liquid membrane forming compound may be used only when the cement concrete base course will not be accessible to traffic before the surface course is placed. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 3 (pp. 67–70), Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96), Ch. 5 (p. 115); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 205–206)
