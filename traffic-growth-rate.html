Traffic Growth Rate

Part of: Traffic Loading is-subtopic-of
Traffic Growth Rate is a topic under Traffic Loading.
traffic-growth-factorprojected-traffic-growthannual-truck-volume-growth-rateanticipated-truck-volume-growth-percentage
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

Traffic Growth Rate is the annual percent growth of truck traffic volume incorporated into the rigorous ESAL calculation so that pavement structures can be sized to accommodate projected increases in loading over their design life, thereby preventing premature failure[1]. It is entered as a uniform percentage for all vehicle classes—unless justified by historical evidence or traffic studies—and processed through DARWin’s compound‑growth algorithm to project daily 18‑kip ESALs across the required structural design period of at least eight and no more than twenty years[1]. By reflecting anticipated truck volume growth, the factor ensures that localized heavy‑truck routes, industrial‑complex connections, and overlay designs remain structurally adequate throughout their performance period[1].

Purpose and design objectives

The Traffic Growth Rate, expressed as the annual percent growth of truck traffic volume, is applied in the rigorous ESAL calculation for a project’s performance period so that the pavement structure can be sized to accommodate the projected increase in loading over its design life and thus avoid premature failure. The primary performance need addressed by this factor is the ability of a pavement to sustain future increases in heavy‑truck traffic, particularly on localized routes and industrial connections where the standard 18‑kip ESAL factor may be inaccurate. Districts are responsible for identifying those routes that require special attention and obtaining from industry the type of truck traffic, axle load weight, distribution and type, current truck volume, and anticipated truck volume growth percentage; this information is then used to evaluate the daily 18‑kip ESALs so that the pavement design reflects the expected rise in load repetitions. All vehicle classes should use the same growth rate unless historical evidence and/or traffic studies justify otherwise, ensuring a consistent basis for estimating future 18‑kip ESALs. For overlay designs, the projected increase in traffic volume must be incorporated together with current Average Daily Traffic and truck percentage so that the pavement remains structurally adequate for the required design life (minimum 8-year and maximum 20-year) and to prevent premature structural failure of pavements being upgraded for ride quality or suspected inadequacy. [1]

[1] — Ch. 7 (pp. 154–159), Ch. 10 (pp. 176–177)

Scope and applicability

Traffic Growth Rate is applied when calculating 18‑kip ESALs for any functional classification of highway, and the Rigorous Method must be employed unless a Simple Method is permitted; the Simple Method may be used on collectors and local roads provided that the resulting layer thicknesses do not exceed the minimum values for those road types, otherwise the Rigorous Method (and its growth rate) must be employed. For all vehicle classes the same annual percent growth of truck traffic volume should be used unless specific evidence justifies a different rate. On cross routes that interchange with limited‑access freeways, if the projected design‑year ADT exceeds 8,000 the Rigorous Method – and thus the growth rate – is required, while lower volumes may rely on minimum daily ESAL tables. Traffic Growth Rate analysis is also appropriate for localized routes that carry heavy truck traffic, especially when the standard 18‑kip ESAL factor may be incorrect; such cases often involve industrial complex connections and require the districts to identify these routes and obtain detailed information on truck types, axle loads, distribution, current volumes, and anticipated volume growth percentages. In overlay design, projected traffic growth is required for any pavement overlay—whether the project seeks to improve ride quality, correct suspected structural inadequacy, or remedy low‑friction values—and it applies to all seven overlay types such as AC overlay of AC pavement, AC overlay of fractured PCC slab, and bonded or unbonded concrete overlays. All overlay designs shall be designed for a minimum 8‑year structural design life and a maximum of 20 years, and the traffic input provides the minimum information needed: current ADT, projected traffic growth, and the truck percentage. [1]

In section 7.1 TRAFFIC ANALYSIS FOR PAVEMENT DESIGN, the Simple Method that relies on a traffic growth rate should be abandoned when any of several conditions are encountered. If the pavement layer thicknesses calculated by the Simple Method would exceed the minimum layer thicknesss prescribed for the roadway type, if rehabilitation structural needs surpass the existing pavement’s capacity, if the project is located on a road other than a collector or local road, if a cross‑route interchange’s design‑year average daily traffic exceeds 8 000, or if an interchange area is fully developed or traffic is expected to decline, then the Rigorous ESAL calculation method becomes the preferred design approach. Likewise, when a route is localized and carries heavy truck traffic—particularly industrial‑complex connections—the standard 18‑kip ESAL factor may not represent actual loads; under those circumstances the usual traffic growth rate approach becomes unsuitable and a design method that uses axle‑load‑specific ESAL values from Appendix C should be employed. The districts must identify such routes, obtain detailed truck type, axle load, weight distribution, current volume and projected growth data from the industry, and coordinate with the BPR to evaluate daily 18‑kip ESALs while accounting for trucks counted twice and any empty‑return loads. [1]

[1] — Ch. 7 (pp. 154–159)

Design inputs and requirements

According to the traffic analysis requirements set out in Section 7.1 of the pavement design guidance, the annual percent growth of truck traffic volume shall be supplied by the Bureau of Planning and Research and applied uniformly to all vehicle classes unless historical evidence or traffic studies justify otherwise; the annual percent growth in truck factor shall be 0% unless justified by historical evidence or loading studies. The compound growth rate calculation method in DARWin must be used, and a directional distribution factor of 50% shall be used unless the BPR directs otherwise or traffic data show this not to be the case (with 100% applied for one‑way ADT counts). For cross routes that interchange with limited‑access freeways, the Rigorous Method must be employed when the design‑year ADT exceeds 8,000. The same section assigns the districts the responsibility to identify routes that require special attention and to obtain from the industry involved the type of truck traffic, axle load weight, distribution and type, current truck volume, and anticipated truck volume growth percentage; this effort may require coordination with the BPR. For localized heavy‑truck routes, the standard 18‑kip ESAL factor can be replaced by the values in Appendix C when determining daily 18‑kip ESALs. In the overlay design process described in Section 10.1 General, traffic growth must be taken from the best available data and is listed as one of the minimum traffic inputs together with current ADT and truck percentage; when district data are lacking it shall be requested from the Bureau of Planning and Research. The projected growth is applied over a structural design life that is constrained to at least 8 years and not more than 20 years, which defines the period for which the growth factor is used, and the resulting daily 18‑kip ESALs derived from this traffic information must be determined in accordance with Chapter 7. [1]

[1] — Ch. 7 (pp. 154–159), Ch. 10 (pp. 176–177)

Design methods and criteria

Traffic Growth Rate is incorporated by entering an annual percentage growth value for truck traffic volume—applied uniformly to all vehicle classes unless justified otherwise—and then applying the compound growth rate calculation method within DARWin’s Rigorous ESAL Calculation (or, when the Simple Method is used, as the Annual Truck Volume Growth Rate). The required tool for this computation is the DARWin software, which performs the compound growth calculations needed to project future 18‑kip ESALs over the design period. [1]

[1] — Ch. 7 (pp. 154–157)

The Traffic Growth Rate shall be expressed as the annual percent growth of truck traffic volume and must be applied using the compound growth‑rate calculation method in DARWin; it shall be the same for all vehicle classes unless historical evidence or traffic studies justify a different rate. When the Simple Method is used, the Annual Truck Factor Growth Rate Percentage shall be set to 0 % while the Annual Truck Volume Growth Rate represents the expected yearly increase over the design life. [1] — Ch. 7 (pp. 154–157)
