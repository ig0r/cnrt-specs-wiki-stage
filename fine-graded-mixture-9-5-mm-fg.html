Fine-Graded Mixture (9.5 mm FG)

Part of: Superpave is-subtopic-of
Fine-Graded Mixture (9.5 mm FG) is a topic under Superpave.
fine-graded-mixfine-graded-mix9-5-mm-fine-graded-mixsp-9-5-mm-fg9-5-mm-fg
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

Fine‑Graded Mixture (9.5 mm FG) is a PennDOT‑specified Superpave asphalt wearing course that uses a fine‑graded aggregate blend limited to a nominal maximum size of 9.5 mm and whose volumetric mixture design is generated with the Superpave Gyratory Compactor before plant mixing for field placement [1][2]. It is intended primarily for thin functional overlays or as a one‑inch wearing layer in unbonded concrete overlays, providing a uniform protective top surface on sound pavements while not serving as the final surface course and allowing thicknesses below the typical 1½ in of a standard 9.5 mm Superpave mix [1][2]. The mix must be placed only on sound existing pavement, within the design‑specified depth limits and traffic‑volume criteria, and it cannot be paired with a PG 64E‑22 binder because that grade is incompatible with the FG mixture [1].

Material purpose and applicability

Fine‑Graded Mixture (9.5 mm FG) is a PennDOT‑specified Superpave Asphalt Wearing Course that uses a fine‑graded aggregate blend with a nominal maximum size of 9.5 mm and is produced as a plant‑mixed asphalt wearing course whose volumetric mixture design is developed with the Superpave Gyratory Compactor and then modified to achieve the fine‑graded distribution [1][2]. In the context of Section 5.12 OVERLAYS, this mix is intended for thin functional overlays and permits thicknesses less than the typical 1½ inch wearing course required by a standard 9.5 mm Superpave mix, allowing it to be placed as a scratch or leveling course on sound pavement surfaces to provide a uniform working platform and correct profile, although it shall not be used as the final surface course [1]. Under Section 10.7 UNBONDED PCC OVERLAY OF PCC PAVEMENT, the same Fine‑Graded mixture is typically placed at a nominal depth of 1 inch to serve as the standard wearing surface in unbonded PCC overlays, providing a uniform protective top layer and, when joint faulting exceeds ½ inch, it may be placed at the joint or the joint milled/grinded to help correct or accommodate joint movement [1]. More generally, when placed on a prepared surface as the top layer of a flexible pavement, the Fine‑Graded mixture provides the wearing surface for the pavement structure [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120), Ch. 10 (p. 185); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (p. 244)

The 9.5 mm Fine‑Grade (FG) Superpave mix may be used as a wearing course only on sound pavement surfaces, and its placement depth must follow the limits in Tables 9.5, 10.4 and 10.5; traffic volumes must fall within the ADT limits shown in Table 10.1 or 10.2, while scratch or leveling courses of the same mix may be used only together with a seal coat or other surface treatment but not as a pavement surface course themselves. When the existing pavement is not sound, when required ADT exceeds the table limits, or when a surface‑course application is needed, another material should be selected. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120)

Composition and characteristics

The Fine‑Graded (FG) 9.5 mm mix is defined as a Superpave asphalt wearing course whose aggregate blend is limited to a nominal maximum size of 9.5 mm and is graded to provide a fine texture suitable for thin functional overlays. In the context of section 5.12 OVERLAYS, this mix may be placed as a scratch or leveling course within the thickness limits of Table 10.4, but it shall only be applied on sound pavement surfaces and must not serve as the final surface course; additionally, the specified binder cannot be PG 64E‑22. Within section 10.7 UNBONDED PCC OVERLAY OF PCC PAVEMENT, the same 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix is typically placed to a nominal depth of 1 inch as the standard thickness for unbonded concrete overlays, and when existing concrete joints exhibit faulting greater than 1/2 inch the specification calls for a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) using this 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix at the joint. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120), Ch. 10 (p. 185)

Key material properties

In the unbonded PCC overlay design, the 9.5 mm Fine‑Graded mix is specified as a Superpave wearing course having a nominal depth of one inch [1]. When a geotextile interlayer is specified, concrete joints that exhibit greater than 1/4‑inch faulting are milled before placement of the fine‑graded overlay [1]. If joint faulting exceeds 1/2 inch, a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) composed of the same 9.5 mm Fine‑Graded mix is placed at the joint [1]. The Fine‑Graded (9.5 mm FG) mixture is characterized through a volumetric mixture design that is generated with the Superpave Gyratory Compactor, which defines the aggregate gradation and other required volumetric properties for the plant‑mixed asphalt wearing course described in Section 410.1 [2].

Durability and deterioration

The 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix is applied as a Superpave wearing course of nominal 1 inch thickness, but its performance depends on the amount of faulting in the underlying concrete joints; when joint faulting exceeds 1/4‑inch a geotextile interlayer must be used and the joints milled, and if faulting exceeds 1/2‑inch the design requires placing a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) 9.5 mm mix at the joint or milling/grinding the joints to reduce the faulting. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (p. 185)

The Fine‑Graded 9.5 mm mix used as a Superpave Wearing Course is more vulnerable when it is placed over concrete joints that have faulting greater than 1/4 inch, and especially when faulting exceeds 1/2 inch, because excessive joint movement can promote distress in the thin wearing layer; limiting faulting to less than or equal to 1/4 inch or milling/grinding joints to reduce faulting helps protect the mix. Providing proper drainage of the interlayer by daylighting it or installing a vertical subdrain also reduces moisture‑related deterioration. A nominal depth of 1 inch for this wearing course is the standard thickness, and these joint‑faulting thresholds and drainage measures together govern its durability. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (p. 185)

Material interactions and compatibility

The 9.5 mm Fine‑Grade (FG) Superpave wearing course is limited to sound pavement surfaces and must not be placed with PG 64E‑22 binder, indicating that this binder grade is incompatible with the FG mix; it also cannot serve as a surface course but may be used as a scratch or leveling layer and can be combined with a seal coat or other surface treatment, underscoring the need for correct binder selection and proper placement to avoid adhesion issues such as stripping. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120)

Influence of production and placement

Construction variations that most affect the long‑term behavior of the 9.5 mm fine‑graded mixture are related to concrete joint faulting: when joints have greater than 1/4‑inch faulting they must be milled before a geotextile interlayer is placed, and if joint faulting exceeds 1/2‑inch the design calls for placing a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) of the 9.5 mm mix at the joint or milling/grinding the joints to reduce the fault. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (p. 185)

Selection, specification, and improvement

In section 5.12 OVERLAYS, the selection of a 9.5 mm Fine‑Grade Superpave wearing course is guided by several design checks: the mix must meet the structural coefficient and the minimum and maximum placement depths shown in Tables 9.5, 10.4 and 10.5; the anticipated average daily traffic must fall within the limits of Table 10.1 or 10.2; it may be placed only on a sound existing pavement surface; the contract for maintenance or group projects must explicitly state the allowable depth range; the mix is limited to wearing‑course applications and cannot be used as a scratch or leveling surface course, although those courses can be combined with a seal coat; and the binder specified for this FG mix may not be PG 64E‑22. In section 10.7 UNBONDED PCC OVERLAY OF PCC PAVEMENT, the 9.5 mm fine‑graded mixture serves as the standard one‑inch Superpave wearing course over an unbonded PCC overlay; when a geotextile interlayer is specified, concrete joints must exhibit faulting greater than 1/4‑inch to be milled; if joint faulting exceeds 1/2‑inch, the design calls for placing a Superpave wearing course (scratch) of the same 9.5 mm mix at the joint or milling/grinding the joint to reduce faulting. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120), Ch. 10 (p. 185)

To enhance the performance of a Superpave Asphalt 9.5 mm Fine‑Grade (FG) wearing or leveling course, the guidelines require that it be placed only on sound pavement surfaces and that the minimum and maximum placement depths and structural coefficient numbers follow Tables 9.5, 10.4, and 10.5, with ADT limits observed from Table 10.1/10.2; the mix may be paired with a seal coat or other surface treatment to improve durability, while the specified PG 64E‑22 binder must not be used for this FG course, implying that an alternative binder should be selected. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120)

Performance evaluation and implications

For thin functional overlays, the 9.5 mm Fine‑Grade (FG) Superpave mix is intended to be placed at a thickness less than the typical 1½ in wearing course, which reduces structural depth in design and allows a thinner construction layer. However it may only be used on sound pavement surfaces, must not serve as the final surface course, and is limited to scratch or leveling applications within the minimum and maximum placement depths specified in the tables; any allowable depth is required to be stated in the contract, and it cannot be paired with PG 64E‑22 binder. In unbonded concrete overlays the design calls for a Superpave wearing course made from a 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix applied at a nominal depth of 1 in, which establishes the required overlay thickness and provides uniform support over the existing pavement. When existing concrete joints show faulting greater than ½ in, the same 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix is placed as a scratch wearing course directly on the joint or the joint is milled/grinded to bring faulting within acceptable limits, thereby influencing construction detail and maintenance of joint integrity. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120), Ch. 10 (p. 185)
