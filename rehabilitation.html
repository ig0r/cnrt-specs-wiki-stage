Rehabilitation

Part of: PennDOT Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242) is-subtopic-of
Rehabilitation is a topic under PennDOT Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242).
rehabilitationrehabilitations
Created 2026-10-23 · Updated 2026-10-26

Rehabilitation is the agency’s decision mechanism for selecting the most appropriate structural treatment to extend a pavement’s service life and improve its load‑carrying or rideability performance without undertaking major capacity expansions, realignments, or geometric upgrades [1]. The process begins with a comprehensive condition assessment that quantifies distress types, drainage, structural state, age, mileage out of cycle and recent surface work, after which feasible alternatives such as concrete or asphalt overlays, base‑layer additions, interlayers or recycling treatments are generated and evaluated through life‑cycle cost analysis to identify the option with the lowest present‑worth total cost [1]. For methods involving fracture‑and‑seat, the selected repair plan must satisfy agency performance criteria—including a detailed fracturing plan, equipment list and verification that the fractured concrete modulus falls within prescribed limits—before construction may proceed, ensuring that the chosen treatment both restores structural capacity and meets established performance standards [2].
