Crack Width

Part of: Cracking is-subtopic-of
Crack Width is a topic under Cracking.
crack-widthcrack-widthsaverage-crack-width
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

Crack width is the measured opening of a pavement crack, joint, or transverse joint that appears on the surface as an open linear gap[1][2]. It is observed on plain‑asphalt surfaces, composite asphalt‑concrete structures, rigid concrete slabs and other pavements where transverse, longitudinal or miscellaneous cracking occurs, with typical acceptance limits such as a Medium Rating of ≤ ½ inch for hot‑in‑place recycled (HIR) candidates or thresholds of ¼ inch in many PennDOT specifications[1][2]. The widening is primarily driven by heavy repeated traffic loading and variable support conditions that cause restrained shrinkage and slab movement, which can increase crack width and diminish load‑transfer efficiency and durability[1].

Description and identification

Crack width is the measured opening of a pavement crack, joint, or transverse joint that appears on the surface as an open linear gap. In the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242, Change No. 10) this term refers specifically to transverse or miscellaneous cracks observed on the pavement surface; for Hot‑In‑Place Recycled (HIR) candidate pavements the average crack width should not exceed the Medium Rating of ≤ 1/2 inch and the depth of such cracking must be less than 1.0 inch, with only surface‑only cracking considered acceptable, any deeper or wider cracking disqualifying the section from HIR treatment [1]. In Section 527—Dowel Retrofit, when a crack or joint width exceeds 1/4 inch the specification calls for expansion joint material rather than pre‑formed cellular polystyrene, and the saw cuts and slot preparation are centered over the existing crack or transverse joint [2]. Section 528—Concrete Pavement Cross‑Stitching treats a longitudinal crack or joint as a surface gap that may be routed to a width of 3/4 inch and a depth of 1 inch before sealing; within the slab the crack is considered to extend through the thickness and is intersected by drilled angled holes placed at approximately mid‑depth for tie‑bar installation [2]. Section 659—High Friction Surface Treatment requires that cracks at least 1/4 inch wide be cleaned of loose material with compressed air and pre‑treated with the mixed binder while avoiding overfilling [2]. Finally, Section 467—Heavy Duty Membranes mandates that visible openings wider than 1/4 inch be cleared of dirt and debris and then filled flush with the surrounding surface using PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28 prior to membrane placement [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 131–133); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (p. 321), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 432–435), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 581–583)

Crack width is recognized by measuring the opening of surface cracks [1][2]. For candidate hot‑in‑place recycled (HIR) projects the average crack width must stay within the Medium Rating of ≤ 1/2 inch and the associated transverse or miscellaneous cracking depth should be a depth < 1.0 inch; because only surface‑only distresses are permitted, these cracks are distinguished from load‑related distress such as rutting (limited to < 1 ½ inches) and from base problems, which are excluded from HIR eligibility [1]. In the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications for full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs, crack width is identified by measuring the opening; any opening wider than 0.007 inch that has a depth exceeding D/3 (where D is slab thickness) must be replaced, while an opening wider than 0.007 inch with a depth less than D/3 requires repair according to the latest version of Control of Cracking in Concrete Structures (ACI 224R), and openings of 0.007 inch or less are sealed using high‑molecular‑weight methacrylate, thereby separating these cracks from other distresses such as spalls covered elsewhere in the specifications [2]. For bonded concrete overlays on asphalt‑surfaced pavements, crack width is identified on the existing asphalt surface by measuring the opening; any opening exceeding three‑quarters of an inch triggers cleaning and filling with non‑shrink grout before the overlay, while intended transverse joints are created by saw cutting to a minimum width of 1/8 inch, and cracks that appear within 1½ feet of a planned joint cause the contractor to skip sawing at that spot, which distinguishes random cracking from designed joint locations [2]. In the construction of plant‑mixed 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlay courses, crack width is identified by measuring the opening of a pavement crack or joint; any crack or joint that exceeds 1/8 inch in width is filled and sealed, distinguishing these cracks from other distresses such as potholes or gouges that are addressed when their depth is greater than 1 inch [2]. For ultra‑thin bonded wearing courses, crack width is identified by measuring the opening of longitudinal and transverse joints; any crack that is 1/4‑inch or wider is classified for sealing with rubberized sealant, separating such cracks from smaller fissures, and also differentiating them from surface conditions like thermoplastic or tape traffic markings that exceed 0.2 inch in thickness, which are removed rather than sealed [2].

Where it occurs

Within the hot‑in‑place recycled (HIR) framework crack width is evaluated on plain asphalt surfaces as well as on composite pavements that consist of a concrete base with an asphalt overlay, and for HIR‑candidate projects the average crack width must not exceed the Medium Rating of ≤ 1/2 inch while the depth of transverse or miscellaneous cracking must be less than 1.0 inch; these limits apply to full‑depth asphalt pavements as well as to composite structures provided the underlying concrete is stable [1]. In the fiber‑reinforced concrete (FRC) overlay context crack width is a concern for plain‑concrete slabs and for asphalt or composite pavements that receive a concrete overlay, with crack openings greater than 1.0 mm associated with a 30 % increase in LTE and tighter openings of less than 0.5 to 1.0 mm reported to improve durability [1]. The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications evaluate crack‑width for a variety of rigid concrete pavements, composite asphalt‑concrete overlays, thin asphalt overlays, ultra‑thin wearing courses, shoulders and other adjacent structures [2]. For plain or reinforced cement‑concrete slabs any longitudinal crack within a lane triggers removal and replacement of the slab, while edge slump must not exceed 1/8 inch between adjacent lanes (or ramps) and 1/4 inch between lanes and shoulders; transverse joints and cracks in concrete shoulders or adjoining structures must be sealed [2]. Full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete slabs require replacement when a crack exceeds 0.007 inch in width and is deeper than one‑third of the slab thickness, whereas cracks wider than 0.007 inch but shallower than that depth are to be repaired according to ACI 224R [2]. In dowel retrofit work on rigid slabs slots are centered over a crack or joint and when the measured width is greater than 1/4 inch expansion‑joint material replaces the standard filler [2]. Section 528 mandates routing of longitudinal cracks or joints in concrete slabs to a width of 3/4 inch and a depth of 1 inch before sealing, and Section 590 requires saw‑cutting or grinding cracks to a width of 3/4 inch by 1 inch depth (or larger) prior to sealing [2]. When a bonded concrete overlay is placed on an existing concrete‑surfaced pavement the saw‑cut width must be at least as wide as any underlying joint or crack measured immediately before placement [2]. For high‑friction surface treatment applied to either asphalt or concrete pavements any crack 1/4 inch or greater in width must be cleaned and pre‑treated with mixed binder [2]. Flexible asphalt pavements receiving a heavy‑duty membrane overlay require cleaning of all joints and cracks, and any that exceed 1/4 inch in width are to be filled flush with the surface before placement [2]. Composite structures involving an existing asphalt base that will receive a bonded concrete overlay require that any crack wider than 3/4 inch be cleaned and filled with non‑shrink grout prior to overlay installation [2]. For thin 6.3‑mm plant‑mixed Superpave asphalt overlays all pavement cracks or joints exceeding 1/8 inch must be filled and sealed before placement, a requirement that extends to vertical elements such as curbs, structures, gutters and adjacent pavements [2]. Ultra‑thin bonded wearing courses applied to flexible pavements call for sealing of any longitudinal or transverse joint or crack that is 1/4 inch wide or larger at least 24 hours before paving [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 131–133), Ch. 10 (pp. 186–188); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 250–345), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–475), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 581–583)

Within hot‑in‑place recycled (HIR) candidate pavements, crack‑width distress is confined to the pavement surface; only surface‑level cracks whose depth is less than 1 inch and whose average width does not exceed the Medium Rating of ≤ ½ inch are permitted, and these include transverse and miscellaneous cracking as well as longitudinal and transverse joints where sealant may be applied, indicating that the measurement is made at the surface rather than within lifts, layer interfaces, or the base/subgrade. In fiber‑reinforced concrete overlays the crack‑width issue is most critical at the contraction joints cut into the slab and at any surface shrinkage cracks; macrofibers typically keep joint openings tighter than 0.5 to 1.0 mm, and when crack widths exceed about 1.0 mm the load‑transfer efficiency of the overlay declines, although fibers can raise LTE by roughly 30 % compared with plain concrete. In slip‑form concrete pavements the manifestation of crack width is observed as longitudinal cracks that develop within a lane and as any crack or spall that may appear anywhere in the concrete slab. The dowel‑retrofit procedure assesses crack width at cracks or transverse joints located within each wheel path, requiring three dowel slots per wheel path to be cut and centered over the crack or joint with saw cuts aligned parallel to the roadway centerline. Cross‑stitching limits consideration of crack width to longitudinal cracks or longitudinal joints that run along the length of a concrete slab and are situated within the project limits, explicitly excluding transverse joints or cracks from this treatment. The ultra‑thin bonded wearing course addresses crack width for cracks that appear on the existing pavement surface, specifically those located along longitudinal and transverse joints, and any such joint reaching a width of ¼ inch or greater must be sealed prior to paving. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 131–133), Ch. 10 (pp. 186–188); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (p. 345), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–435)

Causes and mechanisms

According to Chapter 10, Subsection 10.10, crack‑width concerns in concrete overlays are linked primarily to heavy repeated traffic loading and variable support conditions that occur in higher‑traffic areas; the source does not attribute crack width to climate, moisture, material deficiencies, or design/construction errors. The benefits of fiber‑reinforced concrete are described as reducing crack widths and limiting the number of cracks under restrained shrinkage, with tighter joint openings (less than 0.5 to 1.0 mm) improving durability. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (pp. 186–188)

Crack width in concrete overlays expands as restrained shrinkage and joint opening occur under repeated traffic loading; plastic shrinkage cracking and slab movement further widen existing cracks, especially when the overlay experiences heavy repeated loads on variable support conditions. Conditions that accelerate this progression include higher traffic areas with more heavy repeated loadings and variable support conditions, which increase the tendency for crack widening unless mitigated by fiber reinforcement. Incorporating macrofibers makes the overlay material more resistant to crack growth, helping to limit crack width development. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (pp. 186–188)

Severity levels and rating

For hot‑in‑place recycled (HIR) asphalt candidate pavements, the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242 Change No. 10), Section 5.19 C, specifies that crack width severity is limited to a Medium Rating of ≤ 1/2 inch; the average crack width must not exceed this value [1]. In the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Transportation Specification Publication 408/2026, Section 529.3 Construction for full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs defines three crack‑width severity categories: cracks that are 0.007 inches wide or less are to be sealed with high‑molecular‑weight methacrylate; cracks wider than 0.007 inches but whose depth is less than one‑third of the slab thickness (D/3) require repair in accordance with the latest ACI 224R recommendations; and cracks wider than 0.007 inches and deeper than D/3 mandate full‑slab replacement at no additional cost to the Department [2]. For heavy‑duty membrane installations, Section 467.3 Construction establishes a single treatment threshold: any joint or crack that exceeds 1/4 inch in width must be filled flush to the surface with the specified PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28 material before the membrane is placed [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 131–133); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (p. 321), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 438–447)

In the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242, Change No. 10), section 5.19 on Hot‑In‑Place Recycled Asphalt, candidate HIR projects are evaluated using the Pub. 336 criteria for transverse and miscellaneous cracking, which assign a Medium Rating that requires the average crack width not to exceed ½ inch [1]. The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation Publication 408/2026 specifications, section 529.3 (item 11.b Cracks), assign crack‑width severity by referencing the Control of Cracking in Concrete Structures (ACI 224R) and treat any crack wider than 0.007 inches as a higher‑severity condition [2]. Under that procedure, if such a crack is deeper than one‑third of the slab depth (D/3) the slab must be replaced at no additional cost to the Department; if its depth is less than D/3 it must be repaired in accordance with the latest ACI 224R recommendations [2]. Cracks that are 0.007 inches wide or less are to be sealed using high molecular weight methacrylate [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 131–133); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 438–447)

Effects on performance

Fiber‑reinforced concrete overlays mitigate the adverse effects of wide cracks by providing additional structural capacity and by keeping joint or crack load‑transfer efficiency high, which together extend the pavement’s serviceability period. The tighter joint and crack openings—typically less than 0.5 to 1.0 mm—are associated with improved durability because FRC is more resistant to crack growth and reduces crack deterioration. By limiting crack width, FRC also helps control slab movement and reduces plastic shrinkage cracking, further supporting a longer service life. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (pp. 186–188)

If a crack or spall appears before final inspection it is treated as a defect; the specification requires repair according to Table A and, if not corrected, the slab must be removed and replaced under Section 501.3(t) as defective work. When a longitudinal crack widens to 1/8‑inch or greater the contract calls for eliminating one tie bar in each adjacent slab (Section 501.3(j)2), and any crack that develops within a lane triggers removal of the entire cracked slab (Section 501.3(l)). Thus an untreated crack can progress from a simple surface defect to tie‑bar loss, slab replacement, and ultimately classification as defective pavement requiring full reconstruction. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–375)

Repairs and treatments

According to the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242, Change No. 10), hot‑in‑place recycled (HIR) overlays may address crack width by applying a non‑extensive crack sealant to longitudinal and transverse joints; this approach is limited to projects in which the pavement’s average crack width does not exceed the Medium Rating of ≤ ½ inch, which serves as a selection criterion for candidate HIR pavements (section 5.19) [1]. The same manual also specifies that fiber‑reinforced concrete (FRC) overlays or inlays directly treat crack width by reducing the openings and preserving joint or crack load‑transfer efficiency; FRC can achieve tighter joint and crack openings of less than 0.5 to 1.0 mm, and it is recommended where a thinner slab is required, traffic loading is heavy, support conditions are variable, an increased design or service life is desired, and especially when existing crack widths exceed 1.0 mm (section 10.10) [1].

The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications provide numerous treatments that target crack width under defined conditions. The slip‑form paving method (Section 501.3(g)) calls for eliminating one tie bar in each slab adjacent to a joint when cracks have opened 1/8‑inch or greater, and Section 501.3(j) requires removal and replacement of any slab containing a longitudinal crack within a lane at no additional cost to the Department; Sections 501.3(i) and 501.3(t) further mandate that any crack or spall be repaired before final inspection regardless of measured width (Section 501) [2]. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs, Section 529.3 defines three crack‑width–based actions: when a crack is wider than 0.007 in and deeper than one‑third of the slab thickness (D/3), the entire slab must be replaced; if the same width occurs but the depth is less than D/3, repair shall follow the latest Control of Cracking in Concrete Structures (ACI 224R); and when a crack is 0.007 in wide or less, sealing with high‑molecular‑weight methacrylate is required (Section 529) [2]. The dowel retrofit procedure (Section 527.3) treats cracks or transverse joints by cutting three dowel slots per wheel path centered over the defect and installing a temporary joint material that matches the existing opening; when the measured crack or joint width exceeds 1/4 inch, expansion‑joint material must be used instead of the preformed cellular polystyrene filler (Section 527) [2]. Cross‑stitching of concrete pavement longitudinal joints (Section 528.3) may only be applied to longitudinal cracks or joints, is prohibited on transverse ones, and work may not commence if either ambient air temperature or pavement surface temperature is below 40 °F; after epoxy‑bonded tie bars are placed, the joint must be routed to a width of 3/4 inch and a depth of 1 inch before sealant is applied, and the sealant reservoir is filled to a level 1/8‑inch to 1/4‑inch below the pavement surface (Section 528) [2]. For high‑friction surface treatment preparation (Section 659.3(e)), any crack at least ¼‑inch wide must be pre‑treated with the mixed binder after loose material is removed, the binder must not be overfilled, and this pre‑treatment is required before the HFST binder gels and the treatment is placed (Section 659) [2].

The crack cleaning and sealing method (Section 590.3) addresses crack width on rigid pavements by first sawing or grinding cracks to a width of 3/4 inch and a depth of 1 inch; if the original crack measures between 3/4‑inch and 1 inch wide, it must be widened to 1 inch with a depth of 1¼ inches before sealing, and sealant may not be placed unless both ambient air temperature and pavement temperature are at least 40 °F, the faces are clean and dry, and the sealant reservoir is filled to a level between 1/8‑inch and 1/4‑inch below the surface (Section 590) [2]. The bonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 545.3) permits minor surface defects associated with cracks to be replaced with an overlay after scarifying, cleaning, and installing joint backing material within ±¼ inch of the surface; transverse joints must be saw‑cut directly over all existing cracks once concrete strength allows cutting without significant raveling or chipping, and the cut width shall be at least the width of the underlying crack (Section 545) [2]. Heavy‑duty membranes (Section 467.3) require pre‑membrane filling of joints or cracks that exceed a quarter‑inch opening; these wider cracks must be filled flush to the surface with PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28 after dirt and debris are removed, ensuring a sound substrate for the membrane (Section 467) [2]. The bonded concrete overlay of asphalt‑surfaced pavement (Section 540) mandates that any crack wider than three‑quarters of an inch be cleaned and filled with non‑shrink grout or another Department‑approved material before the overlay is placed, and if a crack occurs at or within 1½ feet of a planned joint location, the sawing operation must be omitted and the crack dealt with according to Section 590 (Section 540) [2]. The Superpave thin‑asphalt overlay (Section 412) requires that during preparation of the existing surface all pavement cracks or joints exceeding 1/8 inch in width be filled and sealed with fillers and sealants meeting PennDOT specifications, thereby addressing crack widths greater than the 1/8‑inch threshold (Section 412) [2]. Finally, the ultra‑thin bonded wearing course (Section 489) specifies that at least 24 hours before paving operations longitudinal and transverse joints and cracks measuring 1/4‑inch and wider shall be sealed with the rubberized sealant called out in Section 469; the sealant over‑band thickness shall be minimized and shall not exceed the tolerances specified in Section 469, thus addressing crack widths of a quarter‑inch or greater (Section 489) [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 131–133), Ch. 10 (pp. 186–188); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 250–345), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–475), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 581–583)

Hot‑In‑Place Recycled asphalt may be applied only when cracks are limited to the surface, are transverse or miscellaneous in type, have a depth less than 1 inch, and an average crack width that does not exceed the medium rating of ≤ ½ inch; if either the depth or width exceeds these limits the HIR treatment is not considered acceptable. In full‑depth precast concrete pavement slabs, a crack wider than 0.007 inches and penetrating deeper than one‑third of the slab thickness requires replacement of the entire slab (Section 529). If the same width exceeds 0.007 inches but its depth is less than one‑third of the slab thickness, the crack may be repaired according to ACI 224R recommendations (Section 529). Cracks that are 0.007 inches wide or narrower may be sealed with high‑molecular‑weight methacrylate (Section 529). For a dowel retrofit, when the crack or joint width exceeds 1/4 inch the method calls for using expansion joint material instead of the standard preformed cellular polystyrene filler (Section 527‑3). Cross‑stitching is permitted only on longitudinal cracks or joints and is expressly forbidden on transverse joints or cracks; before sealing, a longitudinal crack or joint must be routed to a width of 3/4 inch and a depth of 1 inch (Section 528‑3). In the high‑friction surface treatment specification, any crack that is at least ¼ inch wide must be pre‑treated with the mixed binder prior to placing the HFST, while cracks narrower than this threshold are not required to receive the binder pre‑treatment (Section 659‑3(e)). Crack sealing may be applied only after cracks have been sawed or ground to a width of 3/4 inch with a depth of 1 inch, or, if the original crack is between 3/4 inch and 1 inch wide, to a width of 1 inch and a depth of 1 1/4 inches (Section 590‑3). For a bonded concrete overlay on concrete‑surfaced pavements, the transverse joint must be saw‑cut with a width that is at least equal to the width of any existing joint or crack; a cut narrower than the underlying crack may cause debonding and render the overlay unacceptable (Section 545‑3). In heavy‑duty membrane treatment, any joint or crack wider than 1/4 inch must be filled flush with PG‑64S‑22 or PG‑58S‑28 sealant before the membrane is placed; only when this condition is met is the membrane application considered acceptable (Section 467‑3). For a bonded concrete overlay of an asphalt‑surfaced pavement, any existing crack wider than 3/4 inch must be cleaned and filled with non‑shrink grout or another Department‑approved material before the overlay is placed, so the overlay treatment is applicable only when cracks exceeding that width are repaired in this manner (Section 540‑3(e)). In the Superpave thin‑asphalt overlay, any pavement crack or joint that exceeds 1/8 inch in width must be filled and sealed with PennDOT‑specified fillers before the 6.3 mm wearing course is placed, while cracks narrower than that are not required to receive this treatment (Section 413.3(g) of Section 412). For the ultra‑thin bonded wearing course, any longitudinal or transverse joint and any crack that is 1/4‑inch wide or wider must be sealed with the rubberized sealant referenced in Section 469 at least 24 hours before paving; cracks narrower than 1/4‑inch are not addressed (Section 413.3(g) of Section 489). Prevention in design and construction

Incorporating macro‑fiber‑reinforced concrete (FRC) into a pavement overlay is identified as a design measure that reduces crack widths—including plastic shrinkage cracking and crack widening—and can achieve joint or crack openings less than 0.5 to 1.0 mm; this benefit applies when a thinner slab is required, in higher‑traffic or variable‑support conditions, or where an increased design or service life is desired for bonded overlays on asphalt or composite pavements with overlay thicknesses ranging from 3 to 6 in. The same FRC measure also increases load‑transfer efficiency especially when crack widths are greater than 1.0 mm, and it requires proper timing and depth of saw‑cut contraction joints so that the FRC joints activate early enough to avoid premature cracking and dominant joints, thereby further controlling crack width [1]. The slip‑form paving method calls for eliminating one tie bar in each slab adjacent to a joint where cracks have opened 1/8 inch or greater, and also for eliminating one tie bar in slabs adjacent to dominant joints when paving between October 1 and April 1; these actions are intended to limit shear cracking and thereby restrict crack width [2]. Sawing tied longitudinal contraction joints as soon as the concrete has attained sufficient hardness to allow sawing without excessive raveling is prescribed to prevent random cracking that would increase crack width [2]. In dowel‑retrofit work, when a measured crack or joint is 1/4 inch thick or less the slot shall be filled with a 1/4‑inch‑thick preformed cellular polystyrene filler, whereas if the observed crack or joint exceeds 1/4 inch an expansion‑joint material whose thickness matches the larger opening shall be used, directly controlling the repaired joint width [2]. Cross‑stitching of longitudinal pavement joints or cracks ties adjacent slabs together with epoxy‑bonded tie bars; it may be employed only on longitudinal joints/cracks, only when ambient air temperature or pavement surface temperature is at least 40 °F, and only after the crack has been routed to a width of 3/4 inch and a depth of 1 inch, establishing a prescribed maximum opening and limiting further widening [2]. During high‑friction surface treatment preparation any existing pavement cracks that are 1/4 inch or greater in width shall be cleaned and pre‑treated with the mixed binder, allowing the binder to gel before HFST placement, which is intended to limit further opening of the crack [2]. For bonded concrete overlays, transverse joints shall be saw‑cut to a width at least as wide as any underlying joint or existing crack once the concrete has achieved sufficient strength for cutting without significant raveling or chipping, a requirement that prevents debonding and limits crack widening [2]. Under heavy‑duty membranes all joints or cracks wider than ¼ inch shall be filled flush with the surface using PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28 after dirt and debris are removed, directly addressing cracks exceeding the quarter‑inch threshold [2]. In bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavements existing cracks greater than ¾ inch shall be cleaned and filled with non‑shrink grout during surface preparation, a step intended to eliminate large pre‑existing openings that could propagate into wide cracks [2]. Saw cuts are required to be made with a green‑cut saw to a depth of one‑third the slab thickness and a minimum width of 1/8 inch as soon as the concrete can be cut without excessive raveling, providing controlled joints that replace uncontrolled crack widening; sawing is omitted if a crack occurs within 1.5 ft of a planned joint location [2]. When preparing the existing surface for a thin asphalt overlay any pavement cracks or joints exceeding 1/8 inch shall be filled and sealed with PennDOT‑specified fillers, a measure that prevents excessive crack width from propagating into the new overlay [2]. Longitudinal and transverse joints and any cracks measuring 1/4‑inch or wider must be sealed with a rubberized sealant at least 24 hours before paving an ultra‑thin bonded wearing course, this measure being intended to prevent further widening of those existing cracks [2].

The Hot‑In‑Place Recycled overlay guidelines require that the average transverse or miscellaneous crack width of a candidate HIR overlay not exceed the Medium Rating of ≤ 1/2 inch, and this limit is used as a selection criterion to prevent excessive crack opening in the finished surface. The same section permits the use of crack sealant on longitudinal and transverse joints—while extensive crack sealant and over‑banded rubberized joint seal are not acceptable—as a construction practice intended to keep existing cracks from widening during placement. Milling prior to the HIR process is recommended because it can treat deeper cracks and preserve vertical clearance for structures such as curbs or guide rails, which further reduces the likelihood of larger crack openings. In Fiber‑Reinforced Concrete overlay guidance, incorporating synthetic or steel macro‑fibers at a dosage of 0.2 % to 0.5 % by volume is identified as the material requirement that mitigates excessive crack width, with the fibers reducing crack widening, limiting plastic shrinkage cracking, and maintaining joint or crack longitudinal–transverse expansion within tighter openings generally less than 0.5 to 1.0 mm. The specifications require that tie bars be eliminated adjacent to joints where cracks have opened 1/8‑inch or greater. When paving between October 1 and April 1 the contractor shall eliminate one tie bar in each slab next to dominant joints, and longitudinal contraction joints must be sawed as prescribed to prevent random cracking that could produce undesirable crack widths. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete slabs any crack wider than 0.007 in and deeper than one‑third of the slab thickness shall be replaced, whereas a crack of the same width but shallower than D/3 shall be repaired in accordance with ACI 224R; cracks 0.007 in wide or less may be sealed with a high molecular weight methacrylate sealant. When a dowel retrofit is performed, if an existing crack or transverse joint exceeds 1/4 inch the specification requires that expansion joint material be used instead of the preformed cellular polystyrene filler. During concrete pavement cross‑stitching longitudinal cracks or joints must be routed to a width of 3/4 inch and a depth of 1 inch before sealing, and this treatment may only be placed after the faces have been flushed with water, cleaned, and when both ambient air temperature and pavement surface temperature are above 40°F. Cross‑stitching is prohibited on transverse joints or cracks. A hydraulic powered drill that minimizes damage to the concrete surface shall be used for all tie‑bar installations in longitudinal joints; drilled hole diameter shall not exceed 3/8 inch larger than the tie bar, holes shall not extend through the slab bottom, and thorough cleaning with oil‑free air at at least 100 psi is required. For high‑friction surface treatment any existing pavement crack that is 1/4 inch or greater in width must be pre‑treated with the mixed binder after loose material has been removed with compressed air, and overfilling of these cracks is prohibited. In crack cleaning and sealing work cracks are first sawed or ground to a controlled width—normally ¾ inch wide by 1 inch deep, or when the existing opening lies between ¾ inch and 1 inch to 1‑inch wide by 1¼ inches deep—to limit crack width before sealing; faces must then be thoroughly cleaned and if compressed‑air is used it shall be at least 100 psi measured at the source. Joint sealant may only be placed when both ambient air temperature and pavement temperature are 40°F or greater, and the sealant reservoir shall be filled to a level of 1/8 inch to 1/4 inch below the pavement surface. When a bonded concrete overlay is installed transverse joints shall be saw‑cut to a width at least equal to the existing joint or crack width in order to prevent debonding, and this may be done only after the concrete has attained sufficient strength to allow cutting without significant raveling or chipping. For heavy‑duty membranes any joint or crack wider than ¼ inch shall be filled flush with PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28 during surface preparation after debris removal and before the membrane is placed. Existing surface cracks larger than 3/4 in shall be cleaned and filled with non‑shrink grout before the milled asphalt is overlaid, a measure intended to limit excessive crack widths in a bonded concrete overlay. Joints shall be laid out and sawed as soon as the concrete has hardened enough to avoid random cracking; panel dimensions shall not exceed 18 times the overlay thickness and the aspect‑ratio shall not exceed 1.5, with longitudinal joints avoided in wheel paths, thereby preventing crack widening caused by uncontrolled panel geometry. Proper curing shall include placement of a curing compound within 30 minutes of bleed‑water dissipation, provision of insulating blankets when air temperature is ≤45°F, and limiting surface temperature change to no more than 40°F per hour, which serves to prevent thermal stresses that would widen cracks. All pavement cracks or joints wider than 1/8 inch shall be filled and sealed with PennDOT‑specified fillers and sealants before the thin asphalt wearing course is placed, a practice aimed at preventing excessive crack width from remaining in the overlay. Longitudinal and transverse joints and cracks that are 1/4‑inch or wider shall be sealed at least 24 hours before paving with the rubberized sealant specified in Section 469, while minimizing over‑band thickness and width and not exceeding the tolerances of Section 469, a requirement intended to prevent further crack widening. Specification limits and acceptance

For candidate HIR asphalt projects, the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242, Change No. 10) requires that the average crack width shall not exceed the Medium Rating of ≤ ½ inch; this is judged by measuring the average width of transverse and miscellaneous cracks during a Pub. 336 distress evaluation. In the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation Specification Section 529—Full‑Depth Precast/Prestressed Concrete Pavement Slabs, clause 11.b any crack measured wider than 0.007 inch triggers action, with cracks whose depth exceeds one‑third of the slab thickness (D⁄3) requiring replacement and cracks wider than 0.007 inch but with depth less than D⁄3 to be repaired in accordance with ACI 224R; cracks 0.007 inch wide or less are sealed with high‑molecular‑weight methacrylate, and the measurement is made by directly surveying crack width and depth on the installed slab. Section 527—Dowel Retrofit of the same PennDOT specifications states that any existing crack or transverse joint whose measured width exceeds ¼ inch must be treated with expansion‑joint material rather than the standard preformed cellular polystyrene filler, establishing a maximum acceptable width of 0.25 inch before an alternative material is required; the width is judged by measurement. Section 528—Concrete Pavement Cross‑Stitching requires that a longitudinal crack or joint be routed to a width of ¾ inch before sealant is placed, and this dimension governs the measurement and approval of the crack during the sealing step. Section 659—High Friction Surface Treatment (HFST) surface preparation calls for any crack that is ¼ inch or greater in width to be pre‑treated with the mixed binder after loose material is removed, and the acceptance limit is judged by a visual measurement of the crack width. Section 590—Crack Cleaning and Sealing specifies that cracks shall be sawed or ground to a width of ¾ inch with a depth of 1 inch; if the original crack measures between ¾ inch and 1 inch wide, the treated width may be increased to 1 inch with a depth of 1¼ inches, and the acceptance limit is judged by measuring the post‑treatment crack width on site. Section 467—Heavy Duty Membranes requires that joints or cracks over ¼ inch wide be filled flush to the surface with PG 64S‑22 or PG 58S‑28, and crack width is judged by measuring each joint or crack before membrane placement. Section 540—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Asphalt‑Surfaced Pavement mandates that any existing crack whose measured width exceeds three‑quarters of an inch be cleaned and filled with non‑shrink grout or another Department‑approved material before the surface is milled and the overlay placed, with measurement by measuring the crack width. Section 412—Superpave Mixture Design, construction of plant‑mixed thin asphalt overlay courses, preparation of the existing surface specifies that any pavement crack or joint that exceeds 1⁄8 inch in width be filled and sealed with PennDOT‑specified filler and sealant, and the crack width is measured on the existing surface before overlay placement. Section 489—Ultra‑Thin Bonded Wearing Course states that any longitudinal or transverse joint or crack that is ¼‑inch or wider must be sealed at least 24 hours before paving using the rubberized sealant prescribed in Section 469, establishing a crack‑width acceptance limit of ¼‑inch for sealing prior to pavement placement.
