Subgrade Drain

Part of: Subsurface Drainage is-subtopic-of
Subgrade Drain is a topic under Subsurface Drainage.
subgrade-drainslateral-drainslateral-underdrainsvertical-subdrainsubdrainage
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-30

Subgrade drains are subsurface drainage systems installed beneath the pavement subbase to remove excess moisture from the subgrade, thereby preserving support capacity and reducing moisture‑related distresses such as frost heave and traffic‑induced deflection[1][2]. They achieve this by excavating a trench at least two inches into the subgrade, lining it with Class 1 geotextile, placing perforated pipe or prefabricated base drain, and backfilling with hydraulically compacted fine‑aggregate Type A while maintaining smooth side and bottom surfaces and continuous gravity flow[1][2]. The design mandates positive gravity drainage, sealed joints, deflection angles of 45° or less, a minimum trench slope of ¼ inch per foot, and protected outlets to prevent crushing or plugging, ensuring uniform pavement performance and extended service life[1][2].

Purpose and design objectives

Subgrade drains are installed to combat excessive moisture that accumulates within or beneath pavement layers because most pavement problems can be attributed, at least in part, to such water. By removing water from the subgrade they preserve the support capacity of the base, reduce traffic‑induced deflections and frost‑heave, and limit other moisture‑related distresses, thereby extending pavement life and promoting more uniform performance. The Subgrade Drain system is installed to provide positive removal of water from the pavement subgrade, thereby preventing moisture‑induced weakening that can lead to premature pavement failure. To achieve this need the design calls for excavating a trench at least 2 inches into the subgrade and lining it with Class 1 geotextile before placing perforated pipe or prefabricated base drain, then backfilling with fine aggregate Type A in hydraulically compacted lifts while keeping the trench sealed to avoid soil intrusion; all joints and fittings must preclude entry of foreign material and maintain continuous gravity flow. By meeting these construction criteria the drainage system ensures effective subgrade dewatering and protects the structural performance of the pavement. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–522)

A subgrade drain must remove water beneath the pavement so that loss of subgrade support is reduced and the aggregate base gains greater resistance to traffic‑induced deflection, thereby increasing pavement life; it also must limit moisture‑related deterioration of aggregates, freezing‑thawing effects, and fine‑material pumping or rearrangement, which together promote more uniform performance and help prevent non‑uniform frost heave, and its outlets must be protected from crushing or plugging to keep the drainage system functioning properly[1]. The subgrade drain shall be installed so that it reliably removes water while satisfying the structural, durability and safety criteria set out in Section 610.3[2]. Installation requires a trench extending at least 2 inches into the subgrade after fine grading and without disturbing the existing subbase[2]. Fittings shall be limited to deflection angles of 45° or less and all joints shall be sealed to keep foreign material out[2]. Backfill shall be performed with fine‑aggregate Type A in hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding 9 inches, each lift sprayed with one gallon of water per linear foot[2]. Trenches may not remain open more than 2 inches deep overnight or during precipitation and required barricades shall be provided[2]. Class 1 geotextile shall be used, providing at least a six‑inch overlap between sections, and outlet connections must prevent soil intrusion and establish positive gravity flow within 24 hours of trenching[2]. The subgrade drains shall be extended a length sufficient to adequately drain the wet area and, if necessary, along the roadway to achieve a suitable outlet[2]. They shall be constructed under the subbase after the subgrade has been completed but before the subbase is placed and oriented at right angles or as directed[2]. The trench bottom through pavement and shoulder areas shall maintain a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot[2]. Smooth side and bottom trench surfaces shall be provided so that the geotextile does not bridge depressions in the soil and is not damaged by rock projections, thereby maintaining effective subsurface drainage[2]. Structurally the fabric shall be laid flat without stretching, overlapped end‑to‑end an amount equal to the trench width, and covered with aggregate within two weeks so that the fabric is protected from exposure and puncture; any damaged sections must be removed and replaced during construction[2]. For safety and serviceability traffic or construction equipment may not be placed directly on the fabric and all damage must be corrected before backfill is completed[2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–174), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–524)

Scope and applicability

Subgrade Drain is appropriate for both Interstate and non‑Interstate projects where the existing roadway exhibits water damage or sag areas, and it is especially recommended in wet subgrade locations such as cut‑to‑fill transitions, including mainline construction and rehabilitation segments where subsurface moisture threatens support. The same system, also called pipe underdrain or pavement base drain, is appropriate for standard roadway pavements after fine grading has been completed and the adjacent pavement has already been constructed, provided it is not being placed beneath curb sections or other special locations that would require separate provisions. In these applications the drainage system is installed as a Class 1 subsurface drainage system to remove water from the subgrade. Installation requires excavating a trench that does not disturb the subbase and extends at least 2 inches into the subgrade, with side and bottom surfaces smooth so that the geotextile fabric does not bridge depressions or become damaged by rock projections. The trench is lined with Class 1 geotextile laid flat and unstretched, overlapped at roll ends by an amount equal to the trench width, and for prefabricated pavement base drains the overlap must be continuous for at least 6 inches. The guidance strongly encourages using lateral underdrains in these wet subgrade areas and at all transitions from cut to fill cross sections. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–174), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–522)

When the drainage element must be located beneath curbs or other special locations, the standard practice of installing the pavement base drain after the adjacent roadway pavement is built does not apply, so an alternative design approach is preferred. Additionally, construction constraints such as keeping trenches open for no more than 2 inches in depth overnight require that any method which cannot meet this limit be reconsidered. [2]

[2] — SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–522)

Design inputs and requirements

The Pavement Policy Manual specifies that the layout of subgrade drainage must follow PennDOT publications, requiring continuous pavement base drains along the outside edge of the pavement, on the low side of superelevated sections, and on median sides where subsurface water is a problem, as shown in Publication 72M (Roadway Construction Standards, RC‑30M). Base drain outlets must be protected from crushing or plugging with appropriate headwalls per Publication 72M, and the sizing and outlet spacing of pavement base drains must follow the method described in Publication 13M (Design Manual Part 2, Highway Design, Chapter 10, Section 10.3.D). In addition, lateral underdrains are strongly encouraged at wet subgrade areas and at all cut‑to‑fill transitions[1]. The construction specifications in Publication 408/2026 prescribe that the drain be placed according to the Standard Drawings and that pipe connections be limited to deflection angles of 45° or less using manufacturer‑approved fittings. Trench dimensions shall extend at least 2 inches into the subgrade, with a width shown on the Standard Drawings, and trenches deeper than 2 inches may not be left open overnight; such openings must be barricaded in accordance with 67 PA Code Chapter 203. Backfill shall consist of fine‑aggregate Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts that do not exceed 9 inches, each lift being sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot. All joints shall be inspected before backfilling and sealed to prevent entry of foreign material, and the drainage core must have a minimum of 6 inches geotextile overlap. Outlet pipe and fittings shall follow Section 615.3 so that positive gravity flow is achieved within 24 hours of trenching. Each trench shall be excavated and backfilled with adequate tamping, extending a length sufficient to adequately drain the wet area, and shall be positioned at right angles to the roadway centerline or skewed as directed. The drains are to be installed under the subbase after completion of the subgrade but before placement of the subbase, and the trench bottom must be provided with a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot through both pavement and shoulder zones. Where indicated, Class 1 geotextile in accordance with Section 212.3(b) shall be used, and the Subgrade Drain is classified as a Class 1 subsurface drainage system that requires smooth side and bottom trench surfaces so that the fabric does not bridge depressions in the soil or become damaged by rock projections. The geotextile shall be laid flat in the prepared trench without stretching, with overlap of adjacent rolls an amount equal to the trench width, and a top‑overlap of at least one trench‑width as shown on the Standard Drawings. Backfill shall be placed and compacted up to the pipe invert level before installing the pipe, after which the remaining fill shall be completed with compacted aggregate while protecting the fabric, and the fabric may not remain exposed for more than 2 weeks without being covered by aggregate[2].

Design methods and criteria

The subgrade‑drain design follows the procedure outlined in Publication 13M, Design Manual Part 2 (Highway Design) Chapter 10 Section 10.3.D for sizing base drains and determining outlet spacing [1]. PennDOT uses gradation as an indicator of permeability when selecting drain materials or configurations [1]. All outlets located in mowable areas are required to be protected with appropriate headwalls as specified in Publication 72M Roadway Construction Standards [1]. The design also incorporates basic drainage principles that include gravity removal, capillary water control, lowering intersecting water tables, intercepting seepage from cuts, and the use of lateral underdrains where needed [1]. Subgrade‑drain construction begins with excavating the trench, backfilling it, and adequately tamping the backfill [2]. The drain is then extended a length sufficient to remove water from the wet area [2]. Installation occurs under the subbase after the subgrade has been completed but before the subbase is placed, with the pipe positioned at right angles or skewed as directed [2]. Throughout both pavement and shoulder zones the trench bottom must maintain a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot [2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 524)

The subgrade drain system shall be designed to remove free water by gravity, intercept capillary water that cannot drain naturally, and lower any intersecting water tables [1]. To achieve rapid gravity drainage the design requires base and subbase materials that are highly permeable and subbase layers whose slopes are maximized to increase the rate of water removal [1]. Outlets located within mowable areas shall be protected from crushing or plugging by appropriate headwalls as specified in Publication 72M [1]. The sizing and spacing of drains shall follow the method shown in Publication 13M, with outlet spacing kept small to increase the rate of water removal [1]. Lateral underdrains shall be placed in wet subgrade zones and at all cut‑to‑fill transitions as strongly encouraged by the policy [1]. Subgrade drains shall be installed after completion of the subgrade but before placement of the subbase, and each drain must extend a length sufficient to adequately drain the wet area [2]. Drains are to be constructed at right angles to the roadway centerline or skewed as directed, and may be extended along the roadway to achieve a suitable outlet [2]. A minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot shall be maintained on the trench bottom through both pavement and shoulder areas [2]. The trench for the subgrade drain system shall extend at least 2 inches into the subgrade and shall have the width shown on the Standard Drawings, with all fittings limited to deflection angles of 45 degrees or less [2]. A Class 1 geotextile is required for every pavement base drain, and where indicated for pipe underdrain construction splices must provide at least 6 inches of geotextile overlap [2]. The trench shall be backfilled with fine‑aggregate Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding 9 inches, each lift being sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot [2]. Trenches greater than 2 inches deep may not be left open overnight, and all joints, connections, and outlet fittings must be sealed to prevent soil intrusion and inspected before backfilling to ensure continuity of drainage flow [2]. The design and construction shall provide smooth side and bottom trench surfaces so that the geotextile does not bridge depressions in the soil or become damaged by rock projections; the fabric shall be laid flat in the prepared trench without stretching, with adjacent roll ends overlapped by an amount equal to the trench width, and may not be left exposed for more than 2 weeks before being covered with aggregate, any damaged fabric being removed and replaced as specified for the class of fabric used [2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–174), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–524)

Structural section and dimensions

In Section 610—Pipe Underd rain and Pavement Base Drain, the pavement base drain portion of a Subgrade Drain shall be placed in a trench whose width follows the Standard Drawings and whose depth extends at least 2 inches into the subgrade. Backfill material shall be fine‑aggregate Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts that do not exceed 9 inches, with each lift sprayed with water at one gallon per linear foot. When geotextile is used, sections must overlap by a minimum of 6 inches to maintain continuity. No other specific dimensions for pavement, base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab, shoulder, edge‑support, or reinforcement are prescribed in this section. In Section 612—Subgrade Drains, the specification does not prescribe explicit thickness or width dimensions for the pavement, base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab, edge‑support, or reinforcement; the only dimensional requirement is that the trench bottom must be provided with a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot as it passes through the pavement and shoulder zones. All other placement details are described without numeric dimensions. [2]

[2] — SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–524)

Subgrade drain thickness is set by excavating a trench that extends at least 2 inches into the subgrade and then backfilling with fine‑aggregate Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding 9 inches, each lift being sprayed with water at 1.0 gallon per linear foot; the layer configuration includes Class 1 geotextile surrounding the pipe and a minimum of 6 inches geotextile overlap between sections to maintain continuity; joints are made according to the manufacturer’s recommendations while splice connectors are fitted so they do not restrict flow, and tie‑ins to new or existing drainage structures use satisfactory connections with fittings that have deflection angles of 45 degrees or less, ensuring transitions are smooth and non‑restricting to flow. [2]

Materials and mixture design

According to the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242, Change No. 10), section 2.3 DRAINAGE, a Subgrade Drain specification must require that the base and subbase layers consist of highly permeable material so that water can drain by gravity rapidly [1]. The manual further states that this permeability is evaluated primarily through the gradation (particle‑size distribution) of the granular fill, and that the design should provide a sufficient slope in the subbase to enhance gravity drainage while allowing some reduction in drainability to obtain a stable construction platform; no explicit mixture proportions or specific material types are mandated beyond the high‑permeability requirement [1]. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications, Section 610.3 Construction for pipe‑underdrain and pavement‑base drain, require that a Class 1 geotextile be installed as prescribed in Section 212.3(b) for the Subgrade Drain [2]. Perforated pipe must be placed with its perforations facing downward, and all pipe connections shall use fittings whose deflection angle does not exceed forty‑five degrees [2]. The backfill material specified is fine aggregate, Type A, which must be placed in hydraulically compacted lifts no thicker than nine inches, each lift being sprayed with water at a rate of one gallon per linear foot [2]. Outlet pipes are to be protected by galvanized wire‑mesh shields, and the trench bottom must be maintained at a minimum slope of one‑quarter inch per foot through both pavement and shoulder areas [2]. Within Section 612.3 Construction for Subgrade Drains, the trench is excavated, backfilled, and adequately tamped while preserving the same minimum slope requirement, and where indicated a Class 1 geotextile must again be installed in accordance with Section 212.3(b) [2]. The geotextile installation requirements call for smooth side and bottom trench surfaces to prevent bridging or damage, the fabric laid flat without stretching, overlapped at the trench top by an amount equal to the trench width as shown on the Standard Drawings, and covered with aggregate within two weeks so that it is not exposed longer [2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–174), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–524)

According to the Pavement Policy Manual, selection of subgrade drain materials and mixtures is guided by the need for high permeability so that gravity‑drainable water is removed rapidly, protecting structural support and durability [1]. The manual states that base and subbase materials should be highly permeable, using gradation (particle‑size distribution) as an indicator test, although some permeability may be sacrificed to provide a more stable work platform during construction [1]. It further requires that the slope of the subbase layers be maximized and drainage pipe spacing reduced in order to increase the rate of water removal, balancing constructability costs with performance objectives [1]. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania specifications require that a Class 1 geotextile, as defined in Section 212.3(b), line the trench for subgrade drainage to protect the system and contribute to structural integrity, durability, constructability and performance goals [2]. Backfill behind the geotextile must consist of fine‑aggregate Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding nine inches, with each lift sprayed with water at a rate of one gallon per linear foot, thereby ensuring proper compaction and durability [2]. Pipe perforations are to be installed facing down, all fittings limited to deflection angles of 45 degrees or less, and the trench depth must extend at least two inches into the subgrade, with prompt backfilling to maintain contact with the geotextile and prevent soil intrusion [2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–522)

Structural details and interfaces

In accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations (Section 610), joints shall use fittings with deflection angles of 45 degrees or less and splice connectors that do not restrict flow; each joint shall include a minimum six‑inch geotextile overlap and the upgraded pipe end shall be sealed to prevent entry of foreign material. Connections to new or existing drainage structures shall be made with satisfactory connections, outlet pipes and fittings shall be installed as specified with galvanized wire mesh shields, and all transitions shall be smooth and non‑restricting to flow while joints and fittings are protected from damage during construction. The subgrade drain must be installed in a trench that is excavated, backfilled and adequately tamped (Section 612). The trench bottom shall be kept at a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot through both pavement and shoulder areas. Placement occurs under the subbase after the subgrade is completed but before the subbase is laid, oriented at right angles to the roadway centerline or skewed as directed, and extended far enough to drain the wet area and reach a suitable outlet. If required by the contract, a Class 1 geotextile may be placed in accordance with Section 212.3(b). For a Class 1 subgrade drain (Section 212), the geotextile is laid flat in a smooth‑sided and bottomed trench without stretching, with joints formed by overlapping adjacent rolls an amount equal to the trench width and providing at least a minimum trench‑width overlap across the backfill at the trench top as shown on the standard drawings. The fabric must then be covered with compacted aggregate backfill up to the pipe invert, the pipe installed, and the remainder of the trench filled while ensuring that no traffic or construction equipment travels directly on the geotextile and that any exposed fabric is protected by aggregate within two weeks. No additional reinforcement or load‑transfer devices are specified for this class. [2]

[2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–174), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–524)

In subgrade drain design the interfaces between base, subbase and subgrade are treated by using highly permeable granular materials so that gravity‑drainable water is removed rapidly and capillary rise is minimized [1]. The subbase layer is given the maximum practical slope to speed drainage and pipe and outlet spacing is kept as small as practicable to increase removal rates [1]. Lateral underdrains are installed at wet subgrade zones and at every cut‑to‑fill transition to intercept seepage, while continuous edge drains run along the pavement’s outer edge, on the low side of superelevated sections, and on median sections where subsurface water problems exist [1]. All base drain outlets located in mowable areas must be protected from crushing or plugging by appropriate headwalls [1]. Subgrade drains are installed in excavated trenches that are backfilled and tamped, and the drain length shall be extended a length sufficient to adequately drain the wet area [2]. The drainage system is placed under the subbase after the subgrade has been completed but before the subbase is placed, and the trench bottom must maintain a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot through pavement and shoulder areas [2]. At the interface with existing or new drainage structures, fittings shall not exceed deflection angles of 45 degrees, splice connectors shall be fitted so that they do not restrict flow, and all connections—including outlet pipes—shall be installed to preclude soil intrusion into the drainage core or outlet piping and to provide continuity of drainage flow, using approved rather than homemade fittings [2]. The surrounding geotextile shall overlap at least 6 inches between sections, and any tears shall be repaired per the manufacturer’s guidance [2]. For a Class 1 subsurface drainage system, trench side and bottom surfaces must be smooth so that the geotextile does not bridge depressions or become punctured by rock projections; the fabric is laid flat in the prepared trench without stretching, with its top folded back on the sides to receive aggregate and pipe [2]. Aggregate is placed and compacted up to the pipe invert level before pipe installation, after which the remainder of the trench is filled with compacted aggregate while protecting the geotextile; adjacent roll ends shall be overlapped an amount equal to the trench width, and the geotextile may not remain exposed for more than two weeks without being covered with aggregate [2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 58–59); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–174), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–524)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

Subgrade drains must be designed to remove free water by gravity and to lower intersecting water tables; lateral underdrains are strongly encouraged in wet subgrade areas and at all cut‑to‑fill transitions to intercept seepage from excavation sides [1]. The base and subbase materials should be highly permeable so that capillary water is minimized, and subbase slopes should be maximized to promote drainage, while outlet pipes must be protected from crushing or plugging with appropriate headwalls [1]. This drainage approach mitigates moisture‑related climate effects such as frost heave, freezing of trapped water under joints, and loss of support during thaw, thereby preserving pavement performance [1]. In construction, after fine grading the pipe is placed and the trench must extend at least two inches into the subgrade, with the upgrade end of the pipe sealed to keep foreign material out [2]. Class 1 geotextile is required for all base‑drain installations, and smooth side and bottom trench surfaces must be provided so that the fabric does not bridge depressions or become damaged by rock projections [2]. The geotextile is laid flat without stretching, with adjacent rolls overlapped at the trench top by an amount equal to the trench width, and it must be covered with aggregate within two weeks so it is not left exposed [2]. Backfill is performed with fine‑aggregate Type A using hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding nine inches; each lift is sprayed with one gallon of water per linear foot and compacted aggregate is placed up to the pipe invert before installing the drainage pipe, after which the remainder of the trench is filled with additional compacted aggregate [2]. Trenches are never left open during precipitation or overnight, and all drainage materials must be stored on an elevated platform protected from weather and ultraviolet exposure until installation [2].

The Subgrade Drain design must protect all base‑drain outlets from crushing or plugging to ensure proper system function [1]. Where an outlet is located in a mowable area it shall be shielded with the headwalls prescribed in Publication 72M [1]. Lateral underdrains are recommended in wet subgrade zones and at every cut‑to‑fill transition to intercept seepage [1]. The slope of the subbase should be maximized to promote gravity drainage within granular materials [1]. Pipe and outlet spacing shall be kept as small as practicable so that water removal rates are increased, following the sizing and spacing procedures of Publication 13M [1]. All trenches for subgrade drains must be barricaded at all times in accordance with 67 PA Code Chapter 203 and may not be left open during precipitation or overnight; if a trench is left open its depth may not exceed two inches [2]. Connections shall use fittings whose deflection angles do not exceed forty‑five degrees [2]. The trench width shall follow the Standard Drawings while the trench depth must extend at least two inches into the subgrade [2]. Backfill shall be placed in hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding nine inches, using fine aggregate Type A, and each lift shall be sprayed with one gallon of water per linear foot [2]. Each subgrade drain shall be installed at right angles to the roadway centreline, or skewed when directed, and shall be extended a length sufficient to adequately drain the wet area; if necessary the drain may continue along the roadway to achieve a suitable outlet [2]. The trench bottom must be provided with a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot through both pavement and shoulder zones [2]. Installation shall provide smooth side and bottom trench surfaces so that the geotextile does not bridge depressions in the soil or become damaged by rock projections [2]. Traffic or construction equipment may not be placed directly on the fabric, and the fabric must be laid flat without stretching, overlapped at the trench width for adjacent roll ends, and covered with aggregate within two weeks of exposure [2]. Large rocks greater than one foot in size shall be placed on a minimum six‑inch bedding layer to protect the fabric, and a positive slope must be maintained to assure drainage [2].

Verification and expected performance

The completed subgrade drain is verified by having the Inspector‑in‑Charge inspect the pipe before final backfill is placed, confirming that all joints and fittings follow the manufacturer’s recommendations, that splice connectors do not restrict flow, and that connections are sealed to preclude soil intrusion. The reviewer also checks that a minimum of 6 inches of Class 1 geotextile overlap exists between sections, that the pipe is oriented with perforations down unless otherwise directed, and that backfill is placed in hydraulically compacted lifts no greater than 9 inches with each lift sprayed at one gallon per linear foot. Additionally, the outlet pipe and fittings are required to be installed as specified within 24 hours of trenching to ensure positive drainage, and any open trenches shall not exceed two inches in depth or remain uncovered during precipitation.
