Intermediate Joint

Part of: Transverse Joint is-subtopic-of
Intermediate Joint is a topic under Transverse Joint.
intermediate-jointintermediate-joints
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-28

The intermediate joint is a transverse concrete joint that must be introduced in a reconstructed lane of at least 500 ft or in a widened/added lane whenever the pre‑existing transverse joint spacing exceeds twenty feet, so that new joints remain aligned with the original pattern[1]. Its primary function is to control slab cracking and to provide adequate load transfer across slabs, thereby preserving structural durability, safety and serviceability during pavement service[1][2]. The joint is cut perpendicular to traffic so that each resulting panel does not exceed fifteen feet (or ten‑to‑seventeen feet for Jointed Plain Concrete Pavement widenings), the saw‑cut depth equals the full overlay thickness plus one‑half inch, and a backing material is placed and sealed after flushing the cut, ensuring proper bonding and load transfer[2][1].

Purpose and design objectives

In section 4.2 Concrete Pavement Patching, when a single lane is being reconstructed for a length of 500 feet or more the design must keep new joints aligned with the existing ones and an intermediate joint is required whenever the pre‑existing transverse joint spacing exceeds 20 feet; this requirement addresses the need to control cracking and to provide adequate load transfer across slabs during service. In section 4.8 Widening and Lane Additions, when the original transverse joint spacing exceeds 20 feet the widening with JPCP must use a joint spacing between 10 feet and 17 feet, and intermediate joints are incorporated so that slab lengths in the widened portion remain approximately equal; this serves to control slab size and to prevent excessive stress or cracking that could develop from overly long slabs. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96)

The design of an intermediate joint must align the new joints with existing joints whenever a reconstructed lane exceeds five hundred feet and the pre‑existing joint spacing is greater than twenty feet, thereby satisfying the functional requirement of maintaining proper joint spacing for adequate load transfer and crack control, which in turn supports structural durability, safety and serviceability. The intermediate transverse joint shall be cut so that each overlay panel length does not exceed fifteen feet, providing the required functional control for the bonded concrete overlay. For structural performance, RC‑27M requires a fifteen‑foot joint spacing with perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals, while the saw‑cut depth must be equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus 1/2 inch, ensuring proper load transfer across the joint. Durability is achieved by avoiding a defective condition; the average bond strength of the three tests shall not be below one hundred seventy‑five pounds per square inch and no individual test may be below one hundred sixty pounds per square inch, and a sealant reservoir must be flushed with water immediately after sawing to remove slurry. Safety and serviceability are satisfied because the Representative can determine when the pavement may be opened to traffic once the concrete attains the minimum required compressive strength of three thousand six hundred pounds per square inch and the minimum acceptable bond strength as specified, ensuring that the pavement meets the prescribed performance criteria. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–89); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Scope and applicability

For concrete pavement patching, when only one lane of Portland cement concrete pavement is being reconstructed for a length of 500 feet or more, the new joints should be aligned with the existing joints and an intermediate joint must be placed in the new lane to satisfy current joint‑spacing requirements if the existing joint spacing exceeds 20 feet [1]. For widening or lane‑addition projects on existing rigid pavements where the original transverse joint spacing exceeds 20 feet, the widened portion shall be built as Jointed Plain Cement Concrete Pavement and slab lengths shall be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet, with intermediate joints incorporated as necessary so that the slabs in the new section are approximately equal [1]. In bonded concrete overlays of existing concrete‑surfaced pavements, intermediate transverse joints are employed when the spacing of the original transverse joints would create overlay panels longer than fifteen feet; in those cases the contractor cuts additional joints so that no panel exceeds that length [2]. The saw cut for each intermediate joint in such an overlay must extend through the full thickness of the overlay, including any extra thickness used for grade correction, and then an additional one‑half inch beyond the overlay depth [2].

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

According to Section 545.3(i), intermediate transverse joints may be cut only when the resulting overlay panel length is limited to a maximum of 15 feet; consequently, if a pavement design requires panels longer than this limit, an intermediate joint cannot be employed and another jointing or panel design must be chosen. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Design inputs and requirements

In reconstruction projects limited to a single lane that extends at least five hundred feet, the design must align the new joints with the existing joints and must provide an intermediate joint in the new lane whenever the pre‑existing joint spacing exceeds twenty feet; this requirement is drawn from Publication 72M Roadway Construction Standards (RC‑27M) which defines the current joint spacing criteria, so the intermediate joint must be placed as shown on RC‑27M to satisfy those spacing rules. For widening or lane‑addition work on rigid pavements, the design must follow the provisions of Chapter 4, Section 4.8 of the Portland Cement Concrete Pavement Guidelines and Policies; transverse joints shall be aligned with those on the existing pavement, and when the original transverse joint spacing exceeds twenty feet, the new joint spacing shall be limited to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet, with intermediate joints incorporated as necessary so that slab lengths in the widened section are approximately equal. Under the Rigid Pavements specifications of Section 545.3(i), intermediate transverse joints that are cut for a bonded concrete overlay must be spaced such that each overlay panel length does not exceed fifteen feet, and the saw‑cut depth shall equal the full thickness of the overlay—including any additional thickness used for grade corrections—plus one‑half inch. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Design methods and criteria

When reconstructing a single lane that is at least five hundred feet long, designers must align new joints with existing joints and place an intermediate joint whenever the pre‑existing joint spacing exceeds twenty feet in order to meet the current joint‑spacing criteria shown on RC‑27M [1]. Those criteria require fifteen‑foot joint spacing for jointed plain concrete pavement (JPCP) or thirty‑foot spacing for jointed reinforced concrete pavement (JRCP) [1]. Designers must also specify the quantities of New Pavement Joint items, selecting either skewed or non‑skewed baskets as illustrated on RC‑26M to provide perpendicular joints with coated dowel bars and appropriate seals [1]. For intermediate transverse joints in a bonded concrete overlay, the specification requires cutting additional joints between existing transverse joints so that each overlay panel does not exceed fifteen feet in length [2]. The saw cut must extend through the full thickness of the overlay, including any grade‑correction layer, plus an extra one‑half inch [2]. No design equations, analytical models, empirical relationships, or specialized design tools are prescribed for this joint type [2].

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–89); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

For reconstruction of a single lane that is five hundred feet or longer, the design shall align new transverse joints with existing joints and shall provide an intermediate joint whenever the existing joint spacing exceeds twenty feet so that the current joint‑spacing requirements shown on RC‑27M are satisfied [1]. When widening or adding a lane and the original transverse joint spacing exceeds twenty feet, the design shall use Jointed Plain Concrete Pavement and shall adjust the joint spacing to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet, incorporating intermediate joints as necessary so that slab lengths in the widened section are approximately equal [1]. For intermediate transverse joints in a concrete overlay, the design shall limit each overlay panel to no more than fifteen feet between existing transverse joints [2]. The saw‑cut depth for those intermediate joints shall be equal to the full thickness of the overlay—including any grade‑correction thickness—and shall include an additional one‑half inch [2].

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Structural section and dimensions

In the Pavement Policy Manual, section 4.2 Concrete Pavement Patching, when a single lane is reconstructed for a length of 500 feet or more and any existing joint spacing exceeds 20 feet, an intermediate joint must be inserted so that the lane meets the current joint‑spacing rule shown on RC‑27M, which calls for a joint spacing of 15 feet. The same manual, in section 4.8 Widening and Lane Additions, requires that when the original transverse joint spacing is greater than 20 feet, the widening be performed with Jointed Plain Concrete Pavement (JPCP) and the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet, with intermediate joints placed as needed to keep slab lengths in the widened portion approximately equal. The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications, Section 545.3(i), state that for intermediate transverse joints the spacing between existing transverse joints shall be limited so that each overlay panel does not exceed 15 feet in length. Those specifications also require that the saw‑cut depth for an intermediate joint equal the full thickness of the concrete overlay, including any additional thickness used for grade corrections, plus one‑half inch. Neither source provides separate dimensional requirements for pavement thickness, base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab thickness beyond the panel length, shoulder width, edge‑support, or reinforcement associated with an intermediate joint. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

The pavement thickness for an intermediate joint incorporated in a lane addition or widening shall be taken from the existing pavement; full lane additions that are ten feet wide or more are designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic loads dictate design for the actual traffic, while partial widenings of less than ten feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth. The transverse joints in the new section must be aligned with those on the existing pavement; when the original transverse joint spacing is twenty feet or less the slabs are constructed to the same length as the original, but if the original spacing exceeds twenty feet the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet with intermediate joints incorporated so that slab lengths in the widened section are approximately equal. The lane addition or widening shall be tied to the existing pavement. For an intermediate transverse joint placed within a bonded concrete overlay, the overlay panel length shall not exceed fifteen feet. The saw‑cut for such an intermediate joint must be cut to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay, including any additional thickness used for grade corrections, plus one‑half inch. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Materials and mixture design

The specification for an intermediate joint requires that an approved joint‑backing or other joint‑filling material be installed before the overlay is placed, positioned to within ±1/4 inch of the existing pavement surface and capable of remaining in place during paving. After sawing, the joint must be flushed with water, the backing material set to the proper depth, and the joint sealed according to Section 501.3(n). No specific mixture proportions or material property values are detailed in this excerpt; only the performance requirement that the filling material not be dislodged by the paving operation. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Structural details and interfaces

In concrete pavement patching of a single lane at least five hundred feet long, the design must align new joints with the existing pavement joints and must insert an intermediate joint whenever the existing transverse joint spacing exceeds twenty feet; the intermediate joint is placed perpendicular to the traffic direction so that the effective spacing does not exceed twenty feet, and no additional reinforcement or load‑transfer devices are specifically required for that joint beyond its proper alignment and spacing. When widening or adding a lane, transverse joints on the new work shall be aligned with those on the existing pavement; if the original transverse joint spacing is greater than twenty feet, the widening shall employ Jointed Plain Concrete Pavement (JPCP) with joint spacing limited to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet, and intermediate joints shall be incorporated as necessary so that slab lengths in the widened section are approximately equal, again without prescribing any specific reinforcement or load‑transfer system for those intermediate joints. For a bonded concrete overlay, an intermediate joint is created by cutting transverse saw cuts between existing joints such that each overlay panel length does not exceed fifteen feet, and each cut must extend through the full thickness of the overlay (including any grade‑correction material) plus one‑half inch. Prior to placing the overlay, joint backing or another approved filling material shall be installed in all intermediate joints to within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface, after which the sawed joint must be flushed with water, refilled with the backing material to the proper depth, and sealed in accordance with Section 501.3(n). [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

The intermediate transverse joint must be cut so that no overlay panel exceeds fifteen feet in length, with a saw‑cut depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus one‑half inch and a cut width at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack to avoid debonding; after cutting, a sealant reservoir is formed according to the standard drawing, the joint is immediately flushed with water to remove slurry, backing material is placed within ±¼ inch of the surface, and the joint is sealed in accordance with Section 501.3(n). [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

In reconstruction of a single lane that extends five hundred feet or more, the design shall align new joints with existing joints and shall provide an intermediate joint whenever the existing joint spacing exceeds twenty feet so that the joint‑spacing requirements shown on RC‑27M are satisfied. In widening or lane‑addition work, intermediate joints shall be incorporated as needed so that slab lengths in the widened portion are approximately equal, thereby maintaining geometric continuity with the existing pavement. For a bonded concrete overlay, an intermediate transverse joint must be cut between existing transverse joints such that each overlay panel length does not exceed fifteen feet. The saw‑cut depth for that joint shall equal the full thickness of the overlay, including any grade‑correction thickness, plus one‑half inch. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Verification and expected performance

The completed intermediate transverse joint shall be inspected to confirm that the panel length between adjacent joints does not exceed fifteen feet, as required for intermediate joints in Section 545.3(i), and that the saw‑cut depth equals the full thickness of the overlay—including any grade‑correction material—plus one‑half inch, without damaging dowel load transfer devices. After cutting, the joint must be flushed with water to remove sawing slurry before sealant installation, and the joint backing or filling material must have been placed within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface as stipulated for all transverse joints in Section 545.3(d). Finally, bond‑strength testing of the overlay at seven days per ASTM C1583 shall show an average of at least 175 psi and no individual test below 160 psi to verify that the joint performance meets the project’s strength criteria. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)
