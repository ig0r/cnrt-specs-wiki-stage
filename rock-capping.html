Rock Capping

Part of: Subgrade is-subtopic-of
Rock Capping is a topic under Subgrade.
rock-capping
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-29

Rock capping is a subgrade‑strengthening technique applied when the existing subgrade lacks sufficient bearing capacity, whereby a durable rock layer typically 1 to 3 feet thick—using R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1 or the best available rock—is placed over the prepared subgrade and modeled with a California Bearing Ratio of 20[1]. The treatment incorporates a Class 4 Type A geotextile separator and granular material designated 2RC to choke the rock, and the capping material must be compacted to at least six inches within two weeks of geotextile installation while avoiding equipment that could damage the fabric[2]. When used over foamed glass aggregate, the cap provides a protective load‑bearing platform that shields the underlying geotextile and subgrade from traffic stresses, equipment pressure, flood water, and facilitates stable construction activities[2].

Purpose and design objectives

Rock capping is applied as a subgrade‑strengthening measure when the existing subgrade does not provide sufficient bearing capacity [1]. By placing a durable rock layer typically 1 to 3 feet deep of R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1 or best available rock, the effective subgrade CBR can be modeled at 20, allowing the pavement design to rely on a stronger foundation and avoid overly thick structural layers [1]. In projects that use Foamed Glass Aggregate (FGA) as a fill material, Rock Capping is required to provide a protective, load‑bearing layer over the FGA so that the underlying geotextile and subgrade are shielded from traffic stresses, equipment pressure, and flood water while maintaining a stable platform for construction activities [2]. The specification calls for at least six inches compacted thickness of capping material placed within two weeks after the geotextile is installed, with care taken to avoid damaging the fabric and to use compaction methods that do not crush or disturb it [2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Rock capping requires placing at least six inches compacted thickness of an approved capping material over the foamed glass aggregate while ensuring the underlying geotextile remains undamaged, and any damaged geotextile is repaired before or during fill placement. Traffic on exposed, compacted FGA should be minimized, and rutting that exceeds four inches in depth is repaired by excavating to five inches below the bottom of the rut and replacing material with proper compaction. Capping material is placed and compacted within two weeks after geotextile installation, using only equipment that does not crush or disturb the geotextile—pad‑foot rollers are prohibited—and storm water may not accumulate until a sufficient ballast thickness is provided. [2]

[2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Scope and applicability

Rock capping may be employed as a subgrade‑strengthening technique when the existing subgrade CBR is low enough to merit improvement—specifically when the engineer determines that strengthening is cost‑effective for subgrades below or above a CBR of 5 and the project involves at least 25,000 SY on a Traffic Pattern Group 1 to 4. The treatment is modeled using a CBR value of 20 and typically consists of a 1‑to‑3‑foot layer of R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1 or best available rock placed over the subgrade. [1]

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150)

Rock capping becomes less attractive when suitable rock cannot be sourced, when the subgrade CBR is already above a CBR of 5 so that strengthening may not be needed, or when the project does not meet the cost‑benefit threshold of at least 25,000 SY on a Traffic Pattern Group (TPG) of 1 to 4; in such cases alternatives such as lime or cement stabilization, geogrids, or geotextiles should be evaluated. Additionally, rock capping is limited to a typical thickness of 1 to 3 feet and is modeled with a CBR = 20, so projects requiring different depths or modulus assumptions may also favor other treatments. [1]

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150)

Design inputs and requirements

The project site must be free of flooding risk, requiring that material storage and placement areas are not prone to flooding and that any standing water deeper than six inches or ice is removed before rock capping installation. [2] Design must include an evaluation of the thickness of the capping material required to support the anticipated haul‑road loads, and this evaluation must be submitted to the Department for approval; documentation of the make, model and ground pressure of the compaction equipment, which shall fall between 625 lb/ft² and 1,025 lb/ft², must also be provided. [2] The rock capping design is based on locally available durable rock sourced from project excavation, borrow pits, or a quarry, and the cap is typically placed at a thickness of one to three feet using R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1, or the best available rock; a Class 4 Type A geotextile is required as a separation layer in all treatments, and granular material designated 2RC is placed to choke the rock per Publication 408 specifications. [1] Additionally, the capping layer may be selected from embankment, subbase, topsoil or rock and must be compacted to at least six inches thickness over the foamed glass aggregate, with pad‑foot rollers prohibited for this compaction. [2] Storm‑water accumulation must be prevented until a sufficient capping thickness above the anticipated flood elevation is achieved, and all material handling must occur in areas not susceptible to flooding. [2] The subgrade beneath the rock cap is modeled with a California Bearing Ratio of 20, and when lime or cement stabilization is incorporated a structural coefficient of 0.12 is applied. [1] Prior to placement, verification that no water or ice remains on the surface is required as part of existing‑condition checks. [2]

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

According to the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242, May 2015 Edition, Change No. 10), rock capping shall be placed at a depth of 1 to 3 ft using R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1 or the best available rock and is modeled in the pavement design with a subgrade CBR value of 20; the treatment must include a Class 4 Type A geotextile separator and granular material (2RC) to choke the rock as required by Publication 408 specifications. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Transportation Publication 408/2026 Specifications governs the construction of rock capping in Section 218.3, which requires a Class 4 Type A geotextile over the foamed glass aggregate and mandates that the capping material be placed and compacted within two weeks after the geotextile installation. The same specification also stipulates that the capping layer must achieve at least six inches of compacted thickness in all locations. Acceptable capping materials listed in Publication 408 may consist of embankment material as defined in Section 206.3(b), subbase as defined in Section 350.3, topsoil as defined in Sections 801.3 or 803.3, or rock as defined in Section 850.3. Pad‑foot rollers are prohibited for compacting the capping material to prevent damage to the geotextile, and any geotextile damage must be repaired before fill placement. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Design methods and criteria

Rock capping is classified as a subgrade strengthening technique in which a layer of durable rock—typically ranging from one to three feet deep and consisting of R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1, or the best available rock—is placed over the prepared subgrade; its contribution to the structural capacity is represented in pavement design calculations by assigning a California Bearing Ratio value of 20 [1]. The design also mandates the use of a Class 4 Type A geotextile as a separation layer between the capping material and the underlying substrate, and requires granular material designated as 2RC to be placed behind (or “choke”) the rock in accordance with Publication 408 specifications [1]. When the subgrade includes foamed glass aggregate, the construction sequence begins by installing the Class 4 Type A geotextile over the FGA with a minimum twelve‑inch overlap on all edges, after which a compacted capping layer of at least six inches thickness—selected from approved embankment, subbase, topsoil, or rock materials—is placed [2]. The foamed glass aggregate must be laid in uncompacted lifts that do not exceed twenty‑four inches when tracked equipment is used, or twelve inches where only plate compaction is feasible, and the equipment employed for placement must operate with a ground pressure between 625 pounds per square foot and 1,025 pounds per square foot for tracked units or a static weight of 110 to 220 pounds for plate compactors [2]. Compaction of each lift is considered complete after four full passes covering the entire surface, and rollers are prohibited from being applied directly on the FGA layer; all capping material must be placed and compacted within two weeks following geotextile installation, taking care to avoid damage to the fabric [2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Rock capping shall be provided at a depth that is usually 1 to 3 feet and must consist of R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1, or the best available rock [1]. For design calculations the subgrade beneath the cap shall be modeled with CBR = 20 [1]. If lime or cement stabilization is used in conjunction, it shall be represented by a structural coefficient of 0.12 [1]. A Class 4 Type A geotextile is required as a separation layer in all treatments [1]. When rock is used as the capping layer over foamed glass aggregate, it must be placed and compacted to a minimum six‑inch compacted thickness in all locations [2]. The capping material shall be installed within two weeks after the underlying geotextile is placed, and the geotextile may not be damaged or disturbed during placement [2]. Padfoot rollers are prohibited for compacting the capping material, and conventional rollers may only be used after the capping layer has been fully placed [2]. The rock shall meet the specifications set forth in Section 850.3 [2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Structural section and dimensions

According to Section 218.3(d) of the Earthwork specifications, Rock Capping calls for a minimum compacted thickness of six inches of capping material placed over the foamed glass aggregate (FGA) in all locations; no separate dimensional requirements are listed for pavement, base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab, shoulder, edge‑support, or reinforcement. The requirement applies only to the capping layer itself and does not prescribe sizes for any of the other structural elements. [2]

[2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Materials and mixture design

Rock capping shall be placed to a depth of 1 to 3 feet and must consist of R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1, or the Best Available Rock sourced from project excavation, borrow excavation, or a quarry[1]. The subgrade model for this treatment uses a CBR value of 20[1]. A Class 4 Type A geotextile is required as a separation layer and Select Granular Material (2RC) is used to choke the rock[1]. When capping over foamed glass aggregate, specifications shall require a minimum six‑inch compacted layer placed over the foamed glass aggregate[2]. The specification must identify which of the approved capping materials will be used – embankment material (Section 206.3(b)), subbase (Section 350.3), topsoil (Section 801.3 or 803.3) or rock (Section 850.3)[2]. The chosen material is to be compacted to the six‑inch thickness before any rollers are applied, and pad‑foot rollers shall not be used on the capping layer to protect the underlying geotextile[2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Rock capping is usually 1 to 3 feet deep and consists of R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1, or best available rock; for design purposes the subgrade beneath the cap is modeled with CBR = 20 and a structural coefficient of 0.12 when compared with lime or cement stabilized alternatives, and a Class 4, Type A geotextile is required as a separation layer in all treatments, while granular material (2RC) is used to choke the rock as specified in Publication 408 Section 206 [1]. When capping over foamed glass aggregate, the procedure shall begin with installation of a Class 4, Type A geotextile, followed by placement of one of the approved capping materials—embankment material (Section 206.3(b)), subbase (Section 350.3), topsoil (Section 801.3 or 803.3), or rock (Section 850.3)—so that a minimum six‑inch compacted thickness covers the FGA in all locations [2]. The capping material must be placed and compacted within two weeks after the geotextile is installed, and padfoot rollers may not be used for compaction to protect the fabric; conventional compaction equipment should achieve the required density before any further fill layers are rolled [2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Structural details and interfaces

Rock capping is typically a 1‑to‑3 ft deep layer of R‑3 rock (or AASHTO No. 1 or best available rock) placed over a Class 4 Type A geotextile separator, and granular material designated as 2RC must be used to choke the rock in accordance with Publication 408 Section 206 [1]. The Pavement Policy Manual does not prescribe any particular joints, load‑transfer systems, transitions, or other connections for the cap [1]. In contrast, the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications require that the Class 4 Type A geotextile be installed over the foamed glass aggregate with fabric roll‑ends and edges overlapped a minimum of twelve inches, and that each lap be secured by sewing, heat bonding, or a comparable overlapping method; any damaged sections must be repaired in the same manner [2]. After the geotextile is placed, a compacted layer of an approved capping material—such as embankment, subbase, topsoil, or rock—must be laid to at least six inches thickness over the geotextile, and pad‑foot rollers may not be used for compaction so as to avoid damaging the fabric [2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Rock capping shall be placed at a depth of 1 to 3 feet using R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1, or the best available rock, with the subgrade beneath modeled with a CBR value of 20 [1]. At the interface between the capped rock and the overlying pavement layers a Class 4 Type A geotextile shall be installed as a separation layer across the full lane width [1]; the same specification further requires that the fabric roll‑ends and edges be overlapped a minimum of 12 inches in all directions to ensure continuity [2]. Over the geotextile, granular material meeting the 2RC specification shall be placed to choke the rock in accordance with Publication 408 Section 206 [1]. When rock capping is used over Foamed Glass Aggregate, the capping layer must be compacted to at least six inches thickness over the FGA [2]. During placement and compaction of this capping material the geotextile must not be damaged, pad‑foot rollers are prohibited for compacting the capping material, and any damage that does occur must be repaired before or during fill placement [2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

Drainage for rock‑capped sections is achieved by installing pavement base drains and any other appropriate drains as part of the subgrade preparation [1]. Prior to placing the capping material the subgrade must be cleared of standing water deeper than six inches and all ice, and the rock‑capping aggregate should be stored in a location that is not prone to flooding [2]. During placement storm water may not be allowed to accumulate until a sufficient thickness of capping material has been compacted above the anticipated flood elevation so that it functions as ballast; at least six inches of compacted material must be placed over the flood level [2]. Frost‑susceptible soils are mitigated by removing highly frost‑susceptible material or replacing it with a non‑frost‑susceptible layer thick enough to insulate the roadbed, and by providing a stronger base course capable of tolerating reduced bearing support [1]. When rock capping is employed as a subgrade strengthening technique the cap is typically placed one to three feet deep using R‑3 Rock, AASHTO No. 1, or best available rock, and the subgrade may be modeled with a CBR value of 20 [1]. The capping layer must be installed on a geotextile within two weeks after the geotextile is placed, and it must provide a stable, dry platform for subsequent pavement work [2].

[1] — Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 178–179)

For rock capping the design must include a Class 4, Type A geotextile overlapped at least twelve inches in all directions, then place a minimum six‑inch compacted thickness of rock (or other approved capping material) over the foamed glass aggregate; traffic on the underlying FGA is to be minimized until the capping is installed and rollers may not be used directly on the FGA, padfoot rollers are prohibited for compacting the capping layer, and any compaction equipment must stay within the ground‑pressure limits specified for tracked or plate equipment; materials shall be stored in an area not prone to flooding and handling should be limited to prevent damage, while any damaged geotextile must be repaired by overlapping and securing with sewing, heat bonding, or a twelve‑inch overlap. These provisions address accessibility (traffic control), safety (equipment pressure and protection of the geotextile), geometric (minimum thickness and overlaps) and user requirements for the rock capping system. [2]
