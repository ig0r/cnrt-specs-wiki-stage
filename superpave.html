Superpave

Part of: Mix Design is-subtopic-of
Superpave is a topic under Mix Design.

Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

Superpave is a performance‑based asphalt mixture design system that replaces the traditional Marshall method with a volumetric approach using the Superpave Gyratory Compactor to develop a Job Mix Formula tailored to project conditions[1][2]. It selects a performance‑grade (PG) binder and determines mix proportions from a 20‑year equivalent single axle load (ESAL) estimate, placing the project into one of five traffic ranges so that the resulting mix can resist permanent deformation (rutting) at high temperatures and thermal cracking at low temperatures for the specific climate and traffic environment[1][2]. The system also mandates construction controls—minimum air‑temperature limits, prescribed layer thicknesses, moisture‑susceptibility testing, and surface smoothness tolerances—to ensure durable, warranty‑compliant performance of wearing, binder, base, and thin‑overlay courses[1][2].

PennDOT publications covering “Superpave” also include the following subtopics: Fine-Graded Mixture (9.5 mm FG) and Superpave Mixture Size (NMAS).

The Superpave system incorporates Fine‑Graded Mixture (9.5 mm FG) as a specialized wearing course for thin overlays that excludes PG 64E‑22 binder compatibility. [1] It utilizes Superpave Mixture Size (NMAS) to define the nominal maximum aggregate size, which governs lift thickness and links gradation types to specific traffic duty levels. [1]

Purpose and design objectives

Superpave is intended to address pavement failure caused by climate‑induced cracking and traffic‑induced deformation. By selecting an appropriate performance‑grade (PG) binder and using the 20‑year design‑life equivalent single axle load (ESAL) estimate, the system tailors a volumetric mix that can resist permanent deformation (rutting) and thermal cracking at low temperatures for the specific project’s climatic and traffic conditions, replacing the Marshall method with a performance‑based procedure. For high pavement temperatures typical of summer months, the practice provides a stiffer asphalt binder by “bumping” the high‑temperature grade of a PG‑binder to better resist rutting. In overlay applications the system supplies durable thin‑lift mixes such as a 6.3 mm Thin Asphalt Overlay and a 9.5 mm Fine Grade Wearing Course that may be placed at depths below the typical 1½‑inch minimum, thereby correcting structural deficiencies, mitigating surface deterioration, extending service life, and improving ride quality when financial constraints limit overlay thickness. Section 313 of the specifications requires Superpave base‑course construction to avoid placement on wet surfaces or when the air or prepared surface temperature is 35 °F or lower because such conditions can impair compaction and durability. The same section also mandates that the finished asphalt not have irregularities greater than 1/4 inch and that a depth deficiency of 1/2 inch or more that cannot be satisfactorily corrected be considered defective, establishing performance criteria for surface smoothness and thickness. Section 412 requires the 6.3‑mm NMAS thin‑asphalt overlay to conform to the material, gradation and volumetric criteria of Bulletin 27, to incorporate any approved warm‑mix asphalt technology, and to pass a moisture‑susceptibility analysis that includes the WMA additive without compromising the mixture’s resistance to moisture damage, thereby addressing the need for moisture‑resistant thin overlays. Section 419 mandates that each Stone Matrix Asphalt mix be designed and tested for resistance to stripping, requiring a moisture susceptibility analysis, draindown testing, and a particle‑coating test that must show at least 95.0 % of aggregate particles coated so the mixture can be handled, placed and compacted without stripping the asphalt material from the aggregate while satisfying the specified volumetric tolerances. Section 496 employs Superpave to meet the engineering requirement that asphalt concrete pavements satisfy the warranty performance criteria; it mandates a volumetric mix design using the Superpave Gyratory Compactor to establish Job Mix Formulas that achieve the required aggregate‑binder‑filler tolerances and void characteristics, thereby addressing the need for durable, high‑performing flexible pavements capable of meeting the 60‑month warranty. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 111–120); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (p. 215), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 246–352)

Superpave must be designed to resist the climatic and traffic conditions specific to a project location, which constitutes its functional objective. The design life is expressed as a 20‑year ESAL loading estimate that establishes the expected rate of loading and therefore provides the structural requirement that drives mixture proportions. Additionally, the mixture must be capable of resisting permanent deformation (rutting) and thermal cracking at low temperatures to meet durability objectives. For functional performance during construction, the Superpave base course may only be placed on dry surfaces when both the air temperature and the surface temperature exceed 35 °F. Structural integrity of the placed layer is achieved by limiting each compacted lift to a thickness between three inches and six inches for a 25.0 mm mix, or between four inches and eight inches for a 37.5 mm mix, with any required total depth beyond those limits being built in multiple lifts of approximately equal depth. Serviceability is addressed by tolerancing surface irregularities to less than one‑quarter inch, while safety and quality are protected by deeming the pavement defective if a compacted depth deficiency of one‑half inch or more cannot be satisfactorily corrected. [1][2]

Scope and applicability

Superpave is appropriate for Hot Mix Asphalt or Warm Mix Asphalt projects where wearing and binder courses are specified, provided surface‑course thicknesses meet the minimum values in Table 9.5, the existing pavement beneath an overlay is stable (no movement under the roller), has been properly leveled, sealed and patched, and the plans clearly indicate the State Route limits for the paving area. It may also be used on any asphalt‑concrete paving course—wearing, binder or base—that must be designed to resist the climatic and traffic conditions of a specific location using a volumetric mixture design tied to a 20‑year design life ESAL estimate, with the required PG‑binder and mix stiffness selected from the five ESAL ranges (< 0.3 million, 0.3 to < 3.0 million, 3.0 to < 10.0 million, 10.0 to < 30.0 million, ≥ 30.0 million). When the estimated ESAL range exceeds 10 million, the District should specify a polymer‑modified PG 64E‑22 binder for wearing courses where severe rutting or steep grades are expected; when the ESAL range is 0.3 to < 3.0 million, the District may limit the specification to an ESAL range of < 0.3 million for the wearing and binder courses while leaving base courses unchanged, particularly where asphalt cracking is a regional problem and rutting is not a concern. Optional fiber‑modified mixes are permitted on eligible wearing, binder or base courses that use one of the three standard PG binders (PG 58S‑28, PG 64S‑22, PG 64E‑22), and an Asphalt Rich Base Course is required for all new or reconstructed pavements whose total base thickness exceeds 6 inches. Superpave mixture design may be employed for both Conventional Pavement Courses and dedicated Superpave Pavement Courses, but only the three standard PG binders may be specified; a “bumping” of the high‑temperature grade may be applied when higher summer temperatures are expected to obtain a stiffer binder for better rut resistance, and any nonstandard PG binder requires prior approval from the BOCM Materials Division Laboratory Testing Section together with a Superpave Special Study Project. Approved PG 64E‑22 binders are polymer‑modified with styrene block copolymers (SB or SBS) and are listed in Publication 35; provisionally approved PG 64E‑22 binders may be used on any project requiring PG 64E‑22, and the three standard grades together with these approved binders have demonstrated good performance under Pennsylvania’s climatic conditions, various traffic conditions, and a range of project types. For flexible asphalt overlays, Superpave mixes—including thin wearing courses—may be used when the overlay thickness is at least 1 ½ inches unless prior approval is obtained; on non‑Interstate projects this minimum applies only when the existing surface is asphalt, the pavement is structurally sound with less than 2 % requiring patching, and both surface and subsurface drainage are good or will be upgraded to good without a structural upgrade. The 6.3 mm Superpave thin asphalt wearing course may be placed on sound pavements using PG 64E‑22 binder but shall not be used as a scratch or leveling course, whereas the 9.5 mm Fine Grade Superpave wearing course is limited to sound pavement surfaces and must not use PG 64E‑22 binder. Superpave is also appropriate for plant‑mixed asphalt wearing courses—including scratch and leveling variants—and for asphalt binder courses (including leveling) that are placed on prepared surfaces in flexible pavement projects constructed by either standard or rapid‑placement‑system methods, with acceptance based on certification and density acceptance using Percent‑Within‑Limits testing of in‑place density, asphalt content, and sieve gradations. The pavement is considered defective when the payment factors for asphalt content, percent passing the 75 µm (No. 200) sieve, and percent passing the 2.36 mm (No. 8) sieve each equal 85 %, or when any Percent‑Within‑Limits parameter falls below 50 %. Superpave is specified for the asphalt base course and for the SRL‑L wearing course of paved shoulders, requiring a PG 64S‑22 binder and limiting traffic to less than 0.3 million ESALs unless otherwise indicated. For asphalt base courses, Superpave mixtures with nominal maximum aggregate sizes of 25.0 mm or 37.5 mm may be placed only on dry surfaces when the air or surface temperature exceeds 35 °F; placement is prohibited on wet surfaces or when the temperature is 35 °F or lower. When a Superpave 25.0 mm base course requires a compacted depth greater than 6 inches, it must be laid in two or more layers each between 3 and 6 inches thick; when a Superpave 37.5 mm base course requires a compacted depth greater than 8 inches, it must be placed in two or more layers each between 4 and 8 inches thick. The aggregate for wearing courses must meet the SRL designation called for the course, and a 4.75 mm mixture may not be used where an SRL higher than L is required. Reclaimed asphalt pavement (RAP) may be incorporated provided at least 5 % RAP consisting of cold‑milled or crushed asphalt is used; up to 5 % manufacturer‑waste recycled shingles may be added if certified and passing a 9.5 mm sieve, while for asphalt‑rich base courses RAP may not exceed 20 % by weight and recycled asphalt shingles are prohibited; RAP and RAS are not permitted in the 6.3 mm thin overlay courses. Fly ash is prohibited whenever the design traffic is greater than or equal to 3 million ESALs, and hydrated lime may constitute up to 2 % of the weight of total dry aggregate unless otherwise shown on the plans. Superpave is appropriate for standard and rapid‑placement construction of plant‑mixed asphalt wearing courses that are intended to be fine‑graded mixtures, for standard construction of plant‑mixed asphalt base courses, for standard construction of plant‑mixed asphalt rich base courses, and for construction of dense‑graded asphalt pavement courses; in each case the mixture must be designed volumetrically with the Superpave Gyratory Compactor and may incorporate prescribed manufactured additives, modifiers, or plant process modifications as required. For plant‑mixed thin asphalt overlay courses with a nominal maximum aggregate size of 6.3 mm, Superpave is appropriate when virgin aggregates, an asphalt binder, and an approved warm‑mix asphalt technology are sized, uniformly graded and combined to produce a Job Mix Formula that satisfies the material, gradation and volumetric requirements of Bulletin 27; moisture‑susceptibility testing that includes the WMA additive must be performed and the certified JMF submitted for review before production. The aggregate must come from approved producers meeting the specified SRL designation, fine aggregates must meet the uncompacted void content and sand‑equivalent values of AASHTO M 323 Table 6, coarse aggregates must satisfy the maximum abrasion loss in Bulletin 27 Chapter 2A Table 6A, flat‑and‑elongated particle limits of 10 % for a 1:5 ratio or 20 % for a 1:3 ratio, and the minimum crushed‑fragment percentage of AASHTO M 323 Table 6. For asphalt‑rich base course mixtures constructed under the standard and rapid‑placement specifications, Superpave is appropriate when virgin aggregates and binder are used, optionally with warm‑mix additives or modifiers, provided the resulting JMF meets the volumetric requirements of Bulletin 27; RAP may be incorporated but must not exceed 20 % by weight and recycled asphalt shingles are prohibited. The design package must document material sources, calibration factors and any WMA additive and be submitted to DME/DMM at least three weeks before production. For asphalt‑concrete wearing courses on flexible pavements covered by the 60‑month warranty, Superpave is appropriate when the volumetric mix is developed with the SGC, documented on a JMF that uses aggregates having the specified SRL designation from Bulletin 14 (or an equal or better blend), and may include Stone Matrix Asphalt wearing‑course mixes; the design package must contain aggregate and binder percentages, specific gravities, VMA, VTM, VFA, moisture‑sensitivity data and SGC gyrations at Nini, Ndes and Nmax, be signed by a Certified Asphalt Level 2 Plant Technician and submitted at least seven calendar days before use. In the general scope (section 2.1) a Superpave Restricted Performance Specification is not appropriate and another design approach should be selected whenever the existing pavement cannot be shown to be stable, properly leveled, sealed and patched before resurfacing, when the Superpave wearing or binder course thickness does not meet the minimum requirements in Table 9.5, when a uniform nominal depth of at least 5,600 square yards on a prepared surface cannot be provided, or when continuous placement operations are not feasible. The specification permits Stone Matrix Asphalt (SMA) wearing‑course mixes to be submitted for consideration, allowing designers to choose that alternative design approach instead of a strictly Superpave mixture when an SMA wearing course is desired. Any change in material source or other adjustments during construction must be reported in writing and accompanied by a revised joint mix formula, which could also lead to adopting a different mix‑design method. Design inputs and requirements

Superpave design must comply with the Superpave Restricted Performance Specification (RPS) found in Publication 408, Specifications Section 413 [1]. Under this RPS the wearing and binder course thicknesses must satisfy the minimum values shown in Table 9.5 [1]. Asphalt overlays require that the existing pavement be stable (no movement under the roller), properly leveled, sealed and patched before placement [1]. Plans and proposals must explicitly show State Route limits for all surface courses built under RPS on typical sections and tabulation sheets [1]. Projects using RPS must be placed on a prepared surface, have a uniform nominal depth of not less than 5,600 square yards, allow continuous placement operations, and clearly delineate SR paving limits [1]. Designers must base mixture design on a selected performance‑grade (PG) binder and the 20‑year design‑life equivalent single axle loads (ESALs), calculated for all projects regardless of intended service life [1]. The resulting ESAL value places the project into one of five ranges (< 0.3 million, 0.3 to < 3.0 million, 3.0 to < 10.0 million, 10.0 to < 30.0 million, ≥ 30.0 million) that dictate binder selection and mix requirements [1]. When the ESAL range exceeds 10 million a polymer‑modified PG 64E‑22 binder shall be specified for wearing courses [1]. Projects should limit the total number of individual Superpave mix designs to no more than three, with major projects not exceeding five, to avoid confusion and higher costs [1]. If mix quantities are less than 250 tons or less than 1,000 square yards at 1½ inches the ESAL range may be changed up or down by one range; any other changes require PDAU permission [1]. Optional fiber‑modified courses are allowed only with PG 58S‑28, PG 64S‑22, or PG 64E‑22 binders as defined in special provision c00057 [1]. An Asphalt Rich Base Course must be used for new or reconstructed pavements whose total base thickness exceeds 6 inches, providing a bottom layer of 3 to 6 inches with mix designs under 0.3 million ESALs [1]. Only three standard PG‑binders—PG 58S‑28, PG 64S‑22, and PG 64E‑22—are to be specified for both conventional and Superpave pavement courses; the appropriate grade is selected from Table 5.5 to match the project type [1]. A “bumping” practice may raise the high‑temperature grade of a standard binder only with prior approval from BOCM Materials Division Laboratory Testing Section [1]. Nonstandard PG‑binders such as PG 64S‑28 or PG 70‑22 may be used only with prior approval and must be accompanied by a Superpave Special Study Project [1]. Approved PG 64E‑22 binders are listed in Publication 35, Approved Construction Materials (Bulletin 15), and are polymer‑modified with SB or SBS formulations; provisionally approved PG 64E‑22 binders modified with other polymers such as SBR may also be used [1]. Projects requiring nonstandard PG‑binder grades or unapproved PG 64E‑22 binders must be submitted to BOCM, MD for approval and must include one or more standard PG‑binder grades or currently approved PG 64E‑22 binders as control sections [1]. Superpave overlay designs are governed by the specifications in Publication 408; placement depths must follow Table 9.5 (and related tables), weather constraints are limited by Section 413.3(b), and traffic‑volume limits are checked against Tables 10.1/10.2 [1]. On Interstates a Superpave Wearing Course overlay thinner than 1½ inches may be used only with prior PDAU approval, while on non‑Interstate routes the same thin thickness is allowed only when the existing surface is asphalt, structurally sound (less than 2 % patching), and both surface and subsurface drainage are good or will be upgraded [1]. The 6.3 mm Thin Asphalt Wearing Course must use PG 64E‑22 binder and may employ Non‑Tracking Tack Coat per Publication 408 Section 460, whereas the 9.5 mm Fine Grade wearing course shall not use PG 64E‑22 [1]. The design of Superpave mixtures for plant‑mixed dense‑graded courses is governed by the Standard and RPS construction specifications in Section 413, which require volumetric mix development with the Superpave Gyratory Compactor and acceptance based on laboratory thin‑film shear testing of field samples together with Percent Within Limits statistical evaluation [2]. Measurement and payment requirements are detailed in Section 413.4; in‑place density, asphalt content, percent passing the 75 µm sieve and the primary control sieve must be evaluated as Percent Within Limits, and when PWL ≥ 90 the pay factor for a parameter is calculated by PF_X = 100 + 0.4 (PWLx − 90), while for 50 ≤ PWL < 90 a different equation applies [2]. The overall lot pay factor is derived from weighted sums of those individual factors, and the pavement is considered defective if the payment factor for asphalt content, percent passing the 75 µm sieve and percent passing the 2.36 mm sieve are all 85 percent, or if density cores produce a Percent Within Limits less than 50; retesting may be requested in writing when an overall lot pay factor is below 90.00, or when the asphalt‑content or density pay factor for the lot is below 80.00 [2]. Under Section 651.2 material requirements for Superpave base and wearing courses specify a performance grade of PG 64S‑22 and limit design traffic to less than 0.3 million ESALs unless otherwise indicated or directed [2]. Section 313.3 prohibits placement of a Superpave base course on prepared surfaces that are wet or when the air temperature or surface temperature is 35 °F or lower, and mandates that if the indicated compacted depth of a 25.0 mm base course exceeds six inches it be placed in two or more layers of approximately equal compacted depth with no layer less than three inches or greater than six inches; similarly, if the indicated compacted depth of a 37.5 mm base course exceeds eight inches it must be placed in two or more layers each between four inches and eight inches [2]. Aggregate for Superpave mixtures must be supplied by producers listed in Bulletin 14 and must meet the quality requirements of Bulletin 27; uncompacted void content shall be determined according to AASHTO T 304 Method A (or taken from Bulletin 14) and shall conform to AASHTO M 323 Table 6, sand‑equivalent values shall be obtained by AASHTO T 176 and also conform to the same table, and coarse‑aggregate abrasion limits are prescribed in Bulletin 27 Chapter 2A Table 6A with maximum percentages for thin, elongated pieces and crushed fragments as given in AASHTO M 323 Table 6 [2]. For 6.3 mm thin overlays flat‑and‑elongated particle limits are ten percent for a 1:5 ratio and twenty percent for a 1:3 ratio per ASTM D4791, and minimum crushed‑fragment percentages must meet the same table [2]. Recycled materials may be used only under conditions that at least five percent of the mixture consist of cold‑milled or crushed RAP; manufacturer‑waste recycled shingles are limited to five percent by mass with certification per Section 106.03(b)3, and fly ash is prohibited when design traffic is greater than or equal to three million ESALs; for Asphalt Rich Base Course mixtures RAP shall not exceed twenty percent by weight and manufacturer‑waste recycled asphalt shingles are prohibited [2]. Design methods and criteria

Superpave employs a volumetric asphalt mixture design procedure that replaces the Marshall method and uses the Superpave Gyratory Compactor (SGC) as the primary design tool for dense‑graded wearing courses, fine‑graded mixtures, standard base courses, asphalt‑rich base courses, and 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlays [2]. For each mixture a Job Mix Formula (JMF) must be prepared in accordance with the material, gradation, and volumetric requirements of Bulletin 27 Chapter 2A, documented on Form TR‑448A, signed by a certified Asphalt Level 2 Plant Technician, and submitted at least three weeks before production for most mixtures and at least seven calendar days before use for thin overlays [2]. The JMF must include the calibration factors C¯ and 200 C¯ (or Cf) as required by PTM No. 757, and a warm‑mix additive may be added only during production [2]. Aggregate quality is verified with AASHTO T304 Method A to determine uncompacted void content, or the value listed in Bulletin 14, and the resulting uncompacted void content must conform to the limits listed in AASHTO M 323 Table 6; sand equivalent is measured with AASHTO T176 and also must meet those limits, while coarse‑aggregate abrasion loss limits are taken from Bulletin 27 Chapter 2A Table 6A [2]. Flat and elongated particles are limited to a maximum 10 % for a 1:5 ratio and a maximum 20 % for a 1:3 ratio according to ASTM D4791 on material retained on the No. 4 sieve, and minimum crushed‑fragment percentages required by AASHTO M 323 Table 6 must be satisfied [2]. The filler may include hydrated lime up to 2 % of the weight of total dry aggregate, but fly ash shall not be used when the design traffic is greater than or equal to 3 million ESALs [2]. Recycled asphalt pavement may be incorporated at a minimum of 5 % by mass of cold‑milled or crushed material; if recycled asphalt shingles are used they may constitute up to 5 % by mass provided the shingle material passes a 9.5 mm sieve and is blended 50 % with fine aggregate, whereas shredded shingles are prohibited in asphalt‑rich base courses and RAP content in those mixtures may not exceed 20 % by weight; if reclaimed asphalt shingle (RAS) material is proposed, use 5 % RAS by mass of the total mixture [2]. When a warm‑mix additive is used, a moisture‑susceptibility analysis prescribed in Bulletin 27 must be performed and the JMF must include moisture‑sensitivity data such as tensile strength ratio or Hamburg wheel‑tracking results together with a quality‑control plan that provides certified test results within 48 hours [2]. Test specimens are produced with the SGC and their bulk specific gravity is measured according to AASHTO T312; air voids (Va) and Voids in Mineral Aggregate (VMA) are then calculated at Ndesign using the equations in AASHTO R35 as referenced by Bulletin 27 [2]. Quality‑control testing includes draindown testing per AASHTO T305 with a 1‑hour reading, assessment of particle coating degree per AASHTO T195 requiring at least 95.0 % coated particles, and when a warm‑mix additive is used the VMA minimum may be reduced to 16.5 % [2]. Acceptance of a constructed dense‑graded course relies on laboratory testing of field cores with the LTS method and on statistical evaluation using Percent Within Limits (PWL) procedures; for each evaluated parameter—density, asphalt content, percent passing the No. 200 sieve, and primary control sieve—the PWL value must be at least 50 % to avoid a defective lot [2]. When PWL is ≥ 90, the pay factor for the specific parameter is calculated as PF = 100 + 0.4(PWL – 90); when PWL is < 90 and ≥ 50, the pay factor is calculated as PF = 70 + 0.75(PWL – 50) [2]. The individual pay factors for density (PFD), asphalt content (PFAC), No. 200 sieve (PF200), and primary control sieve (PFPCS) are combined into an overall lot pay factor according to OLPF = 0.5·PFD + 0.3·PFAC + 0.1·PF200 + 0.1·PFPCS, and the final lot payment equals the contract unit price multiplied by OLPF divided by 100 [2]. For each project the designer must calculate 20‑year design life equivalent single axle loads (ESALs) and place those values into one of five predefined ESAL ranges—less than 0.3 million; 0.3 to < 3.0 million; 3.0 to < 10.0 million; 10.0 to < 30.0 million; or ≥ 30.0 million—and then select the appropriate performance‑grade (PG) binder, with polymer‑modified binders such as PG 64E‑22 required when the range exceeds 10 million ESALs [1]. Under the Superpave mixture design system binder selection is limited to three standard PG‑binders—PG 58S‑28, PG 64S‑22, and PG 64E‑22—which must be chosen according to the project type and pavement course; when higher summer temperatures are expected the practice of “bumping” the high‑temperature grade (for example moving from PG 58S‑28 to PG 64S‑28) may be used to obtain a stiffer binder that resists rutting, and any nonstandard PG‑binder may only be used after prior approval from BOCM and completion of a Superpave Special Study Project [1]. Superpave design also relies on a set of reference tables that serve as the primary design tools; Tables 9.3, 9.5, 10.4, and 10.5 are used to obtain the structural coefficient number, the minimum and maximum placement depths, and the allowable scratch or leveling course thicknesses for each mix size, while Tables 10.1 and 10.2 provide the average daily traffic (ADT) limits that govern proper application [1]. The specified binder grade PG 64E‑22 must be used for all standard 6.3 mm wearing‑course overlays but is not permitted for 9.5 mm Fine Grade applications, and a minimum one‑inch thickness is required when a leveling course is placed to correct profile or cross‑section [1].

The contractor must provide material that ensures the pavement meets warranty performance criteria by selecting the appropriate type and class of asphalt binder, aggregate and, when needed, filler; for wearing‑course layers the aggregate shall have the specified SRL designation from a source listed in Bulletin 14, and any blend may be used provided it is 50 % by weight, with Joint Mix Formulas established through a volumetric mix design using the Superpave Gyratory Compactor and documented on Form TR‑448A at least seven calendar days before use [2].

Structural considerations are addressed by following the structural coefficient and the minimum and maximum placement depths listed in Tables 9.3, 9.5, 10.4 and 10.5, so only mixes such as Superpave 25.0 mm, 19.0 mm, 12.5 mm or 9.5 mm are used at those depths to achieve the required cross‑section, with a leveling course required to be at least 1‑inch thick and thin overlays required to meet the depth limits in Table 9.5; Superpave Wearing Course thickness less than 1½ inches is allowed only with prior approval, and on Interstates a Superpave Wearing Course overlay thinner than 1½ inches may not be placed without prior approval from PDAU [1]. Constructability is protected by prohibiting the combination of a binder course with a leveling course and requiring separate scratch or leveling courses where needed, while most projects should have no more than three individual mix designs per project and major projects should not exceed five individual mix designs; the ESAL range may be changed up or down by one range for mix‑design quantities less than 250 tons or less than 1,000 square yards at 1½ inches to minimize the number of mix designs per project [1]. Durability and performance are further ensured by using PG 64E‑22 binder for standard 6.3 mm wearing courses (but not for 9.5 mm Fine Grade courses), limiting applications to sound pavement surfaces, satisfying ADT limits in Tables 10.1/10.2, and performing moisture‑susceptibility analysis as required; fiber‑modified courses improve crack resistance, and the specified aggregate quality criteria together with RAP limits and moisture testing support long‑term performance [1][2].

Structural details and interfaces

Superpave specifications require the use of a Safety Edge as a standard pavement edge treatment on the outside edge of asphalt pavements and shoulders, applied to wearing or binder courses that are at least 1 ½ inches deep and not exceeding a total thickness of 5 inches; this detail adds roughly one percent to the material quantity. In addition, an asphalt paving interlayer may be placed between overlay layers—options include geotextile paving fabric, fiberglass mesh grids (with pressure‑sensitive adhesive or tack coat), or composite reinforcing grids—to provide moisture barrier and mitigate reflective cracking, provided the interlayer is compatible with the Superpave mix and installed according to Publication 408 Section 466. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (pp. 115–120)

In projects that adopt the Superpave Restricted Performance Specification (section 2.1 GENERAL), the interface between the existing pavement and the new Superpave wearing or binder course must be prepared so that the old surface is stable—no movement under the roller—and properly leveled, sealed and patched before any resurfacing material is placed [1]. The thickness of the Superpave courses must satisfy the minimum values given in Table 9.5, and any surface‑level corrections or cross‑slope adjustments should be accomplished with a dedicated leveling course rather than an asphalt separation layer [1]. Plans and proposals must clearly show the State Route limits for the RPS paving so that the extent of each interface is documented [1]. Within Superpave paving courses (section 5.9 SUPERPAVE PAVING COURSES), the interface between layers is addressed by selecting a performance‑grade binder and mix type that correspond to the projected ESAL range; when the estimated ESAL range exceeds ten million ESALs, the District should specify a polymer‑modified (PG 64E‑22) binder for wearing courses, including leveling or binder courses where severe rutting conditions are existing or expected [1]. Optionally, an Asphalt Rich Base Course may be placed in the bottom three to six inches of the first base lift so that the lowest layer has the highest tensile strength and better resistance to bottom‑up cracking and moisture damage; all ARBC materials are < 0.3 million ESAL (50 gyrations) mix designs with design air voids of 2.5 % [1]. Designers also limit the total number of distinct mix designs—generally no more than three for most projects and five for major projects—to keep interfaces consistent [1]. For overlays (section 5.12 OVERLAYS), a separate scratch or leveling course must be placed before any resurfacing, with the leveling course required to be at least 1‑inch thick and counted as a structural layer when used to correct profile [1]. When an existing asphalt overlay sits on a concrete or brick base, the design calls for retaining a minimum of 1 ½ inches of the original asphalt so that adequate bond can be achieved unless coring shows debonding, in which case full removal is required [1]. Edge treatment is provided by the Safety Edge incorporated into the wearing or binder course; its depth must be no less than 1 ½ inches and shall not exceed 5 inches [1]. Where reflective cracking or moisture intrusion is a concern, an asphalt paving interlayer—such as geotextile fabric, fiberglass mesh, or composite reinforcing grid—may be installed between the old and new layers; the interlayer must be compatible with the Superpave mix, thermally stable, and typically used with an overlay of at least 1 ½ inches to allow milling of the grid [1]. When a Superpave base course requires a compacted depth that exceeds the single‑lift limits, the design calls for placing the asphalt in two or more lifts of roughly equal thickness; each lift must not be less than 3 inches nor more than 6 inches for a 25.0 mm mix when the total depth is more than 6 inches [2]. If the indicated compacted depth of a Superpave 37.5 mm asphalt base course is more than 8 inches, each lift must not be less than 4 inches nor more than 8 inches [2]. The finished surface is considered defective if any irregularities exceed 1/4 inch, and core testing must verify that the compacted depth does not fall short by 1/2 inch or more; deficiencies beyond these limits require immediate correction [2].

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

Superpave addresses climatic and moisture‑related environmental effects by tailoring the mixture to the project’s specific climate through selection of a performance‑grade binder and by using advanced performance testing that predicts how the mix will behave under those conditions. [1] For additional protection against moisture damage, the specification permits the use of an Asphalt Rich Base Course whose mixes are limited to < 0.3 million ESALs and have design air voids of 2.5 % to give the bottom layer the highest tensile strength and improve resistance to bottom‑up cracking and moisture damage. [1] Superpave overlays must be placed only when the existing surface drainage is good or will be upgraded to good and the subsurface drainage is likewise good or will be upgraded to good for the project; thin wearing courses on non‑Interstate pavements therefore require these drainage conditions in addition to a sound underlying asphalt. [1] When moisture protection is needed, a geotextile paving fabric or other approved interlayer may be used as a pavement moisture barrier that also reduces reflective cracking, and the selection should consider climate factors such as freeze‑thaw cycles, which can be mitigated by using a thicker overlay together with an interlayer to delay cracking in harsh environments. [1] In accordance with Section 313.3 of the Superpave specifications, a base course shall not be placed on prepared surfaces that are wet or when the air temperature or the surface temperature is 35 °F or lower; if work is halted because of weather conditions, the Representative may allow the Contractor to place limited quantities of base course that are en route to the project. [2] Section 413.2(b) requires moisture to be addressed through moisture‑susceptibility testing as part of the mix design, and it permits the use of a Warm Mix Asphalt (WMA) technology additive, modifier or foaming process solely for evaluating those test results, with the resulting JMF cover sheet required to record the additive dosage, material code and the susceptibility data. [2] Section 419.1(f) mandates that mix designs include moisture susceptibility analysis as required by Bulletin 27 and verify that any WMA additive does not reduce the mixture’s resistance to moisture; a draindown test performed according to AASHTO T 305 with a one‑hour reading is required, the mix must meet the tolerances in Table B, and the degree of particle coating must be at least 95.0 % to ensure asphalt remains adhered under wet conditions. According to the Pavement Policy Manual section 5.12 OVERLAYS, Superpave overlay designs must incorporate a safety‑edge geometry by providing a tapered edge treatment on the outside of asphalt pavements and shoulders, applied to wearing or binder courses that are at least 1 ½ inches thick and not deeper than five inches total. Verification and expected performance

For Superpave Special Study projects the District must coordinate with LTS and NECEPT and follow a sampling plan that may include detailed pre‑construction manual pavement distress surveys, additional asphalt binder sampling, loose box sampling and core sampling during construction, followed by post‑construction manual pavement distress surveys and core sampling for a period of three to five years after construction; these activities are used to verify compliance, constructability, performance and consistency with the project requirements. The first step in checking the completed Superpave mixture is the submission of a signed Joint Mix Formula (JMF) prepared by a certified Asphalt Level 2 plant technician together with the producer’s quality‑control plan and, where required, calibration factors (C¯ and 200 C¯ or Cƒ and 200 Cƒ). The JMF must be delivered to the District Maintenance Engineer/District Materials Manager at least three weeks before the planned start of mixture production for plant‑mixed courses (Section 413) and at least seven calendar days before any placement for warranty‑controlled work (Section 496); production may not begin until the DME/DMM has reviewed and approved the submission. The reviewer checks that the JMF conforms to the volumetric, gradation and material requirements of Bulletin 27, that all aggregate sources are listed in accordance with Bulletin 14, that the nominal maximum aggregate size and any specified RAP or RAS percentages (minimum 5 % each where required, not exceeding twenty percent RAP by weight for rich‑base courses) are observed, and that any warm‑mix additive or modifier is incorporated only after the base JMF has been approved. Moisture‑susceptibility testing results such as TSR data or draindown values must be documented on the JMF cover sheet and meet the tolerances of Tables D and E; a percent of coated particles of at least 95.0 % is required to demonstrate that the mix can be handled, placed and compacted without stripping. For base‑course placement the inspector confirms that placement does not occur on wet surfaces or when air or prepared‑surface temperature is 35 °F or lower, and then verifies that the compacted depth of each layer falls within the prescribed limits (no layer less than 3 inches and no more than 6 inches for a 25.0 mm mix, and no layer less than 4 inches and no more than 8 inches for a 37.5 mm mix). After compaction full‑depth cores are drilled at one random location per 3,000 sq yd of finished base (and any additional suspect spots) and the depth is measured; pavement deficient in depth by ½ inch or more that cannot be satisfactorily corrected is deemed defective, as are surface irregularities greater than ¼ inch. In‑place performance of plant‑mixed courses is evaluated by calculating percent‑within‑limits (PWL) values for density, asphalt content, the 75 µm sieve and the primary control sieve; when each PWL value is at least 50, the corresponding pay factor is computed using the prescribed equations—100 + 0.4(PWL‑90) when PWL ≥ 90 or 70 + 0.75(PWL‑50) when 50 ≤ PWL < 90—and the overall lot pay factor is derived from the weighted sum of those individual factors. Any PWL that falls below 50 triggers a defect determination (PWLD or PWLAC < 50 makes the lot defective; PWL200 < 50 is defective unless the average 75 µm sieve result is within ±2.0 % of the JMF, and PWLPCS < 50 sets PFPCS to 60), prompting the District Executive to order leave‑in‑place with a reduced overall lot pay factor or full removal and replacement. When test results are borderline—such as an overall lot pay factor below 90, an asphalt‑content pay factor under 80, or a density pay factor under 80—the contractor may request a written retest within three weeks, providing supporting quality‑control data for the Department’s consideration. Throughout construction certified quality‑control test results must be turned over to the Representative within 48 hours of each material sample to confirm that the actual aggregate, filler and binder match the design and satisfy performance criteria; any change in material source requires written notification to the DME/DMM together with a revised JMF, thereby maintaining consistency with project requirements. Each producer must prepare a QC plan as specified in Section 106 and submit it at the start of the project; production may not begin until the QC plan and the JMF have been submitted and approved.
