Saw Cutting

Part of: Contraction Joint is-subtopic-of
Saw Cutting is a topic under Contraction Joint.
saw-cutssawed
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-28

Saw cutting is a controlled concrete‑cutting operation that creates full‑depth transverse and longitudinal slots in pavement to provide the precise joints required for dowel‑bar retrofits, removal of failed slab sections, and control of random cracking [1][2]. It works by advancing a diamond‑tipped blade (or, for initial vertical cuts, a wheel saw with carbide‑steel tips) equipped with a blade guard, alignment guides, water‑cooling and cut‑depth controls, producing full‑depth cuts whose kerf is limited to one‑half inch before traffic can be reopened [2]. The defining characteristics are that the cuts are made only after the concrete has attained sufficient strength to prevent raveling or chipping, are positioned at least 24 inches from existing cracks and away from reinforcement, extend to the required depth (often D/3 or full thickness plus ½ inch depending on application), and are immediately flushed with water to remove slurry before sealant installation [1][2].

Purpose and applicability

Saw cutting is employed to create the precise slots required for a dowel‑bar retrofit, thereby addressing insufficient load transfer across transverse joints or cracks in heavily loaded concrete pavements. In removal of a failed concrete section saw cutting solves the problem of removing the interior portion without damaging the surrounding pavement by providing full‑depth transverse cuts that form a trench to relieve compression before lift‑out. The initial full‑depth cuts are made from the outside edge toward the longitudinal cut, and a second transverse cut of at least 1 inch minimum depth is placed at each end of the patch area; these cuts must not extend into adjacent pavement, must stay at least 24 inches away from existing cracks, and must avoid cutting reinforcement bars. A wheel saw with carbide‑steel tips may be used for the full‑depth cuts, while penetration into the subbase may not exceed ½ inch. Saw cutting solves the problem of uncontrolled, random cracking in newly placed concrete slabs by creating transverse and longitudinal contraction joints that act as weakened planes, directing where cracks will develop. The operation is performed only after the concrete has attained sufficient strength to prevent significant raveling or chipping, and the freshly cut joint is immediately flushed with water to remove sawing slurry that could otherwise impede proper sealant installation. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements saw cutting solves the problem of random cracking by cutting the pavement vertically to about one‑quarter of its thickness at the planned joint locations, with cuts placed behind the rolling operation as soon as possible and no later than eighteen hours after placement. For bonded concrete overlays saw cutting solves the problem of joint debonding by providing a clean, properly sized cut that matches or exceeds the width of any underlying joint or crack; the cut must extend through the full thickness of the overlay plus an additional ½ inch while avoiding damage to dowel load transfer devices. When installing full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs saw cutting solves the problem of establishing a proper longitudinal joint between the new slab and existing pavement by overcutting the existing longitudinal joint to a straight, smooth surface that defines where the old pavement will be removed and where the new slab edges will meet. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 89–90); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–469)

Saw cutting is appropriate when full‑depth longitudinal and transverse cuts are required along the perimeter of a concrete pavement patch, provided a diamond‑tipped blade saw with water cooling, blade guard, alignment guides and cut‑depth controls is used and the resulting cuts do not exceed one‑half inch in width before traffic is allowed; full‑depth cuts may extend into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches so long as dowel bars are not damaged. An alternate wheel saw with carbide steel tips may be employed only prior to making those full‑depth transverse cuts, and its penetration must be limited to minimize subbase disturbance, with the method discontinued if results are unsatisfactory. Saw cutting is also appropriate for creating the initial full‑depth transverse cuts that start at the pavement edge and proceed toward the longitudinal cut, as well as for the second transverse cuts that must be at least 1 inch deep at each end of the patch; these cuts may not extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders, must be placed outside a 24‑inch zone from any existing crack, and should avoid cutting reinforcement bars—if a bar is cut the partial‑depth cut must be changed to a full‑depth cut and an additional partial‑depth cut added. A wheel saw with carbide steel tips may be used for the full‑depth cuts to provide a trench that relieves slab compression, but the Representative shall discontinue use of the wheel saw if the results are unsatisfactory, in which case removal of concrete should be accomplished with jackhammers or hand tools instead. For transverse contraction joints in slip‑form paving, saw cutting is appropriate once the concrete has hardened enough to be cut without excessive raveling and to prevent random cracking; the cuts must reach a depth of D/3 with a width of 1/8 inch as required by Section 501.3(i). When paving during the cold‑season window of October 1 through April 1, an early entry saw or another approved method should be used instead to promote dominant joint development in the adjacent lane. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements, saw cutting is appropriate when the plans specify control joints; the joint should be saw‑cut to a depth of one‑quarter of the compacted thickness and the cut must be made no later than 18 hours after placement to avoid random cracking. When an alternative to a saw cut is desired, such as for edge compaction, the specification allows using an edging shoe on the paver set at an angle of 15 to 30 degrees instead of cutting. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs, saw cutting is appropriate when the project requires a full‑depth cut along the limits of the precast slabs and the proposed longitudinal joints; a diamond saw must be used and a wheel saw with carbide steel tips may not be used. After the diamond‑saw cuts are made, the existing concrete or composite pavement should be removed by the lift‑out method rather than by any other removal technique. In bonded concrete overlays over asphalt‑surfaced pavements, sawing is the only acceptable technique for creating the transverse joints; it should be performed as soon as the slab has hardened enough to permit sawing without excessive raveling and thereby prevent random cracking. However, if a crack already exists at or within 1.5 feet of the intended joint location—either before or during the cutting operation—the specification requires that sawing be omitted and the situation addressed by other provisions. When a full‑depth control joint is required in existing pavement and the lines have been indicated or directed, saw cutting is appropriate; the work is performed during construction using a saw blade or cutting wheel to produce neat lines, after which the excavation must be kept graded to drain, sealed with smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment, and the surface resealed at day’s end to avoid subgrade saturation. If the material to be removed is rock that cannot be taken out by other excavating methods, drilling and blasting should be used instead, following the same line locations while minimizing disturbance to adjacent material. [2]

Required inputs

Saw cutting in the various concrete pavement applications described in the report requires specific equipment, material conditions, mixture properties, and site constraints that differ by section. For Section 516, the saw used for cutting the perimeter of a concrete patch must be equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls; cooling water, slurry and dust from the operation may not enter any lane opened to traffic, traffic may be allowed on the patched area for a maximum of 72 hours after cutting, and only full‑depth cuts whose width does not exceed 1/2 inch may be opened to traffic. Section 518 permits the use of a wheel saw having carbide‑steel tips for making full‑depth vertical cuts from the pavement edge toward the longitudinal cut, requires a second transverse cut of at least 1 inch depth at each end of the patch area, and stipulates that cuts shall not be located within 24 inches of an existing crack, shall not extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders, and shall avoid cutting reinforcement bars—if any bar is cut the partial‑depth cut becomes a full‑depth cut and another minimum‑1‑inch cut is added; wheel penetration into the subbase is limited to a maximum of 1/2 inch, and if break back exceeds 2 inches or subbase disturbance exceeds 1 inch the disturbed material must be removed and replaced, with any required new bond breaker consisting of two layers of at least 4.0‑mil polyethylene sheeting, while small vibratory compactors are used to re‑compact any surface that was disturbed. Section 501 specifies that saw cutting of transverse contraction joints shall be performed with a saw that includes guides, a blade guard, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth control, that the contractor shall have sufficient spare equipment on site before concrete placement, and that cuts are made only after the concrete has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling, using a depth of D/3 and a width of 1/8 inch; any non‑membrane curing cover must be removed no later than 1/2 hour before cutting. Section 501 further notes that sawing may proceed day or night regardless of weather, and when work occurs between October 1 and April 1 an early‑entry saw or other approved method is recommended to achieve the required joint development. Section 545 requires that the concrete overlay be placed when the fresh concrete temperature is between 65°F and 90°F and the prepared surface temperature be less than 120°F immediately before placement, with fog‑misting or protective covers used to maintain proper moisture and temperature; joint backing or other approved joint filling material must be installed to within ±1/4 inch of the pavement surface in all existing transverse joints prior to overlay. After the concrete has attained sufficient strength for cutting without significant raveling or chipping, saw cuts are made to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus 1/2 inch, with a width at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack; intermediate transverse joints must be spaced so that no panel exceeds 15 feet in length and longitudinal joints require a sealant reservoir cut 1/4 inch wide to the full depth of the overlay plus 1/2 inch, after which the joint is flushed with water and any curing compound on joint faces must be removed before sealant installation. Section 545 further requires that the concrete achieve a compressive strength of at least 3,600 psi and a bond‑strength average of 175 psi (each test not less than 160 psi) before traffic opening. Section 502 mandates that the RCC mixture be placed and compacted within a temperature range of 50°F to 90°F, be free of frost, lumps or crusts, and be kept moist for curing; control joints shall be cut with an approved saw (or wheel cutter) set to a depth of one‑quarter of the compacted pavement and performed behind the rolling operation no later than eighteen hours after placement so that raveling is avoided. Section 529 requires that saw cutting of precast concrete pavement slabs be performed with a diamond saw, not a wheel saw with carbide steel tips, and that it cut through the full depth of the slab limits and proposed longitudinal joints after an accurate survey layout has been established; overcutting sufficient to produce a straight, smooth joint may be done up to five calendar days before concrete removal, provided only pavement that can be replaced within the workday is removed. Section 504 specifies that before a saw‑cut is made the asphalt wearing course must be cooled to less than 140°F and the cut must be placed within ±¼ inch of the concrete‑asphalt interface no later than seven days after the joint is placed; the cut is required to produce a reservoir that is 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide, and if wet sawing is used the reservoir should be flushed immediately with water. Section 504 also requires that sealing material temperatures follow the manufacturer’s safe‑temperature limits, be kept as close as possible to the recommended pouring temperature, and not be held at that temperature for more than six hours; a tape bond breaker is placed on the bottom of the reservoir (no more than ⅛ inch up the faces), sealing may only be done when the air temperature is between 40°F and 90°F, and the reservoir faces must be clean, dry and free of oil or moisture before the material is placed. Section 540 requires a green cut saw that cuts each joint to a depth equal to one‑third of the slab thickness and at least 1/8 inch wide after the fresh concrete has hardened enough to permit sawing without excessive raveling; early‑entry saws are recommended, the cutting window may be determined with FHWA HIPERPAV or similar software, and operation may continue day or night regardless of weather. Section 540 further states that if a curing membrane other than white‑pigmented liquid is present it must be removed from the joint area just before cutting (no more than ½ hour) and replaced immediately after, and joints are left unsealed and cleaned of debris after sawing. Section 323 mandates that after a saw‑cut is made in the roller‑compacted concrete control joint, all loose debris shall be cleared and an additional layer of emulsified asphalt shall be placed along the joint as required by Section 323.3(j)5; the emulsified asphalt shall be applied uniformly and void‑free in accordance with Section 460.3(b), curing compounds are prohibited for this operation, and the equipment used to place the emulsified asphalt must be approved by the Representative and must not damage, mar, or alter the compacted RCC surface. Finally, Section 203 requires that during construction the pavement be full‑depth saw cut with a saw blade or cutting wheel to create neat lines as directed; the excavation must be kept graded to drain and sealed with smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment so that the subgrade does not become saturated, the surface should be sealed at the end of each workday to avoid deterioration, and where needed temporary drainage structures or channels may be provided to maintain proper moisture conditions. [2]

[2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (p. 143), SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (p. 219), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–469)

In Sections 516.3(b), 518.3 and 519.3 the contractor shall use a saw equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls when cutting the perimeter of a concrete patch, and the same equipment requirements are reiterated for all other pavement saw‑cutting operations. Full‑depth cuts must actually be full depth; any saw cut that is greater than ½ inch in width or that is not full depth may not be opened to traffic (516.3(b), 519.3). For new multilane concrete pavements the transverse contraction joint shall be cut to a depth of D/3 and a width of 1⁄8 inch, with the centreline of the cut required to fall within ±1 inch of the dowel basket or mechanically implanted dowel centreline (501.3(g)). In bonded‑concrete overlays the transverse joint shall be cut to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch and the width shall be at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack so that dowel load‑transfer devices are not damaged (545.3). The same D/3 depth and 1⁄8‑inch minimum width apply to concrete‑overlay joints formed with a green‑cut saw, which is the only acceptable technique for creating those joints (540.3). Longitudinal cuts along a patch perimeter must be placed either in an existing joint or within the adjacent lane but not more than 1 foot from that joint, and the centreline of each longitudinal cut shall be located within ±¼ inch of the interface between asphalt courses and concrete when a pavement‑relief joint is being cut (504.3(f)). Temporary rigid separators used during cutting may not exceed 1⁄8 inch in thickness, wheel‑saw penetration into the subbase must be limited to ½ inch, and jackhammers employed for strip removal are limited to a maximum weight of 30 pounds; any break back greater than 2 inches or damage to reinforcement requires a new full‑depth transverse cut beyond the affected area (518.3). Saw cuts shall not be located within 24 inches of an existing crack, shall not cross an existing crack, and sawing shall be omitted if a crack is present at or within 1.5 feet of the intended joint location (540.3). For overlay panels the longer dimension may not exceed eighteen times the overlay thickness and the length‑to‑width ratio may not exceed 1.5; intermediate transverse joints must be placed so that no panel exceeds fifteen feet in length, each cut being made to the same full‑thickness‑plus‑½‑inch depth (540.3, 545.3). All control joints indicated on the plans for roller‑compacted concrete shall be saw‑cut to a depth equal to one‑quarter of the compacted pavement thickness and must be performed behind the rolling operation as soon as practicable but no later than eighteen hours after placement using early‑entry equipment set according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, with care taken to avoid raveling or any other damage (502.3(a)). For full‑depth precast or prestressed slabs a diamond saw shall be used to cut through the entire slab depth and the intended longitudinal joint; wheel saws with carbide steel tips are prohibited, and cuts may be made as early as five calendar days before concrete removal provided only concrete that can be replaced during the same workday is removed (529.3). Transverse joints between precast slabs shall not exceed ½ inch in width and shall be at least ¼ inch along their entire length; any joint narrower than ¼ inch must be saw‑cut to achieve the minimum width (529.3). Relief‑joint cuts shall be placed directly above the interface of the asphalt courses and concrete pavement within ±¼ inch, may not be made until the wearing‑course temperature has fallen below 140 °F, must be completed within seven days after the relief joint is placed, and shall form a reservoir that is 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide (504.3(f)). Prior to any cutting for bonded overlays the prepared surface must pass a bond‑strength test at seven days in accordance with ASTM C1583, requiring an average of at least 175 psi and each individual result not less than 160 psi; if these criteria are not met additional testing is required (545.3). A comprehensive quality‑control plan that includes the joint layout, sampling for strength testing and the required pre‑construction testing must be submitted for Department review and approved before any construction activities begin (540.3). For patch repairs the contractor shall submit a QC plan at least two weeks before work commences describing equipment, personnel, methods and testing frequencies, and the plan shall not establish action points at either the upper or lower specification limits (519.3). Traffic may not be allowed on saw‑cut areas until the maximum seventy‑two‑hour post‑cut period has elapsed (516.3(b)). [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–469)

Equipment and personnel

For preparation of saw‑cut control joints, Section 545.3 (bonded concrete overlay on concrete‑surfaced pavements) requires a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier to uniformly remove the old surface across the entire cutting path and shot‑blasting, sand‑blasting or water‑blasting equipment to eliminate loose concrete and rust from exposed reinforcement; a clean compressed‑air stream that is free of oil, moisture and other contaminants and that delivers at least 100 psi measured at the source must also be applied. Section 540.3 (bonded concrete overlay on asphalt‑surfaced pavement) likewise calls for a compressed‑air stream meeting the same minimum pressure of 100 psi and free of oil or moisture, and when air temperature is 45 °F or less insulating blankets shall be placed to prevent rapid heat loss and later removed in accordance with maturity limits; pneumatic hammers may be employed to remove material in locations that cannot be reached by a milling machine before the cuts are made. The actual saw‑cutting operation is defined in Section 540.3, where a green cut saw—preferably an early‑entry model—shall be used to cut each joint to a depth equal to one‑third of the slab thickness (D/3) and to a minimum width of 1⁄8 in; no separate equipment for concrete placement, compaction or consolidation is enumerated in either Section 545.3 or Section 540.3. For finishing after the cuts, Section 323.3 (roller‑compacted concrete base course) requires that any loose debris generated by the saw cutting be removed before the asphalt wearing course is placed, using equipment that does not damage, mar or alter the compacted RCC surface; diamond grinding of the RCC surface is not required. Curing requirements differ: Section 545.3 calls for application of a curing compound at a rate of at least one gallon per 125 ft² and, when forecast air temperature is 45 °F or less, the use of adequate insulating blankets to prevent rapid heat loss; Section 540.3 similarly requires applying a white‑pigmented liquid curing compound at a rate of one gallon per 125 ft² (or as recommended) after sawing; in contrast, Section 323.3 specifies that curing shall be accomplished by applying emulsified asphalt with equipment approved by the Representative and expressly prohibits the use of curing compounds. Testing equipment is prescribed in both Sections 545.3 and 540.3: Section 545.3 mandates provision of test equipment conforming to ASTM C1583 for bond‑strength verification after placement, while Section 540.3 requires that cores be drilled according to PTM No. 606 and measured with an apparatus conforming to PTM No. 614 for depth verification. [2]

[2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (p. 219), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 458–469)

In Section 516—Concrete Pavement Patching, Saw cutting shall be performed by personnel who are trained to operate a saw equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls, as required for the perimeter cuts; the project must also include a QC plan that identifies joint‑sawing actions and is reviewed before work begins, ensuring that adequate equipment and trained personnel are available prior to concrete placement. In Section 519—Full Depth Longitudinal Concrete Joint Repair, the saw‑cutting crew must include a Representative who surface‑marks the repair areas before any cutting begins, and it must contain trained personnel capable of operating a diamond‑tipped blade saw with the same equipment list; the operator is expected to follow the prescribed sequence of making full‑depth longitudinal cuts parallel to existing joints before transverse cuts while ensuring that cooling water, slurry and dust do not enter any lane opened to traffic, and adequate equipment and trained personnel must be available prior to concrete placement to maintain continuity of work. In Section 529—Full Depth Precast/Prestressed Concrete Pavement Slabs, saw cutting is performed by qualified sawcutters who must operate a diamond saw to cut the full‑depth limits of the precast slabs and proposed longitudinal joints and are prohibited from using a wheel saw with carbide steel tips; the layout of these cuts must be surveyed accurately beforehand and may be placed up to 5 calendar days before concrete removal, and sawcutters appear as a distinct crew role in the precast pavement planning meeting alongside the project superintendent, foreman, surveyor, grout installers and the technical representative, who is required on site for at least the first two days of slab installation to oversee the work. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 408–447)

Work sequence

Before any saw cutting can be initiated, each applicable specification requires a set of preparatory actions. For SECTION 516—Concrete Pavement Patching, a Quality Control (QC) Plan must be prepared as specified in Section 106.03(a)2.a and submitted for review, the Representative must surface‑mark all patch areas in advance of the sawing operations, and traffic protection measures from Section 901.3(j) must also be in place prior to cutting. In SECTION 518—Continuously Reinforced Concrete Pavement Patching, the crew must first make initial full‑depth transverse saw‑cuts as shown on the Standard Drawings, starting at the outside edge of the pavement and extending toward the longitudinal cut, and no saw cuts may extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders that are to remain in place. SECTION 519—Full Depth Longitudinal Concrete Joint Repair requires that a QC Plan be submitted for review at least 2 weeks before the start of repairs, the Representative must surface‑mark repair areas in advance of the sawing operations, and if full or partial depth patching is adjacent to longitudinal joint repairs all such patches must be constructed before any longitudinal repairs are carried out. Under SECTION 501(g) Slip‑Form Paving Method, load‑transfer units or dowel bars must already be installed and secured at their designated locations, the exact centrelines of future transverse joints must be accurately marked on the slab, any curing cover other than white membrane must be removed from each joint location before cutting, and the concrete must have hardened enough so that sawing will not cause excessive raveling or random cracking. For SECTION 545—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Concrete‑Surfaced Pavements, the freshly placed concrete overlay must have attained sufficient strength so that cutting will not cause significant raveling or chipping before transverse joints are sawed. In SECTION 502(a) Roller Compacted Concrete Pavements, the RCC pavement must already be placed and compacted by rolling, joint locations must be shown on the Plans, and the cut must begin no later than eighteen hours after placement. SECTION 529—Full Depth Precast/Prestressed Concrete Pavement Slabs requires that a complete surveying and engineering effort be finished, theoretical surface elevations and slab dimensions be determined from the proposed longitudinal joint locations, and an accurate survey layout of those longitudinal joints be provided prior to saw‑cutting. According to SECTION 504—Pavement Relief Joint, the pavement relief joint construction sequence outlined in Section 504.3 must be completed, including excavation for the sleeper slab after the subbase is built, placement of the sleeper slab, polyethylene sheeting as shown on the Standard Drawings, a base course with a thin coating of PG 64S‑22 on all joint faces before fresh asphalt mixtures are laid, and the asphalt wearing course must then be constructed in accordance with Section 413.3 and allowed to cool below 140°F before any saw cuts are made. Finally, SECTION 540—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Asphalt‑Surfaced Pavement mandates that an approved comprehensive QC Plan be in place and accepted, and construction‑related work may not start until that plan is accepted; if joint spacing is not indicated on the plans, a joint layout plan must be developed and accepted by the Department, and the joints must be laid out before the concrete overlay is placed; saw cutting may only begin after the freshly placed concrete has hardened sufficiently to allow sawing without excessive raveling, thereby preventing random cracking. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–469)

The contractor first determines the appropriate sawing window, often using FHWA’s HIPERPAV or a similar approved software, and confirms that the concrete has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling and to prevent random cracking. Saw cutting is then performed with early‑entry saws as recommended; each saw must be equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls, and it is positioned so that cooling water, slurry and dust are kept from traffic. Prior to any cutting the Representative surface‑marks repair areas or joint locations on the pavement using tacks or other approved methods, ensuring that each transverse contraction joint centreline falls within ±1 inch of the dowel‑basket centreline. All required full‑depth longitudinal cuts along the perimeter of the patch are made first; where only one lane is being patched a full‑depth cut follows the existing longitudinal joint for the entire length of the patch, while for multiple lanes a parallel cut no more than 1 foot from the existing joint or a cut in the joint with a temporary rigid separator not greater than 1/8 inch thick may be used. These longitudinal cuts are made parallel to the existing longitudinal joint and are planned so that no saw cuts extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders that are to remain in place. After the longitudinal cuts, full‑depth transverse cuts are placed at the surface‑marked locations; the initial transverse cuts are made from the outside edge of the pavement toward the longitudinal cut, ensuring a full‑depth vertical cut, and a second transverse cut at each end of the patch area is then made with at least a 1‑inch minimum depth, positioned parallel to and outside the initial cut as shown on the Standard Drawings. Saw cuts are not located within 24 inches of an existing crack and must avoid reinforcement bars; if any reinforcement bar is inadvertently cut, the partial‑depth cut is converted to a full‑depth cut and another partial‑depth (minimum 1 inch) saw‑cut is added as specified. When break‑back or spalling occurs a new full‑depth transverse cut is made beyond the damaged area, and extensions into adjacent pavement may go up to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars are not harmed, with cuts never extending into an adjacent concrete curb. For bonded concrete overlays the overlay must first gain sufficient strength; then each transverse joint is saw‑cut directly over the existing joint to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay (including any additional thickness used for grade corrections) plus ½ inch, and the cut is made at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack. Intermediate joints are spaced so that the overlay panel length does not exceed 15 feet, and longitudinal joints are saw‑cut to form a sealant reservoir ¼ inch wide and the full depth of the overlay plus ½ inch. All joints are cut with a green‑cut saw to a depth of D/3 and a minimum width of 1/8 inch; any initial cuts are extended through the slab edge only when this can be done without causing damage, and after each cut the joint is flushed with water, an oversized backer rod (width plus 50 percent) is placed in the top of the sawed joint, and joint movement is monitored throughout curing, replacing the backer rod if the joint opens. For roller‑compacted concrete the contractor begins sawing behind the rollers as soon as possible but no later than eighteen hours after placement, using an early‑entry saw set according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, and cuts control joints to a depth of one‑quarter of the compacted RCC pavement without causing raveling or other damage. Prior to cutting precast or prestressed concrete slabs the site is surveyed and an accurate layout of the proposed longitudinal joints is provided; a diamond saw is then used to cut full‑depth lines at the limits of the precast slabs and the planned longitudinal joints, avoiding wheel saws with carbide steel tips. After the cuts are made the existing concrete or composite pavement is removed by the lift‑out method, installing lifting devices and lifting out the material in one or more pieces without disturbing the subbase; cutting and drilling may be done up to five calendar days before removal, but only concrete that can be replaced during the workday shall be removed. When a pavement relief joint is placed the asphalt wearing course must first cool below 140 °F; within seven days a saw‑cut is made directly above the interface between the asphalt courses of the relief joint and the concrete pavement, keeping the cut within ±¼ inch of the required location, extending through any existing widening and shaping a reservoir that is 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide, and if wet sawing is used the reservoir is immediately flushed with water. Throughout all operations cooling water, slurry and dust are prevented from entering any lane opened to traffic, drainage is maintained as required, and the surface is sealed at the end of each workday to prevent deterioration before work can resume. According to SECTION 516 the saw‑cutting work must be staged so that only one lane is patched at a time when a two‑lane width would block traffic, and the patch area must be surface‑marked before cutting; cooling water, slurry and dust generated by the diamond‑tipped blade are not permitted to enter any lane that is open to traffic, and if rain occurs while the patch remains open an outlet must be excavated through the shoulder at the lowest point of the patch as directed. SECTION 540 allows saw cutting to be performed at any time of day or night irrespective of weather, provided the concrete has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling; the timing of the cut must follow placement when the asphalt surface temperature is less than 120 °F, and insulation blankets are required if ambient air temperature is 45 °F or lower to prevent rapid heat loss before sawing. Section 203 requires that during saw cutting the operation be staged so that the excavation remains graded to drain, and the cut surface be sealed with smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment to keep the subgrade from becoming saturated; this prevents water infiltration that could compromise the joint. The surface should also be sealed at the end of each workday to avoid deterioration caused by weather or lingering moisture, and temporary drainage structures or channels may be required to control runoff. Quality control and acceptance

For concrete pavement patching (Section 516) the inspector must verify that a diamond‑tipped blade saw equipped with a blade guard, alignment guides, water cooling system and cut‑depth controls is used; that all required full‑depth longitudinal cuts are made before any transverse cuts; that no cooling water, slurry or dust enters any lane to be opened to traffic; that each cut does not exceed 1/2 inch in width and is truly full depth; that cuts do not extend into adjacent concrete curbs or damage dowel bars; and that transverse extensions are limited to full depth plus 2 inches when dowels remain intact. For continuously reinforced concrete pavement patching (Section 518) the inspection must verify that no cuts extend into pavement or shoulders that remain in place, that the initial transverse cuts are full‑depth vertical cuts, that each secondary cut is at least 1 inch deep, that cuts are not placed within 24 inches of an existing crack and do not intersect reinforcement bars, that if a bar is cut the cut must be upgraded to a full‑depth cut with an additional minimum‑depth cut as prescribed, that the wheel saw used for the full‑depth cuts may penetrate the subbase no more than ½ inch and must be discontinued if results are unsatisfactory, that jackhammers employed for strip removal do not exceed 30 pounds of weight, that any break back of the underside greater than 2 inches or damage to reinforcement bars requires a new full‑depth transverse cut beyond the affected area, and that subbase disturbance deeper than 1 inch must be removed and replaced with concrete. For full‑depth longitudinal joint repair (Section 519) the inspector verifies that the saw is equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, blade guard, alignment guides, water cooling system and cut‑depth controls; that cooling water, slurry and dust are prevented from entering any lane opened to traffic; that cuts opened to traffic do not exceed 1/2 inch in width; that full‑depth longitudinal cuts are made before any transverse cuts; and that the saw cuts are parallel to the existing longitudinal joint with no break back or spalling observed. For slip‑form paving (Section 501) the contractor must verify that the cut is positioned within ±1 inch of the dowel‑basket or mechanically implanted dowel centerline, that the saw blade produces a depth equal to one‑third of slab thickness and a width of 1⁄8 inch, that the equipment includes guides, a blade guard, water cooling and cut‑depth control, that each initial cut either penetrates the slab edge or is later extended through the edge without causing damage, and that after cutting the joint is immediately flushed to remove all slurry and an oversized backer rod (width plus 50 percent) is placed on top of the sawed joint. For bonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 545) the inspection must verify that each transverse, intermediate and longitudinal cut reaches a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay—including any extra grade‑correction concrete—plus 1/2 inch while ensuring no damage to existing dowel load transfer devices; that the cut width is at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack in the existing pavement; that joint widths are measured immediately before placement of the overlay so cuts can be accurately located; and that after each cut the joint is flushed with water to remove sawing slurry before sealant installation, confirming that the prepared joint faces meet the specified dimensions and cleanliness criteria. For roller‑compacted concrete pavement construction (Section 502) the inspector must verify that each control joint is cut with an approved saw to a depth equal to one‑quarter of the compacted pavement thickness, that the cut is made no later than eighteen hours after placement, and that the sawing does not cause raveling or other damage to the face of the joint; for cold vertical joints the inspector must also confirm that the cut extends through the full depth of sound compacted material, starts at least six inches from the exposed edge, and that any excess RCC is removed and the joint cleaned and moistened before placement of the adjacent lane. For full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs (Section 529) the inspection shall confirm that a diamond saw is used to cut full depth through the slab limits and proposed longitudinal joints, that wheel saws with carbide steel tips are not employed, that the layout, depth and width of each cut match the pre‑placement plan, that any transverse joint opening less than 1/4 inch is re‑sawcut to achieve a minimum of 1/4 inch, and that longitudinal joints are over‑cut sufficiently to provide a straight, smooth joint. For pavement relief joints (Section 504) the inspection must verify that each cut is placed directly above the asphalt‑asphalt interface within ± 1/4 inch, that the asphalt wearing course temperature has dropped below 140 °F before cutting, that the work is completed no later than seven days after the relief joint placement, that the cut is extended through any existing widening and produces a reservoir exactly 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide, and if wet sawing is used the reservoir shall be immediately flushed with water. For bonded concrete overlay of asphalt‑surfaced pavement (Section 540) the inspector must confirm that each saw cut is made with a green‑cut saw to a depth equal to one‑third of the slab thickness and that the kerf width is at least 1/8 inch; that cuts are extended through the slab edge only when doing so will not cause edge damage; that any cut located within 1.5 feet of an existing crack must be omitted and addressed as required by Section 590; that when curing covers are removed to permit cutting the removal time may not exceed ½ hour; and that after sawing the joint faces must be cleared of all deleterious material before sealing. Curing, protection, and handoff

When saw cuts are made for a dowel‑bar retrofit, the parallel slots are prepared and cleaned, epoxy‑coated dowels are placed, and the voids are filled with concrete patching material which is then cured to develop strength. For other joint work that follows saw cutting, any joints or cracks that will be patched but not overlaid are sealed with a rubberized joint sealing material as specified in Publication 408, providing protection against water and chloride ingress. After full‑depth perimeter and transverse saw cuts on a concrete patch, traffic may be allowed for no more than 72 hours provided the cuts are full depth and not wider than ½ inch; any cut that exceeds that width or is less than full depth must remain closed to traffic. The concrete patch is then cured in accordance with Section 501.3(l) using either a white poly‑alpha‑methylstyrene liquid membrane forming curing compound or the permitted Type 2 white pigmented liquid membrane forming curing compound, and rapid‑set repairs are cured according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. Following saw cutting for a full‑depth longitudinal concrete joint repair, the repair area may remain open to traffic for no more than 72 hours, and any excavated portion must be backfilled or patched within 48 hours and not left unpatched over weekends or holidays; during this interval traffic is required to be protected from drop‑off conditions as referenced in Section 901.3(j). When a pavement cured by means other than white membrane curing compound is sawed, the curing cover may be removed only where the joint will be cut and removal may not exceed one‑half hour; the initial cut is then flushed thoroughly to eliminate slurry, an oversized backer rod (width plus 50 %) is placed in the top of the joint, and the joint is monitored during the remaining cure period with the curing compound reapplied if a white membrane was originally used. The joint must remain protected and continue to cure until it is sealed. After saw cutting an overlay on concrete‑surfaced pavement, curing material is applied within 30 minutes of bleed‑water dissipation and kept on the surface until the pavement attains the strength required for traffic opening under Section 545.3(l); if ambient temperature is forecasted at 45°F or less, insulating blankets are placed to prevent rapid heat loss and are removed only when temperature exceeds 45°F or compressive strength passes 3 600 psi, with removal done so that surface temperature does not change more than 40°F in any one‑hour period. A curing compound is spread uniformly at a minimum rate of 1 gallon per 125 sq ft (or the manufacturer’s recommendation, whichever is greater), and a second equal application may be made immediately after the sawing operation; joint faces must be protected from the compound or cleaned before sealant installation. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements, control joints are saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth no later than 18 hours after placement; once the cuts are made, the surface is kept moist and protected by applying at least one of the prescribed curing methods for a minimum of 72 hours until the pavement reaches a compressive strength of at least 3 000 psi. Acceptable cures include water cure with a misting spray truck or similar device that maintains uniform moisture without washing out the surface, placement of white polyethylene sheeting as detailed elsewhere, or immediate application of a curing compound that yields a solid opaque film. After saw cutting the RCC control joints and before placing the asphalt wearing course, loose debris must be removed and additional emulsified asphalt is applied along the joints; curing compounds are prohibited, so protection relies on a uniform void‑free coating of emulsified asphalt applied over the entire RCC surface using equipment approved by the Representative that will not damage or mar the pavement. The application follows Section 460.3(b) requirements and must be continuous without voids, and once this cure period is complete the pavement may be opened to local traffic prior to the wearing course. During construction of Class 1, 1A, and 1C excavations, full‑depth saw cuts are made, the excavation is kept graded to drain and sealed using smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment so that the subgrade does not become saturated; at the end of each workday the surface is sealed to protect it from deterioration until work can resume. Expected performance and maintenance

Break back or spalling may develop in the existing slab or an adjacent lane, and when that occurs a new full‑depth transverse saw‑cut beyond the affected area is required; damage in an adjacent lane must be repaired with at least a Type A concrete patch at no additional cost (Section 516). Full‑depth cuts must not extend into an adjacent concrete curb (Section 516) and any cut greater than 1/2 inch in width or less than full depth may not be opened to traffic (Sections 516 and 519). Cuts made across an existing crack or within 24 inches of one are prohibited because they may propagate the crack (Section 518). If reinforcement bars are cut, corrective action requires converting the partial‑depth cut to a full‑depth cut and adding another partial‑depth cut of at least 1 inch (Section 518). Break back greater than 2 inches or damage to existing reinforcement bars also mandates a new full‑depth transverse saw‑cut beyond the area of break back (Section 518). Full‑depth cuts may be allowed to extend transversely into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars in the adjacent lane are not damaged; damage must be repaired if it occurs (Section 519). Sawing before the concrete has hardened enough can cause excessive raveling, chipping, or random cracking, so joints should be cut only after sufficient strength is achieved (Sections 501, 540, 545). Joints that are not cut to the required depth of D/3 and width of 1/8 inch may be ineffective, leading to weakened joints and premature panel cracking (Sections 501 and 540). Initial cuts that stop short of the slab edge can cause blowouts or edge damage of green concrete; such cuts must be promptly extended through the edge without causing further harm (Sections 501, 540). For roller‑compacted concrete, control joints shall be sawed to 1/4 depth within 18 hours after placement and must avoid raveling or other damage; random cracking that occurs before sawing must be repaired as directed (Section 502). In bonded concrete overlays, the cut depth must equal the full thickness of the overlay plus 1/2 inch while avoiding damage to dowel load‑transfer devices; insufficient depth, width, or damage to dowels can compromise joint performance (Section 545). The saw‑cut width should be at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack to prevent debonding; failure to meet this tolerance may result in defective work such as inadequate bond strength or surface defects like honeycombing (Section 545). For pavement relief joints, cuts that do not fall within ± 1/4 inch of the interface between asphalt courses and concrete are declared defective; cutting before the asphalt wearing course cools below 140°F or later than 7 days after joint placement can also cause non‑compliance (Section 504). Sawing should be omitted when a crack occurs at or within 1.5 feet of a joint location, and such conditions must be addressed separately (Section 540). [2]

The Pavement Policy Manual advises that preventive maintenance of saw‑cut joints should begin with resealing as soon as the joints first exhibit distress, before overall serviceability declines, and monitoring must concentrate on early signs of joint distress so rehabilitation can be applied while the pavement remains restorable. [1] When rehabilitation becomes necessary, the manual specifies a type 1 joint rehabilitation for existing saw‑cut joints that involves constructing new sealant reservoirs sized 0.75 in by 0.75 in and resealing the joint in accordance with Publication 408 specifications; if spalling or faulting is present, partial‑depth or full‑depth longitudinal joint repairs, slab stabilization, dowel‑bar retrofits using a power‑driven self‑propelled saw to make two parallel cuts per slot (cut parallel to the roadway centerline), or full‑depth patching may be employed. [1] Section 516 of the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation Specification 408 requires that preventive maintenance for saw cutting be documented in a quality‑control plan and that the cutting be performed with a diamond‑tipped blade equipped with water cooling, alignment guides and cut‑depth controls; full‑depth longitudinal cuts must be made along the patch perimeter before any transverse cuts, and cooling water, slurry and dust must be kept out of traffic lanes. [2] Monitoring in Section 516 limits saw‑cut width to not exceed 1/2 inch and mandates that cuts be full depth before they are opened to traffic; if break back or spalling is observed a new full‑depth transverse cut must be placed beyond the damaged zone, and any damage to an adjacent lane must be repaired with at least a full‑depth Type A concrete patch at no additional cost. [2] Traffic may be allowed on patched areas for a maximum of 72 hours after cutting, but cuts that are wider than 1/2 inch or not full depth may not be opened to traffic. [2] In Section 518 the preventive maintenance requirement states that saw cuts must never extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders and must be spaced at least 24 inches from any existing crack. [2] Monitoring under Section 518 calls for verification that reinforcement bars are not severed during cutting and that wheel‑saw penetration into the subbase does not exceed a maximum of 1/2 inch; if reinforcement bars are cut, corrective action is to make the partial‑depth cut a full‑depth cut and add another partial‑depth cut of at least 1 inch. [2] Section 519 adds that a QC plan describing the diamond‑tipped blade, blade guard, alignment guides, water cooling system and cut‑depth controls must be submitted at least 2 weeks before repairs begin, and monitoring permits traffic on repaired areas for a maximum of 72 hours only if saw cuts are not greater than 1/2 inch in width and excavated repair areas are not left un‑patched for more than 48 hours or over weekends or holidays; any break back or spalling again triggers a new full‑depth transverse cut beyond the damaged zone, and all saw‑cuts extending beyond patch limits must be sealed after placement. [2] Section 501 prescribes preventive maintenance that sawing of joints should occur as soon as concrete has attained sufficient hardness to avoid excessive raveling and to prevent random cracking, with a cut depth of D/3 and a width of 1/8 inch; if initial cuts do not extend through the slab edge they are to be extended later when it can be done without causing damage. [2] After cutting, the joint is flushed with pressurized water and an oversized backer rod (width plus 50 %) placed in the top of the sawed joint; monitoring the movement of the joint throughout the cure period requires removal and replacement of the backer rod if the joint opens. [2] Sawing must be completed within 1/2 hour after curing covers are removed, and second‑stage cuts for sealant reservoirs are not made until at least 72 hours after concrete placement; any crack or spall occurring before final inspection is repaired according to approved repair procedures at no additional cost. [2] Section 545 requires waiting until the concrete is strong enough for cutting without significant raveling or chipping, measuring joint widths immediately prior to overlay placement, and making transverse cuts that are full‑thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch while avoiding damage to dowel load‑transfer devices; cut width must be at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack. [2] Monitoring in Section 545 includes flushing each cut with water immediately after sawing to remove slurry that could hinder sealant installation, and failure to meet depth, width, or damage criteria triggers repair actions such as removal and replacement of the defective overlay per the defective‑work provisions. [2] Section 502 mandates preventive maintenance for control joints by cutting to a depth of 1/4 of the compacted RCC pavement behind the rolling operation as soon as possible, using early‑entry saws set to the manufacturer’s recommendations and beginning no later than 18 hours after placement; monitoring watches for random cracks that appear before the cut, and any such cracks must be repaired as directed by the Representative at no additional cost. [2] Finished joints may also be sealed in accordance with Section 501.3(n). [2] Section 540 calls for preventive maintenance that sawing of joints be performed as soon as concrete has sufficiently hardened to avoid excessive raveling and prevent random cracking, using a green‑cut saw set to a depth of D/3 and a minimum width of 1/8 inch; monitoring omits cutting where a crack occurs at or within 1.5 feet of the joint location and requires that cuts be extended through the slab edge as soon as it can be done without causing damage, after which all deleterious material is cleaned from the joint. [2] Section 323 specifies that after RCC control joints are cut, loose debris must be cleared before the next surface layer and an additional coat of emulsified asphalt applied uniformly and void‑free using equipment approved by the Representative that will not damage or mar the compacted RCC surface; no curing compounds are permitted for this stage.
