Saw Cutting

Part of: Contraction Joint is-subtopic-of
Saw Cutting is a topic under Contraction Joint.
saw-cutssawed
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-29

Saw cutting is a construction method that employs a diamond‑tipped blade saw—fitted with a blade guard, alignment guides, water‑cooling and cut‑depth controls—to produce precise slots or joints in concrete pavements [2]. These cuts are used to form dowel‑bar retrofit slots, full‑depth transverse trenches for patch removal, and contraction or control joints that provide a predetermined weakened plane to improve load transfer across heavily loaded joints or cracks and to direct crack development [1][2]. The required depth varies with pavement type—typically one‑third of slab thickness for plain concrete, one‑quarter of compacted RCC, or the full overlay thickness plus ½ inch—and cutting is initiated only after the concrete has hardened sufficiently to prevent excessive raveling [2].

Purpose and applicability

Saw cutting is employed to create precise slots that enable dowel‑bar retrofits, thereby addressing insufficient load transfer across transverse joints or cracks in heavily loaded concrete pavements where such joints would benefit from improved load transfer. [1] In patching applications, saw cutting creates full‑depth transverse cuts that form a trench across the slab, relieving compression in the pavement and allowing the interior portion of a patch to be removed without disturbing adjacent pavement or reinforcement bars. [2] By forming the required transverse and longitudinal contraction joints at the appropriate stage of concrete hardening, saw cutting ensures that the pavement develops predictable, controlled cracks instead of random, uncontrolled cracking. [2] For roller‑compacted concrete pavements, saw cutting provides control joints by cutting to one‑quarter depth of the compacted thickness with an approved saw after rolling, and the sawing must begin as soon as possible but no later than eighteen hours after placement, thereby supplying a predetermined weakened plane for crack development. [2] In bonded concrete overlays, saw cutting is performed only after the fresh concrete has reached sufficient strength to avoid raveling or chipping; the cut must extend through the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch and be at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack, which prevents debonding of the new slab from the existing pavement. [2] When installing full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete slabs, saw cutting with a diamond saw creates full‑depth cuts along planned slab limits and longitudinal joint locations, overcutting the existing longitudinal joint to a sufficient depth so that the old concrete can be lifted out in whole pieces without disturbing the subbase. [2] For concrete overlays on asphalt‑surfaced pavement, saw cutting is the only acceptable method for creating joints; by sawing after the slab has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling to a depth of one‑third the slab thickness and at least 1/8 inch wide, it releases shrinkage or temperature stresses and prevents random cracking. [2]

Saw cutting is appropriate when full‑depth longitudinal and transverse cuts are required along the perimeter of a concrete pavement patch; the work shall be performed with a diamond‑tipped blade saw equipped with a blade guard, alignment guides, water cooling and cut‑depth controls, and the resulting cuts may not exceed 1/2 inch in width or be less than full depth if they will be opened to traffic. An initial shallow cut before the full‑depth transverse cuts may be made with a wheel saw having carbide steel tips, provided its penetration is limited to minimize disturbance of the subbase and it is discontinued if the results are unsatisfactory. For continuously reinforced concrete pavement patching, saw cutting is appropriate for creating the initial full‑depth transverse cuts from the pavement edge toward the longitudinal cut and for the secondary minimum‑depth transverse cuts at each end of a patch, which shall be at least 1 inch deep; methods must assure full‑depth vertical cuts, and a wheel saw with carbide steel tips may be used to provide a trench before lifting out the failed area, provided its penetration into the subbase does not exceed 1/2 inch. Saw cutting shall not be used where the cut would extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders that remain in place, across an existing crack, within 24 inches of an existing crack, or when it would cut reinforcement bars during partial‑depth cuts; if a wheel saw yields unsatisfactory results the Representative shall discontinue its use and another removal method should be employed. In slip‑form paving, saw cutting is appropriate for transverse and longitudinal contraction joints once the concrete has hardened enough to allow sawing without excessive raveling and before random cracking occurs; cuts shall be made to a depth of D/3 and a width of 1/8 inch, centered over dowel or load‑transfer units as required. During the early season (October 1 to April 1) an early‑entry saw or another approved method may be used instead of conventional sawing, and when the pavement is being formed with side forms, or when conventional sawing cannot be performed, the joint should be created by loosening or removing the side forms or by other acceptable methods. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements, saw cutting is the prescribed method for control joints; the pavement shall be cut to one‑quarter depth of the compacted RCC and sawing must begin no later than eighteen hours after placement, performed behind the rolling operation. For cold vertical joints that are not fresh, a full‑depth cut using an approved saw or wheel cutter at least six inches from the exposed edge is required, although where a drop extension or edging shoe is used on the paver and the joint is placed up to fifteen degrees from vertical, the cutting requirement may be waived; in such cases an edge shoe may be employed as an alternative to saw cutting. When full‑depth cuts are needed to define the limits of precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs and their longitudinal joints before slab placement, saw cutting is appropriate only with a diamond saw; the cuts may be made up to five calendar days before the concrete is removed, and only concrete that can be replaced during the workday should be removed. Wheel saws with carbide steel tips are not permitted, so any method other than a diamond‑saw full‑depth cut shall not be used under this specification. In bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavement, saw cutting should be employed when control joints are required because the specification states that sawing is the only acceptable technique for creating those joints; it must be done once the concrete has hardened enough to allow cutting without excessive raveling. For removal of asphalt surface material, saw cutting (or chipping with pneumatic hammers) may be used only where a profiling mill cannot reach, otherwise profiling milling shall be the primary method. During construction, saw cutting is appropriate when a full‑depth cut of existing pavement is required; a saw blade or cutting wheel shall be used to create neat lines as indicated or directed, and the excavation should then be graded to drain, sealed with smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment, and the surface sealed at the end of the workday. When the material to be removed is rock that cannot be taken out by other excavating methods, drilling and blasting shall be used instead, completing the excavation to the indicated lines while causing the least disturbance to adjacent material. Required inputs

Saw cutting shall be initiated only after the concrete has hardened sufficiently to avoid excessive raveling (Section 501.3(g); Section 504.3). For asphalt wearing courses, the hot‑mix must have cooled below 140°F before any cut is made (Section 515.3; Section 504.3) and all cuts must be completed within seven days after placement of the overlay or relief joint (Section 515.3; Section 504.3).

For plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements, the joint shall be cut to a depth equal to one‑third of slab thickness with a width of 1/8 inch (Section 501.3(g)). For roller‑compacted concrete pavements, the saw cut shall be made to a depth equal to one‑quarter of the compacted slab thickness; cutting must begin no later than 18 hours after placement and the equipment used shall not tear or ravel the edge (Section 502.3(a)). For continuously reinforced concrete pavement patches, full‑depth vertical cuts are required using a wheel saw equipped with carbide‑steel tips; penetration of the wheel into the subbase may not exceed ½ inch and any cut that exceeds this shall be discontinued (Section 518.3). In addition, no saw‑cut may be located within 24 inches of an existing crack or cut across an existing crack (Section 518.3).

For bonded concrete overlays, the concrete temperature at placement must be between 65°F and 90°F and the surface temperature prior to placement must be less than 120°F; the pavement must have gained sufficient strength to permit cutting without significant raveling or chipping (Section 545.3(h)). The cut depth for a bonded overlay shall equal the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch, and the width shall be at least as wide as the underlying joint while avoiding damage to dowel load‑transfer devices (Section 545.3). For asphalt overlays whose total depth is three‑and‑one‑half inches or greater, an initial cut one‑eighth inch wide shall be made to a depth of at least one‑and‑½ inches or one‑third of the overlay thickness, whichever is greater; all cuts are placed directly over existing transverse joints with a positional tolerance of ±1 inch (Section 515.3).

For precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs, saw cutting shall be performed with a diamond‑tipped blade that cuts full depth of the slab limits and proposed longitudinal joints; wheel saws with carbide‑steel tips are not permitted (Section 529.3). A green‑cut saw, preferably an early‑entry model, may also be used for concrete overlays (Section 504.3).

Saw‑cutting equipment shall be equipped with a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls; cooling water, slurry and dust shall be prevented from entering any lane opened to traffic (Sections 516.3, 519.3). For pavement relief joints the cut shall produce a reservoir 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide; if wet sawing is employed the reservoir shall be immediately flushed with water (Section 504.3).

Curing covers other than white membrane must be removed from the joint location prior to sawing and removal may not exceed ½ hour before cutting (Section 501.3(g)). After each cut, joint faces shall be flushed with water and protected from curing compound; sealing of joints is permitted only when ambient air temperature is between 40°F and 90°F unless otherwise approved (Section 515.3). Cuts opened to traffic may not exceed ½ inch in width and traffic may be allowed on repaired or patched areas for no more than 72 hours after cutting (Sections 516.3, 519.3). For roller‑compacted concrete control joints, the pavement may be opened to local traffic only after the prescribed cure period has been completed (Section 504.3). [2]

For concrete pavement patching (Section 516), a Quality‑Control Plan prepared in accordance with Section 106.03(a)2.a must be submitted for review before work to describe the action points for joint sawing; the saw shall be equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls, and all cuts are required to be full depth. Any cut opened to traffic may not exceed 1/2 inch in width; longitudinal cuts shall be made parallel and not more than 1 foot from the existing longitudinal joint, and a temporary rigid separator used between lanes may not be greater than 1/8 inch thick. For corner repairs only, the saw must cut at least 2 inches in depth and extend a minimum of 2 inches beyond the damaged area, while transverse cuts at patch limits may extend into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars are not damaged. For continuously reinforced concrete pavement patching (Section 518), the procedure requires an initial full‑depth transverse cut from the outside edge toward the longitudinal cut as shown on the standard drawings, followed by a second transverse cut of at least 1 inch minimum depth placed parallel to and outside the first cut at the distance indicated for the rebar splice; cuts may not extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders, may not be within 24 inches of an existing crack, and partial‑depth cuts must avoid cutting reinforcement bars. If a bar is cut, the affected cut must be made full depth and another partial‑depth (minimum 1 inch) cut placed as specified. When using a wheel saw for the full‑depth cuts, penetration into the subbase may not exceed 0.5 inch, jackhammers used to remove concrete strips are limited to a maximum weight of 30 pounds, and any break back greater than 2 inches requires a new full‑depth transverse saw‑cut beyond the broken area. For full‑depth longitudinal concrete joint repair (Section 519), a QC Plan must be submitted at least two weeks before work describing equipment, personnel, testing frequencies and corrective action points; saw cutting shall be performed with a diamond‑tipped blade equipped with a blade guard, alignment guides, water cooling and cut‑depth controls, making all required full‑depth longitudinal cuts along the repair perimeter before any transverse cuts. Saw cuts greater than 1/2 inch in width may not be opened to traffic, and after cutting traffic on the repaired area is permitted for no more than 72 hours; cooling water, slurry and dust must not enter lanes open to traffic. Full‑depth saw cuts at repair limits may extend transversely into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars are not damaged. For slip‑form paving of plain or reinforced cement concrete (Section 501), transverse and longitudinal contraction joints must be accurately marked with tacks or other approved methods so that the cut centre lies within ±1 inch of the dowel‑basket or implanted‑dow​el centreline; the cuts shall be made using equipment equipped with guides, a blade guard, water‑cooling and depth‑control, and each joint is required to be cut to a depth equal to one‑third of slab thickness (D/3) and a width of 1/8 inch. Sawing must occur as soon as the concrete has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling, any initial cuts that do not reach the slab edge shall be extended through the edge when it can be done without damage, and after each cut the joint is flushed to remove slurry. For asphalt overlays (Section 515), a QC plan outlining the control of locating and referencing transverse joints must be submitted at least 21 days before operations; before or on the first day of joint referencing the contractor must demonstrate to the Representative that existing transverse joints can be relocated within a ±1 inch tolerance, stopping work and amending the plan if this tolerance is not achieved. All saw‑cuts shall be made directly above the existing transverse joints within ±1 inch after the wearing course has cooled below 140 °F, and cutting and sealing must be completed within seven days of placing the wearing course; cuts are to be extended through any widening and separate cuts provided joint offsets exceed 1 inch. If the total overlay depth is 3½ inches or greater, an initial saw‑cut 1/8 inch wide to a depth of 1½ inches or one‑third of the total overlay thickness, whichever is greater, shall be made; if the wearing course will not be placed in the same season, a 1/8‑inch‑wide cut to at least 1 inch deep or one‑third of that layer’s thickness, whichever is greater, shall be provided. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502), control joints shown on the plans shall be saw‑cut to a depth equal to one‑quarter of the compacted RCC pavement using an approved early‑entry saw set according to the manufacturer’s recommendations; the cuts must be made behind the rolling operation as soon as possible but no later than eighteen hours after placement while avoiding raveling or other damage. For bonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 545), transverse joints are saw‑cut only after the concrete is strong enough to cut without significant raveling or chipping; each cut must be to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay (including any additional thickness used for grade corrections) plus ½ inch and must not damage dowel load transfer devices. Intermediate transverse joints are required whenever an overlay panel would exceed 15 feet, using the same depth rule. Longitudinal joints are saw‑cut to create a sealant reservoir that is 1/4 inch wide and full overlay depth plus ½ inch. Before placing the overlay, bond strength of the prepared surface shall be tested at seven days in accordance with ASTM C1583; the area is considered defective if the average of three test results is below 175 psi or any individual test is below 160 psi. For full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs (Section 529), the design mandates using a diamond saw to cut through the full depth of the slab limits and the proposed longitudinal joint locations, expressly forbids wheel saws with carbide steel tips, allows the cuts (and lift‑out holes) to be made up to five calendar days before concrete removal, and requires that the layout of these cuts be coordinated during the pre‑placement meeting as part of the installation plan. For pavement relief joints (Section 504), the transverse saw‑cut must be placed directly above the interface between the asphalt relief joint and the concrete pavement and its location shall be within ± 1/4 inch of that interface; the cut may not be made until the wearing course has cooled below 140°F, and it must be completed within 7 days after the relief joint is placed. The saw‑cut is required to extend through any existing widening and to create a reservoir that is 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide, with immediate flushing when wet sawing is employed. For bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavement (Section 540, as modified), joints are laid out according to the project plans or an approved joint‑layout plan; panel dimensions are limited to no more than eighteen times the overlay thickness and the length‑to‑width ratio shall not exceed 1.5, and longitudinal joints must be avoided in wheel paths. Saw cutting is the sole acceptable method for forming these overlay joints, and cuts shall be made with a green‑cut saw to a depth equal to one‑third of the slab thickness (D/3) and a minimum width of 1⁄8 inch once the concrete has hardened sufficiently to prevent excessive raveling. Early‑entry saws are recommended, and the sawing window may be determined using FHWA’s HIPERPAV or similar software. If a crack is found at or within 1.5 feet of a planned joint location before or during sawing, cutting shall be omitted and addressed as specified in Section 590. All cuts must be cleaned of debris and shall not be sealed at this stage. [2]

Equipment and personnel

According to SECTION 518—CONTINUOUSLY REINFORCED CONCRETE PAVEMENT PATCHING, preparation for saw‑cut control joints requires a wheel saw equipped with carbide steel tips; the concrete strips at each end of the patch are removed with jackhammers limited to 30 lb maximum together with hand tools, and lifting devices are installed to raise the cut sections. If the subbase is disturbed more than one inch, small vibratory compactors are used to re‑compact the surface, and before new concrete is placed two layers of polyethylene sheeting having a minimum thickness of 4.0 mil are laid as a bond breaker. Under SECTION 545—BONDED CONCRETE OVERLAY OF CONCRETE‑SURFACED PAVEMENTS, preparation calls for a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier to uniformly remove the existing surface, followed by shot‑blasting, sand‑blasting or water‑blasting equipment to clean loose concrete and rust; a compressed‑air stream of at least 100 psi is then applied to blow off standing water just before placement. Curing is achieved by applying a curing compound at a minimum rate of 1 gallon (±0.1 gallon) per 125 ft², and when forecast air temperatures are 45°F or less adequate insulating blankets must be provided until the concrete warms above that threshold. Bond‑strength testing after placement is performed in accordance with ASTM C1583 using the test equipment specified by the contract; the specification does not prescribe separate equipment for the actual placement, compaction, consolidation, or final finishing of the overlay. Within SECTION 540—BONDED CONCRETE OVERLAY OF ASPHALT‑SURFACED PAVEMENT, preparation includes profile milling the existing asphalt to the depth indicated in the plans and using pneumatic hammers to remove any inaccessible areas; a clean compressed‑air stream free of oil or moisture at a minimum pressure of 100 psi is then used to blow debris from the milled face. Saw cutting itself is performed with early‑entry green cut saws set to cut to one‑third the slab thickness and to produce a joint at least 1/8 inch wide, and the timing of cuts may be planned using FHWA’s HIPERPAV or similar software. When ambient conditions require temperature control, fog‑misting equipment or protective covers together with insulating blankets are employed to keep the surface below 120°F without standing water, and insulating blankets are retained during curing until the concrete reaches the required strength. Curing materials are applied after finishing and texturing, any curing film over a joint location is removed just before sawing and replaced immediately afterward. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 405–457)

In accordance with Section 516.3, Section 519.3 and Section 529.3, saw cutting must be performed with a diamond‑tipped blade saw that is equipped with a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls; the operator must be trained to use this equipment safely and to keep cooling water, slurry and dust from entering open traffic lanes. The crew must include qualified sawcutters who are trained to operate the diamond‑tipped blade saw, as well as personnel required to protect traffic, and a Representative who surface‑marks repair areas before cuts are made. For precast slab installations, qualified sawcutters attend the precast pavement planning and pre‑placement meetings together with the project superintendent, foreman, surveyor, grout installers and the technical representative, who must be on site for at least the first two days of slab installation to oversee the cutting. Wheel saws with carbide steel tips are prohibited, and cuts may be placed as early as five calendar days before concrete removal, provided that only concrete that can be replaced during the workday is removed. The specification also calls for ensuring adequate equipment and trained personnel are available prior to concrete placement. [2]

Work sequence

Before any saw cutting is performed under Section 516, the contractor must prepare a QC Plan as specified in Section 106.03(a)2.a and submit it for review, the Representative must surface‑mark the patch areas in advance of the sawing operations, and traffic protection measures such as drop‑off safeguards per Section 901.3(j) also must be in place. Before any partial‑depth (minimum one‑inch) transverse saw‑cut can be placed under Section 518, the concrete slab located between the two full‑depth transverse cuts must first be removed; this removal is done after the initial full‑depth cuts and prior to the one‑inch minimum depth cuts, using a wheel saw for the full‑depth trench and jackhammers or hand tools for the end strips while protecting existing reinforcement and subbase. Before any saw cutting can be performed under Section 519, a QC Plan must be submitted for review at least two weeks before repairs begin, all full‑ or partial‑depth patches that are adjacent to longitudinal joint work must be constructed prior to the longitudinal repair phase, and the Representative must surface‑mark the repair areas in advance of the sawing operations. Before any saw cutting can be performed under Section 501, the locations for all transverse contraction joints must already be identified – dowel‑bar positions are to be staked in advance of paving and the centreline of each joint must be accurately marked with tacks or other approved methods so that the cuts will fall directly over the dowels; additionally, the required saw‑cutting equipment and spare parts must be on site before concrete placement. Before any saw cutting can be performed under Section 515, the existing transverse joints must be located and referenced at each end prior to placing any asphalt courses, a QC plan that describes the personnel, equipment and methods for this work must be prepared and submitted to the Representative at least 21 days before referencing begins, joint referencing may not start until the Representative has reviewed the QC plan; on or before the first day of joint referencing and before any asphalt is placed, the contractor must demonstrate to the Representative that they can accurately relocate the joints using the reference data within a ±1‑inch tolerance, and saw cuts may only be made after the asphalt course has cooled below 140 °F. Before any saw‑cut control joints are made under Section 502, the RCC pavement must first be rolled – the rolling operation must be completed and the pavement placed behind that rolling before cutting can start, with sawing begun no later than eighteen hours after placement. Before any saw‑cutting operation under Section 545.3(h), the newly placed concrete must have cured to a condition where it is strong enough for cutting the joints without significant raveling or chipping, and the surface temperature must be less than 120 °F immediately prior to placement of the overlay; in addition, joint backing or other approved joint‑filling material must already be installed within ±1/4 inch of the pavement surface in all existing transverse joints before the concrete is placed. Before any full‑depth diamond saw cuts are made under Section 529, a complete surveying and engineering effort must be finished: the theoretical surface elevations and slab dimensions have to be determined from the proposed longitudinal joint locations, and an accurate survey layout of those longitudinal joints must be provided prior to sawcutting; this work is required before slabs are fabricated or existing pavement is removed. For the pavement relief joint work described in Section 504, all preparatory steps listed in 504.3(a) through (e) must be finished before any saw‑cutting can be performed; these steps include excavating the subbase for the sleeper slab, constructing the sleeper slab as required by Section 501.3, installing polyethylene sheeting per the standard drawings, painting the joint faces with a thin coat of PG 64S‑22, placing the asphalt base course in accordance with Section 313.3, and constructing the asphalt wearing course as specified in Section 413.3, and the wearing course must have cooled to a temperature below 140 °F before saw cutting may commence. For bonded concrete overlay of an asphalt‑surfaced pavement covered by Section 540, the project’s comprehensive QC Plan must be submitted for the Representative’s review and accepted because construction‑related work may not begin until that approval is received; if the plans do not indicate joint spacing, a joint layout plan must be developed and approved by the Department before concrete placement, and the asphalt surface must be cleaned, dried and cooled to less than 120 °F immediately before placing the concrete overlay, and saw cutting may only start once the freshly placed concrete has hardened enough to permit sawing without excessive raveling. [2]

To begin saw cutting for concrete pavement repairs, a saw equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls is deployed. All required full‑depth longitudinal cuts are made along the perimeter of the patch before any transverse cuts are made; for a single‑lane patch this means cutting fully through the existing longitudinal joint the entire length of the patch, whereas when multiple lanes are patched one at a time either a cut parallel to and not more than 1 foot from the existing joint is made in the adjacent lane or a full‑depth cut in the joint is made with a temporary rigid separator no greater than 1/8 inch thick. After the longitudinal cuts, full‑depth transverse cuts are executed at the surface‑marked locations, and if break‑back or spalling occurs a new full‑depth transverse cut must be placed beyond the damaged area; transverse cuts may extend into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars are not harmed. Saw cuts greater than 1/2 inch in width or less than full depth are not permitted to be opened to traffic, and traffic may use the patched area for a maximum of 72 hours after cutting. In continuously reinforced concrete patching, first ensure that no saw cuts extend into the adjacent pavement or shoulders and avoid existing cracks; then make initial full‑depth transverse cuts from the outside edge of the slab toward the longitudinal cut using a wheel saw with carbide steel tips while limiting penetration into the subbase to a maximum of ½ inch. After those full‑depth cuts are in place, remove the concrete between them (lifting it out in one or more pieces) and strip the ends with jackhammers not exceeding 30 pounds. Finally, cut a second transverse saw‑cut at each end of the patch area to a minimum depth of 1 inch, positioned parallel to and outside the initial cuts as shown on the standard drawings, taking care not to cut reinforcement bars; if any bar is cut, convert that cut to full depth and add another 1‑inch minimum cut as required. For full‑depth longitudinal concrete joint repair, a QC plan is submitted at least two weeks before the start of repairs and the repair areas are surface‑marked; then, using a saw equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water cooling system and cut‑depth controls, all required full‑depth longitudinal saw cuts are made along the perimeter of the patch before any full‑depth transverse saw cuts are placed at the marked locations, making each cut parallel to the existing longitudinal joint; do not allow cooling water, slurry, and dust from the sawing operation to enter any lane opened to traffic and do not allow saw cuts greater than 1/2 inch in width to be opened to traffic, after which traffic may use the repaired area for a maximum of 72 hours. In slip‑form paving, the centerline of each transverse contraction joint is accurately marked using tacks or other approved methods; once the concrete has sufficiently hardened, saw cuts are made with guided, water‑cooled equipment to a depth of D/3 and a width of 1/8 inch, extending through the slab edge when safe; only sufficient curing cover is removed to provide space for sawing and the removal period may not exceed ½ hour. For asphalt overlays, a QC plan is prepared and approved to locate and reference each existing transverse joint before any asphalt is placed, and the crew must demonstrate the ability to relocate those joints within ± 1 inch; once the wearing course has been laid and cooled below 140°F, saw‑cuts are made directly above the referenced joints also within ± 1 inch, using a 1/8‑inch wide cut to a depth of at least 1½ inches or one‑third of the overlay thickness when the total overlay is 3 ½ inches or greater (or a minimum 1‑inch depth if placed in a later season), and all cutting must be completed within 7 days after placement of the wearing course. In roller‑compacted concrete, control joints are cut after the rolling operation by using early entry saws set to the manufacturer’s recommendations; the cuts are made at the locations shown on the plans and are cut to 1/4 depth of the compacted RCC pavement. Saw cutting is performed as soon as possible behind the rollers to prevent random cracking, beginning no later than 18 hours after placement, with care taken to cut without causing raveling or other damage to the pavement. For bonded concrete overlays, the locations of all transverse joints are accurately marked with guide nails, tacks or other approved devices before the concrete overlay is placed; after the overlay cures to a strength that will not cause raveling or chipping, a saw cut is made directly over each existing joint to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch and to a width at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack, taking care not to damage dowel load transfer devices. The curing compound may be applied in two equal rates with the second rate applied immediately after the sawing operations have been completed; after sawing, the freshly cut joints are flushed with water to remove slurry before backing material is placed and the joints are sealed as specified. In full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs, a diamond saw is employed to cut through the full depth at the limits of each precast slab and at the proposed longitudinal joint locations, while wheel saws with carbide steel tips are prohibited; next, lifting devices are installed and the lift‑out method is used to remove the concrete or composite pavement enclosed by those full‑depth cuts in one or more pieces without disturbing the subbase. Sawcutting and drilling of lift‑out holes may be performed up to five calendar days before removal and only concrete that can be replaced within the workday should be taken out; finally, the existing longitudinal joint is over‑cut sufficiently to provide a straight, smooth joint, cutting no more pavement than required. For pavement relief joints, the asphalt wearing course is allowed to cool below 140°F, after which, within 7 days of placing the relief joint, a saw‑cut is made directly above the interface between the asphalt courses and the concrete pavement positioned within ±¼ inch; the cut extends through any existing widening to form a reservoir that is 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide, and if wet sawing is used the reservoir is immediately flushed with water. Following the saw cutting of RCC control joints for a roller‑compacted concrete base course, all loose debris generated by the operation must be removed, additional emulsified asphalt is applied along the freshly cut joints as specified, and a uniform void‑free layer of emulsified asphalt is then placed over the RCC surface before any asphalt wearing course or surface treatment is installed. In class 1, 1A, and 1C excavation, a full‑depth saw cut of the existing pavement is performed along the neat lines indicated using a saw blade or cutting wheel; after the cut the excavation is kept graded to drain and sealed with smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment so that the subgrade does not become saturated, and at the end of each workday the surface is sealed to prevent deterioration. Staging and logistics

The saw‑cutting work is controlled through a QC Plan that defines action points for all construction phases, including joint sawing and sealing, and it requires traffic‑protective measures such as drop‑off protection and limits any excavated patch area to remain un‑patched for no more than 48 hours; if longer exposure is needed adjacent to open lanes the area must be graded and compacted to prevent movement. After a full‑depth saw cut the patched portion may carry traffic for up to 72 hours provided the cuts are at least full depth and not wider than 1/2 inch. Equipment and material flow are managed by ensuring adequate equipment and trained personnel are on site and that sufficient hauling units are scheduled to maintain continuity of placement, while cooling water, slurry and dust from the saw must be kept out of any lane opened to traffic. Under Section 518—Continuously Reinforced Concrete Pavement Patching, saw cuts shall not extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders, shall be located at least 24 inches from any existing crack and may not cross an existing crack; a second transverse cut of at least 1 inch depth is required at each end of the patch area, wheel‑saw penetration into the subbase is limited to ½ inch, jackhammers larger than 30 pounds are prohibited, and break‑back of the existing pavement underside may not exceed 2 inches. Section 516—Concrete Pavement Patching further restricts full‑depth cuts wider than 1/2 inch from being opened to traffic, requires saw‑cutting work to be staged so that traffic lanes are protected from drop‑off hazards and from any cooling water, slurry or dust generated by the diamond‑tipped blade, prohibits such material from entering an opened lane, and if rain occurs while a patch area remains open an outlet must be excavated through the shoulder at the lowest point of the patch with any resulting shoulder damage repaired before traffic returns; when a cut or excavated patch remains exposed adjacent to live lanes for more than 48 hours the contractor must grade and compact suitable material or employ another approved method to protect traffic. For asphalt overlays, Section 515—Sawing and Sealing of Asphalt Overlays permits cutting only after the overlay has cooled below 140 °F, mandates that cuts be placed directly over each existing transverse joint within ±1 inch, requires completion of all cuts and subsequent sealing within 7 days of placing the wearing course, and prohibits sealing unless ambient air temperature is at least 40 °F and not above 90 °F. Section 545—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Concrete‑Surfaced Pavements requires concrete to be placed when its temperature is between 65 °F and 90 °F and the prepared surface to be below 120 °F before overlay; if forecast air temperature is 45 °F or less insulating blankets must be used and removed only after air temperature exceeds 45 °F or the concrete reaches a compressive strength of 3,600 psi, with the rate of surface temperature change not exceeding 40 °F in any one‑hour period, standing water must be drained, oil and grease drippings prevented on the cleaned surface, and each saw cut flushed immediately with water to eliminate slurry that could impair sealant placement. Section 540—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Asphalt‑Surfaced Pavement (540.3(i)) states that the only acceptable method for creating concrete‑overlay joints is saw cutting as soon as the slab has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling and to prevent random cracking; the work may proceed continuously day or night regardless of weather conditions, but any curing cover removed to provide clearance for the saw must not exceed a half‑hour removal time, joint layout shall follow the spacing indicated on the plans (or an approved layout) and shall avoid longitudinal joints in wheel paths, the excavation being cut shall be kept graded to drain, the area should be sealed with smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment so the subgrade does not become saturated, the surface must be sealed at the end of each workday to prevent deteriorating conditions, and temporary drainage structures or channels may be required. Quality control and acceptance

In Section 516—Concrete Pavement Patching, the inspector must verify that the saw is equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls; that every cut intended for traffic opening is full depth and does not exceed 1/2 inch in width; that all required full‑depth longitudinal cuts are made before any transverse cuts; that cooling water, slurry or dust is prevented from entering an opened lane; that any temporary rigid separator placed between the patch and the adjacent lane is no thicker than 1/8 inch; and that for corner repairs the saw depth is limited to 2 inches and extends at least 2 inches beyond the damaged area while avoiding intrusion into adjacent curbs. In Section 518—Continuously Reinforced Concrete Pavement Patching, cuts must stop at the edge of the pavement and shall not extend into adjacent pavement or shoulders; the initial transverse cuts shall be full‑depth vertical cuts and each end cut shall have a minimum depth of 1 inch; no cut may be located within 24 inches of an existing crack nor cross an existing crack; partial‑depth cuts are prohibited from cutting reinforcement bars, and if any bar is cut the inspector must verify that the cut has been upgraded to a full‑depth cut with an additional ≥1‑inch partial cut as prescribed; wheel‑saw penetration into the subbase shall not exceed ½ inch; jackhammer size shall not exceed 30 lb; break‑back of the underside greater than 2 inches or reinforcement damage requires a new full‑depth cut, and any subbase disturbance deeper than 1 inch must be removed and replaced. In Section 519—Full Depth Longitudinal Concrete Joint Repair, the saw used for cutting the patch perimeter must be equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls; cooling water, slurry and dust must be prevented from entering any lane opened to traffic; saw cuts that will be opened to traffic may not exceed 1/2 inch in width; break back or spalling must be avoided, and if it occurs a new full‑depth transverse cut must be made beyond the damaged area; when extending full‑depth cuts at repair limits the extension may not exceed full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars in the adjacent lane are not damaged. In Section 501—Plain or Reinforced Cement Concrete Pavements, the centre of each transverse joint cut shall be located within ±1 inch of the dowel‑basket or mechanically implanted dowel centreline; the cut depth shall equal one‑third the slab thickness (D/3) and the width shall be 1/8 inch; immediately after the initial cut the joint must be thoroughly flushed of slurry, an oversized backer rod whose width is the cut width plus 50 percent placed in the top of the joint, and the joint monitored for any opening throughout the cure period until sealing; curing covers may be removed no later than ½ hour before sawing. In Section 515—Sawing and Sealing of Asphalt Overlays, each cut must be placed directly above the identified transverse joint and fall within ±1 inch tolerance; the asphalt surface must have cooled to below 140 °F before cutting begins; cutting and subsequent sealing must be completed no later than 7 days after the wearing course is placed; for overlays three‑and‑a‑half inches or deeper the initial cut shall be 1/8 inch wide and deep to at least 1½ inches or one‑third of the total overlay thickness, whichever is greater (or a minimum depth of 1 inch when placed in a later season); cuts must be confined to the lane containing the joint with separate cuts provided whenever joints are offset more than 1 inch. In Section 502—Roller Compacted Concrete Pavements, control‑joint saw cuts shall be made to one‑quarter depth of the compacted RCC pavement, performed behind the rolling operation with early‑entry saws set according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, begun no later than eighteen hours after placement, and executed without causing raveling or other damage to the pavement. In Section 545—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Concrete‑Surfaced Pavements, each saw cut shall be located at the marked joint centre; its depth shall equal the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch without harming dowel load‑transfer devices; its width shall be at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack; joint widths shall be measured immediately before placement; after cutting the joint must be flushed with water to remove slurry; and the sealant reservoir dimensions—¼ inch wide for longitudinal joints and at least the underlying joint width for transverse cuts—shall be achieved. In Section 529—Full Depth Precast/Prestressed Concrete Pavement Slabs, saw cutting shall be performed with a diamond saw that cuts through the full depth of the slab limits and proposed longitudinal joints; wheel saws with carbide‑steel tips are prohibited; the resulting joint width shall not fall below the minimum of ¼ inch, and any transverse joint measured at less than this value must be re‑sawcut to obtain a minimum of ¼ inch. In Section 504—Pavement Relief Joint, the saw‑cut placed at the interface of the relief joint and concrete pavement shall lie within ±¼ inch of the specified line; cutting shall not begin until the asphalt wearing course has cooled below 140 °F; the cut shall be completed no later than seven days after the joint is placed; the cut shall extend through any existing widening, creating a reservoir approximately one inch deep by ½ inch wide, and if wet sawing is used the reservoir shall be immediately flushed with water. In Section 540—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Asphalt‑Surfaced Pavement, each saw cut shall be inspected to confirm it reaches a depth of D/3 and attains at least the minimum width of 1/8 inch; the concrete shall have hardened sufficiently to prevent excessive raveling; no cut shall be placed within 1.5 feet of an existing crack; any initial cuts that do not extend through the slab edge shall be extended when it can be done without causing damage; after cutting the joint must be cleaned of all deleterious material. In Section 323—Roller Compacted Concrete Base Course, after each saw cut the joint shall be inspected to confirm that all loose debris from the cutting operation has been removed before any asphalt is placed; the subsequent application of emulsified asphalt along the control joints shall be checked for a uniform, void‑free coating across the RCC surface; and the equipment used to place this asphalt shall be verified as approved by the Representative and shown not to damage, mar, or otherwise alter the compacted pavement. Safety and environmental controls

In Section 516—Concrete Pavement Patching (516.3 Construction), the rotating diamond‑tipped blade used for saw cutting creates a worker hazard of contact with the blade or flying debris, so the equipment must include a blade guard, alignment guides, a water cooling system and cut‑depth controls to protect operators. The operation also generates cooling water, slurry and dust that must be kept out of any lane opened to traffic because those materials pose slip and visibility hazards to the public. In Section 519—Full Depth Longitudinal Concrete Joint Repair (519.3 Construction), workers are exposed to blade‑related injury and airborne particles, so the specification requires a diamond‑tipped blade together with a blade guard, alignment guides, a water cooling system and cut‑depth controls. For the public, the procedure forbids cooling water, slurry or dust from entering an opened lane and mandates protection of drop‑off conditions as specified in Section 901.3(j); additionally, only saw cuts that are 1/2 inch in width or less may be opened to traffic, limiting exposure of vehicles and pedestrians. In Section 504—Pavement Relief Joint (504.3 Construction), the specification mandates that saw cutting shall not be performed until the asphalt wearing course has cooled below 140°F, indicating a safety concern for workers from hot‑asphalt surfaces; no public hazards are identified in this section. Curing, protection, and handoff

When saw cuts are made for a dowel‑bar retrofit, the parallel slots are prepared and cleaned, epoxy‑coated dowels are placed, and the voids are filled with concrete patching material which is then cured to develop strength [1]. For other joint work that follows saw cutting, any joints or cracks that will be patched but not overlaid are sealed with a rubberized joint sealing material as specified in Publication 408, providing protection against water and chloride ingress [1]. After saw cutting, traffic may be allowed on patched areas for up to 72 hours provided the cuts are full depth and not wider than ½ inch; any excavated patch left open longer than 48 hours must be graded, compacted or otherwise protected from traffic as directed, and if rain occurs while the area is open an outlet shall be cut in the shoulder to drain water and any resulting shoulder damage repaired at no cost [2]. The concrete placed after saw cutting is cured with a liquid‑membrane forming curing compound permitted by Section 501.3(l) (or Type 2 white pigmented compound), and rapid‑set patching material must be cured according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, after which grout is placed around the patches before opening to traffic [2]. In slip‑form paving any non‑white‑membrane curing cover may be removed only where the joint will be cut and removal cannot exceed ½ hour; an oversized backer rod whose width equals the joint width plus 50 % is placed in the joint to retain moisture, and if a white membrane curing compound was originally applied it must be reapplied after the cut as required by Section 501.3(l)1.c, with the joint remaining under curing protection until the permanent sealant is placed [2]. For asphalt overlays saw cutting may occur only after the asphalt has cooled below 140 °F and the work must be sealed within seven days of placing the wearing course; reservoir faces must be thoroughly clean and dry, sealing material may be placed only when ambient temperature is between 40 °F and 90 °F unless the Representative permits otherwise, and the material must be heated and kept within the manufacturer’s safe pouring temperatures [2]. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements after saw‑cut control joints are made the RCC surface must be kept moist immediately after final rolling and in‑field density testing; one or more curing methods such as water cure with a misting‑spray equipped truck, white polyethylene sheeting, curing compound applied to give a uniform solid opaque film, or cold‑weather curing per Section 501.3(l)2 are then maintained for a minimum of 72 hours and until the slab reaches at least 3,000 psi compressive strength, with the surface protected from vehicular traffic during this period [2]. In bonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements curing materials are applied after finishing and texturing operations and within 30 minutes of bleed‑water dissipation, then remain in place until the pavement reaches the strength required for opening to traffic as specified in 545.3(l); when forecast air temperature is 45 °F or less insulating blankets are provided to prevent rapid heat loss and may be removed when air temperature exceeds 45 °F or average compressive strength exceeds 3,600 psi, provided surface temperature change does not exceed 40 °F within any one‑hour period; a curing compound is applied homogenously at a minimum rate of 1 gallon (±0.1 gallon) per 125 ft² or as the manufacturer’s recommendations, and if conditions warrant it may be applied in two equal rates with the second coat placed immediately after sawing, while joint faces must be protected from exposure to curing compound or cleaned thoroughly before sealant installation [2]. For pavement relief joints after the asphalt wearing course has cooled below 140 °F a saw‑cut is made within ±¼ inch of the interface and extended through any widening to create a reservoir that is 1 inch deep by ½ inch wide; if wet sawing is used the reservoir is flushed with water immediately, the cut must be performed within seven days of placing the pavement relief joint, faces are cleaned and dried before sealing, and sealing material is placed only when air temperature is between 40 °F and 90 °F (unless a Representative permits otherwise), after a tape bond‑breaker is applied to the bottom of the reservoir and the material is kept within manufacturer’s recommended heating and pouring temperatures, filling the reservoir to 1/8‑to‑1/4 inch below the pavement surface without spill onto the surface [2]. In bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavements any existing curing cover must be removed only at the joint location, removal may not exceed ½ hour, and the covering is replaced immediately after the cut; if a white‑pigmented liquid membrane‑forming compound was used the joint area is cured as specified in Section 501.3(l)1.c, and insulating blankets are provided when air temperature is 45 °F or less, removable once temperature exceeds 45 °F or compressive strength exceeds 3,000 psi, ensuring surface temperature does not change more than 40 °F in any one‑hour period [2]. After saw cutting the RCC control joints of a roller‑compacted concrete base course all loose debris must be removed and an additional coat of emulsified asphalt is placed along the joints and over the entire RCC surface as required by Section 460.3(b); curing compounds are prohibited, and once the prescribed cure period is complete the RCC pavement may be opened to local traffic before the asphalt wearing course or surface treatment is placed [2]. During saw cutting the pavement is cut full depth with a blade along the prescribed lines; the excavation must be kept graded to drain and compacted with smooth‑wheeled compaction equipment, which also seals the surface to keep the subgrade from becoming saturated, and at the close of each workday the exposed surface is sealed to protect it from deteriorating before further work can continue [2].

For concrete pavement patching (Section 516—Concrete Pavement Patching), after the saw‑cutting operation construction traffic may be permitted on the patched area for a maximum of 72 hours, provided that all cuts are full depth and do not exceed 1/2 inch in width; any cut wider than 1/2 inch or not full depth must remain closed to traffic. For full‑depth longitudinal concrete joint repair (Section 519—Full Depth Longitudinal Concrete Joint Repair), after the saw‑cutting operation traffic may be allowed on the repair areas for a maximum of 72 hours, as long as any saw cuts that are opened to traffic do not exceed ½ inch in width and cooling water, slurry, or dust from the sawing does not enter any lane that is open to traffic. In slip‑form paving (Section 501—Plain or Reinforced Cement Concrete Pavements, paragraph (g)), pavement that has undergone saw cutting may be opened to construction traffic or users only after all joints widened by second‑stage cuts have been sealed, no further second‑stage sawing has been performed, a backer rod is placed and kept near the surface, the concrete has reached its initial strength, and the required curing procedures have been satisfied. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502—Roller Compacted Concrete Pavements), work completed under saw cutting may be opened to construction traffic or users only after the RCC pavement’s curing period has been fully satisfied, at which point it can be opened to traffic as specified in Section 502.3(j). For bonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 545—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Concrete‑Surfaced Pavements), work completed under saw cutting may be opened to construction traffic or users once the Representative determines that the concrete has attained the minimum required compressive strength of 3,600 psi as measured by cylinder testing in accordance with PTM No. 604 and the bond strength meets the minimum acceptable level specified in Section 545.3(e). For bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavement (Section 540—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Asphalt‑Surfaced Pavement), saw joints must be cut with a green‑cut saw to a depth of one‑third the slab thickness and at least 1/8 inch wide as soon as the concrete has sufficiently hardened to permit sawing without excessive raveling; after sawing, all curing materials shall remain in place until the overlay attains the strength required for opening to traffic as defined in Section 540.3(q). Insulation blankets may be removed only when the air temperature exceeds 45°F or when the average compressive strength of the concrete exceeds 3,000 psi, and removal must not cause a temperature change greater than 40°F in any one‑hour period; the curing cover over a joint area may be taken off for no more than ½ hour to allow sawing, after which it must be replaced immediately. For roller‑compacted concrete base courses (Section 323—Roller Compacted Concrete Base Course), after the saw‑cut control joints have been cleared of loose debris and the required emulsified asphalt has been applied, the RCC pavement must remain in its cure period; when that cure period is complete the pavement may be opened to local or construction traffic even though the asphalt wearing course or surface treatment has not yet been placed. Expected performance and maintenance

Under Section 516‑3 (Concrete Pavement Patching), break back of the underside of the existing pavement or spalling of concrete may develop; full‑depth cuts are not allowed to extend into an adjacent concrete curb and any damage to that curb must be repaired by the contractor at no cost. Section 518‑3 (Continuously Reinforced Concrete Pavement Patching) lists several potential distresses, including extending cuts into adjacent pavement or shoulders, cutting across an existing crack, locating saw‑cuts within 24 inches of an existing crack, inadvertently cutting reinforcement bars when making partial‑depth cuts, allowing the wheel saw to penetrate more than ½ inch into the subbase or to cut into remaining pavement, causing excessive break back of the slab underside greater than 2 inches, and disturbing the subbase deeper than 1 inch. According to Section 501‑3 (Plain or Reinforced Cement Concrete Pavements), if initial saw cuts fail to extend through the slab edge, blowouts or edge damage of green concrete may occur and must be repaired at no additional cost; cutting too early may cause excessive raveling of fresh concrete and lead to uncontrolled random cracking; and cuts that are not aligned with dowel‑bar centers can misplace the joint, which is considered a defect requiring correction. Section 540‑3 (Bonded Concrete Overlay of Asphalt‑Surfaced Pavement), modifying Section 501‑3, requires that saw cutting be timed so the concrete is sufficiently hardened to avoid excessive raveling and random cracking—cutting too early can produce those distresses—and mandates omission of a saw cut when a crack exists at or within 1.5 feet of the planned joint location until the condition is addressed. Section 515‑3 (Sawing and Sealing of Asphalt Overlays) requires that a saw‑cut be placed directly above an existing transverse joint within ± 1 inch; any cut outside this tolerance is declared defective and must be repaired in accordance with Section 105.12, and if the joint‑referencing process cannot consistently locate joints within ± 1 inch, work shall be stopped and the quality‑control plan revised. Under Section 504‑3 (Pavement Relief Joint), a saw‑cut that is not placed within the required ± ¼ inch tolerance at the interface between the asphalt courses of the relief joint and the underlying concrete pavement is also declared defective per Section 105.12. Section 502‑3 (Roller Compacted Concrete Pavements) specifies that control joints be sawed to a depth equal to one‑quarter of the compacted pavement and that cutting shall begin no later than 18 hours after placement; if the cut is delayed or performed improperly, random cracking may develop and the cut edges can ravel, requiring repair as directed by the Representative. According to Section 545‑3 (Bonded Concrete Overlay of Concrete‑Surfaced Pavements), the concrete must be strong enough to avoid significant raveling or chipping; the cut depth must equal the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch and the width must be at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack, otherwise debonding may occur, and any damage to dowel load‑transfer devices renders the joint defective and requires removal and replacement under the defective‑work provisions. Section 519‑3 (Full Depth Longitudinal Concrete Joint Repair) states that full‑depth longitudinal cuts shall be made before transverse cuts; if break back or spalling occurs, a new full‑depth transverse cut must be placed beyond the affected area; full‑depth cuts at repair limits may extend transversely into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars in the adjacent lane are not damaged, with any damage requiring repair at no additional cost; and cooling water, slurry, and dust from the sawing operation must not enter any lane opened to traffic. Preventive maintenance for saw‑cut operations is initiated by submitting a quality‑control (QC) plan well in advance of work, with the Pavement Policy Manual requiring resealing as soon as joints begin to show distress, while Publication 408 specifications (as cited in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania DOT Specification) mandate that the QC plan be submitted at least two weeks before concrete pavement patching or full‑depth longitudinal joint repair and must describe the diamond‑tipped blade saw, its guard, alignment guides, water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls. For asphalt overlay projects the same specification requires a documented QC plan to be submitted at least 21 days before placement of any asphalt course, to locate and reference each existing transverse joint within a ± 1 inch tolerance, and to verify that this tolerance is maintained during construction; if the tolerance is not met work must stop and the QC plan amended. The saw used for all operations shall be equipped with a diamond‑tipped blade, a blade guard, alignment guides, a water‑cooling system and cut‑depth controls as stipulated in Publication 408 and reinforced by the specification. Monitoring of saw‑cut joints should focus on early signs of joint distress so that rehabilitation can be applied while the pavement remains restorable, as described in the Pavement Policy Manual. Visual inspection for premature cracking is required after cutting control joints on roller‑compacted concrete pavements, and any random cracking observed before cutting must be repaired as directed by the Representative; this monitoring ensures that cut depth, timing and location meet specified tolerances. For new sealant reservoirs the Pavement Policy Manual specifies a size of 0.75 in by 0.75 in for pavement/shoulder joints, and these reservoirs are constructed during type 1 joint rehabilitation performed on existing saw‑cut joints. Type 1 joint rehabilitation involves constructing new sealant reservoirs and resealing the joint in accordance with Publication 408 specifications; if spalling or faulting is present the specification permits partial‑depth or full‑depth longitudinal joint repairs, slab stabilization, dowel‑bar retrofits that use a power‑driven self‑propelled saw to make two parallel cuts per slot aligned parallel to the roadway centerline, or full‑depth patching. When break‑back or spalling occurs after a cut, both the Pavement Policy Manual and the specification require a new full‑depth transverse saw‑cut to be made beyond the damaged zone before repairing the concrete with at least a Type A patch, and the additional repair is provided at no extra cost. In Sections 516 and 519 of the specification saw cuts opened to traffic may not exceed a width of ½ inch, and traffic may remain on the repaired area for a maximum of 72 hours; excavated repair zones must be patched within 48 hours, and any cut extending beyond the patch limits shall be sealed. For control joints on roller‑compacted concrete pavements the specification requires saw cuts to a depth equal to one‑quarter of the compacted RCC thickness and to be performed no later than 18 hours after placement; debris must be removed before placing the wearing course and additional emulsified asphalt applied along the joints as specified. For new concrete pavement joints cut to a depth of D/3 and a width of 1⁄8 inch, the specification calls for immediate flushing of the joint with water, placement of an oversized backer rod (width plus 50 %), monitoring of joint movement through the cure period, and replacement of the backer rod if the joint opens; a second‑stage saw cut to create the sealant reservoir is required only after at least 72 hours have elapsed. When cutting joints in bonded concrete overlays, the specification mandates a cut depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch and a width at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack; after each cut the joint must be flushed with water, protected from curing compound, and bond strength tested at seven days according to ASTM C1583, with the area deemed defective if the average of three test results is below 175 psi or any individual result is less than 160 psi, in which case additional testing and possible removal of the overlay are required. For precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs the specification requires full‑depth saw cuts using a diamond saw (wheel saws with carbide steel tips prohibited) up to five calendar days before slab removal; joint quality is monitored by checking transverse joint widths, and any joint less than ¼ inch at any point must be re‑sawcut to obtain a minimum of ¼ inch. In all cases saw cuts must avoid existing cracks by maintaining a minimum distance of 24 inches from any crack, and if a reinforcement bar is inadvertently cut the corrective action is to convert that cut to a full‑depth cut and add another partial‑depth (minimum 1 inch) saw‑cut as specified. The overall preventive maintenance program therefore combines timely QC planning, equipment specifications, dimensional controls on cuts, monitoring of joint distress and bond strength, and prescribed repair actions such as resealing, new sealant reservoir construction, partial or full‑depth repairs, dowel‑bar retrofits, and patching to maintain pavement serviceability.
