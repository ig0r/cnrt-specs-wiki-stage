Construction Joint

Part of: Rigid Pavement is-subtopic-of
Construction Joint is a topic under Rigid Pavement.
construction-jointconstruction-joints
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-29

A construction joint is a deliberately created transverse or longitudinal discontinuity in a concrete pavement that is installed whenever concreting is interrupted for more than about thirty minutes, providing a defined location where fresh concrete can be placed adjacent to hardened concrete without loss of bond or uncontrolled cracking [1]. The joint incorporates a bulkhead with dowel bars (or other load‑transfer units) positioned in the center of the cut, and its geometry is limited—transverse gaps ¼ to ½ inch wide, longitudinal gaps no greater than ¾ inch, and vertical differential across any joint not to exceed ¼ inch—to ensure proper load transfer and limit movement [1]. By cutting or tooling the face, cleaning and moistening it (or applying approved backing material), and sealing the ends after placement, the construction joint controls cracking, preserves structural integrity, and must meet bond‑strength requirements of at least 175 psi average (each test ≥160 psi) and specified compressive‑strength criteria before traffic is permitted [1].

Purpose and design objectives

When concrete placement is interrupted longer than a short period—specifically more than a 30‑minute interruption—a transverse construction joint with a bulkhead must be formed so that fresh concrete can be placed adjacent to hardened concrete without uncontrolled cracking or loss of bond; the bulkhead equipped with dowels provides a defined location for load transfer and preserves structural integrity. For full‑depth precast/prestressed slabs, a construction joint is required to limit the finished vertical differential across any joint to not exceed 1/4 inch and to keep transverse joint widths between a minimum of 1/4 inch and a maximum of 1/2 inch and longitudinal joints no greater than 3/4 inch, thereby preventing cracking and maintaining load transfer. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements a cold construction joint is required when the time between placing adjacent lanes or successive lifts exceeds 60 minutes because loss of bond, moisture evaporation, and random cracking may otherwise occur; cutting, cleaning and moistening the vertical face (or keeping a moist surface for horizontal joints) ensures a continuous bond and controls cracking. For bonded concrete overlays on concrete‑surfaced pavements the construction joint is provided to control the interface between fresh overlay and existing hardened pavement so that debonding does not occur; transverse joints must be saw‑cut over all existing joints with a width at least equal to the underlying joint, backing material placed within ±1/4 inch of the surface, and bond strength testing per ASTM C1583 must achieve an average of 175 psi or higher (each test ≥160 psi). In unbonded concrete overlays the construction joints are used to control cracking by providing planned separation lines that accommodate slab movement; saw cuts must be at least D/3 within 12 inches of pavement edges and at least D/4 elsewhere, panels shall have an aspect ratio not exceeding 1.5 and a maximum dimension no greater than 18 times the overlay thickness, which together prevent random cracking and premature failure. For bonded concrete overlays on asphalt‑surfaced pavements the construction joint serves to control cracking by establishing predetermined separation planes; joints must be spaced so that each panel does not exceed 18 times the overlay thickness and its longer dimension divided by the shorter does not exceed 1.5, and they are cut with a green‑cut saw to a depth of D/3 and at least 1/8 inch wide as soon as the concrete is hard enough to avoid excessive raveling. In pervious concrete pavements construction joints are required to create a controlled separation that prevents loss of aggregate and keeps the designed void structure open; the joint must be cut or rolled when the concrete has achieved sufficient compressive strength so that aggregate will not be dislodged, and any fines generated by saw cutting must be removed by vacuum to avoid clogging the pores, thereby preserving permeability and structural performance. In concrete pavement patching the construction joint provides a reliable connection between new fresh concrete and the existing hardened slab; full‑depth saw‑cuts at the interface, load‑transfer dowels installed in the transverse faces of the existing pavement, and a sealant reservoir that is sealed ensure load transfer across the repair area and protect the joint from ingress. [1]

[1] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 362–474)

Under the requirements of Sections 501, 502, 527, 529, 540, 545, 548 and related specifications, a construction joint must provide a functional means to continue placement after interruptions; when concreting is interrupted for more than 30 minutes the joint shall be formed as a transverse bulkhead joint and shall be located at least 10 feet from any other transverse joint to preserve slab integrity. The joint also serves as a movement path for partial‑depth repairs, requiring the prescribed joint filler—1/2‑inch expansion joint material when abutting an existing transverse joint (extending at least 1/2 inch below the repair bottom where possible) and 1/4‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene when abutting an existing longitudinal joint. Structurally, dowel bars shall be placed in bulkheads by drilling or inserting into fiberglass sleeves so that load‑transfer units remain aligned with the center of the dowels, and a minimum slab length of 10 feet between joints is required. Load transfer at transverse joints shall be provided by the specified dowel‑slot or embedded‑dowel systems and tie bars along longitudinal joints; when slabs are opened to traffic before grout placement a minimum of two incompressible shims shall be installed in each transverse joint to prevent drift or spalling. Joint width shall not exceed ½ inch for transverse joints (with a minimum of ¼ inch along the entire length) and shall not exceed ¾ inch for longitudinal joints, while vertical differential across any joint shall not exceed ¼ inch; saw cuts shall be made to a depth equal to one‑third the slab thickness and a width of at least 1/8 inch. Panel dimensions shall not exceed eighteen times the concrete overlay thickness and the aspect ratio of any panel should be as close to 1.0 as feasible and shall not exceed 1.5, thereby limiting panel size to control cracking. In dowel‑retrofit work (Section 527) each dowel must have a minimum ½‑inch clearance from all slot surfaces, may not exceed a vertical tilt or horizontal skew of 0.25 inch displacement of each end relative to the midpoint, and longitudinal and horizontal translation are limited to 1 inch each; non‑metallic expansion caps shall be used, the slot filled with concrete patching material and vibrated to consolidate. Durability is addressed by sealing all transverse and longitudinal joints with an approved hot‑pour or silicone sealant, achieving a minimum bond strength averaging 175 psi (each test not less than 160 psi) as verified by ASTM C1583, and requiring the overlay to reach at least 3,600 psi compressive strength before traffic is allowed. Concrete shall be placed when its temperature is between 65 °F and 90 °F and surface temperature below 120 °F; curing practices—including insulation when air temperature ≤45 °F and a curing‑compound application rate of 1 gallon per 125 ft²—prevent rapid temperature changes, moisture loss and debonding. For pervious concrete systems (Section 520) construction joints shall be cut to roughly one‑quarter slab thickness either by cross‑rolling immediately after final finishing or by saw cutting once sufficient compressive strength is developed; any saw‑cut joint shall have its generated fines removed by vacuum to keep the void system from clogging, and if curing is interrupted the polyethylene sheet and curing regime must be reinstated within 60 minutes. Safety provisions include restricting longitudinal joints from wheel paths, limiting jackhammer weight to no more than 30 pounds (or 15 pounds if damage occurs), requiring air‑blasting at a pressure of at least 100 psi free of oil and moisture, and permitting traffic opening only after the cured concrete attains at least 1,000 psi compressive strength as verified by cylinder testing or the maturity method. Serviceability is ensured by limiting finished surface deviation to not exceed 3⁄16 inch as measured with a 12‑foot straightedge, matching the final surface finish to the existing pavement profile including wheel ruts, maintaining joint surfaces moist to prevent excess moisture loss, and preserving infiltration performance of pervious concrete through proper joint construction and void protection. [1]

[1] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 360–474)

Scope and applicability

Construction joints are appropriate for plain or reinforced cement‑concrete pavements when a concreting interruption exceeds 30 minutes, requiring a transverse joint formed with a bulkhead; such a joint must not be placed within 10 feet of another transverse joint and cannot be used if the slab length at the time of interruption would be less than 10 feet, in which case the concrete to the preceding joint is removed. When existing concrete pavements are widened, new construction or contraction joints should be aligned with existing ones where possible; otherwise they shall not be placed within 10 feet of an existing joint or crack, and all ends of transverse joints and cracks must be sealed to prevent mortar intrusion when adjacent pavement, shoulders, or structures are added. Construction joints are also appropriate for rigid‑pavement projects that employ full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs (PCPS) as defined in Section 500; the approach requires a manufacturer‑listed PCPS system and concrete mix designs meeting a minimum compressive strength of 4,925 psi and flexural strength of 650 psi. Slab lengths may be up to 15 feet (or up to 20 feet if prestressed) with a sacrificial thickness of 1/4 inch; transverse joint widths shall be at least 1/4 inch and shall not exceed 1/2 inch, while longitudinal joint widths shall not exceed 3/4 inch. Dowel bars, tie bars, load‑transfer methods and expansion‑joint locations are provided in accordance with ambient temperature conditions. For concrete pavement patching, construction joints are appropriate when a new patch must be placed adjacent to an existing hardened slab on rigid pavements; this includes Type A patches (6 feet to 20 feet long), Type B patches (20.1 feet to 65 feet long) and Type C patches (65.1 feet to 500 feet long) as well as corner repairs or replacement of an expansion joint, provided the patch is not less than one lane width. Full‑depth longitudinal and transverse saw cuts along the patch perimeter are required; coated dowel bars with 3/4‑inch (or 1/2‑inch for corners) expansion‑joint material are installed where an existing joint is retained, a 1/4‑inch polystyrene board bond breaker is placed in the longitudinal joint of Type A and B patches, tie bars are used for Type C patches, and all anchoring material must set within five minutes. Saw cuts shall not be greater than 1/2 inch in width. In roller‑compacted concrete (RCC) pavements, construction joints provide vertical or horizontal connections between lanes or lifts; a joint is considered fresh when the adjacent RCC material is placed within 60 minutes of the previous placement, otherwise it is treated as a cold joint that must be prepared by cutting, cleaning and moistening the faces before the next pour. Cold horizontal joints may require cement slurry or mortar grout made from the same RCC materials, and control joints are saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth where indicated; sealing follows the specifications of Section 501.3(n). Construction joints are employed when a bonded concrete overlay is placed on existing concrete‑surfaced pavements for full‑depth repairs, elevation corrections or overlays of CRCP; joint backing or approved filling material shall be positioned within ±1/4 inch of the pavement surface before placement and concrete must be placed at a temperature between 65 °F and 90 °F. After curing, transverse and longitudinal joints are saw‑cut to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus 1/2 inch; intermediate transverse joints shall be spaced so that panel length does not exceed 15 feet, and edge slump may not exceed 1/4 inch in the outside 6 inches next to the shoulder and 1/8 inch in the outside 6 inches next to an adjacent lane. For unbonded concrete overlays on concrete‑surfaced pavements, joint layout shall follow panel size limits such that maximum panel dimensions do not exceed 18 times the overlay thickness and the aspect ratio (longer dimension divided by shorter dimension) does not exceed 1.5; saw cuts must be at least D/3 within 12 inches of the edges and at least D/4 elsewhere, with a minimum cut width of 1/4 inch, and may be planned with FHWA’s HIPERPAV or similar software. When a bonded concrete overlay is placed on asphalt‑surfaced pavements, construction joints separate fresh overlay from hardened concrete; joint spacing must follow the project plans or an approved layout, maximum panel dimensions shall not exceed 18 times the overlay thickness, the longer‑to‑shorter dimension ratio shall not exceed 1.5, longitudinal joints are avoided in wheel paths, and joints shall be created only by saw cutting, preferably with early‑entry saws. Construction joints are also appropriate for dowel retrofits at existing cracks or transverse contraction joints on rigid pavements; the procedure calls for three dowel slots per wheel path centered over the joint, using a power‑driven saw to make two parallel cuts and a jackhammer not exceeding thirty pounds (or fifteen pounds if damage occurs), cleaning the slot within specified time limits, placing 1/4‑inch‑thick preformed cellular polystyrene material with a Type B coated dowel bar treated with bond‑breaker lubricant, adding non‑metallic expansion caps and epoxy bonding compound, and finally filling the slot with concrete patching material. For joint rehabilitation after spall repairs on rigid pavements, construction joints are appropriate when unrepaired spalls are 2 inches or less and an existing joint is at least as wide as the required sealant reservoir; water blasting may be performed at a minimum of 10 gpm at 7,000 psi and compressed‑air cleaning at 100 psi of vertical faces before sealing. A tape bond breaker is placed on the bottom of the reservoir with no more than 1/8 inch extension up the face, joint backing material is provided as needed, and sealant is applied in accordance with Section 501.3(n); for Type 2 joints the portion of an existing steel plate within the reservoir may be removed, allowing at most 1/4 inch of plate to protrude. Construction joints are also appropriate for pervious concrete pavement systems wherever the design calls for them; a joint may be formed by tooling into fresh pervious concrete immediately after final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness or by saw cutting after the concrete has attained sufficient compressive strength to keep aggregate from being dislodged, and any saw‑cut joints must have fines removed by vacuum to prevent clogging. [1]

When the removal of unsound concrete in a partial-depth repair becomes excessive or damages dowel bars, the repair area effectively turns into a full‑depth pavement patch and the specification requires constructing a full‑depth concrete pavement patch instead of using a construction joint. Likewise, if the Representative judges the dowel bars to be misaligned or extensively deteriorated, a full‑depth patch or corner repair is prescribed. Additionally, partial-depth repair zones that are less than 24 inches apart must be combined into a single repair, which can eliminate the need for separate construction joints. Finally, if delamination runs through an existing joint and the QC Plan does not contain a detailed procedure to reestablish and protect that joint from mortar intrusion, maintaining two separate minimum repair areas is not permitted, prompting use of an alternative repair method. Design inputs and requirements

For full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs (Section 529), the design of a construction joint must incorporate site‑specific survey data such as elevations of existing abutting pavement and core locations to establish slab dimensions and longitudinal joint positions. Traffic considerations are addressed through planning meetings that define lane‑closure procedures, work direction with traffic flow, and equipment routing. Material requirements include approved concrete mix designs that meet the minimum compressive strength of 4,925 psi and flexural strength of 650 psi together with specified reinforcement, dowel bars and tie‑bars. Environmental input is required in the form of ambient average daily temperatures and exposure conditions at the project site to size panels and locate expansion joints. Geotechnical information calls for evaluation of the subbase and removal of soft, wet or unsuitable material followed by placement and compaction of No. 2A coarse aggregate within grade tolerances. Existing‑condition data such as overcutting of longitudinal joints and verification of joint widths are also needed to achieve the prescribed joint geometry. For bonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 545), design of a construction joint must consider environmental temperature limits, requiring concrete placement when the concrete temperature is between 65 °F and 90 °F and mandating insulating blankets if the forecast air temperature is 45 °F or less until temperatures rise. Traffic criteria for opening to traffic are based on achieving at least 3,600 psi compressive strength and meeting the bond‑strength thresholds set in Section 545.3(e) (average ≥175 psi and each test ≥160 psi). Material specifications require curing compound applied at a minimum of 1 gallon per 125 ft² and sealing of joints after saw‑cutting to the full overlay thickness plus ½ inch. Existing‑condition data include the scarification depth called out in Section 591.3, removal of loose reinforcement, thorough cleaning of the surface, and measurement of existing transverse and longitudinal joint widths before overlay placement, with joint backing installed within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface. [1]

[1] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 438–469)

Under Section 501.3 of the Rigid Pavements specifications a construction joint must be governed by a field‑operation quality‑control plan that is submitted at least two weeks before paving and that includes procedures to ensure proper joint construction, procedures to ensure timely sawing and proper sealing of joints, and procedures to perform a concrete pavement joint wipe test; manual vibrators shall not be operated in contact with transverse or longitudinal joint assemblies and may not be applied in one place for more than five seconds. For slip‑form paving, Section 501.3(g) states that any interruption longer than a 30‑minute period must be treated by constructing a transverse joint with a bulkhead, that such a joint may not be placed within 10 feet of another transverse joint, and that dowels must be placed in the bulkheads either by drilling or by inserting them into fiberglass dowel sleeves; load‑transfer units must be secured in advance of paving and dowel locations accurately marked so that transverse cuts are centered over the dowels. When a dowel retrofit is required, Section 527.3 requires a power‑driven self‑propelled saw capable of making two parallel cuts per dowel bar slot and the use of a jackhammer weighing not more than 30 pounds to remove concrete between saw cuts; final cleaning must be completed within 30 minutes before concrete placement, and vertical tilt or horizontal skew of each dowel end relative to the midpoint may not exceed 0.25 inch. Construction joints performed in accordance with Section 513.3 must follow either Type 1 or Type 2 procedures after spall repairs; any unrepaired spalls two inches or less shall be cleaned and sealed, a tape bond breaker placed on the bottom of the reservoir may not extend up the face more than 1/8 inch, water blasting shall employ a blaster with a minimum capacity of 10 gallons per minute at 7,000 psi, and all vertical faces shall be media‑blasted and then cleaned with a compressed air stream measuring 100 psi before sealing as prescribed in Section 501.3(n). For pervious concrete pavement systems, Section 520.3(k) requires joints to be placed at the locations shown on the design and either to be tooling into the fresh concrete immediately after final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness or to be saw‑cut only after the concrete has attained sufficient compressive strength to prevent aggregate from being dislodged; any saw‑cut joint must have the generated fines removed by vacuum, and if curing is interrupted the polyethylene sheeting and curing must be reinstated within sixty minutes of the initial removal. Construction of a pressure‑relief joint under Section 511.3 shall be performed by sawing the joint opening four inches wide through the full depth and full width of the pavement, removing the existing concrete material, cleaning all vertical faces so that they are free of oil, moisture, and other contaminants, and coating the joint sides with either the sealant or a lubricant adhesive in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations. When a concrete pavement patch is placed, Section 516 requires the construction joint to be formed by providing full‑depth saw‑cuts at the existing pavement/patch interface, installing load‑transfer dowels in the transverse faces of the existing pavement, constructing a sealant reservoir and sealing the joint; patches shall be between six feet and twenty feet long for Type A, between 20.1 feet and 65 feet long for Type B, and between 65.1 feet and 500 feet long for Type C, and in all cases the patch shall not be less than one lane width. The design and execution of construction joints for rigid pavement patching are further governed by Section 516.3, which references Section 501.3 for joint geometry, dowel‑bar treatment, and concrete mix criteria; saw cuts forming the joint may not exceed 1/2 inch in width or be less than full depth, longitudinal joints shall be saw‑cut 1/4 inch wide and 1 inch deep with a 1/4‑inch polystyrene board bond breaker for Type A and B patches, dowel bars placed in the joint shall be coated or lubricated on at least one‑half of their length unless pre‑lubricated, must be embedded with an anchoring material that reaches initial set within five minutes, and verification by core drilling shall show voids less than 0.50 inch; traffic may be opened only after the Representative confirms that the concrete has reached a minimum compressive strength of 1,000 psi as measured on cylinders in accordance with PTM No. 604. Continuously reinforced concrete pavement patches are governed by Section 518, which mandates a single‑course, full‑depth, continuously reinforced placement of at least one lane width, a partial‑depth saw‑cut at the patch interface together with a sealant reservoir that is sealed; for Type A joints the length shall be between six feet and twenty feet when tied splices are used or between four feet and twenty feet when welded splices are used, for Type B joints the length shall be between 20.1 feet and 65 feet, and for Type C joints the length shall be between 65.1 feet and 500 feet. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements, Section 502.3 defines a fresh vertical or horizontal joint as one where the adjacent lane or lift is placed within 60 minutes of placing the previous lane or lift, although the Representative may adjust this limit based on retarders or weather; for fresh horizontal joints the surface must be kept moist and all loose material cleaned before the second lift, while cold joints require cutting the pavement vertically for the full depth at least six inches from the exposed edge with an approved saw, removing excess concrete, cleaning the joint, and immediately moistening the vertical face before placement; control joints are to be saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth of the compacted pavement behind rolling as soon as possible using early‑entry saws per manufacturer recommendations and beginning no later than 18 hours after placement, with sealing performed when indicated in Section 501.3(n). Bonded concrete overlays are governed by Section 545.3, which requires joint backing or an approved filling material to be placed within ±1/4 inch of the pavement surface before overlay placement, mandates that transverse joints be saw‑cut over existing joints to a depth equal to the full overlay thickness plus ½ inch with a cut width at least as wide as the underlying joint, specifies that bond strength between old and new concrete must be verified by testing at seven days in accordance with ASTM C1583 using lot testing per PTM 1 and that the average of three test results must not be below 175 psi while each individual result must be at least 160 psi, limits concrete placement temperature to between 65°F and 90°F and requires the prepared surface temperature to be less than 120°F, and calls for curing to follow the modified provisions of Section 501.3(l). Unbonded concrete overlays are governed by Section 548.3, which dictates that joint layout must follow the spacing shown on the project plans or, if none is provided, a Department‑approved joint plan must be prepared and installed before concrete placement; longitudinal joints shall be avoided in wheel paths; panel dimensions are limited to a maximum of 18 times the overlay thickness and an aspect ratio that should be as close to 1.0 as feasible but may not exceed 1.5, with slight deviations permitted only to match adjacent structures; saw cuts defining the joints must be made with a depth no less than D/3 within 12 inches of the pavement edges and no less than D/4 anywhere, using sawing as the sole acceptable method, and all joint faces must be cleaned of deleterious material before sealing. Bonded concrete overlays over asphalt‑surfaced pavements are governed by Section 540.3, which requires joint spacing to follow the project plans or an approved Department layout when none are shown, limits panel dimensions to no more than 18 times the overlay thickness with a longer‑to‑shorter dimension ratio at or below 1.5, avoids longitudinal joints in wheel paths, allows sawing as the only acceptable technique for creating construction joints, specifies that cuts be made when the concrete has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling using early‑entry saws as recommended by FHWA’s HIPERPAV or similar software, requires a cut depth of D/3 with a minimum width of 1/8 inch, and after sawing mandates that all joints be cleaned of deleterious material and not sealed at that time. Partial‑depth concrete pavement repairs are governed by Section 525.3, which requires a quality‑control plan approved as required by Section 106.03(a)2; when the repair abuts an existing transverse joint the contractor shall install 1/2‑inch expansion joint material and when it abuts an existing longitudinal joint shall install 1/4‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene, each extending at least 1/2 inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible; the completed joint must then be sealed in accordance with Section 512.3 for longitudinal joints and Section 521.3 for transverse joints. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs, Section 529.3 mandates that the transverse joint width shall not exceed 1/2 inch and shall be a minimum of 1/4 inch along its entire length, that longitudinal joints must not exceed 3/4 inch, that load transfer across those joints must follow the methods prescribed in Section 705.3 using dowel bars or tie bars as shown on the approved shop drawings, and that grout placed in dowel slots and joint gaps must reach a minimum compressive strength of 2,500 psi before traffic may be opened. Design methods and criteria

Construction joints are governed by a series of procedural controls and design criteria that vary with the pavement type and construction method. In Section 501.3(g) for slip‑form paving, if concreting is interrupted for more than 30 minutes a transverse construction joint shall be constructed using a bulkhead; dowels are placed in the bulkhead either by drilling or by inserting them into fiberglass sleeves, and no new transverse joint may be located within 10 feet of an existing one. The joint is then sawed to a depth equal to one‑third the slab thickness (D/3) and a width of 1⁄8 inch. In Section 529.3 for full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs, transverse joint widths shall be between ¼ inch and ½ inch while longitudinal joint widths shall not exceed ¾ inch; vertical differential across any joint shall not exceed ¼ inch (differences up to ½ inch may be corrected by diamond grinding), load transfer is provided with dowel or tie bars coated with bond‑breaker, joint locations and panel sizes are determined using the standard expansion‑coefficient values from the AASHTO Mechanistic‑Empirical Pavement Design Guide, and traffic may not be permitted until grout placed through specified ports reaches a compressive strength of at least 2 500 psi. In a bonded concrete overlay over asphalt‑surfaced pavement the joint layout follows the project plans or an approved joint layout plan, with maximum panel dimensions not to exceed eighteen times the overlay thickness and a longer‑to‑shorter side ratio not to exceed 1.5; all joints are created by sawing with a green‑cut saw to a depth of D/3 and a minimum width of one‑eighth inch, longitudinal joints are avoided in wheel paths, transitions are required at the overlay limits, and where a transition overlies existing treated base course or concrete pavement a geotextile debonding layer is placed; no reinforcement is prescribed for the construction joint. In a partial‑depth repair that abuts an existing transverse joint a half‑inch expansion joint material shall be installed in the joint, while an adjacent longitudinal joint requires a quarter‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene filler; both materials should extend at least half inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible and the joint may be re‑established by saw‑cutting through the full depth of the new concrete if it can be cut without damage, after which transverse joints are sealed per Section 521.3 and longitudinal joints per Section 512.3. Any reinforcement that becomes exposed in a jointed reinforced concrete pavement during removal shall be cut and removed, whereas reinforcement in continuously reinforced concrete is left intact; dowel bars exposed in the transverse joint should receive a bond‑breaking coating if they show little sign of corrosion, otherwise a full‑depth patch is required. In a construction joint that is being retrofitted with dowels the joint must be cut so that the centre of each dowel aligns at the mid‑depth of the pavement slab—or at the mid‑depth of the thinner slab when thickness varies—and three parallel slots are provided per wheel path centered over the crack or transverse joint; each slot is filled with a 1/4‑inch‑thick piece of preformed cellular polystyrene cut to fit, leaving an opening for the dowel; Type B coated dowels receive a bond‑breaker lubricant applied at least one hour before concrete placement, are seated on non‑metallic chairs that give a minimum 0.5‑inch clearance from all slot surfaces, and have 1½‑inch expansion caps installed with a ¼‑inch gap to the cap bottom; epoxy bonding compound is then applied per the manufacturer’s recommendations and the slot is filled with concrete patching material and vibrated so the dowel does not move. For general joint rehabilitation construction joints must be prepared by sawing a sealant reservoir to the width and depth shown on the standard drawings for the existing joint spacing, centering it on the opening and flushing it with water immediately after cutting; a tape bond breaker is placed on the bottom of the reservoir and may not extend up the face more than 1/8 inch; any spalls two inches or less are cleaned and sealed before work proceeds. When the existing joint is as wide as or wider than the required reservoir, it is cleaned to the necessary depth, optionally using a water‑blaster that operates at least 10 gpm and 7,000 psi, and joint backing material is added if needed; all vertical faces are media‑blasted and then cleared with compressed air at 100 psi before applying the sealant as specified in Section 501.3(n). For Type 2 rehabilitation the portion of an existing steel plate within the new reservoir is removed, allowing no more than a ¼ inch protrusion into the reservoir. In pervious concrete pavement systems construction joints shall be placed at locations specified either by tooling into the fresh pervious concrete immediately after final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness with a cross‑rolling tool, or by saw cutting after the concrete has achieved sufficient compressive strength to prevent aggregate from being dislodged; if the joint is saw cut any fines produced shall be removed by vacuum to avoid clogging. When curing is interrupted the polyethylene sheeting and curing must be reinstated within 60 minutes of the initial removal. For pressure‑relief joints the joint opening is sawn four inches wide, full depth and full width of the pavement; the existing concrete material is removed and all vertical faces are cleaned so that oil, moisture or other contaminants are absent; both sides of the joint are then coated with either sealant or lubricant adhesive according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. For concrete pavement patching joints a full‑depth saw‑cut is required at the existing pavement/patch interface, load transfer dowels must be installed in the transverse faces of the existing pavement, a sealant reservoir is constructed and the joint sealed; for a new pavement joint a load transfer unit is provided, a sealant reservoir is constructed and the joint sealed. Drainage, environment, and accessibility

Construction joints are required to have their ends sealed after the joint faces are clean and dry, and they must be cured and protected so that moisture does not escape; the pavement surface and sides shall be maintained at a temperature of not less than 40 °F until sufficient strength is achieved, with an additional day of curing added if the temperature falls between 40 °F and 35 °F and the concrete considered defective below 35 °F. Designs for full‑depth precast or prestressed slabs shall incorporate blockouts for any required drainage structures, and the concrete mix design must consider ambient average daily temperatures and site exposure conditions to determine appropriate panel sizes and expansion‑joint locations; moisture protection is provided by undersealing all slabs placed directly on granular or cementitious bedding with the specified cementitious or polyurethane grout, while the subbase must be prepared by removing soft, wet or unsuitable material and compacting coarse aggregate to furnish a stable, dry foundation, and longitudinal and transverse joints shall be sealed as required. In pavement patching operations, if rain occurs while the joint area is open an outlet shall be excavated through the shoulder at the lowest point of the patch and any resulting shoulder damage repaired; cooling water, slurry and dust from saw cutting shall not enter lanes opened to traffic, and patch areas may not remain un‑patched for more than 48 hours or over weekends or holidays, with grading, compaction or other protection required when longer exposure adjacent to open lanes is unavoidable. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements the joint surfaces must be kept moist until the next lift or lane is placed; fresh horizontal joints shall maintain a moist surface, cold vertical joints shall be cut, cleaned and moistened immediately before placement of the adjacent lane, and cold horizontal joints may require cement slurry or mortar grout to prevent excess moisture loss. Concrete placement shall not occur when the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 pounds per square foot per hour unless windbreaks or fog‑mist mitigation are employed, and work must be discontinued if ambient air temperature falls to 40 °F or if the plastic concrete temperature approaches 90 °F, at which point cooling water or aggregates shall be used to keep the mix between 50 °F and 90 °F. Prior to joint formation the subgrade or subbase shall be free of ponded water, frost or frozen material and uniformly moistened. In bonded concrete overlays on concrete‑surfaced pavements the prepared surface for a construction joint must be free of standing water; the Department may require measures to drain or remove any water before placement. Concrete temperature at placement shall be between 65 °F and 90 °F, and the pavement surface temperature must be less than 120 °F immediately before overlay, with fog‑misting, protective covers or blankets permitted to achieve those conditions. When forecast air temperatures are 45 °F or lower adequate insulating blankets shall be provided to prevent rapid heat loss, and they may be removed only after the temperature exceeds 45 °F or the average compressive strength exceeds 3,600 psi, ensuring that the rate of temperature change at the concrete surface does not exceed 40 °F within any one‑hour period. All standing water shall be completely blown off with compressed air and joint faces protected from curing compound before sealant installation. For bonded concrete overlays on asphalt‑surfaced pavements construction joints must be placed on a completely dry surface; any standing water after milling shall be drained immediately, and the asphalt surface temperature shall be less than 120 °F before concrete placement, with fog‑misting or protective covers allowed while ensuring no moisture remains. After sawing, curing compound may be applied but joint faces shall be protected from exposure to it or cleaned before sealant installation. Insulating blankets shall be used when air temperature is 45 °F or less and removed only after the temperature exceeds 45 °F or concrete strength surpasses 3,000 psi, with removal performed so that the surface temperature change does not exceed 40 °F in any one‑hour period. The contractor’s paving‑operation quality‑control plan shall contain procedures to ensure proper joint construction and to manage moisture and climate effects; concrete may not be placed if the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 pounds per square foot per hour unless windbreaks or fog‑misting are employed, placement must be stopped when ambient air temperature drops to 40 °F (45 °F for accelerated‑strength concrete), and when plastic concrete temperature rises to 90 °F cooling water or aggregates shall be used to maintain the mix temperature between 50 °F and 90 °F. In pervious concrete pavement systems joints may be formed either by tooling into the concrete immediately after final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness or by saw cutting after sufficient compressive strength is attained; fines generated by a saw cut shall be removed by vacuum so that the joint remains open for drainage. If curing is interrupted, polyethylene sheeting and curing must be reinstated within 60 minutes of removal. Placement shall not occur when air temperatures are projected below 40 °F or when rain is forecast before finishing and curing; if rain does fall production shall stop, a construction joint shall be formed and polyethylene sheeting applied. Verification and expected performance

The completed construction joint is verified through a series of checks that together demonstrate compliance with the project specifications, constructability, performance and consistency with design requirements. For slip‑form paving, the verifier must confirm that all load‑transfer units were secured before paving and that dowel locations were accurately marked so that the transverse saw cut falls over the centre of each dowel; the dowel assembly may not show vertical tilt or horizontal skew greater than 0.25 inch at either end, nor longitudinal translation greater than 1 inch or horizontal translation greater than 1 inch. Edge‑slump is measured with a 12‑foot straightedge before the concrete loses plasticity and again after hardening, and the slump must not exceed 1/8 inch between adjacent lanes or ramps and must not exceed 1/4 inch between lanes and shoulders; after curing the surface tolerance is checked with a straightedge and the pavement may not vary more than 3/16 inch parallel to or perpendicular to the centreline. The joint is then inspected for proper sealing, cleaned of debris, and must pass the wipe test before any sealant is placed. For full‑depth precast or prestressed slabs, after each slab is set the construction joint must be inspected to confirm that transverse joint widths do not exceed 1/2 inch and are at least 1/4 inch along their full length, with any longitudinal joint not exceeding 3/4 inch; if a transverse gap falls below 1/4 inch it shall be saw‑cut to achieve the minimum. The finished vertical differential across every joint shall not exceed 1/4 inch, and any measured differential greater than this but not more than 1/2 inch may be corrected by diamond grinding, while larger differentials require regrading of the bedding before the next slab is placed. Traffic shall not be allowed until the grout in the joints reaches a minimum compressive strength of 2,500 psi, and all joint faces must be sealed as required. For concrete pavement patching, the QC plan requires a dowel‑bar anchoring check in which the Representative randomly selects three locations, drills a 4‑inch core through each anchored dowel and accepts the joint only if the voids around the dowel perimeter are less than 0.50 inch; next the surface of the patch is tested with a 12‑foot straightedge in both longitudinal direction and across the joint and any high point or depression of 3/16 inch or more must be ground out, retested, and if still excessive the area is deemed defective and must be removed and replaced. The joint may be opened to traffic only after curing when compressive strength testing of cylinders shows at least 1,000 psi. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements, compliance is verified by confirming that fresh vertical or horizontal joints were placed within 60 minutes of the adjacent lane or lift, that any cold joint was cut through the full depth of sound compacted material, cleaned of loose debris and moistened before the next placement, that control joints were saw‑cut to one‑quarter the pavement thickness no later than 18 hours after rolling, that sealing is applied when specified, and that field density testing after final compaction shows a minimum compaction greater than 96 % of the maximum wet density for all lifts. For bonded concrete overlays, verification begins by confirming that joint backing or approved filling material has been placed to within ±1/4 inch of the pavement surface before overlay placement; the joint is then located and saw‑cut to the full overlay thickness plus ½ inch with a width at least as wide as the underlying joint, the cut is flushed with water and the sealant reservoir installed. Bond strength of the prepared surface is tested in the presence of the Inspector at seven days according to ASTM C1583, and the area is deemed defective if the average of three test results is below 175 psi or any individual result is under 160 psi. Opening to traffic requires a compressive strength of at least 3,600 psi and compliance with the minimum bond‑strength values. For unbonded concrete overlays, verification requires that any transverse expansion joints have been identified, marked and precut in the plastic concrete, that each saw cut meets the required depth of at least D/3 within 12 inches of the pavement edges and at least D/4 elsewhere with a minimum width of 1⁄4 inch, and that the joint is cleaned of all deleterious material and sealed. Panel dimensions are measured to ensure they do not exceed 18 times the overlay thickness and that the aspect ratio does not exceed 1.5; joint spacing follows the project plans or an approved layout and longitudinal joints are avoided in wheel paths. The joint is also inspected for any cracking outside the sawed lines, which would be considered defective, and curing‑cover removal at the joint location is confirmed to have been no longer than ½ hour with joint faces protected from curing compound before sealant installation. For bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavements, inspection confirms that joint spacing follows the project plans or an accepted layout, that panel dimensions do not exceed 18 times the concrete overlay thickness and that the longer‑to‑shorter dimension ratio does not exceed 1.5, that sawing was performed with a green cut saw to a depth of D/3 and at least 1/8 inch wide after the concrete hardened sufficiently, that all deleterious material has been removed from the joint faces before any sealant is applied, and that sealing is carried out only with an approved hot‑pour or silicone sealant. For pressure‑relief joints, verification is achieved by confirming that the sawed opening measures 4 inches in width across the full depth and full pavement width, that all existing concrete has been removed, and that the vertical faces are cleaned of oil, moisture and any other contaminants before sealant or adhesive is applied; compliance also requires that the joint sides be coated with the sealant or lubricant adhesive exactly as recommended by the manufacturer. The field‑operation QC Plan, which must be submitted at least two weeks before paving and approved prior to any concrete placement, must contain procedures to ensure proper joint construction, timely sawing and sealing of joints, and a concrete pavement joint wipe test; these actions are used to verify that the completed construction joint complies with project requirements, is constructible, and performs as intended. In a bonded concrete overlay of a concrete‑surfaced pavement (Section 545), the construction joint is expected to achieve sufficient bond strength and concrete compressive strength so that durability and safe traffic service are maintained, thereby controlling resistance to debonding and supporting the pavement’s service life. Bond strength shall be tested at seven days in accordance with ASTM C1583 using three test locations; the average of the three results must be at least 175 psi and each individual result must not be less than 160 psi, otherwise the area is considered defective. The pavement may be opened to traffic only after cylinders indicate a compressive strength of at least 3,600 psi as measured in accordance with PTM No. 604 and the minimum acceptable bond‑strength criteria are met.

For a bonded concrete overlay of an asphalt‑surfaced pavement (Section 540), the construction joint is expected to resist premature cracking and provide a surface that can support traffic once the overlay reaches adequate strength. Panel dimensions should not exceed 18 times the concrete overlay thickness, and the longer‑to‑shorter side ratio should not exceed 1.5. Each joint shall be sawed with a green‑cut saw to a depth of one‑third the slab thickness and a minimum width of 1/8 inch, and all transverse and longitudinal joints shall be sealed with an approved hot‑pour or silicone sealant in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations. Performance is verified by confirming that the joint layout complies with the spacing limits, that saw cuts meet the specified depth and width, that sealants are applied correctly, and finally by opening to traffic only after the concrete attains a compressive strength of at least 3,000 psi measured either through cylinder testing or by the maturity method. Additional quality‑control inspections of core depth are performed as described in Section 540.3(s). [1]
