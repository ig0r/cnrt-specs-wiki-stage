Construction Joint

Part of: Rigid Pavement is-subtopic-of
Construction Joint is a topic under Rigid Pavement.
construction-jointconstruction-joints
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-29

Construction joints are deliberately created interfaces in rigid‑pavement work where fresh concrete must be placed adjacent to hardened concrete, providing a controlled location that prevents weak cold joints, uncontrolled cracking, loss of load transfer, delamination and differential settlement while preserving structural continuity[2]. They are formed whenever concreting is interrupted beyond prescribed limits (typically 30 minutes for slip‑form or 60 minutes for roller‑compacted concrete) by establishing a full‑depth transverse bulkhead or saw cut, installing dowel bars or other load‑transfer devices, and sealing the joint to meet dimensional tolerances such as a transverse width of ¼–½ in. and a vertical differential not exceeding ¼ in.[2] The resulting joint delivers functional continuity, durability, safety and a smooth riding surface for the completed pavement[2].

Purpose and design objectives

Construction joints are incorporated in pavement construction to provide a controlled location where fresh concrete can be placed adjacent to hardened concrete, thereby preventing weak cold joints, uncontrolled cracking, loss of load transfer, debonding, differential settlement and delamination while preserving structural continuity. In Section 501.3(g) for slip‑form paving, a transverse joint formed with a bulkhead and dowels is required when the concreting operation is halted for more than a 30‑minute interruption to maintain pavement integrity after an unavoidable pause. In Section 502.3(a) for roller‑compacted concrete, a fresh vertical joint must be constructed within 60 minutes of the adjacent lane so that a continuous bond is assured; joints exceeding this limit are classified as cold joints and must be cut, cleaned and moistened to prevent excess moisture loss and preserve structural integrity, while control joints are saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth to control random cracking. In Section 529.3 for full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs, transverse joint widths shall not exceed 1/2 inch and shall be at least 1/4 inch along their entire length, longitudinal joints shall not exceed 3/4 inch, vertical differentials across any joint shall not exceed 1/4 inch, and prescribed load‑transfer devices are required to maintain structural continuity. In Section 548.3 for unbonded concrete overlays, joints must be saw‑cut at least one‑third of the slab thickness within 12 inches of the pavement edges, have a minimum width of 1/4 inch, and be laid out according to the project‑specified spacing so panel dimensions remain within the limits for unbonded overlays. In Section 545.3 for bonded concrete overlays on concrete‑surfaced pavements, joints are saw‑cut to a depth equal to the full overlay thickness plus 1/2 inch, with a width at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack, immediately flushed of slurry, backed and sealed, and later verified by a seven‑day bond test that shall average no less than 175 psi and shall not fall below 160 psi for any individual specimen. In Section 540.3 for bonded concrete overlays on asphalt‑surfaced pavement, construction joints are sawed as soon as the concrete is sufficiently hardened to prevent random cracking; panel dimensions shall be limited to a maximum of 18 times the overlay thickness and an aspect ratio not exceeding 1.5 so that shapes prone to premature cracking are avoided. In Section 525.3 for partial‑depth repairs, construction joints address concrete delamination that propagates through existing pavement joints by maintaining the joint functional when fresh repair material is placed adjacent to a hardened slab; when the repair abuts an existing transverse joint, 1/2 inch expansion joint material shall be installed, and when it abuts an existing longitudinal joint, 1/4 inch preformed cellular polystyrene shall be installed extending at least 1/2 inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible. In Section 360.3 for asphalt‑treated permeable base course, a construction joint is required at the end of each day’s work or when an interruption of more than 30 minutes occurs so that a straight transverse joint formed with a temporary vertical bulkhead (or by sawing) creates a full‑depth, full‑width joint that maintains a uniform uncompacted depth adjacent to the compacted lane and provides a smooth joint after compaction. In Section 521.3 for transverse joint cleaning and sealing, the procedure is intended to provide a clean, properly prepared joint surface so that sealant can bond effectively and maintain pavement integrity; this requires completely removing all existing sealing material, neoprene compression seals, asphalt material and other foreign material, cleaning to a minimum depth of 1 ½ inches, using a compressed‑air stream of at least 100 psi measured at the source, installing backer rod sized 25‑35 % larger than the opening to achieve the specified recessed seal depth, applying sealant only when the joint is clean, dry and the ambient and pavement temperatures are 40°F or greater, and filling the joint reservoir to within 1/8‑1/4 inch below the surface. In Section 420.3 for pervious asphalt pavement systems, construction joints are required whenever work is stopped for more than thirty minutes or at the end of each day so that a clean, full‑depth vertical joint can be formed between the newly placed concrete and the previously hardened section, preventing surface irregularities and helping maintain structural continuity despite interruptions in placement. In Section 520.3 for pervious concrete pavement systems, the joint is provided to prevent damage to the load‑bearing matrix and preserve infiltration capacity; it is achieved by tooling into the pervious concrete immediately upon final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness after the concrete has attained sufficient compressive strength to prevent aggregate from being dislodged, and by vacuuming away any fines produced from the operation to avoid clogging of the void system. In Section 530.3 for long‑life concrete pavement, joint provision is aimed at preventing moisture loss from concrete in and adjacent to joints during and after sawing, thereby ensuring proper curing and reducing the risk of cracking or durability problems; the quality‑control plan must include a method to cure and protect joints during the curing period and verification of a minimum of the first five transverse joint locations across the full width of the pavement placed. In Section 516.1 for concrete pavement patching, a construction joint created by full‑depth saw cuts at the existing pavement/patch interface, installation of transverse load‑transfer dowels, construction of a sealant reservoir and sealing of the joint addresses the need to join new concrete to hardened pavement while providing reliable load transfer across the joint and protecting it from infiltration, thereby maintaining the structural performance of the repaired section. The joint thereby provides functional continuity, structural load transfer, durability against cracking, safety for users and serviceability of a smooth surface. In sections 501.3, 529.3, 548.3, 516.3, 502.3, 545.3, 540.3, and 360.3, when a concreting interruption exceeds 30 minutes the joint shall be formed as a transverse bulkhead joint that incorporates dowels placed in fiberglass sleeves; the joint may not be located within 10 feet of any other transverse joint; the dowel‑bar locations must be accurately marked so that the saw cut is centered over the dowels, after which the joint is to be sawn, cured, protected and sealed before traffic or construction equipment is permitted, thereby providing functional continuity, structural load transfer, durability against cracking, safety for users and serviceability of a smooth surface. In full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs the transverse joint width shall not exceed 1/2 inch and shall be at least 1/4 inch along its entire length; the longitudinal joint width shall not exceed 3/4 inch; the finished vertical differential across any joint shall not exceed 1/4 inch (allowable up to 1/2 inch only if diamond‑grinded to meet tolerance); load transfer must be provided by dowel bars or tie bars as detailed in the installation plan; all joints shall be sealed and the grout used for dowels, ties and bedding shall achieve a minimum compressive strength of 2,500 psi before traffic is permitted. In unbonded concrete overlays the joint location and spacing shall follow the project plans or an approved layout, with panel dimensions limited so that the longer dimension does not exceed 18 times the overlay thickness and the aspect ratio does not exceed 1.5; saw cuts defining the joint shall be made to a depth of at least D/3 within 12 inches of the pavement edges and at least D/4 elsewhere, after which all deleterious material is removed and both transverse and longitudinal joints are sealed; longitudinal joints shall not be placed in wheel paths and any concrete panel that exhibits cracking outside the sawed joint shall be considered defective. In concrete pavement patching the construction joint shall be a full‑depth cut no wider than 1/2 inch to permit placement of fresh concrete next to hardened pavement while preventing traffic exposure of undersized cuts, and it shall incorporate the specified expansion‑joint material—3/4 inch for joints adjacent to existing expansion joints or 1/2 inch for corner repairs—and a bond‑breaker where required to maintain load transfer without restraint; dowel bars embedded in the joint must be anchored so that voids around their perimeter are less than 0.50 inch, all longitudinal and transverse joints shall be sealed and finished to match the surrounding texture with surface irregularities limited to 3/16 inch after grinding, the joint area shall be protected from traffic drop‑off hazards and kept free of water intrusion, and traffic may be opened only after the concrete attains a minimum compressive strength of 1,000 psi as verified by cylinder testing. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements the construction joint shall provide a continuous bond between new and previously placed RCC pavement by being placed within 60 minutes of the adjacent lane or lift; when this time limit is exceeded the joint must be prepared as a cold joint by cutting the full depth at least 6 inches from the edge, cleaning loose material and moistening the face before placement; control joints shall be saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth of the compacted slab and initiated no later than 18 hours after placement; when required cold horizontal joints must receive cement slurry or mortar grout of the same mix to prevent moisture loss. In bonded concrete overlays the construction joint shall be located precisely by marking the centre of each existing transverse joint before overlay placement; after the concrete has attained sufficient strength it must be saw‑cut to a depth equal to the full overlay thickness plus ½ inch without damaging dowel load transfer devices, with the cut width at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack; backing or other approved filling material shall be placed within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface and remain in place during paving; a sealant reservoir of the required width is formed and the joint faces are flushed with water before sealant installation; bond strength between the existing concrete and the overlay shall be verified by ASTM C1583 testing requiring an average of at least 175 psi and each individual test not less than 160 psi, opening to traffic is permitted only when compressive strength reaches 3,600 psi and the minimum bond strength is satisfied; edge slump adjacent to shoulders and lanes shall not exceed ¼ inch and ⅛ inch respectively. In bonded concrete overlays over asphalt‑surfaced pavement the construction joint layout must be developed and approved before concrete placement, with panel dimensions limited to a maximum of eighteen times the overlay thickness and an aspect ratio not exceeding 1.5; joints are to be cut only after the slab has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling, using a green‑cut saw to a depth of D/3 and a minimum width of 1/8 inch, and the cuts must be extended through the slab edge to prevent blowouts; longitudinal joints shall be avoided in wheel paths and if a crack is found within 1.5 feet of a planned joint location the saw cut is omitted until the condition is addressed, after which the joints are cleaned of all deleterious material and are not sealed at this stage. In longitudinal construction joints in asphalt‑treated permeable base course the design shall provide an overlap of 1 inch to 2 inches between the new material and the previously placed lane and maintain a uniform uncompacted depth adjacent to the compacted lane so that after compaction the joint is smooth, supporting serviceability and structural continuity. In transverse construction joints whenever work stops at the end of each day’s work or when more than a 30‑minute interruption occurs in ATPBC paving operations a temporary vertical bulkhead shall be installed to form a straight transverse construction joint that shall be the full depth and width of the ATPBC, or the contractor may saw the joint, thereby ensuring functional durability and structural integrity. In section 521.3 the construction joint shall be cleaned by removing all sealing material, neoprene compression seals, asphalt and other foreign matter so that a fresh concrete surface is exposed to at least 1½ inches depth, after which it must be air‑blasted with an oil‑free stream of at least 100 psi measured at the source; sealant may be placed only when the joint is clean and dry and both ambient air and pavement temperatures are 40 °F or greater, a backer rod whose diameter is 25 % to 35 % larger than the joint opening shall be positioned so that its top lies one to two times the joint width plus ¼ inch below the surface, providing a recessed sealant depth‑to‑width ratio of 1:1 to 2:1 and the reservoir filled to a level of ⅛ inch to ¼ inch below the surface. In section 420.3 for pervious asphalt pavements the joint must create a smooth transition by overlapping the new paving course 1 inch to 2 inches over the previously placed lane and maintaining an uncompacted depth adjacent to the compacted lane so that the joint remains smooth after compaction; if work stops for more than 30 minutes a straight transverse full‑depth, full‑width construction joint shall be formed either with a temporary vertical bulkhead or by sawing before paving resumes; surface irregularities greater than 3/8‑inch must be corrected immediately without generating contaminating fines, any depth deficiency of ½‑inch or more requires removal and replacement, and the joint area must achieve a minimum average infiltration rate of 200 inches per hour. In section 520.3 for pervious concrete systems the joint shall be located where the design calls for it and created either by tooling immediately after final finishing to a depth of approximately one‑quarter of the slab thickness or by saw cutting once the concrete has attained sufficient compressive strength to keep aggregate from being dislodged; when a saw cut is used all fines generated must be removed with vacuum to prevent clogging of the pervious matrix, and if curing is interrupted the polyethylene sheet and curing regime must be reinstated within 60 minutes of the initial removal. In section 530.3 for long‑life concrete pavement the joint shall be protected and cured during the curing period to prevent moisture loss from concrete in and adjacent to the joint, a concrete temperature control plan shall be provided to control temperatures that exceed 80 °F at placement; LTU assemblies forming the joint shall remain anchored to the foundation and their vertical and horizontal alignment shall be verified within 18 hours after each day’s pavement placement and finishing for quantities greater than 700 SY by nondestructive methods, verification shall include a minimum of the first five transverse joint locations across the full width and one joint location for each 2,640 LF, and dowel bars incorporated in the joint shall meet the requirements of Section 501.3(i) before or after concrete placement. In section 519.3 when a construction joint is repaired the saw cuts opened to traffic shall not exceed 1/2 inch in width, traffic may resume on repaired sections within a maximum of 72 hours after saw cutting, excavated repair areas shall not remain un‑patched for more than 48 hours (including weekends or holidays), full‑depth transverse cuts at the repair limits may extend into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars are not damaged, and the final surface finish shall match the existing cross‑section with all original longitudinal and transverse joints resealed to preserve durability and serviceability. In section 511.3 the pressure‑relief construction joint shall be sawed 4 inches wide, extending through the full depth and across the entire pavement width, after which all vertical faces shall be cleaned to eliminate oil, moisture and other contaminants, and the joint sides shall be coated with a sealant or lubricant adhesive in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations, thereby providing functional capacity to receive fresh concrete adjacent to hardened sections while supporting structural continuity, durability and safe serviceable performance. Scope and applicability

Construction joints are employed in plain or reinforced cement‑concrete rigid pavements when a concreting operation is interrupted for more than a 30‑minute period; under that condition a transverse joint is formed with a bulkhead and dowels are placed by drilling or into fiberglass sleeves, provided the new joint is not located within 10 feet of any other transverse joint. The approach also applies whenever new lanes, shoulders, or structures are built adjacent to existing concrete pavement, where the new joint should be aligned with existing joints or cracks where possible (Section 501). For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete slab pavements, construction joints are intended when new slabs are placed adjacent to existing hardened concrete; the concrete must meet a minimum compressive strength of 4,925 psi and a flexural strength of 650 psi, with a sacrificial thickness of 1/4 inch added to the top surface. Transverse joints may not exceed 1/2 inch in width and must be at least 1/4 inch along their entire length; longitudinal joints may not exceed 3/4 inch, and vertical differentials across any joint must stay within 1/4 inch (Section 529). In unbonded concrete overlays of existing concrete‑surfaced rigid pavements, construction joints are created by saw cutting after the overlay has hardened; panel dimensions shall not exceed 18 times the overlay thickness and the aspect ratio should not exceed 1.5. Saw cuts must be at least D/3 within 12 inches of the pavement edges and at least D/4 elsewhere, and longitudinal joints should be avoided in wheel paths; the method is restricted to saw cutting (Section 548). When patching or repairing rigid concrete pavements, construction joints are used whenever fresh concrete must be placed next to existing hardened concrete; the method applies during lane‑by‑lane patching of Type A, B, or C sections and calls for full‑depth saw cuts, coated dowel bars with bond‑breaker lubricant, and appropriate expansion joint filler. The longitudinal joint of Type A and B patches must contain a 1/4‑inch full‑depth polystyrene board bond breaker; corner repairs use 1/2‑inch expansion joint material to reestablish the original transverse joint location, and any joint that replaces an existing expansion joint requires 3/4‑inch expansion joint filler. Saw cuts may not exceed 1/2 inch in width, and traffic may be opened only after the concrete reaches a minimum compressive strength of 1,000 psi as measured by cylinder testing (Section 516). In roller‑compacted concrete pavements, construction joints are used for vertical lane separations and horizontal lift interfaces. When the adjacent RCC material is placed within 60 minutes of the previous lane or lift, a fresh joint provides a continuous bond; if more than 60 minutes elapse, the joint is treated as a cold joint and is prepared by cutting vertically through the full depth in sound compacted material at least 6 inches from the exposed edge, cleaning loose material, and moistening the face (or applying cement slurry or mortar grout made with the same RCC materials for horizontal joints) before placement. Control joints are cut to one‑quarter the slab thickness where indicated on the plans (Section 502). For bonded cement‑concrete overlays of existing asphalt‑surfaced pavements, construction joints are required; joint layout must follow spacing shown on project plans or a Department‑approved layout if none is provided. Longitudinal joints should be avoided in wheel paths and panel dimensions shall not exceed 18 times the concrete overlay thickness with an aspect ratio not exceeding 1.5. Joints are cut with a saw once the concrete has hardened enough to prevent excessive raveling, and transitions at the overlay limits over existing treated base or concrete pavement require a geotextile debonding layer (Section 540). In asphalt‑treated permeable base course pavements, construction joints are specified; longitudinal joints are created by overlapping the ATPBC over the edge of the previously placed lane by 1 inch to 2 inches, providing a smooth transition after compaction. A transverse construction joint is required at the end of each day’s work or whenever an interruption exceeds a 30‑minute pause; it is formed with a temporary vertical bulkhead that extends the full depth and width of the ATPBC, although the contractor may alternatively saw the joint (Section 360). Similarly, pervious asphalt pavement systems use construction joints when installing the pervious paving course. Longitudinal joints are created by overlapping the new course over the edge of the previously placed lane by 1 inch to 2 inches, while transverse joints are required at the end of each workday or whenever a pause longer than 30 minutes occurs, formed either with a temporary vertical bulkhead or by sawing a full‑depth joint. The transverse joint must extend through the entire depth and width of the pervious paving course (Section 420). Construction joints are also appropriate for pervious concrete rigid pavements; they are placed at locations called out by the design either by tooling into the fresh concrete immediately after final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness or by saw‑cutting once the concrete has achieved sufficient compressive strength to prevent aggregate from being dislodged, and any fines produced by a saw cut must be removed. A joint may also be formed when curing is interrupted, provided that the polyethylene curing sheet is reinstated within 60 minutes of the initial removal, and it is required whenever rain interrupts placement, at which point production is stopped, a construction joint is made, and the curing sheet applied (Section 520). Construction joints are appropriate for plain cement‑concrete base courses when longitudinal joints are indicated or formed simply to aid construction; the standard drawings for cement concrete pavement joints should be used and, in full‑width construction, sawing of the joint is not required. Transverse joints follow the provisions of Section 501.3(i)1 and this guidance applies specifically to the base‑course portion of a pavement (Section 301). The construction‑joint procedure described in Section 519.3 is intended for rigid concrete pavements where a full‑ or partial‑depth patch must be placed next to an existing longitudinal joint repair, and when a full‑depth repair is required directly adjacent to that joint; in such situations the patch is installed before the longitudinal repair and any full‑depth repair adjoining the joint is combined with the longitudinal repair along the joint segment (Section 519). According to Section 501‑3(g), if a concreting operation is interrupted for more than 30 minutes the specification requires forming a transverse joint with a bulkhead rather than using a construction joint, and when the interruption leaves insufficient concrete to create a slab at least ten feet long the excess mix must be removed and an alternative joint arrangement employed. Section 548‑3 states that when existing pavement exhibits joint faulting greater than 1/2 inch a scratch course or milling shall be used instead of a construction joint; likewise, if a crack is found at or within 1.5 feet of a planned joint location the sawing for a construction joint shall be omitted and the condition addressed per Section 590, and panel layouts whose longer dimension exceeds eighteen times the overlay thickness or whose length‑to‑width ratio exceeds 1.5 are outside the limits for conventional construction‑joint panels, requiring an alternative joint configuration. In Section 516‑3, when a patch replaces an existing expansion joint while the adjacent lane’s expansion joint remains in place the specification requires installing 3/4‑inch expansion joint material instead of a construction joint, and if any crack or spall occurs at any location before final inspection the pavement must be removed and replaced rather than repaired with a construction joint. Section 525‑3 specifies that when delamination runs through an existing pavement joint the repair shall be performed by maintaining the joint as two separate minimum repair areas unless the QC Plan provides a detailed procedure for reestablishing and protecting the joint, making a conventional construction joint unsuitable; also, if removal of unsound concrete or damage to dowel bars would cause a partial‑depth repair to become a full‑depth patch, a full‑depth concrete pavement patch shall be constructed. Section 360‑3 allows that when ATPBC paving is stopped for more than a 30 minute interruption or at the end of each day’s work the contractor may saw construction joints as an acceptable alternative to installing a temporary vertical bulkhead. Section 301‑3 indicates that where longitudinal joints are needed merely for construction convenience a tied joint shall be formed rather than a conventional saw‑cut construction joint, and when work is performed in full‑width sections the sawing step may be omitted entirely, so an alternative joint design supersedes the use of a construction joint. Design inputs and requirements

For full‑depth precast/prestressed concrete pavement slabs (Section 529), the construction‑joint design must be based on a site survey that establishes the elevations of the existing abutting pavement and the layout of proposed longitudinal joints, and it also requires identification of all core locations to be taken from the existing pavement. The installation plan must include traffic information such as delivery‑truck routes and protection measures for traffic during slab placement. Material data must include the concrete mix design, which shall account for ambient average daily temperatures and exposure conditions at the project site, and must meet a minimum compressive strength of 4,925 psi and a flexural strength of 650 psi. Environmental input is limited to the ambient temperature and exposure conditions used for thermal‑expansion calculations, while geotechnical information calls for removal of unsuitable subbase material and placement of compacted No. 2A coarse aggregate within a tolerance of plus or minus 1/2 inch of grade.

For bonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 545), site and environmental conditions require that concrete temperature be between 65°F and 90°F at placement, the surface temperature be less than 120°F before placing, and protection against rapid heat loss be provided when forecast air temperature is 45°F or less using insulating blankets that must be removed once temperature exceeds 45°F or compressive strength surpasses 3,600 psi; the rate of temperature change may not exceed 40°F within any 1‑hour period. Existing‑condition information includes accurate marking of all existing transverse and longitudinal joints before paving, removal of exposed steel plates and loose reinforcement, scarifying to the depth indicated in Section 591.3, and installing joint backing or filling material within ±1/4 inch of the pavement surface that will not be dislodged during placement. Material requirements specify using a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier, shot/blast or water‑blast equipment for cleaning, applying curing compound at a minimum of 1 gallon (±0.1 gallon) per 125 square feet, and providing joint saw cuts equal to the full overlay thickness plus ½ inch while maintaining a cut width at least as wide as the underlying joint. Traffic considerations dictate that opening to traffic is permitted only when concrete reaches a compressive strength of 3,600 psi and bond‑strength testing shows an average of at least 175 psi with each individual test not less than 160 psi, and edge slump limits are set at 1/4 inch next to the shoulder and 1/8 inch next to an adjacent lane. Construction joints for rigid pavements are governed by a series of sections within the Rigid Pavements specifications, each imposing distinct standards and constraints according to pavement type and construction method. Under Section 501.3(i) for plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements, if more than a 30‑minute interruption occurs in concreting operations a transverse joint shall be constructed using a bulkhead; dowels shall be placed in those bulkheads by drilling or by inserting them into fiberglass dowel sleeves; a transverse joint shall not be located within 10 feet of another transverse joint; and when the concrete on hand cannot produce a slab at least 10 feet long at the time of interruption the excess concrete must be removed back to the preceding joint and disposed of. Section 501.3 also requires that finished vertical differential across any joint shall not exceed 1/4 inch and that traffic shall not be allowed on the slab until the grout has reached a minimum compressive strength of 2,500 psi. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs Section 529.3 mandates that the width of any transverse joint between the slabs shall not exceed 1/2 inch and shall be at least 1/4 inch along its entire length; any transverse joint that is less than 1/4 inch at any point must be sawcut to obtain a minimum of 1/4 inch. The width of longitudinal joints shall not exceed 3/4 inch, the finished vertical differential across any joint shall not exceed 1/4 inch, and traffic shall not be permitted until grout has attained a compressive strength of 2,500 psi. Unbonded concrete overlays are covered by Section 548.3 which limits panel aspect ratios to not exceed 1.5 and requires maximum panel dimensions to be no greater than eighteen times the overlay thickness; joint spacing shall follow the project plans or a layout plan accepted by the Department when none are provided; saw cuts that create joints shall be at least D/3 within twelve inches of pavement edges and at least D/4 elsewhere, with a minimum width of 1/4 inch. Concrete pavement patching is addressed in Section 516.3 which references the design, mix and form criteria of Section 501.3 and requires a quality‑control plan prepared in accordance with Section 106.03(a)2.a; full‑depth saw cuts shall be made for all joint edges and may not exceed 1/2 inch in width or be less than full depth when opened to traffic; transverse cuts may extend into adjacent pavement up to full depth plus two inches provided dowel bars are not damaged; joint preparation calls for three‑quarter‑inch expansion‑joint material when replacing an existing joint, a one‑quarter‑inch polystyrene board bond breaker in longitudinal joints of Type A and B patches, and dowel bar holes no larger than 1/8 inch over the bar diameter with anchoring material that reaches initial set within five minutes; traffic may be allowed only after cylinders show a minimum compressive strength of 1,000 psi measured according to PTM No. 604 and un‑patched areas must not remain open more than forty‑eight hours. Roller‑compacted concrete pavements are governed by Section 502.3(a) which defines a fresh joint as a vertical joint between lanes or a horizontal joint between lifts where the adjacent RCC material is placed within sixty minutes of placing the previous lane or lift, although the Representative may adjust this limit based on retarders or weather; cold joints shall be prepared by cutting the full depth at least six inches from the exposed edge with an approved saw or cutter, cleaning and moistening the face before placement; control joints are required to be saw‑cut to one‑quarter the thickness of the compacted RCC as shown on the plans; any sealing of joints shall follow the provisions of Section 501.3(n). Bonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements are covered by Section 545.3 which requires concrete placement when temperature is between 65 °F and 90 °F, installation of joint backing or other approved filling material to within ±¼ inch of the existing pavement surface before overlay placement, bond‑strength testing at seven days in accordance with ASTM C1583 with the average of three tests not below 175 psi and each individual test at least 160 psi, saw cuts defining the joint extending through the full overlay thickness plus one‑half inch without damaging dowel load‑transfer devices, edge slump not exceeding ¼ inch in the outer six inches next to a shoulder and ⅛ inch next to an adjacent lane, and opening to traffic only after concrete attains a compressive strength of at least 3,600 psi and satisfies the minimum bond‑strength criteria. Bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavements are governed by Section 540.3(i) which mandates that joint spacing follow the project plans or an approved layout, longitudinal joints shall be avoided in wheel paths, panel dimensions shall not exceed eighteen times the overlay thickness with a length‑to‑width ratio not exceeding 1.5, only saw cutting is permitted using early‑entry saws or FHWA HIPERPAV software to determine the sawing window, joints shall be cut to a depth of one‑third the slab thickness and at least 1/8 inch wide as soon as the concrete has hardened enough to prevent excessive raveling, and cuts may be omitted if a crack lies within 1.5 feet of the joint location. Partial‑depth repairs are addressed in Section 525.3 which requires a quality‑control plan prepared and approved in accordance with Section 106.03(a)2; transverse joints shall receive ½ inch expansion joint material and longitudinal joints shall receive ¼ inch preformed cellular polystyrene, each extending at least ½ inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible, and the finished joints shall be sealed as directed in Sections 512.3 and 521.3. The overall requirement for a field operation quality‑control plan is set out in Section 501.3 which obligates the contractor to submit a comprehensive plan at least two weeks before paving; the plan must include procedures to ensure proper joint construction, timely sawing and sealing of joints, and performance of a concrete pavement joint wipe test, and it must be approved prior to any paving. Section 516 also specifies that construction joints for patches shall have full‑depth saw cuts at the interface between existing pavement and a new patch, load‑transfer dowels installed in the transverse faces of the existing pavement, a sealant reservoir constructed and the joint sealed; new‑pavement joints require the same sealant reservoir together with a load‑transfer unit before sealing. Section 518 requires that a construction joint at a full‑depth concrete patch be formed by partial‑depth saw cuts at the existing pavement/patch interface, a sealant reservoir provided and the joint sealed, and governs allowable patch lengths: patches using tied splices may be between 6 feet and 20 feet long, those using welded splices between 4 feet and 20 feet long, longer patches limited to 20.1 feet to 65 feet (Type B) or 65.1 feet to 500 feet (Type C). Design methods and criteria

In slip‑form paving, a construction joint is required whenever concreting is halted for more than a 30‑minute interruption; the joint shall be formed as a transverse bulkhead and shall be spaced at least 10 feet from any other transverse joint, and each side of the joint must contain enough concrete to provide a slab that is at least 10 feet long—if that length cannot be achieved, the excess concrete shall be removed back to the preceding joint. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs, the construction joint shall include a transverse gap that does not exceed ½ inch and shall be at least ¼ inch along its entire length; longitudinal joints may not exceed ¾ inch, and the finished vertical differential across any joint shall not exceed ¼ inch, although temporary differentials up to ½ inch are permitted provided the slab is later diamond‑ground to meet the ¼‑inch tolerance. All longitudinal and transverse joints shall be sealed as specified in Section 501.3(n), and the load‑transfer method at transverse joints shall follow the accepted PCPS system or the fabricator’s shop drawings. In unbonded concrete overlays, panel dimensions shall not exceed eighteen times the overlay thickness and the ratio of the longer to the shorter dimension shall not exceed 1.5, with an aspect ratio as close to 1.0 as feasible; saw cuts defining the joints shall be at least D/3 deep within 12 inches of the slab edges and at least D/4 deep elsewhere, and joint spacing shall follow the project plans or, if none are provided, a department‑approved layout that avoids longitudinal joints in wheel paths. For concrete pavement patching, the construction joint shall be cut full depth with saw cuts no greater than ½ inch in width; for corner repairs the cut shall be only 2 inches deep and extend at least 2 inches beyond the damaged area, a temporary rigid separator may not exceed 1/8 inch thickness, and expansion‑joint material must be ¾ inch thick adjacent to an existing expansion joint (or ½ inch for corner repairs). Dowel bars placed in the joint shall receive bond‑breaker lubricant applied at least one hour before concrete placement, anchoring material shall set within five minutes, and acceptance of dowel anchorage shall be limited to voids around the perimeter of less than 0.50 inch on any core sample; the finished joint surface may not exceed a high point or depression of 3/16 inch when measured with a 12‑foot straightedge, and traffic may be opened only after the concrete attains a minimum compressive strength of 1,000 psi as verified by cylinder testing. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements, a fresh joint is defined as a vertical joint between lanes or a horizontal joint between lifts where the adjacent RCC material is placed within 60 minutes of the previous lane or lift, although the Representative may adjust this limit based on retarders or weather; if the interval exceeds 60 minutes the joint shall be treated as a cold joint and prepared by cutting the vertical face at least 6 inches from the exposed edge, cleaning loose material and moistening the surface before placement, while cold horizontal joints require a moist surface and may need cement slurry or mortar grout of the same RCC mix. Control joints specified on the plans shall be saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth of the compacted pavement no later than 18 hours after placement, and any sealing required shall follow Section 501.3(n). For bonded concrete overlays, joint backing or other approved filling material shall be installed to within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface before placement and must remain secure during paving; transverse joints shall be saw‑cut to a depth equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch (without damaging dowel load‑transfer devices) and the cut width shall be at least as wide as the underlying joint or crack, after which a sealant reservoir is formed, flushed, backfilled and sealed. Bond‑strength testing in accordance with ASTM C1583 shall show an average of three 7‑day tests of at least 175 psi and each individual test not less than 160 psi. Concrete temperature shall be between 65°F and 90°F and surface temperature prior to placement shall be below 120°F; edge slump is limited to ¼ inch in the outer 6 inches next to the shoulder and ⅛ inch next to an adjacent lane, and opening to traffic is permitted only when compressive strength reaches a minimum of 3,600 psi and the bond‑strength criteria are satisfied. In bonded overlays on asphalt‑surfaced pavements, joint layout shall follow the spacing shown on the plans or, if none is indicated, a department‑approved layout; panel dimensions shall not exceed eighteen times the overlay thickness and the longer‑to‑shorter side ratio shall not exceed 1.5, with longitudinal joints avoided in wheel paths. Sawing is the only acceptable technique for creating overlay joints, and joints shall be cut as soon as the concrete has hardened enough to prevent excessive raveling using a green‑cut saw to a depth of D/3 with a minimum width of ⅛ inch; early‑entry saws are recommended and continuous day‑or‑night sawing may be used regardless of weather conditions. For partial‑depth repairs, each repair area shall be rectangular with minimum dimensions of 15 inches in length by 10 inches in width by 2 inches in depth, and any two areas spaced less than 24 inches apart shall be combined into a single repair zone; when the repair abuts an existing transverse joint, ½‑inch expansion‑joint material shall be installed in the joint extending at least ½ inch below the bottom of the repair, and when it abuts an existing longitudinal joint, ¼‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene shall be installed with the same extension. If the repair material can be sawed without damage, the joint shall be re‑established by sawing through the full depth of the repair and then sealing longitudinal joints as specified in Section 512.3 and transverse joints as specified in Section 521.3. In asphalt‑treated permeable base courses, longitudinal construction joints shall be formed by overlapping the ATPBC over the edge of the previously placed lane by 1 inch to 2 inches and maintaining a uniform uncompacted depth that yields a smooth joint after compaction; transverse construction joints are required at the end of each day’s work or whenever an interruption exceeds 30 minutes, and shall be created as a full‑depth, full‑width vertical bulkhead (or may be sawed). The same longitudinal overlap rule applies to pervious asphalt pavement systems, where transverse joints must also extend the full depth and width of the paving course; any surface irregularity greater than 3/8 inch shall be corrected immediately and any depth deficiency of ½ inch or more shall be removed and replaced. Construction‑joint faces shall be cleaned to at least a 1½‑inch depth with all existing sealing material removed; air‑blasting shall use a compressed‑air stream of at least 100 psi measured at the source and must be free of oil, moisture or other contaminants. A backer rod whose top is one to two times the joint width plus ¼ inch below the pavement surface shall be placed using a rod 25–35 % larger in diameter than the opening to achieve a recessed sealant depth‑to‑width ratio of 1:1 to 2:1, and sealant may be placed only when the joint is clean and dry and ambient air and pavement temperatures are 40°F or greater, with the joint reservoir filled to a level of ⅛ inch to ¼ inch below the surface. In pervious concrete pavement, construction joints shall be built at the specified locations either by tooling into the pervious concrete immediately after final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness or by saw cutting after the concrete has attained sufficient compressive strength to prevent aggregate dislodgement; if saw cut, fines produced shall be removed by vacuum, and if curing is interrupted the polyethylene sheeting and curing must be reinstated within 60 minutes of the initial removal. In plain cement concrete base courses, any longitudinal joint provided for construction convenience shall be a tied joint as shown on the standard cement‑concrete pavement joint drawings, and when such a joint extends across the full width of the base course it may be placed without sawing; no additional design thresholds or safety factors are stipulated for these joints. Long‑Life Concrete Pavement requires a concrete temperature control plan that limits temperatures exceeding 80°F at placement, and a quality‑control plan that mandates plastic air content of 7.0 % ± 1.5 % for each truck lot before incorporation; after each day’s placement when more than 700 square yards are placed, the LTU vertical and horizontal alignment of construction joints shall be verified within 18 hours by an acceptable nondestructive method, checking at least the first five transverse joint locations across the full width and one location per 2 640 linear feet. The plan must also include a method to cure and protect joints during the curing period so that moisture loss from concrete in and adjacent to joints is prevented during and after joint sawing. For full‑depth longitudinal concrete joint repair, a quality‑control plan shall be submitted at least two weeks before repairs begin; after saw cutting, traffic may be permitted on the repaired area for no more than 72 hours only if cuts are not wider than ½ inch, excavated areas may not remain un‑patched for more than 48 hours or over weekends/holidays, and any full‑depth transverse cut extending into adjacent pavement is limited to full depth + 2 inches provided dowel bars are undamaged; subbase irregularities exceeding 1 inch shall be corrected before placement, and adequate equipment, trained personnel and sufficient hauling units shall be provided to maintain continuity of concrete placement. Pressure‑relief joints shall be created by sawing an opening four inches wide that extends through the full depth and across the entire width of the pavement; before any sealing material is placed all existing concrete must be removed and the vertical faces cleaned so that oil, moisture and other contaminants are absent, after which the joint sides shall be coated with a sealant or lubricant adhesive in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations. Structural section and dimensions

The slip‑form paving method for plain or reinforced cement‑concrete pavements (Section 501.3(g)) requires each slab to be at least 10 feet long and prohibits placing a transverse joint within 10 feet of another transverse joint; if an interruption occurs before enough concrete exists to form such a slab, the excess concrete shall be removed back to the preceding joint, and when pavement is removed and replaced because of a defective joint the replacement length between transverse joints shall be at least 10 feet for reinforced cement‑concrete pavements or at least 6 feet for plain cement‑concrete pavements. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs (Section 529.3) the construction joint calls for slab lengths no less than 6 feet and no greater than 15 feet (20 feet if the slab is prestressed), each slab shall be cast 1/4 inch thicker than the nominal design thickness, transverse joint widths shall not exceed 1/2 inch and shall be at least 1/4 inch along their entire length, longitudinal joints shall not exceed 3/4 inch, and the finished vertical differential across any joint shall not exceed 1/4 inch; the subbase surface must be shaped to a firm, even plane within a tolerance of plus or minus 1/2 inch of grade and contour, and bedding variations greater than 1/8 inch over the length of a slab shall be corrected before placement. In unbonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 548.3) the construction joint requires geotextile interlayers to be overlapped by eight inches ± two inches and to extend at least four inches beyond the pavement edge, panel dimensions shall keep an aspect ratio no greater than 1.5 (as close to 1.0 as feasible) and may not exceed eighteen times the overlay thickness, saw cuts for joints shall be at least one‑third of slab depth within twelve inches of the edges and at least one‑quarter of slab depth elsewhere, a minimum joint width of one‑quarter inch shall be provided, and any existing joint faulting greater than one‑half inch must be reduced by a scratch course or milling before overlay placement. Concrete pavement patching (Section 516.3) permits saw cuts that define a construction joint to be no wider than ½ inch and must be full depth, although at the patch limits they may extend transversely into adjacent pavement up to full depth + 2 inches; the longitudinal joint shall have a ¼‑inch wide, 1‑inch deep saw cut together with a ¼‑inch thick full‑depth polystyrene board bond breaker, and expansion joint material placed in the joint shall be ¾ inch thick (½ inch for corner repairs); subbase surface irregularities that would affect the joint must not exceed 1 inch in depth, deeper disturbance shall be removed and replaced, and dowel‑bar holes shall not be larger than 1/8 inch beyond the bar diameter. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502.3(a)(i)) a construction joint requires that a cold vertical joint be cut through the full pavement thickness at a location at least 6 inches from the exposed edge, and any control joint shall be saw‑cut to a depth equal to one‑quarter of the compacted RCC pavement thickness. Bonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements (Section 545.3) specify that transverse or longitudinal saw cuts shall be equal to the full thickness of the overlay plus ½ inch and shall have a width at least as wide as the underlying joint, intermediate transverse joints may not exceed 15 feet in panel length, sealant reservoirs for longitudinal joints shall be ¼ inch wide, and edge slump shall be limited to a maximum of ¼ inch in the outer six inches adjacent to the shoulder and to a maximum of ⅛ inch in the outer six inches next to an adjacent lane. Bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavements (Section 540.3) limit panel dimensions between joints to not exceed eighteen times the concrete overlay thickness and require that the longer‑to‑shorter side ratio shall not exceed 1.5, while each sawed joint shall be cut to a depth of D/3 with a minimum width of ⅛ inch. In asphalt‑treated permeable base courses (Section 360.3(k)) longitudinal construction joints must be placed so the fresh ATPBC overlaps the edge of the previously placed lane by 1 inch to 2 inches and the uncompacted depth adjacent to a compacted lane shall be kept uniform to provide a smooth joint after compaction; transverse construction joints required at the end of each day’s work or when an interruption exceeds a 30‑minute pause shall be full depth and width of the ATPBC, although the contractor may saw the joint instead of using a temporary bulkhead. The pervious asphalt pavement system (Section 420.3(j)) requires longitudinal joints to overlap the fresh paving course over the previously placed lane edge by 1 inch to 2 inches, and transverse joints made at the end of each workday or after a pause must extend through the full depth and width of the pervious paving course; no dimensions are given for base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab, shoulder, edge‑support, or reinforcement. The pervious concrete pavement system (Section 520.3) calls for the construction joint to be made to approximately one‑quarter of the slab thickness and mandates that exterior formed edge surfaces have a radius of not less than ¼ inch; no dimensions are specified for base, subbase, treated‑soil, pavement, shoulder, edge‑support, or reinforcement. The pressure relief joint (Section 511.3) creates a joint opening that is 4 inches wide, extending the full depth of the concrete and spanning the full width of the pavement; no dimensions are provided for base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab, shoulder, edge‑support, or reinforcement. Materials and mixture design

In slip‑form paving operations a transverse construction joint with a bulkhead shall be constructed when an interruption exceeds 30 minutes, and dowels shall be placed in that bulkhead either by drilling or by inserting them into fiberglass dowel sleeves; a transverse joint may not be placed within 10 feet of another transverse joint. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs the contract must require a concrete mix design that is submitted at least 28 days before slab production and shown by trial batch test data to reach a minimum compressive strength of 4,925 psi and a minimum flexural strength of 650 psi, and each precast slab shall be cast 1/4 inch thicker than the nominal design thickness. The joint must also specify grout mix designs for dowel‑slot and bedding grouts that attain a minimum compressive strength of 2,500 psi before traffic is allowed, and joint geometry is limited to a transverse gap not exceeding 1/2 inch and not less than 1/4 inch, with longitudinal gaps not exceeding 3/4 inch. In concrete pavement patches the specification must identify the expansion‑joint filler size—3/4‑inch material when replacing an existing expansion joint and 1/2‑inch material for corner repairs—and the use of coated dowel bars with a bond‑breaker lubricant applied to at least one‑half of the length of Type B dowels (Type A dowels do not require lubricant). The anchoring material used to embed the dowels must be formulated to reach an initial set within 5 minutes and a data sheet for that material must be provided; a temporary rigid separator may not exceed 1/8‑inch thickness, and dowel‑hole diameters may be no more than 1/8‑inch larger than the coated dowel bars. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements the specifications must call for a cement slurry or mortar grout that is made with the same materials and proportions as the RCC pavement mixture when a cold horizontal joint is required, and any joint sealing shall be performed in accordance with Section 501.3(n). In bonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements an approved joint backing or other filling material shall be placed within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface and must remain in place during paving; bond‑strength testing according to ASTM C1583 shall produce an average not less than 175 psi and each individual test not below 160 psi, and a curing compound shall be applied uniformly at a minimum rate of 1 gallon per 125 ft² (±0.1 gallon) on all exposed concrete surfaces. In bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavement the specification must require that any existing cracks wider than three‑quarters of an inch be cleaned and filled with non‑shrink grout or another Department‑approved material capable of providing bond to the new concrete, and the construction joint is created by sawing the hardened slab to a depth equal to one third of the slab thickness and to a minimum width of 1/8 inch. When a partial‑depth repair meets an existing transverse joint the construction joint specification must call for installing a ½‑inch expansion‑joint material in that joint, and when it meets an existing longitudinal joint it must call for installing a ¼‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene; both the expansion‑joint material and the preformed cellular polystyrene should extend at least ½ inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible. Construction joint specifications must also require premolded expansion‑joint filler with a minimum thickness of ½ inch for structures, at least ¾ inch where the pavement adjoins bridges or culverts, and a minimum of ¾ inch against mortar‑filled railway rails (or ¼ inch along each mortar‑filled rail when constructing alongside tracks). For asphalt‑treated permeable base course construction joints must be formed with the same ATPBC material as the surrounding pavement; transverse joints shall extend the full depth and width of the ATPBC, while longitudinal joints shall overlap the adjacent lane edge by 1 inch to 2 inches. In transverse joint cleaning and sealing the construction joint must be prepared by completely removing any existing sealing material, neoprene compression seals, asphalt, or other foreign matter and then cleaning the concrete face to a minimum depth of 1½ inches; if air blasting is used the compressed‑air stream shall be at least 100 psi measured at the source and free of oil, moisture, and contaminants. A backer rod whose diameter is 25 % to 35 % larger than the joint opening shall be placed so that its top lies one to two times the joint width plus ¼ inch below the pavement surface, providing a recessed sealant depth‑to‑width ratio of 1:1 to 2:1, and sealant may only be applied when the joint is clean and dry, ambient air and pavement temperatures are 40 °F or greater, and the joint reservoir is filled to a level 1⁄8 inch to ¼ inch below the surface. For pressure‑relief joints the joint opening must be sawed 4 inches wide through the full depth and full width of the pavement, and after cleaning of oil, moisture, and other contaminants sealing material either a sealant or lubricant adhesive shall be applied in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations. Structural details and interfaces

Construction joints are defined and detailed differently according to the pavement type, construction method, and applicable specifications. In slip‑form paving, when concreting is halted for more than a 30‑minute interruption, a transverse construction joint shall be constructed using a bulkhead and dowel bars shall be placed in the bulkhead by drilling or inserting into fiberglass sleeves; a transverse joint shall not be constructed within 10 feet of another transverse joint, and if there is insufficient concrete to form a slab at least 10 feet long, the concrete shall be removed back to the preceding joint. When load‑transfer units are used, they shall be staked in place in advance of paving operations so that dowel locations can be accurately marked for saw cuts. For full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs, transverse joints shall be between 1/4 inch and no more than 1/2 inch wide along their entire length, while longitudinal joints shall not exceed 3/4 inch. Load‑transfer reinforcement shall be provided by dowel bars and tie bars cast into the slab as shown on the shop drawings; the dowels and bars shall be positioned square to the edge and parallel to the top surface within ±1/8 inch of the indicated locations, and a bond‑breaker coating shall be applied to the dowels. Tie bars shall be installed along longitudinal joints for continuous paving sections greater than 65 feet in length. In an unbonded concrete overlay, dowel bar assemblies shall be secured with anchors that limit vertical or horizontal movement to no more than 1/4 inch and the anchoring plan shall be approved by the Representative; shipping wires shall remain intact unless a magnetic tomography‑based device is used for verification. Existing transverse expansion joints in the underlying pavement shall be identified, precut in the plastic concrete, and two full‑depth saw cuts shall be placed on each side of the precut joint, with the saw cut depth required to be at least D/3 within 12 inches of the edge and at least D/4 elsewhere; after cutting all joints shall be cleaned and sealed. Panel dimensions shall maintain an aspect ratio as close to 1.0 as feasible and not exceed 1.5, with a maximum panel size limited to 18 times the overlay thickness. At the limits of the overlay transitions shall be constructed by placing a geotextile debonding layer that overlaps adjacent sheets by 8 inches ± 2 inches and extends at least 4 inches beyond the pavement edge. In concrete pavement patching, construction joints shall be defined by full‑depth longitudinal and transverse saw cuts; when both lanes are patched simultaneously a Type L joint shall be constructed as shown on the Standard Drawings, whereas for one‑lane work a 1/4‑inch full‑depth polystyrene board bond breaker shall be provided in the longitudinal joint of Type A and B patches (no bond breaker in Type C patches). Tie bars shall be required for corner repairs and all Type C patches, and where an existing expansion joint is replaced a 3/4‑inch expansion joint filler shall be placed in the adjacent joint (1/2‑inch for corner repairs); all constructed longitudinal and transverse joints shall be sealed after placement. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements, a joint formed when the adjacent RCC material is placed within 60 minutes shall be considered fresh and shall simply continue the bond; if the interval exceeds 60 minutes the joint shall be treated as cold, requiring a full‑depth vertical cut at least 6 inches from the exposed edge, removal of excess material, cleaning and moistening of the joint (horizontal cold joints may also require cement slurry or mortar grout). Control joints shall be saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth of the compacted RCC pavement no later than 18 hours after placement, and any joints indicated for sealing shall be sealed as specified in Section 501.3(n). For a bonded concrete overlay on concrete‑surfaced pavements, all existing transverse joints shall be prepared by installing joint backing or an approved filling material to within ±1/4 inch of the pavement surface and any loose or exposed reinforcement—including steel plates—shall be removed; the transverse joints shall then be saw‑cut directly over the existing joints to a depth equal to the full overlay thickness plus ½ inch, with a width at least as wide as the underlying joint and without damaging dowel load‑transfer devices. A sealant reservoir shall be built per the standard drawing, the joint flushed, backing placed and sealed. Intermediate transverse joints shall be added so that each overlay panel does not exceed 15 feet in length and shall be cut to the same full‑thickness‑plus‑½‑inch depth. Longitudinal joints shall be saw‑cut with a ¼‑inch‑wide sealant reservoir to the full depth plus ½ inch and then sealed, while a neat vertical edge face shall be required along both sides of the overlay; edge slump shall be limited to ¼ inch next to the shoulder and ⅛ inch next to an adjacent lane. Transitions at the overlay edges shall be constructed as shown on the plans, and where an existing treated base course or concrete lies beneath the overlay a geotextile debonding layer must be placed. If the new repair can be sawed without damage, the joint should be re‑established by sawing through its full depth and then sealed according to Sections 512.3 (longitudinal) and 521.3 (transverse). In pervious concrete pavement, construction joints must be placed at the locations called for in the plans, either by tooling into the pervious concrete immediately after final finishing to approximately one‑quarter slab thickness using a pervious‑concrete cross‑rolling tool or by saw cutting once the concrete has attained sufficient compressive strength to prevent aggregate dislodgement; when saw cut, any fines generated must be removed by vacuum to avoid clogging, and if curing is interrupted the polyethylene sheet and curing must be reinstated within 60 minutes of removal. In plain or reinforced cement‑concrete pavements (Section 501) a construction‑joint interface is treated as a transverse joint whenever the concreting operation is interrupted for more than 30 minutes; in such cases a bulkhead shall be installed to form a straight full‑depth joint and dowels or fiberglass sleeves are placed, and the joint may not be located within 10 feet of another transverse joint. In roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502) a joint is considered fresh when the next lift or adjacent lane is placed within 60 minutes of the previous placement; in that case the interface shall be kept moist and any loose material removed before the new concrete is laid. Load transfer units are installed adjacent to existing joints as shown on the Standard Drawings and all constructed longitudinal and transverse joints are sealed per Section 501.3(n). For continuously reinforced concrete pavement patches (Section 518) partial‑depth saw cuts are provided at the pavement/patch interface, a sealant reservoir is constructed and the joint is sealed; this work applies to patches that are one course thick and not less than one lane width.

In partial‑depth repairs (Section 525) where the repair meets an existing transverse joint a half‑inch expansion joint material shall be placed in the joint and extended at least one‑half inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible, and where it meets an existing longitudinal joint a quarter‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene shall be installed with the same minimum embedment. If the repair material can be sawed without damage, the joint should be re‑established by sawing through the full depth of the repair, and joints are then sealed in accordance with Section 512.3 for longitudinal joints and Section 521.3 for transverse joints.

Pressure relief joints (Section 511) are created by sawing an opening that is four inches wide and extends through the full depth and across the entire width of the pavement; the existing concrete material is then removed, all vertical faces are cleaned to eliminate oil, moisture and any other contaminants, and finally the joint sides are coated with a sealant or lubricant adhesive as recommended by the manufacturer. Drainage, environment, and accessibility

In full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs the mix design must account for ambient average daily temperatures and exposure conditions at the project site to determine correct panel size and expansion joint locations; blockouts are provided in the slabs for existing utility castings and proposed drainage structures, transverse joints shall be no wider than 1/2 inch and not less than 1/4 inch along their entire length, longitudinal joints shall not exceed 3/4 inch, and all joints are sealed as specified to address moisture and environmental exposure. For construction joints in an unbonded concrete overlay the geotextile interlayer must be terminated in a drainable layer or at a daylighted location so that free drainage is not impaired; the fabric is kept clean, dampened but not saturated and its surface temperature may not exceed 120°F before placement. Curing materials are applied after finishing and texturing within 30 minutes of bleed‑water dissipation, and adequate insulating blankets are provided to prevent rapid heat loss when air temperature is 40°F or less, with removal only after the temperature exceeds 40°F or concrete strength surpasses 3,600 psi and surface temperature change does not exceed 40°F in any one‑hour period; asphalt interlayer surfaces also must be kept below 120°F using misting, white‑pigmented curing compound, lime water or other approved methods. In concrete pavement patching the construction joint design must provide a means to drain rainwater by excavating an outlet through the shoulder at the lowest point of any open patch area when it rains, and cooling water, slurry and dust from the saw‑cutting operation must be kept out of lanes opened to traffic; appropriate curing compounds may be used to protect the concrete from moisture during placement. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements joints are required to remain moist to avoid excess moisture loss; vertical cold joint faces are moistened immediately before placing the adjacent lane, horizontal cold joints are kept moist and may receive cement slurry or mortar grout as needed, and the placement pattern is arranged so that curing water from earlier lifts does not cause erosion or runoff on the fresh joint or subbase. Joints are considered fresh only when the next lift is placed within 60 minutes, a limit that may be adjusted based on ambient temperature, wind and humidity; if the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 lb/ft²·hr, mitigation such as windbreaks or fog misting must be employed before joint work proceeds. In bonded concrete overlays of concrete‑surfaced pavements the contractor shall provide measures acceptable to the Department to drain or remove standing water from the prepared surface and must completely clean the surface before overlay placement; concrete may be placed only when its temperature is between 65°F and 90°F, and the existing pavement surface must be kept below 120°F immediately prior to placement, with fog‑misting, protective covers or blankets used as needed. When forecast air temperature is 45°F or less, adequate insulating blankets shall be applied to prevent rapid heat loss and may be removed only when air temperature exceeds 45°F or average compressive strength exceeds 3,600 psi, provided surface temperature change does not exceed 40°F within any one‑hour period. For bonded concrete overlays of asphalt‑surfaced pavements construction joints must be placed on a dry substrate; after milling any standing water must be drained and no moisture may remain before concrete placement, and the asphalt surface must be less than 120°F using fog‑misting or protective covers if required. Joint sawing can be performed day or night regardless of weather once the slab has hardened enough to avoid raveling, and during curing insulating blankets are required when air temperature is 45°F or less and may only be removed after temperature exceeds 45°F or concrete reaches at least 3,000 psi compressive strength, ensuring surface temperature does not change more than 40°F in any one‑hour period. In pervious concrete pavement systems construction joints shall be cut or tool‑rolled to about one‑quarter slab thickness either immediately after final finishing or after the concrete has achieved sufficient compressive strength; saw‑cut fines must be removed by vacuum to keep the joint from clogging, and if curing is interrupted the polyethylene curing sheet must be reinstated within 60 minutes. Placement shall not occur when air temperature is projected below 40°F or expected to drop lower at any time during the first 24 hours after placement, and rain forecast before final finishing and curing also prohibits placement; should rain begin, production must be stopped, a construction joint formed, and the polyethylene sheet applied. Finally, for long‑life concrete pavement the construction plan must include a concrete temperature control plan that specifies actions when concrete temperatures exceed 80°F at placement to address climate effects, and the quality‑control plan must contain a method to cure and protect joints during the curing period so that moisture loss from concrete in and adjacent to the joint is prevented both during and after joint sawing. No specific drainage requirement is provided for this pavement type. [1]

Verification and expected performance

After placement of full‑depth precast or prestressed concrete pavement slabs the construction joint must be inspected to confirm that the transverse joint width does not exceed 1/2 inch and is a minimum of 1/4 inch along its entire length; any portion less than 1/4 inch must be sawcut to obtain a minimum of 1/4 inch. The finished vertical differential across the joint shall not exceed 1/4 inch, and if it falls between 1/4 inch and 1/2 inch the slab may remain in place and be subject to diamond grinding before traffic opening. All dowel and tie‑bar slots must be grouted and the grout must reach a minimum compressive strength of 2,500 psi before traffic is allowed, and all longitudinal and transverse joints shall be sealed as specified. For an unbonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements each saw cut must be verified to have a depth of at least D/3 within 12 inches of the pavement edges and at least D/4 elsewhere, with a minimum width of 1/4 inch; deleterious material must be removed before sealant installation. If a crack is found at or within 1.5 feet of a joint location sawing shall be omitted and the condition addressed per Section 590, and concrete panels that show cracking outside of the sawed joints are considered defective. The joint layout must also be checked against the approved plan to ensure proper spacing, a panel aspect ratio not exceeding 1.5, and a maximum panel dimension no greater than eighteen times the overlay thickness, and joint faces must be free of curing compound or properly cleaned before sealant installation. For concrete pavement patching the completed construction joint is verified through the quality‑control plan that requires a dowel‑bar anchoring check, surface‑tolerance inspection with a 12‑foot straightedge, sealing verification, and strength testing before traffic; on the first day of dowel‑bar anchoring the Representative randomly selects three locations and drills a 4‑inch core, and the joint is accepted only when voids around each dowel are less than 0.50 inch. Any high points or depressions of 3/16 inch or more must be ground smooth, all longitudinal and transverse joints must be sealed as specified, and pavement may be opened to traffic only after cylinders show a minimum compressive strength of 1,000 psi. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements the completed construction joint is verified on site by the Representative in accordance with Section 502.3(i); fresh vertical or horizontal joints must have been placed within 60 minutes of the adjacent lane or lift, while cold joints must be prepared by cutting the full depth at least 6 inches from the exposed edge, removing loose material, cleaning the face and moistening it immediately before the next placement (or may use a drop extension or edging shoe up to 15° from vertical). Control joints are required to be saw‑cut to one‑quarter depth of the compacted RCC pavement as soon as possible but no later than 18 hours after placement, without causing raveling, and any joint that is specified to be sealed must follow the sealing requirements of Section 501.3(n). For a bonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements backing material must be installed within ±1/4 inch of the existing pavement surface before the overlay is placed and must remain secure during paving. After curing a 7‑day bond strength test per ASTM C1583 shall be performed on five random locations per lot, and compliance requires an average of at least 175 psi with each individual result not less than 160 psi. The transverse joint is then saw‑cut to a depth equal to the full overlay thickness plus 1/2 inch, with a width at least as wide as the underlying joint, flushed of slurry, and sealed according to Section 501.3(n); pavement may be opened to traffic only when compressive strength reaches 3,600 psi and the bond strength meets the minimum acceptable value defined in Section 545.3(e). For a bonded concrete overlay of asphalt‑surfaced pavement the completed construction joint must be verified against the joint layout approved by the Department—either the spacing shown on the plans or a department‑approved layout when none is indicated—and the resulting panels must not exceed 18 times the concrete overlay thickness nor have an aspect ratio greater than 1.5. Saw cuts must be made with a green cut saw after the concrete has hardened enough to avoid excessive raveling, cutting to a depth of D/3 and a minimum width of 1/8 inch; any joint where a crack occurs within 1.5 feet must be addressed as specified in Section 590 rather than sawn, and after sawing joints must be cleaned of all deleterious material and left unsheathed, with sealing not permitted. For plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements compliance, constructability, performance, and consistency of a completed construction joint are verified through the field‑operation quality‑control plan that must be submitted at least two weeks before paving begins and accepted prior to any placement; the plan must detail procedures to ensure proper joint construction, timely sawing and proper sealing of joints, and to conduct a concrete pavement joint wipe test, all of which are carried out on site and observed by the Representative to confirm that the joint meets the project specifications. For asphalt‑treated permeable base course the completed construction joint is checked by confirming longitudinal joints overlap the edge of the previously placed lane by 1 inch to 2 inches, and transverse joints installed at the end of each day’s work or after any interruption longer than thirty minutes are formed with a temporary vertical bulkhead (or may be sawed) and extend the full depth and width of the ATPBC. The Representative then inspects surface tolerance at the joint and adjacent paving notches using a ten‑foot straightedge, moving it no more than five feet between stages, and corrects any irregularity greater than 1/2 inch by loosening or adding material without grinding. Depth compliance is verified by drilling a six‑inch diameter test hole to the full depth of the ATPBC for each three thousand square yards (or less) of completed base course, with additional holes required where deficiencies are suspected, and sections deficient by 1/2 inch or more are removed and replaced. For pervious asphalt pavement systems the completed transverse construction joint is checked for compliance by first performing surface‑tolerance testing across the joint with a 12‑foot straightedge; any irregularity greater than 3/8 inch must be corrected immediately, and defects that remain after final compaction require removal and replacement. Depth near the joint is then confirmed by drilling one 6‑inch diameter test hole for each 3,000 square yards of completed course and ensuring the measured depth is within 1/2 inch of the specified level; sections short by 1/2 inch or more are removed and replaced. Infiltration performance is assessed on three selected areas per 10,000 sq ft using ASTM C‑1701, and any portion that does not achieve an average infiltration rate of at least 200 inches per hour must be removed and replaced, thereby satisfying the constructability, performance, and project requirements set out in Section 420.3(j). For long‑life concrete pavement after each day’s placement exceeding 700 SY the contractor must verify vertical and horizontal alignment of the construction joint within 18 hours using a nondestructive method acceptable to the Representative; verification must include at least the first five transverse joint locations across the full pavement width and one joint per 2,640 LF of pavement placed. The quality‑control plan also requires a concrete temperature control plan, sampling of the first truck for plastic air content that must be 7.0 % ± 1.5 %, and a method to cure and protect joints during curing to prevent moisture loss, thereby ensuring compliance, constructability, performance, and consistency with the project requirements.
