Drainage Structure

Part of: Subsurface Drainage is-subtopic-of
Drainage Structure is a topic under Subsurface Drainage.
drainage-structuresdrainage-structure
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-29

Drainage structures are engineered components installed in roadway pavements and related foundations to convey water away, thereby preventing moisture‑related damage that could compromise structural integrity. They achieve unrestricted positive gravity flow by placing pipe underdrains or pavement‑base drains in trenches extending into the subgrade, using fittings with limited deflection angles, sealed joints, geotextile protection, and properly compacted fine‑aggregate backfill; when flowable backfill is used, solid outlet pipes or thin pre‑formed drains protected by a Class 4 Type A geotextile are required. The design also calls for smooth, uniformly firm trench bottoms, adequate bedding, and protective endwalls to maintain alignment, resist intrusion, and ensure long‑term durability. [2]

Purpose and design objectives

Drainage structures are intended to prevent water‑related problems that could compromise the structural integrity of pavements and foundations by ensuring that water can exit the system effectively. In flowable backfill applications, they keep foundation drains free of intrusion and contamination from the backfill material, which is achieved by locating base drain outlets away from areas where flowable backfill will be placed, furnishing solid outlet pipes in intersecting zones, installing a preformed drain no more than 2 inches thick between the wall and the fill with outlets spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall, and protecting the drains with a Class 4, Type A geotextile membrane. For subsurface drain outlets, the design must guarantee positive gravity flow so that moisture is removed from beneath the pavement; this requires a uniformly firm, smooth trench bottom, proper backfilling and compaction, and protective endwalls as shown on the standard drawings to maintain outlet functionality and resist deterioration. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–189), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 526)

The drainage structure must provide unrestricted positive flow; this is achieved by using splice connectors that do not restrict flow, selecting fittings whose deflection angles are 45 degrees or less, and sealing pipe ends so that foreign material cannot enter, while all joints and connections shall preclude soil intrusion (Section 610). Structural integrity for the pipe‑underdrain and pavement‑base drain is obtained by installing the pipe in a trench that does not disturb the subbase, backfilling with fine‑aggregate Type A in hydraulically compacted lifts whose depth does not exceed 9 inches, spraying each lift with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot, and providing a minimum of 6 inches of Class 1 geotextile overlap between sections (Section 610). Durability is further supported by protecting joints during construction, applying galvanized‑wire‑mesh shields to outlet pipes, and storing all drainage material under protective covering until installation (Section 610). Safety and serviceability requirements for this portion dictate that trenches shall not be left open during periods of precipitation or overnight, shall not remain open greater than 2 inches in depth overnight, and must be barricaded at all times in accordance with the applicable code (Section 610). When flowable backfill is used, the structure must be shielded from intrusion and contamination by installing a Class 4, Type A geotextile membrane around foundation drains and pipe bedding, and by placing a pre‑formed drain no more than 2 inches thick between the wall and the fill with outlet openings spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall (Section 220). Forms that retain lateral fluid pressure from unhardened flowable backfill shall be designed for a minimum factor of safety of 1.3, and backfill lifts shall not exceed 4 feet unless approved by the Representative (Section 220). The pipe bedding must provide the required support from trench bottom to the pipe, prevent floating through the use of lifts or ballast, and maintain proper alignment as monitored per Section 601.3(c) (Section 220). For subsurface drain outlets, positive gravity flow is ensured by excavating a uniformly firm and smooth trench bottom and laying the pipe on at least two inches of satisfactorily tamped embankment material; pipe connections are limited to a maximum 45‑degree bend and must be joined with appropriate couplings (Section 615). Backfill shall be placed in four‑inch layers of satisfactory material, each layer thoroughly tamped and compacted, especially under pavement or shoulder as specified, and outlet openings shall be protected in accordance with the standard drawings (Section 615). Durability is addressed by shielding plastic pipe ends from sunlight deterioration using suitable endwalls and backfill. Safety is provided by installing a red subsurface drain outlet marker facing approaching traffic and embedding it at least eighteen inches deep, thereby satisfying visibility requirements for serviceability. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–189), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

Scope and applicability

The drainage‑structure design described in Section 610 is intended for roadway pavements where either a pipe underdrain or a pavement‑base drain is required. A pipe underdrain is installed after fine grading has been completed, whereas a pavement‑base drain is placed after the adjacent roadway pavement has been constructed except when it is located beneath curb sections or other special locations. The design also applies whenever the work falls within a Class 3 excavation, which includes excavation for new grade separation and drainage structures, and it may be used for a Class 4 excavation when the depth of the excavation exceeds the standard depth for pavement‑base drains, pipe underdrains, subsurface drain outlets, or subgrade drains. For these applications the trench is excavated adjacent to the pavement edge without disturbing the subbase and extended at least 2 inches into the subgrade. All base‑drain work, and pipe underdrains when indicated, shall use Class 1 geotextile as specified in Section 212.3(b). The backfill shall consist of fine aggregate Type A placed in lifts not exceeding 9 inches, each lift being hydraulically compacted and water‑sprayed. All fittings must have deflection angles of 45 degrees or less, perforated pipe is to be installed with the perforations facing downward, and connections are required to prevent soil intrusion and to maintain continuous gravity flow. In addition, drainage structures are specified for Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach (GSBA) projects. For both new bridge construction and retrofits, subsurface drainage systems are provided at the abutment and at the far end of the GSBA fill as shown on the Standard Drawing. The foundation drain in these cases is a 6‑inch‑diameter pipe; for new bridge structures the foundation drain at the abutment is incorporated in the lump‑sum bridge cost, while for an existing‑structure retrofit the drains are measured and paid by linear foot from pipe outlet to pipe outlet, including excavation, drainage pipe, coarse‑aggregate backfill, and geotextile (Class 4 Type A separation layer). [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 146–204), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–522)

Design inputs and requirements

Under the incidental construction specifications in Section 610.3, pipe underdrains shall be placed after fine grading is completed and pavement base drains only after the adjacent roadway pavement has been constructed; trench excavation must be performed without disturbing the subbase, all connections shall use fittings with deflection angles of 45 degrees or less, follow manufacturer‑approved joint details and be sealed to prevent foreign material entry, perforated pipe perforations shall face down and semicircular pipe shall have its smooth circular portion at the bottom. Class 1 geotextile specified in Section 212.3(b) is required for all base drain work, prefabricated drains shall be installed in trenches of the width shown on the Standard Drawings and a depth extending at least 2 inches into the subgrade, and the trench shall be backfilled with fine aggregate Type A using 9‑inch (maximum) hydraulically compacted lifts sprayed with water at 1.0 gallon per linear foot; trenches greater than 2 inches in depth may not be left open overnight and barricading must comply with 67 PA Code Chapter 203, while outlet piping and fittings follow Section 615.3 and shall be installed within 24 hours of trenching to provide positive drainage. The excavation required for new grade‑separation and drainage structures is classified as Class 3 Excavation under Section 204, whereas pipe culverts, underdrains, subsurface drain outlets and related utilities are treated as Class 4 Excavation. When flowable backfill is used, base drain outlets shall not intersect areas where the backfill is placed and solid base‑drain outlet pipes must be furnished in any area that does intersect the backfill; a preformed drain may be installed between a wall and the flowable fill only if it is no more than 2 inches thick and its outlets are spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall unless the Representative directs otherwise, and foundation drains shall be protected from intrusion and contamination by using a Class 4, Type A geotextile membrane. Section 615.3 requires that the trench bottom be uniformly firm and smooth to provide positive gravity flow, pipe shall be laid on a 2‑inch layer of satisfactorily tamped embankment material and joined with connecting bands or split couplings, backfill shall be placed in 4‑inch layers of satisfactory embankment material with each layer thoroughly tamped, and the portion beneath pavement or shoulder shall be compacted as required by Section 601.3(f); outlet openings are protected per the Standard Drawings, connections to underdrains or base drains shall use suitable couplings with a maximum 45‑degree bend (or series of such bends), concrete endwalls shall be provided when outlets are not tied into existing drainage, plastic pipe ends shall be shielded from sunlight with satisfactory endwalls and backfill, and each subsurface drain outlet shall be marked with a red marker embedded at least 18 inches deep. In the geosynthetic stabilized bridge approach, the foundation drain is defined as a 6‑inch diameter pipe installed as part of the abutment foundation; for new bridge structures the foundation drain is incorporated in the lump‑sum bridge cost, while for an existing GSBA retrofit the drains are measured and paid on a linear‑foot basis from pipe outlet to pipe outlet and include all excavation, drainage pipe, coarse‑aggregate backfill and geotextile. The bridge structure plan and the Standard Drawings govern drainage in the GSBA; for new bridges the foundation drain shall be constructed exactly as indicated in the bridge structure plan and no separate GSBA foundation drain may be installed, and at completion of the fill a subsurface drainage system shall be provided at both the abutment and the far end of the GSBA fill as shown on the Standard Drawing, which constitute the project constraints that dictate location, type and detailing of all drainage structures associated with the approach. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 146–204), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

Design methods and criteria

According to Section 610—Pipe Underd rain and Pavement Base Drain, the construction procedure requires that pipe underdrain be placed only after fine grading is finished, while pavement base drain is installed after the adjacent roadway pavement unless it is a curb or special location, and the trench must be excavated without disturbing the subbase. Connections to new or existing drains shall use fittings whose deflection angles do not exceed 45 degrees and pipe joints must follow the manufacturer’s recommendations, with perforations oriented downward for perforated pipe; the upgrade end of each pipe must be sealed to keep foreign material out. For prefabricated pavement base drains the contractor must submit the installation method at least two weeks in advance, excavate a trench adjacent to the pavement edge that extends at least 2 inches into the subgrade, place the drain vertically, and backfill with fine‑aggregate Type A using hydraulically compacted lifts no greater than 9 inches, spraying each lift with water at one gallon per linear foot while keeping any open trench no deeper than 2 inches overnight. All splice connectors must be fitted so they do not restrict flow, a minimum of six inches of Class 1 geotextile overlap is required between sections, and outlet piping for positive gravity flow must be installed within twenty‑four hours of beginning trenching to ensure continuous drainage. In Section 220—Flowable Backfill the contractor must design formwork to resist lateral fluid pressure equal to the total weight of the unhardened backfill with a minimum factor of safety of 1.3; a pre‑formed drain no thicker than 2 inches is placed between the wall and the fill and its outlets are spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall; foundation drains must be protected from intrusion by a Class 4, Type A geotextile separation membrane, and pipe bedding must meet the minimum depth shown in the standard drawings. Section 615—Subsurface Drain Outlets specifies that excavation of the trench shall produce a uniformly firm, smooth bottom to ensure positive gravity flow at the outlet. The pipe is laid on a 2‑inch layer of satisfactorily tamped embankment material and joined with connecting bands or split couplings; after acceptance of the outlet pipe, backfill proceeds in 4‑inch layers of satisfactory embankment material with thorough tamping, and the portion under pavement or shoulder is compacted as required by Section 601.3(f). The outlet opening is protected as shown on the Standard Drawings, connections to an underdrain or pavement base drain are made with suitable couplings using a maximum 45‑degree bend, concrete endwalls may be constructed when not tied to existing structures, plastic pipe ends are shielded from sunlight, and a red subsurface drain outlet marker is embedded a minimum of 18 inches on the traffic‑facing side. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–189), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

In accordance with Section 610‑3 of the incidental construction specifications for pipe underdrains and pavement base drains, drainage structures must be installed so that pipe or base‑drain joints use fittings whose deflection angles do not exceed 45 degrees; the trench that receives a prefabricated pavement base drain shall have a width as shown on the Standard Drawings and its depth shall extend at least 2 inches into the subgrade, while backfill lifts shall be limited to a maximum of 9 inches, each lift being hydraulically compacted and sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot; geotextile seams shall overlap by a minimum of 6 inches to prevent soil intrusion, and open trenches may not exceed a depth of 2 inches overnight. Under Section 220‑3 for flowable backfill, the drainage structure must incorporate solid base‑drain outlet pipes wherever the pipe intersects flowable backfill; when a pre‑formed drain is used it shall be no more than 2 inches thick and its outlets shall be spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall, and any formwork that retains the backfill must be designed to resist lateral fluid pressure equal to the total weight of unhardened flowable backfill with a minimum factor of safety of 1.3. According to Section 615‑3 for subsurface drain outlets, the outlet trench shall be excavated so that its bottom is uniformly firm and smooth to provide positive gravity flow, and the pipe shall rest on a 2‑inch layer of satisfactorily tamped embankment material; after placement the trench shall be backfilled in 4‑inch layers of satisfactory material with thorough tamping, and connections to underdrains or base drains must use suitable couplings and be limited to a maximum 45‑degree bend (or series of such bends); where outlets are not tied into existing structures concrete endwalls shall be provided, plastic pipe ends shall be protected from sunlight, and each outlet shall be marked with a red subsurface drain outlet marker embedded at least 18 inches on the side facing approaching traffic. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–189), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

Structural section and dimensions

For pipe underdrain and pavement base drain the construction specifications set thicknesses by requiring the trench to extend at least 2 inches into the subgrade and by limiting each backfill lift to a maximum of 9‑inch hydraulically compacted layers; layer configuration is defined by placing the pipe after fine grading, installing the base drain after the adjacent roadway pavement, and separating the drainage core with Class 1 geotextile as prescribed in Section 212.3(b); joint spacing follows the manufacturer’s recommendations for pipe joints and splice connectors must be installed so they do not restrict flow, with a minimum of 6‑inch geotextile overlap between sections; transitions are required to be smooth and non‑restricting to flow, and tie‑ins to existing drainage structures must use fittings whose deflection angles are 45 degrees or less and must be sealed to prevent soil intrusion. [1]

[1] — SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–522)

Materials and mixture design

For pipe underdrain and pavement‑base drain installations, the drainage structure must incorporate Class 1 geotextile as required for all pavement‑base drain constructions; when pipe underdrain work is specified it shall employ perforated or semicircular pipe with the perforations facing down unless directed otherwise, and fittings whose deflection angles do not exceed 45 degrees. Backfill material shall be fine aggregate Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts no greater than 9 inches thick, each lift being sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot. Where prefabricated pavement base drains are used, outlet pipes must be provided with galvanized wire‑mesh shields and all joints sealed to prevent soil intrusion. For flowable backfill applications the specification must call for a flowable backfill type that conforms to Table A, with a minimum material temperature of 50 °F (maintained at no less than 50 °F for at least 24 hours) and lift heights not to exceed 4 ft unless approved. A pre‑formed drain no more than 2 in thick shall be provided with outlets spaced at not more than 15 ft through the wall. The mix shall be capable of achieving a slump of at least 3 in for dike construction, and must pass daily slump, air‑content (AASHTO T152/T196) and yield (AASHTO T121/ASTM C136) tests as well as meet the compressive‑strength criteria in Table A verified by PTM No.604 cylinders. A Class 4 Type A geotextile membrane shall be used to separate the backfill from foundation drains, and the contractor may adjust water on‑site to retain flowability. For subsurface drain outlets the pipe must rest on a two‑inch layer of satisfactorily tamped embankment material, with joints made by connecting bands or split couplings. After placement the trench shall be backfilled in four‑inch layers using satisfactory embankment material that is then thoroughly tamped; the portion beneath pavement or shoulder shall be compacted as required elsewhere. Where the outlet is not tied into an existing system, concrete endwalls shall be provided for protection, and if a plastic pipe is used its ends must be shielded from sunlight with satisfactory endwalls and backfill material. For geosynthetic‑stabilized bridge approaches the drainage structure specification calls for a Class 4 Type A geotextile as the separation layer, coarse aggregate borrow excavated as No. 57 and No. 2A, and a foundation drain pipe of 6‑inch diameter together with its associated excavation, coarse‑aggregate backfill, and geotextile wrapping. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–204), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

Drainage structures that employ a pipe underdrain or pavement‑base drain shall use Class 1 geotextile for all such installations and, when indicated, for the pipe underdrain work. The backfill for these trenches must be fine aggregate Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding 9 inches (maximum); each lift shall be sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot to achieve proper moisture content and compaction. Trench dimensions follow the Standard Drawings, with width as shown and a depth extending at least 2 inches into the subgrade. Pipe joints and fittings must follow manufacturer recommendations and be limited to deflection angles of 45 degrees or less to maintain flow capacity and constructability; perforated pipes are installed with perforations facing down unless directed otherwise, and all connections are required to prevent soil intrusion and avoid flow restriction, thereby satisfying structural integrity, durability, and performance requirements. For drainage structures that use flowable backfill, the indicated type must be furnished in accordance with Table A and any field adjustments to the mix may be made only if the resulting material still achieves the criteria specified in Table A. The mixture’s performance shall be verified by daily slump testing according to AASHTO T 119, air‑content testing according to AASHTO T 152 (without applying an aggregate correction factor) or T 196, yield testing according to AASHTO T 121 and ASTM C136, and compressive strength testing of molded cylinders; non‑conforming material shall be removed or may be subject to liquidated damages. Placement shall not occur at a material temperature below 50 °F and the material must be maintained at no less than 50 °F for a minimum of 24 hours. Lift thickness shall not exceed 4 feet unless approved, and protective measures such as a Class 4 geotextile membrane and pre‑formed drains shall be provided to ensure structural integrity and constructability. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–189), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–522)

Structural details and interfaces

Drainage structures must be joined to new or existing systems with satisfactory connections and fittings whose deflection angles do not exceed 45 degrees; splice connectors are installed so they do not restrict flow, joints are made in accordance with the pipe manufacturer’s recommendations, and the upgraded end of each pipe is sealed to keep foreign material out. Pipe joints are formed by joining with connecting bands or split couplings, and transitions to underdrains or pavement base drains are limited to a maximum 45‑degree bend or series of such bends using suitable couplings, fittings, or special connectors; connections may include satisfactory links to existing drainage structures when indicated. Concrete endwalls are provided to protect outlets that are not tied into new or existing systems, and where plastic pipe is used the ends must be shielded from sunlight with satisfactory endwalls and appropriate trench backfill material. Transitions between sections require at least a 6‑inch geotextile overlap and must be smooth and non‑restricting to maintain continuous gravity flow; outlet fittings are installed per Section 615.3 to prevent soil intrusion. The drainage structure must incorporate a preformed drain no more than 2 inches thick placed between the wall and the flowable backfill, with outlet openings spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall; solid base‑drain outlet pipes are required wherever the pipe intersects the flowable fill, and foundation drains must be protected from intrusion by a Class 4, Type A geotextile membrane. In GSBA retrofits the drainage structure calls for a 6‑inch diameter foundation drain installed from pipe outlet to pipe outlet, including excavation, placement of the drainage pipe, coarse aggregate backfill and a surrounding geotextile separation layer; the geotextile is laid as required and overlapping at roll edges and seams serves as incidental joints. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–204), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

For pipe underdrains and pavement base drains, each drain shall be joined to new or existing drainage structures with satisfactory connections and fittings whose deflection angles do not exceed 45 degrees, and the pipe ends must be sealed to prevent foreign material intrusion. The trench for a prefabricated base drain shall extend at least 2 inches into the subgrade and be backfilled in lifts no greater than 9 inches of fine aggregate Type A that are hydraulically compacted; the first lift is placed simultaneously with the drain to ensure full contact with the geotextile, and any tears in the geotextile shall be repaired according to the manufacturer. Connections between drainage cores shall use splice connectors that do not restrict flow and shall provide a minimum of 6 inches of overlapping geotextile, and all fittings and outlets shall be installed so that soil cannot intrude and flow remains smooth and non‑restricting. For drainage structures located in flowable backfill, a Class 4, Type A geotextile membrane shall shield foundation or base drains from the backfill, and a preformed drain not more than 2 inches thick shall be placed between a retaining wall and the fill with outlet openings spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall. Base‑drain outlets shall be located so that they do not intersect areas where flowable backfill is placed; where an intersection would occur, solid base‑drain outlet pipes shall be furnished. When backfilling around pipe structures, adequate bedding shall be provided from trench bottom to the pipe, and the pipe shall be protected from intrusion of the flowable material, movement, floating and misalignment. For subsurface drain outlets, the trench shall be excavated so that the bottom is uniformly firm and smooth to provide positive gravity flow, and the pipe shall be placed on 2 inches of satisfactorily tamped embankment material and joined with connecting bands or split couplings. After acceptance of the outlet pipe, the trench shall be backfilled in 4‑inch layers of satisfactory embankment material, each layer being thoroughly tamped, and the portion beneath pavement or shoulder shall be compacted as specified in Section 601.3(f). The outlet shall be connected to an underdrain or pavement base drain using suitable couplings and fittings or special connectors with a maximum 45‑degree bend (or series of such bends), and where indicated concrete endwalls or protective endwalls for plastic pipe shall be provided; the completed opening shall be marked with a red subsurface drain outlet marker embedded at least 18 inches from the surface. [1]

[1] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 187–189), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

Subsurface drainage systems must be assembled using joints that follow the manufacturer’s guidance. Pipe sections are typically linked with either connecting bands or split‑couplings, and splice connectors are placed within the drainage core so that they do not impede flow. All fittings used for changes in direction are limited to a deflection of 45 degrees or less, whether a single bend or a series of bends, thereby providing smooth, non‑restricting transitions. Where sections abut, at least six inches of Class 1 geotextile must overlap the joint to maintain continuity and protect against soil intrusion. Connections to underdrains or pavement base drains are made with appropriate couplings, fittings, or special connectors that preserve unrestricted flow. Outlet pipes and other terminal points are protected by galvanized‑wire mesh shields and, when not tied into an existing drainage network, by concrete endwalls; plastic pipe ends receive similar endwall protection and backfill to shield them from sunlight and mechanical damage. These measures collectively ensure the structural integrity of the drainage system while allowing efficient water removal beneath pavements. [1]

[1] — SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (pp. 521–526)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

Drainage structures are installed only after fine grading; pipe underdrains are placed before the pavement base drain and the latter is positioned after adjacent roadway pavement is constructed, except in curb sections. Trenches must be excavated without disturbing the subbase and backfilled promptly so that no trench deeper than 2 inches remains open during precipitation or overnight. Class 1 geotextile is required for all base drains and, when used with pipe underdrains, must be sealed at the upgrade end to keep foreign material out, while perforated pipes are installed with perforations facing down and semicircular pipe placed smooth side down. Prefabricated pavement base drains must be stored on an elevated platform protected from weather and UV, shipped in protective covering, and installed within a trench that extends at least 2 inches into the subgrade; they are then backfilled with fine aggregate Type A using hydraulically compacted lifts no greater than 9 inches thick, each lift sprayed with water at 1.0 gallon per linear foot. All connections, fittings, and outlet pipes are to be installed according to manufacturer‑approved details to prevent soil intrusion and ensure smooth, non‑restricting flow. Solid base drain outlet pipes are provided where flowable backfill intersects them and a preformed drain no more than 2 inches thick is placed between the wall and the fill with outlets at not more than 15 feet through the wall; flowable backfill shall not be placed through flowing water, and any material damaged by rain or flowing water must be removed and replaced. Flowable backfill shall not be placed at a material temperature below 50 °F or if the air or surface temperature is 40 °F or lower, and the in‑place backfill temperature must be maintained at no less than 50 °F for a minimum of 24 hours, with the finished surface protected from frost, erosion, and damage by approved covers. The trench for subsurface drain outlets is excavated so the bottom is uniformly firm and smooth, ensuring positive gravity flow in the outlet; pipe is laid on a 2‑inch layer of satisfactorily tamped embankment material, then the trench is backfilled in 4‑inch layers of satisfactory material with thorough tamping, and the portion under pavement or shoulder is compacted as specified in Section 601.3(f). When plastic pipe is used, ends are protected from sunlight deterioration with concrete endwalls or suitable backfill, and outlet openings receive the protection shown on the Standard Drawings. A red subsurface drain outlet marker is placed on the traffic‑facing side of the opening and embedded a minimum of 18 inches. Subsurface drainage systems are provided at both the abutment and the far end of the geosynthetic‑stabilized bridge approach as shown on the Standard Drawing; all geosynthetic materials are protected from temperatures greater than 140 °F, direct sunlight, and debris, and they are stored elevated from the ground surface to limit moisture exposure. Drainage structures shall be installed using fittings whose deflection angles do not exceed 45 degrees, and the trench for a prefabricated pavement base drain must follow the Standard Drawings with the width shown there and extend at least 2 inches into the subgrade. Backfill shall be placed in hydraulically compacted lifts no more than 9 inches thick; each lift is to be sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot, and trenches shall not be left open greater than 2 inches deep overnight, being barricaded in accordance with Chapter 203. All pipe ends must be sealed to prevent foreign‑material entry, connections are required to preclude soil intrusion, and a minimum of 6 inches of geotextile overlap shall be provided between sections. The drainage structure shall be designed so that any formwork can sustain the lateral fluid pressure of unhardened flowable backfill with at least a 1.3 factor of safety, and base‑drain outlets shall not intersect areas where flowable backfill is placed; solid outlet pipes shall be used where intersections would occur. A preformed drain no more than 2 inches thick shall be provided between the wall and the fill, with outlets spaced at not more than 15 feet through the wall, and lifts shall not exceed 4 feet unless approved by the Representative. Foundation drains must be protected from intrusion and contamination by a Class 4, Type A geotextile membrane, and pipe bedding must maintain proper alignment and prevent floating or movement during placement. Excavate the trench leaving the bottom uniformly firm and smooth to ensure positive gravity flow, then lay pipe on top of 2 inches of satisfactorily tamped embankment material before joining with connecting bands or split couplings. Backfill the trench in 4‑inch layers of satisfactory embankment material, thoroughly tamp each layer, and compact the portion under pavement or shoulder as specified while providing outlet opening protection as shown on the Standard Drawings. Connect to the pipe underdrain or pavement base drain using suitable couplings with a maximum 45‑degree bend (or series of such bends), and if plastic pipe is used protect its ends from sunlight with satisfactory endwalls and backfill material; concrete endwalls may be constructed where required. Finally, mark the subsurface drain outlet with a red marker on the side facing approaching traffic and embed the markers a minimum of 18 inches. [1]
