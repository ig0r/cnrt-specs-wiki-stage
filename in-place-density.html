In-Place Density

Part of: Compaction is-subtopic-of

Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

In‑Place Density is the field measurement of the compacted density of placed pavement or earthwork material that determines whether the required field‑density targets have been achieved [1]. It serves as the primary quality‑control check feeding the Percent Within Limits (PWL) payment metric, with contractors expected to attain densities near the middle of the specified range—generally 92.0 % to 98.5 % for Standard and RPS wearing courses—to receive full pay factors, while lower results trigger reduced payments or corrective action [1]. The evaluation is performed by direct‑transmission nuclear gauge readings or laboratory‑tested cores in accordance with the applicable PTM (e.g., No. 402, AASHTO T 310), and each material type has its own acceptance limits such as ≥97 % of maximum dry density for embankments or ≥96 % of target density for cold‑recycled asphalt bases [2].

Purpose and quality objectives

In‑Place Density evaluates the compacted asphalt material’s ability to meet its required field density, serving as the performance characteristic that feeds into the Percent Within Limits (PWL) quality metric; contractors must achieve consistent density results near the middle of the target field density range—92.0 % to 98.5 % for Standard and RPS construction of Wearing Courses—to receive full pay factors, with lower densities leading to reduced payments [1]. In‑Place Density is also used to evaluate the compacted density of asphalt pavement at longitudinal joints, with acceptance determined by testing pavement cores; projects that meet the specified criteria must include a bid item for the evaluation of Asphalt Pavement Longitudinal Joint Density and the associated incentive/disincentive payment, and the testable joint length must exceed 12 500 feet [1]. In‑Place Density testing is used to evaluate the degree of compaction achieved in the placed material, thereby controlling the compacted density characteristic for each pavement or earthwork component [2]. For embankments and fills (Section 206), the test determines the in‑place dry density with a nuclear gauge according to PTM No. 402 and verifies that the density is not less than 97 % of the maximum dry density, while the top three feet must reach 100 % of the maximum dry density and moisture be within –3 % of optimum; Type 1 granular material for the full width of embankments or fills must also be compacted to 97 % of the maximum dry density, whereas Type 2 granular material must be compacted to a condition of non‑movement under the compaction equipment [2]. For special rolling of soil or granular material (Section 208), the same 97 % of maximum dry density applies to embankment foundations or existing embankments deeper than three feet, and 100 % of maximum dry density applies to structure foundations, subgrades, or material within three feet of the proposed subgrade elevation; moisture is checked after each pass according to AASHTO T 191 or T 310 and stability is indicated when the measured moisture‑density does not increase by more than 1 % over three consecutive passes [2]. For cold‑recycled asphalt base course (Section 341), a target density is established according to PTM No. 402, the in‑place density of each 3 000‑square‑yard area is determined according to AASHTO T 310 and must be at least 96 % of the PTM 402 target; if an area falls below this threshold additional compaction is attempted and, when still insufficient, the crew must scarify, adjust moisture content, and re‑compact as directed [2]. For crushed‑aggregate base course type DG (Section 312), each placed layer must be compacted to not less than 100 % of the maximum density determined according to PTM No. 106, Method B and measured in accordance with AASHTO T 191, and any failure triggers additional compaction or, if necessary, loosening, re‑moistening, reshaping and recompacting until the required density is achieved [2]. For full‑depth reclamation (Section 344), an average in‑place density is established from at least ten random nuclear gauge tests on a control strip and compared with a minimum of 95 % of the laboratory‑compacted maximum density at optimum moisture content; any new control strip must meet 98 % of the original strip density, and the n = 1 density of each 3 000‑square‑yard lot is measured according to PTM No. 402 after the test strip demonstrates that the minimum density specified in Section 344.3(e)3.a can be achieved [2]. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502), direct‑transmission measurements made within 30 minutes after rolling must show a minimum of 85 % of the maximum wet density for lifts less than 6 in and 90 % for lifts greater than or equal to 6 in, and after final rolling the compacted material must achieve a minimum compaction greater than 96 % of the maximum wet density; acceptance further requires that the final average for each lot cannot be less than 98 % of the maximum wet density and no individual test may be less than 96 % of the maximum wet density, otherwise the pavement is deemed defective [2]. For plant‑mixed asphalt courses (Section 413), in‑place pavement density is measured with an approved nuclear gauge according to PTM No. 402 or an approved electrical‑impedance gauge according to PTM No. 403 and may be verified by cores; acceptance may be based on non‑movement, optimum‑rolling pattern, or core testing provided the mixture depth meets Table G minimums and the layer does not exceed 1 in depth or 110 lb/yd² for non‑movement cases, and the percent of theoretical maximum density satisfies the Department’s payment factor (≥ 100); a lot whose density acceptance is by non‑movement or optimum‑rolling pattern receives a Pavement Density Pay Factor of 100.00, whereas core‑based acceptance requires each of PWLD, PWLAC, PWL200 and PWLPCS to be at least 50 %; if PWLD reaches 90 % or more a higher pay factor is calculated according to the specified equation, and when either one of PWLD or PWLAC is less than 50 % the lot is defective [2]. For stone matrix asphalt (Section 419), cores are tested according to PTM No. 715 (and PTM No. 716 if needed) to determine bulk density and Gmm, and the resulting percent of theoretical maximum density is used to calculate a density payment factor; densities more than 2.0 % below the minimum or above the maximum in Table F trigger reduced payment and may halt paving after repeated failures, while across all asphalt specifications densities that are no more than 2.0 % outside the limits of Table E receive a 90 % payment factor and larger deviations render the work defective [2]. For thin‑asphalt overlay courses (Section 412), In‑Place Density evaluates whether the compacted mixture does not move under the compaction equipment and whether the measured density corresponds to an optimum rolling pattern established by a control strip, with density determined by an approved nuclear gauge per PTM 402 or an approved electrical‑impedance gauge per PTM 403 [2]. For longitudinal joints of asphalt pavement (Section 405), In‑Place Density assesses whether the material placed at the joint has been compacted to meet performance requirements; five random 6‑inch cores are obtained and tested according to PTM 715 or PTM 716, and the results must meet a lower specification limit of 91.0 % [2]. For pervious concrete pavement systems (Section 520), In‑Place Density evaluates the oven‑dry density of the hardened concrete after placement; three 4‑inch diameter cores are removed for each 5 000 sq ft area, sent to the laboratory for ASTM C1754 testing, and acceptance requires that the average hardened density be within a tolerance of ± 5 pounds per cubic foot and not exceed 130 pounds per cubic foot [2].

In‑Place Density testing is employed as the primary quality‑control check that confirms each placed layer has attained the density required for structural stability, durability and the performance criteria set out in the specifications, thereby preventing problems such as insufficient compaction, material movement, rutting, cracking, loss of strength, excessive permanent deflection, flushing of binder, roller marks or inadequate permeability. [2] Under the General provisions of the Pavement Policy Manual (section 2.1), contractors must achieve a consistent in‑place density near the middle of the target field‑density range—specifically 92.0 % to 98.5 % for Standard and RPS wearing courses—to receive full payment, while densities at or near the bottom of that range may trigger reduced pay factors. [1] For embankments and fills (Section 206—Placement and Compaction of Embankment and Fill) the contractor must determine in‑place moisture and density according to PTM No. 402 using the direct transmission method, compact the material to not less than 97 % of the maximum dry density, and compact the top three feet to 100 % of the maximum dry density; if these levels are not met, the areas must be reworked, reshaped, recompacted or removed and replaced followed by an acceptance retest. Scope and applicability

In‑Place Density testing is employed for a wide range of materials and construction situations. For asphalt paving contract items that are measured by the Percent Within Limits (PWL) quality measure, the District must evaluate the condition of the base and select a density acceptance method in accordance with Publication 408 Section 413.3(j), typically using core testing, and this requirement shall be written as a Special Provision in the bid documents so bidders can plan their work [1]. The procedure applies to both standard and Restricted Performance Specification (RPS) wearing‑course constructions where contractors are expected to achieve density results near the middle of the target field density range, for example 92.0 % to 98.5 % [1]. In‑Place Density is also applicable to any asphalt pavement project that qualifies as an RPS pavement or a standard pavement on the National Highway System, provided that density is verified through pavement cores and the longitudinal joint being evaluated extends more than twelve‑thousand five hundred feet; when those three conditions are met, the contract shall contain a bid item for evaluating longitudinal joint density with associated incentive or disincentive payments, and the void‑reducing asphalt membrane provision may not be used on the same pavement placements within that project [1].

In‑Place Density testing is required whenever soil, Type 1 granular material, or Type 2 granular material are placed and compacted as part of an embankment or fill under Section 206.3; the moisture‑density relationship must be established according to PTM No. 106 and in‑place moisture and dry density determined by the direct‑transmission nuclear gauge method prescribed in PTM No. 402 (or back‑scatter where coarse), with gauges calibrated annually and daily standardized, and any area that does not meet the specified density must be reshaped, re‑compacted or replaced and retested before further work proceeds [2].

For plant‑mixed asphalt courses covered by Section 413—including Superpave wearing, binder, leveling, safety edge, thin‑lift 6.3 mm overlay, and Stone Matrix Asphalt (SMA) mixtures—density acceptance may be performed by non‑movement, optimum‑rolling pattern, or pavement cores; for non‑movement or optimum‑rolling pattern the Pay Factor is 100.00 and PWLD, PWLAC, PWL200 and PWLPCS must each be greater than or equal to 50 to avoid a defective lot, while a value less than 50 for either PWLD or PWLAC renders the lot defective [2]. Non‑movement may be used for scratch or leveling courses less than 1 inch deep or ≤110 lb/yd², for paving or patching strips narrower than 4 ft, and for materials placed in small quantities not exceeding 500 tons in a continuous placement; optimum‑rolling pattern may be applied to the same small‑quantity and unstable‑base situations, to leveling courses that are ≥1 inch deep or ≥110 lb/yd², and to mixtures placed at less than the minimum compacted depths listed in Table G [2]. Pavement cores are required for RPS construction and for standard extended‑season paving unless waived by the Representative, and they are appropriate when materials are placed at compacted depths equal to or greater than the minimum depths in Table G on stable, uniform bases [2].

For SMA placements of 150 tons or less, density may be accepted by optimum‑rolling pattern or non‑movement, and areas inaccessible to rollers may use mechanical vibrating hand tampers; cores must be taken from each sublot and each production or placement date, tested according to PTM No. 715 (or PTM No. 716 if needed), with at least three test results required and paving stopped if two consecutive lots or a total of three lots do not meet the 100 % density payment factor [2].

In‑Place Density testing is also mandated for cold‑recycled asphalt base courses under Section 341.3; each three‑thousand‑square‑yard area must be measured in accordance with AASHTO T 310 and achieve at least 96 % of the PTM 402 target density, with scarifying, moisture adjustment and re‑compaction required if that minimum is not met [2]. Crushed‑aggregate base course layers described in Section 312.3 must each be compacted to not less than 100 % of maximum density as determined by PTM No. 106 Method B, with field density measured according to AASHTO T 191 and remedial action required if the requirement is not satisfied [2]. Longitudinal joints created when a new paving layer abuts a previously placed layer require five randomly located six‑inch diameter cores per joint; percent compaction is accepted only if it meets the lower specification limit of 91.0 % [2].

Test methods and procedures

In‑place density testing is performed according to the procedures and standards that apply to each material type and construction activity. For embankments and fills, Section 206 requires the contractor to determine moisture and dry density in the field by applying PTM No. 402 with the direct transmission method using a Nuclear Density/Moisture Gauge calibrated to AASHTO T 310 (or, for Troxler gauges, PTM No. 418); gauges must be calibrated every 12 months and daily standardization is recorded on Form TR‑4276AC; acceptance moisture‑density tests are performed at the Representative‑directed frequencies in Table B and, if the required density is not achieved, the area must be reworked, reshaped, recompacted and retested; a failed acceptance retest triggers a check of the gauge’s standard count per PTM No. 418 or AASHTO T 310 before proceeding, and when acceptance results are satisfactory the Representative conducts verification testing with an independent gauge on one of the first three acceptance tests and at least one verification for every ten acceptance tests thereafter, accepting the result only if the independent wet‑density reading is within ±2.0 lb/ft³ and the moisture reading is within ±0.8w of the contractor’s values. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements, Section 502 mandates field density tests in accordance with ASTM C1040 Test Method A‑Direct Transmission no later than thirty minutes after rolling, using only the wet‑density reading provided by the equipment; test locations are selected per PTM No. 1 with at least five measurements taken directly behind the paver and another five after final compaction for each 500 cubic yards placed, and results are expressed as a compaction percentage calculated from recorded density divided by the computed RCC unit weight; directly behind the paver a minimum compaction of 85 % of the maximum wet density must be achieved for thicknesses less than six inches and 90 % for thicknesses greater than or equal to six inches, while after final rolling a compaction greater than 96 % of the maximum wet density is required for all thicknesses; the rollers used shall conform to Section 108.05(c)3, being self‑propelled smooth dual steel‑drum rollers of at least eight tons in static or vibratory mode. For plant‑mixed asphalt courses covered by Section 413, in‑place density may be measured by extracting six‑inch diameter pavement cores using the drilling procedure of PTM No. 729, cleaning and towel‑drying them immediately, and then determining bulk density (Gmb) with PTM No. 715 and maximum theoretical density (Gmm) with PTM No. 716 together with AASHTO T 209 as modified in Bulletin 27; no conditioning is performed, only drying, and the minimum sample size may be waived for the six‑inch core; the percent of theoretical maximum density is compared to limits where a lot is defective if the percent within limits is less than 50, and pay factors are calculated using the equations for PWLD ≥ 90 or between 50 and 90; retesting of density cores is not allowed when any core was coated with paraffin wax per PTM No. 716, otherwise repeatability is checked per PTM No. 5 and non‑repeatable results are replaced by the retest values, while the contractor provides traffic control, drilling equipment, backfilling within 24 hours and covers all associated costs. In‑place density may also be verified by an optimum‑rolling pattern approach; for this the Inspector‑in‑Charge, the Representative and the contractor’s certified asphalt field technician must first determine density with an approved nuclear gauge (e.g., Troxler Electronics Model 3411B, Campbell Pacific MC‑2, Seaman MC‑2, TransTech PQITM 300/301 or Troxler PaveTracker) in accordance with PTM No. 402, or with an electrical impedance gauge per PTM No. 403, the gauge being operated by a licensed nuclear gauge operator and having a current ASTM D2950 calibration certificate; the optimum‑rolling pattern is then established per the same PTM, documented on Form TR‑4276B/C and the course compacted accordingly; if the target density is not achieved the Representative may suspend paving and require a new pattern. For stone matrix asphalt courses, Section 419 requires each original density core to be tested for bulk density and maximum theoretical density using PTM No. 715 and PTM No. 716; after drilling six‑inch cores per PTM No. 729, the cores are washed, towel‑dryed and backfilled within 24 hours, then one core per production or placement date having the highest bulk density is selected for Gmm testing following AASHTO T 209 as modified in Bulletin 27 with no conditioning, only drying, and a waived minimum sample size; when the mixture contains porous aggregate whose coarse or fine aggregate water absorption value is ≥ 1.5 % and the calculated percent of theoretical maximum density produces any sublot with a density pay factor less than 100.00, the supplemental procedure for porous aggregates is applied; repeatability of bulk‑density results is evaluated using PTM No. 5; if repeatable the original values are used, otherwise retest values replace them. For stone matrix asphalt (SMA) pavement construction, a trial demonstration must place at least 100 tons of mix at a location identified on the approved paving operation QC Plan; after placement three loose plant samples are obtained for asphalt content, gradation and draindown and three pavement cores are extracted for density testing using the laboratory methods prescribed in Bulletin 27; the designated laboratory must possess equipment that has passed an AASHTO re:source On‑Site Laboratory Assessment and must be calibrated, maintained and logged according to the Representative’s requirements, and all testing equipment required for mixture and density HOLA samples is provided by the Department at the approved laboratory and must meet the calibration and maintenance standards outlined in the QC plan; core density results must fall within the specification limits for both single‑sample and multiple‑sample tolerances, and if any result fails those limits a new demonstration strip must be performed. For full‑depth reclamation, Section 344 requires a quality‑control plan to be prepared at least two weeks before work begins and all equipment—including the self‑propelled rotary reclaimer with an integral liquid proportioning system, the motor grader for placement, a 25‑ton (or greater) pneumatic tire roller for breakdown and intermediate rolling when the mix is emulsion or foamed asphalt and less than eight inches in depth, a 25‑ton vibratory padfoot roller for deeper mixes, and a 12‑to‑14‑ton steel‑drum static roller for finish rolling—must be calibrated and operational before deployment; a 300‑foot test strip is constructed to demonstrate the full FDR process, verify material application rates and confirm that the minimum density specified in section 344.3(e)3.a can be achieved, and if the required density is not attained additional strips are built until control is established; once the test strip meets the required density, n=1 in‑place density of the reclaimed section is determined for each 3,000 square‑yard lot according to PTM No. 402. For cold‑recycled asphalt base courses, Section 341 establishes the target density by consulting PTM No. 402 and requires compacting the material to at least 96 % of that target using rollers specified in Section 108.05(c); the in‑place density for each 3,000‑square‑yard segment is then measured according to AASHTO T 310 and, if a segment falls below the minimum and further rolling does not achieve compliance, the crew must scarify the area, adjust its moisture content and re‑compact, recalculate a new target density per PTM 402 and compact again until all measured areas meet or exceed the required density. For Superpave thin‑asphalt overlays, Section 412 requires on‑site density determination with an approved nuclear gauge in accordance with PTM 402 or an approved electrical impedance gauge per PTM 403; the measurement must show non‑movement of the mix under the compaction equipment, a control strip is constructed in the presence of the Representative and the contractor’s certified asphalt field technician to establish the optimum rolling pattern, readings are recorded on the forms prescribed by PTM 402 and submitted to the Representative, and the gauge operator must be licensed and provide an annual calibration certificate per ASTM D2950 with recalibration required if the gauge is damaged or repaired. For special‑rolling operations, Section 208 mandates that moisture and dry density of the compacted layer are verified after each compaction pass using either AASHTO T191 or AASHTO T310; the roller must be a pneumatic‑tire, sheepsfoot/pad foot, smooth steel drum vibratory, or loaded triaxle dump truck as specified in Section 208.3(a) and operated at no more than 2 mph (static mode for fine soils), the procedure calls for first compacting as required by Section 206.3(b), then sampling several random locations after each pass, and when the measured moisture‑density does not increase by more than 1 % over three consecutive passes and the material appears stable special rolling may proceed, otherwise the material must be dried and recompacted. For crushed‑aggregate base courses, Section 312 requires that the maximum dry density of the mix be first established using PTM 106 Method B and field density then determined with AASHTO T191; compaction equipment prescribed in Section 108.05(c) must compact each layer until the measured density is not less than 100 % of that maximum value, and if a layer falls short additional passes are required, with thorough loosening, remoistening, reshaping and recompacting of the entire area mandated if extra compaction remains insufficient, replacement being a last resort; all testing and verification steps are performed on each placed layer before subsequent layers are laid. For longitudinal joint density, Section 405 requires extracting five 6‑inch diameter core samples on each joint located randomly according to PTM 1 and PTM 729, drilling with a 6‑inch coring drill as soon as possible but no later than the following day, handling the cores without compression or distortion, labeling them and delivering them to the Representative within 24 hours for testing in accordance with PTM 715 or PTM 716, backfilling the holes with the same mix and compacting and sealing the mixture within 24 hours, and comparing the results to a lower specification limit of 91.0 %; if results fall below this threshold the contractor may adjust mixture design or placement operations. For pervious concrete pavements, Section 520 directs that three 4‑inch diameter cores are removed for every 5,000 ft² as directed by PTM 1, sent to laboratory where oven‑dry density and void content are measured according to ASTM C1754, the average core length must be 3/8‑inch + 3/4‑inch with no individual core deviating more than –½‑inch, the resulting density must fall within a tolerance of ±5 lb/ft³ and not exceed 130 lb/ft³ while void content must be within ±5 % of the average voids from accepted test sections, unit weight of plastic concrete is checked on every truck using ASTM C1688, and if any in‑place density or permeability values fall outside these limits the pavement must be removed and replaced to meet the specified criteria. For roller‑compacted concrete acceptance, Section 502 requires that compaction percentages recorded after rolling result in a final average for each lot that cannot be less than 98 % of the maximum wet density and no individual test may be less than 96 % of that maximum; if a lot fails these limits cores are taken and the material is tested for 28‑day compressive strength according to AASHTO T24 at no expense to the department, with core holes filled afterward. For depth measurement in courses with density acceptance by lots, Section 413 directs that the Inspector will measure the depth of each sublot according to PTM 737 using the density acceptance samples; if yield results indicate insufficient course depth the inspector must drill one 6‑inch diameter core for each 500 tons of material placed as directed by PTM 1, and surface uniformity is evaluated using a 12‑foot straightedge with the pavement deemed defective if irregularities exceed 3/16‑inch, depth is more than 1/4‑inch below design, or depth is more than 1/8‑inch low in three or more adjacent core locations. For plant‑mixed 6.3 mm wearing courses (Section 412) density shall be determined with an approved nuclear gauge in accordance with PTM 402 or with an approved electrical impedance gauge in accordance with PTM 403 while the Representative and the contractor’s certified asphalt field technician are present; a control strip is constructed for each course using the PTM 402 control‑strip technique to establish the optimum rolling pattern, and density acceptance requires that the mat does not move under the compaction equipment and that the control‑strip readings meet the optimum‑rolling‑pattern criteria. When moisture‑density test data are unavailable (Section 208) a test‑strip approach shall be used; after each compaction pass the in‑place moisture and density are measured at several pre‑determined random locations according to AASHTO T 191 or AASHTO T 310, and proceeding to special rolling is allowed only when the measured moisture‑density does not increase by more than 1 % over three consecutive passes and the material appears stable. For longitudinal joint lots (Section 405) the Representative shall pick one core location in every sublot using PTM No 1 and PTM No 729, while the contractor may take a companion core for quality‑control; on the first day of paving a separate quality‑control strip is formed by taking five 6‑inch diameter cores at random locations on the joint, and all density results are compared against the lower specification limit of 91.0 %. For pervious concrete pavement (Section 520) unit‑weight testing of the plastic concrete shall be performed on every truck; for hardened concrete three 4‑inch diameter cores shall be identified and removed from each 5,000 sq ft area with locations chosen according to PTM No 1, and core void content is accepted only if it falls within ± 5 % of the average voids measured in the approved test sections; infiltration testing requires three areas per 10,000 sq ft selected according to PTM No 1. For stone‑matrix asphalt wearing courses (Section 419) density measurements must be obtained from core samples that represent the placed mixture; for a trial demonstration the contractor shall obtain three pavement cores and test them for density, and during production the contractor shall monitor pavement cores on every lot, using those cores as the representative in‑place density data for the material, process and completed work. For full‑depth reclamation (Section 344) a 300‑foot test strip is first constructed to demonstrate the reclamation process; the strip must meet the minimum density specified in 344.3(e)3.a, and if it fails additional strips are built until control is established. Once a compliant strip is demonstrated, an n = 1 density of the reclaimed material is determined for each 3,000‑square yard lot in accordance with PTM No 402. For density acceptance by lots (Section 413) the Inspector shall obtain a density‑acceptance sample from every sublot and measure its depth according to PTM No 737; if calculated yield shows insufficient course depth additional cores shall be taken at a rate of one 6‑inch core per 500 tons placed. If samples for density are no more than 2.0 % below the minimum or no more than 2.0 % above the maximum limits of Table E, payment will be 90 % of the contract unit price. [2]

Acceptance criteria and tolerances

According to the Pavement Policy Manual, In‑Place Density acceptance is evaluated through the Percent Within Limits (PWL) approach, which captures both the mean and standard deviation of field density measurements; districts must select the appropriate method for density acceptance in accordance with Publication 408 Section 413.3(j), and contractors are expected to obtain density values near the middle of the target field density range—illustrated as 92.0 % to 98.5 % for Standard and RPS wearing‑course construction—to achieve full payment, while results toward the lower end may trigger reduced pay factors.[1] For embankments and fills (Section 206) compacted soil must be placed to not less than 97 % of the maximum dry density over the full width, while the top three feet must reach 100 % of the maximum dry density.[2] Type 1 granular material must be kept within ±2 % of optimum moisture at the time of compaction and verification is acceptable only when independent nuclear gauge readings are within ±2.0 lb/ft³ for wet density and within ±0.8 w for moisture, where w is the percent moisture content.[2] In special rolling (Section 208) the same percentage‑of‑maximum‑dry‑density targets apply to embankment foundations or existing embankments more than three feet below the proposed subgrade elevation (≥97 %) and to structure foundations, subgrades and material within three feet of the proposed subgrade (100 %); moisture must lie between minus 3 % of optimum moisture content and optimum moisture content.[2] When no prior moisture‑density data exist, crews may proceed only after three consecutive passes show a moisture‑density increase of not more than 1 % and visual stability is confirmed, and instability is judged unacceptable when permanent deflection or rutting exceeds one‑half inch for existing subgrade or spread footings on soil, or exceeds one inch for embankment foundations.[2] For crushed aggregate base course (Section 312) each layer must be compacted to not less than 100 % of the maximum density as established by PTM No. 106 Method B and verified with field density testing according to AASHTO T191; if a layer fails, additional compaction is required and, if still insufficient, the low‑density area must be loosened, remoistened, reshaped and re‑compacted or removed and replaced.[2] Cold‑recycled asphalt base course (Section 341) requires a minimum in‑place density of 96 % of the PTM No. 402 target density on each 3,000‑square‑yard area as measured by AASHTO T310; if the target cannot be met after further rolling, the area must be scarified, moisture adjusted and re‑compacted, and a new PTM No. 402 target density is then applied with the same 96 % minimum.[2] Full‑depth reclamation (Section 344) material must achieve at least 95 % of the laboratory‑compacted maximum density at optimum moisture content (PTM No. 106); verification is performed on a control strip by taking a minimum of ten random nuclear gauge tests and averaging them, and if the initial strip fails the average, additional compaction is required and any replacement strip must meet a density equal to 98 % of the original strip density.[2] Superpave plant‑mixed asphalt courses (Section 413) are accepted on the basis of Percent Within Limits; a PWL of less than 50 % renders the pavement defective, when PWL is between 50 % and 90 % the pay factor is calculated as 70 + 0.75(PWL‑50), and when PWL is 90 % or greater the factor is 100 + 0.4(PWL‑90).[2] Lots that achieve density acceptance by non‑movement or by an optimum‑rolling pattern receive a density payment factor of 100.00; non‑movement acceptance applies to scratch or leveling layers less than one inch in depth or equal to or less than 110 pounds per square yard, or when the mixture does not move under compaction equipment.[2] Optimum‑rolling pattern acceptance requires measurement with an approved gauge and compliance with the minimum compacted depths listed in Table G; core testing is required and any individual increment that falls outside the lower and upper specification limits in Table I must be subjected to extreme‑value analysis at the 5 % significance level (PTM No. 4).[2] After discarding extremes, a lot must retain at least three test results; if fewer than three remain, the lot is still accepted as specified in Section 413.3(h)2.a.1.[2] A lot is considered acceptable only when the calculated percent of theoretical maximum density satisfies the criteria in Section 413.4 and the density payment factor percentage is ≥ 100 for two consecutive lots or for three total lots; if this threshold is not met, paving operations must be stopped.[2] Stone‑matrix asphalt (Section 419) follows a similar PWL approach; the pavement is deemed defective when the payment factor for both asphalt content and the percent passing the 75 µm sieve equals 85 %, and if only one lot characteristic has a PWL less than 64 % the District Executive may permit the defective lot to remain in place, otherwise removal or replacement is required.[2] Extreme density values are screened using PTM 4 at the 5 % significance level, and values that lie no more than ±2 % of the minimum or maximum limits in Table F are allowed (resulting in a 90 % payment factor); any result beyond those bounds is considered defective work and must be removed.[2] Acceptance requires three or more acceptable test results per lot.[2] Roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502) require ASTM C1040 direct‑transmission field density tests performed as soon as possible and no later than 30 minutes after rolling; directly behind the paver a minimum compaction of 85 % of the maximum wet density is required for thicknesses less than six inches and 90 % for thicknesses greater than or equal to six inches, and after final rolling the compaction must exceed 96 % of the maximum wet density for all thicknesses.[2] At least five tests are taken behind the paver and at least five after final compaction for each 500 cubic‑yard placement; the lot’s final average compaction may not be less than 98 % of the maximum wet density and no individual test result may be less than 96 % of the maximum wet density, and if either limit is violated the lot must be cored and the cores tested for 28‑day compressive strength according to AASHTO T24, with a minimum compressive strength of 3,500 psi required before the pavement may remain in place at full price.[2] Longitudinal joint density (Section 405) is evaluated by a Percent Within Limits calculation with a lower specification limit of 91.0 % and no upper limit; cores taken from each sublot are tested according to PTM 715 or PTM 716, results must be submitted within 24 hours, and acceptance hinges on achieving at least the 91.0 % compaction threshold across the lot.[2] Pervious concrete pavement systems (Section 520) require three 4‑inch diameter cores per 5,000 sq ft; the average core length must equal 3/8 inch plus 3/4 inch and no individual core may be shorter than –½ inch; oven‑dry density must stay within a tolerance of ±5 pounds per cubic foot of the average hardened densities of two test sections and must not exceed 130 pounds per cubic foot, while void content must be within ±5 % of the average voids recorded for the accepted test section.[2] Visual inspection criteria also apply: macrotexture segregation is unacceptable when the average texture‑depth difference between segregated and non‑segregated areas exceeds 0.024 inch, and flushing is deemed defective when the average surface texture depth is less than or equal to 0.006 inch.[2] For Superpave courses the finished surface must be checked with a 12‑foot straightedge and any irregularity exceeding 3/16 inch is considered defective; course depth measured by core samples is deficient if overall deficiency exceeds 1/4 inch or if localized deficiency greater than 1/8 inch occurs in three or more adjacent cores.[2] Partially completed lots may be up to 2.0 % below the minimum or up to 2.0 % above the maximum limits of Table E and still be acceptable; results beyond those bounds render the pavement defective.[2]

In‑place density is assessed primarily through the Percent Within Limits (PWL) methodology, which combines the mean of measured densities with their standard deviation into a single quality metric; the standard‑deviation component of PWL influences the graduated pay factors applied to pavement work [1]. Contractors are required to obtain consistent density results near the middle of the target field density range of 92.0 % to 98.5 % for Standard and RPS construction of Wearing Courses in order to maximize payments, whereas results at or near the bottom of that range may trigger reduced pay factors [1]. The District determines the appropriate acceptance level by reviewing contract items and following Publication 408 Section 413.3(j), with density acceptance normally expressed as a special provision in the bid documents [1]. For Superpave courses covered by Section 413, the PWL is calculated for each core; when the PWL is greater than or equal to 90, the density pay factor is set to 100 plus 0.4 times the amount above 90, and when the PWL is less than 90 but at least 50 the factor is 70 plus 0.75 times the amount above 50, while any PWLD or PWLAC below 50 renders the lot defective [2]. Individual pay factors for density, asphalt content, 200‑mesh aggregate, and percent coarse stone are then combined with a weighted formula of 0.5 × PF_D + 0.3 × PF_AC + 0.1 × PF_200 + 0.1 × PF_PCS to obtain an overall lot payment factor [2]. Mat‑density acceptance requires each sublot density value to be calculated and rounded to the nearest tenth of a percent of theoretical maximum density according to ASTM E29, after which a lot average is computed from those values and likewise rounded; any individual result outside the lower or upper limits in Table I triggers an extreme‑value analysis at the 5 % significance level, and if fewer than three results remain the lot may still be accepted [2]. Failure of two consecutive lots or a total of three lots to meet the density payment factor percentage of at least 100 requires stopping paving operations [2]. During spreading and finishing, each core is compared with the single‑sample limits in Table E; a result within those limits yields full payment, a result no more than 2.0 % below the minimum or above the maximum yields 90 % payment, and any result beyond that range is deemed defective work; when three or more sublots are present the density samples from each sublot are aggregated and evaluated with PWL pay‑factor adjustments as the lot‑average assessment [2]. For Stone Matrix Asphalt covered by Section 419, bulk‑density values for each core are recorded and checked for repeatability according to PTM No. 5; repeatable original values determine the density payment factor that enters the formula Lot Payment = CP × (2 PD + PB + PA)/400, while lack of repeatability requires use of retest values [2]. If only one characteristic in a lot has a percent within limits less than 64, the District Executive may allow the defective lot to remain and pay fifty percent of the contract unit price [2]. Construction acceptance compares each core’s percent compaction with mat‑density tolerances; an out‑of‑tolerance result may be discarded after an extreme‑value analysis at the 5 % significance level provided at least three test results remain, and density lots are accepted only when three or more valid results exist [2]. For partially completed lots of two sublots or fewer each sample is evaluated individually against Table F; deviations no more than 2.0 % from the limits receive ninety percent payment, larger deviations render the work defective, and the lot as a whole is accepted when the remaining individual results satisfy the Table F limits and the three‑sample minimum; otherwise paving must be stopped after two consecutive lots or a total of three lots fail to meet the density payment factor percentage of one hundred [2]. Roller Compacted Concrete specified in Section 502 relies solely on individual field density tests performed directly behind the paver; each test must achieve at least 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts less than six inches and at least 90 % for lifts six inches or greater, and after final rolling every result must exceed 96 % of the maximum wet density, with a minimum of five tests behind the paver and five after final compaction per five hundred cubic yards; acceptance is based on those individual results with no lot‑average or variability criteria, although the final average for each lot may not be less than 98 % of the maximum wet density and any test below 96 % causes the lot to fail even if the overall average meets the 98 % requirement [2]. Full‑Depth Reclamation in Section 344 requires each three‑thousand‑square‑yard lot to be compacted to at least 95 % of the laboratory‑compacted maximum density; ten random nuclear gauge measurements are taken, and both each individual reading and the calculated average are compared with the 95 % threshold, requiring additional compaction if any fall below; when a new control strip is required its average must be at least 98 % of the original control‑strip density [2]. Cold Recycled Asphalt Base Course in Section 341 mandates that each three‑thousand‑square‑yard area’s in‑place density, determined according to AASHTO T 310, reach at least 96 % of the PTM No. 402 target; if a measurement falls below this minimum the contractor must first attempt additional compaction and, if still insufficient, scarify, adjust moisture content, recompact or establish a new PTM‑derived target density and again compact to ninety‑six percent of it; the specification provides no averaging of results over a lot nor any separate evaluation of variability [2]. Superpave thin‑asphalt overlay courses in Section 412 evaluate each mat‑density sample from a partially completed lot against the single‑sample limits in Table E, accepting when those criteria are met; the department also applies a density payment factor percentage to each test result and incorporates it into the overall payment adjustment [2]. Lot‑level acceptance is determined by computing the percent of measurements that fall within the upper and lower specification limits using Table F, with extreme values screened according to PTM 4 at the 5 % significance level, and if removing an outlier would leave fewer than three results the lot is still accepted per Section 412.3(h)2.a.1 [2]. Longitudinal joint density in Section 405 uses six‑inch cores tested by PTM 715 (or PTM 716 when required) to obtain a percent compaction value; any core that reaches at least 91.0 % is counted as within limits, and the Representative calculates both the average in‑place density for the lot and the Percent Within Limits, which reflects how many individual cores meet the lower specification limit of 91.0 % while no upper limit is applied; the PWL thus provides a measure of lot‑level compliance and variability [2]. For pervious concrete pavement systems in Section 520 each core removed for In‑Place Hardened Concrete must have an individual length not less than minus one‑half inch, and its oven‑dry density is measured according to ASTM C1754; the average core length from three cores must fall within three‑eighths inch plus three‑quarters inch, and the two test sections’ average hardened densities become the reference value for the remainder of the project, allowed to deviate by plus or minus five pounds per cubic foot and not to exceed one hundred thirty pounds per cubic foot; void content is accepted if it stays within plus or minus five percent of the average voids measured in the cores of the approved test section, providing a check on variability across the lot [2].

Nonconformance and corrective action

When acceptance testing for moisture and density under Section 206.3(a) shows that the required density is not obtained, the contractor must promptly rework, reshape, and recompact or remove and replace damaged or unsatisfactory areas and then perform an acceptance retest before placement or testing of another area; if a retest still fails, the standard count of the acceptance‑test gauge must be checked on its reference standard block in accordance with PTM No. 418 or AASHTO T 310 and any faulty gauge repaired or replaced before work proceeds; should the gauge be functioning properly but density remains out of spec, a new moisture‑density test per PTM No. 106 is required to determine if the moisture‑density relationship has changed, and if it has, new target values are established for all testing, otherwise the contractor must review their operations, make adjustments within specification requirements, and amend the QC Plan to obtain acceptable results. Under Section 413.3(j) when the in‑place pavement density percent‑within‑limits (PWLD) is found to be less than 50, the lot is classified as defective; the District Executive may either leave the lot in place and apply an overall lot pay factor of 70 or order removal and replacement of the entire lot at no additional cost with new acceptance sampling, and when two or more of PWLD, PWLAC or PWL200 are each less than 50, the DE is required to direct removal and replacement of the whole lot; the contractor may request a retest of a defective lot within three weeks of the LTS test results release, providing supporting data for the request. When a field density test performed no later than 30 minutes after rolling shows compaction below the required minimum, the contractor must apply additional compaction as prescribed; if the paver does not achieve at least 95 % of the maximum wet density, a self‑propelled smooth dual steel drum roller weighing a minimum of 8 tons and operating in static or vibratory mode shall be used to obtain primary compaction before final rolling, and after the final rolling operation the pavement must reach a compaction greater than 96 % of the maximum wet density for all lift thicknesses, with testing repeated until that acceptance criterion is satisfied. For stone matrix asphalt work, when density testing shows a non‑conforming lot, the prescribed action is to remove and replace the lot; however, if only one lot characteristic falls below the limit of less than 64, the District Executive may allow the contractor to leave the defective lot in place and pay for it at fifty percent of the contract unit price, and the contractor may request a retest in writing within three weeks of the LTS test results release, supplying quality‑control data and justification; the LTS will then retest the original density cores unless they were coated with paraffin wax. When density testing shows a sample outside the allowable range, the contractor must halt paving, investigate the cause and submit corrective actions to the Representative for approval before work can resume; if the deviation is no more than 2.0 % below the minimum or above the maximum limits of Table E, payment is reduced to 90 %, but any deviation that exceeds 2.0 % in either direction classifies the pavement as defective and, unless written otherwise by the District Executive, requires removal of the non‑conforming material and replacement with fresh hot mix and may trigger lot termination and payment adjustments. Under Section 344.3(e)3.a, when inspection or testing shows that the in‑place density of a full‑depth reclamation segment does not meet contract requirements, full‑production placement must be halted and the nonconforming aspects identified and corrected before any further production proceeds; if the initial test strip cannot demonstrate adequate control to achieve the desired reclaimed roadway, additional test strips are required until the necessary control is established, after which normal production can resume and the n = 1 density of each 3,000‑square‑yard lot shall be determined for each lot according to PTM No. 402. For a cold‑recycled asphalt base course under Section 341, when a field test shows that the in‑place density over a 3,000 sq yd area is below the required minimum, the contractor must first attempt additional compaction; if this does not achieve the minimum, the area must be scarified, its moisture content adjusted, and re‑compacted; should the density still be below the minimum while the base appears uniform and stable, a new target density is to be calculated in accordance with PTM No. 402 and the layer compacted to at least 96 % of that revised target, and if the completed base course is judged unacceptable, work must be stopped and may resume only after the cause of the deficiency has been corrected. For a plant‑mixed 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlay under Section 412, when density testing indicates a non‑conforming lot, the contractor must submit a written retest request to the District Executive within three weeks of the LTS test release together with quality‑control results, control charts, companion sample data and any trend analysis that justify the request; the Department may allow retesting, which shall be performed with the inspector present, appropriate traffic control, drilling two 6‑inch cores at locations offset 24 inches from the original samples, rinsing the cores, backfilling the holes within 24 hours with an asphalt mixture of matching JMF or the mixture used for subsequent courses and compacting and sealing it, employing the same test method unless a written change is approved, and the cost of the retest shall be deducted from the lot payment as specified in Table H. Under Section 502.3(m), when the final average density for an RCC lot is less than 98 % of maximum wet density or any individual test is less than 96 %, the material should be cored and the cores tested for 28‑day compressive strength at no expense to the Department, the core holes are filled with RCC material or a rapid‑set concrete patching material, and if the tested area also fails to achieve a minimum compressive strength of 3 500 psi, the lot is considered defective in density and, unless otherwise directed in writing by the District Executive, the defective pavement must be removed and replaced. Documentation and reporting

Under Section 206, the In‑Place Density file must contain the submitted Compaction Control QC Plan (Form CS‑206) and the daily gauge standardization sheet that documents the current annual calibration and projected monthly decay limits for each nuclear density/moisture gauge. Under Section 413, verification testing performed by an independent gauge is recorded on Form TR‑4276AD and is considered acceptable when the independent readings are within ±2.0 lb/ft³ for wet density and within ±0.8 % for moisture compared with the contractor’s gauge; all personnel conducting the tests must carry a valid nuclear gauge operator certification, the Inspector‑in‑Charge’s approval of non‑movement or optimum‑rolling pattern acceptance is recorded on Form TR‑4276B or C, and a copy of the nuclear gauge annual calibration certificate per ASTM D2950 together with the operator’s training documentation is filed. Under Section 502 for roller‑compacted concrete pavements, the file shall include the approved Field Operation QC Plan and wet‑density readings taken according to ASTM C1040 within 30 minutes of rolling, with compaction percentages calculated by the prescribed equation for each test location selected per PTM No.1; at least five measurements directly behind the paver and at least five after final compaction are logged for every 500 cubic yards placed together with lift thickness and roller type, and the log must record whether pre‑rolling compaction of 85 % (lifts < 6 in.) or 90 % (lifts ≥ 6 in.) and post‑rolling compaction greater than 96 % were achieved. Under Section 413 for asphalt courses, the documentation must record the core locations and bulk‑density test results obtained by PTM No. 715/716, any Gmm results from AASHTO T 209 as modified, and the calculated percent‑within‑limits (PWLD) together with the Pay Factor for In‑Place Density (PFD), which is set to 100.00 for non‑movement or optimum‑rolling pattern acceptance; when PWLD falls below 50 the lot is deemed defective and the District Executive may apply an Overall Lot Pay Factor of 70 or order full removal and replacement. Each density‑acceptance core is identified by ECMS project number, lot and sublot, station/offset, placement date, mixture type and classified as Sample Class AS (or FV), sealed in a container with uniquely numbered security tape recorded on Form TR‑447, and at least three cores per sublot are required. The record must also list the Gmm verification sample identification (ECMS project number, lot/sub‑lot, location, date, mixture type) and note any difference greater than ±0.030 between its laboratory result and the contractor’s daily Gmm value, which then triggers use of the lab value for percent‑theoretical‑maximum‑density calculations; a repeatability assessment comparing original and retest bulk densities is required to decide whether original or retest values will be used for the payment factor percentage for density (PD), and certified asphalt field technicians must provide their certification card on site. Corrective actions are documented whenever acceptance criteria are not met: if an area fails to meet the required in‑place dry density, the contractor must promptly rework, reshape, recompacted or replace the unsatisfactory zone and conduct a repeat acceptance test; if the retest still fails, the gauge standard count is checked, the gauge calibrated or repaired, and a new moisture‑density relationship test per PTM No. 106 may be required before amending the QC Plan. When bulk‑density values are below the required minimum (for example less than 95 % of laboratory maximum density for full‑depth reclamation control strips), records shall include any additional compaction passes, construction of a new control strip with its own set of ten random tests whose average density is at least 98 % of the original strip density, and moisture‑content adjustments verified by ASTM D2216, D44643 or AASHTO T 265. If target density is not achieved for asphalt, corrective actions include establishing a new optimum‑rolling pattern or suspending paving; work must be stopped if cores are not taken within one day after placement, if two consecutive lots or three total lots fail to reach the 100 % density payment factor, or if measured density deviates more than 2.0 % above or below Table E limits, in which case the contractor must investigate the cause, submit a corrective proposal to the Representative and obtain approval before resuming work. Additional corrective measures such as employing a self‑propelled smooth dual steel drum roller when pre‑ or post‑rolling thresholds are not met, backfilling core holes within 24 hours with SMA or Superpave mixture of the same NMAS and PG grade, and documenting all actions together with the corresponding test results are also required. Under Section 341 for cold recycled asphalt base course, the In‑Place Density record must show the target density taken from PTM No. 402, the measured density for each 3,000 sq yd segment obtained by AASHTO T 310, and the resulting percentage of that target; any segment that fails to reach the required minimum of 96 % of the PTM 402 target must be noted together with the corrective action taken, such as scarifying, adjusting moisture content, re‑compacting, or establishing a new PTM 402 target density, and the Representative’s stop‑work decision if the base course remains unacceptable. Under Section 412 for Superpave thin asphalt overlay courses, documentation must record that density was measured in the presence of the Representative and a certified asphalt field technician using an approved nuclear gauge per PTM 402 or an approved electrical impedance gauge per PTM No. 403, with the control strip technique employed to establish the optimum rolling pattern and observations noted that the mixture did not move under compaction equipment; all gauge readings are entered on the PTM 402 forms and submitted together with a copy of the nuclear‑gauge annual calibration certificate per ASTM D2950 and the operator’s training documentation, and any areas found loose, broken, or deficient must be removed, replaced with fresh 6.3 mm hot mix and re‑compacted according to the verified optimum pattern. Under Section 208 for special rolling, moisture‑density test results obtained after each compaction pass are recorded at several pre‑determined random locations in accordance with AASHTO T 191 or T 310, and it is noted whether the density increase was not more than 1 % over three consecutive passes before special rolling is authorized; the records also capture observed permanent deflection or rutting values—greater than ½ inch for existing subgrade or cuts and greater than 1 inch for embankment foundations—as criteria for unsatisfactory stability, together with the Representative’s approval or disapproval, and any corrective actions such as drying the material by disking or scarifying when moisture content exceeds optimum, or undercutting/excavating to a depth of 1 foot (for deflection greater than 0.5 inch and less than or equal to 3 inches) or up to 3 feet (for deflection greater than 3 inches) with the prescribed geotextile placement and backfill procedures. Under Section 312 for crushed aggregate base course, the In‑Place Density file must contain the maximum density value established by PTM No. 106 Method B and the field density results obtained with AASHTO T 191 for each compacted layer, together with any moisture‑control test data taken at delivery; for every layer that does not achieve not less than 100 % of the specified maximum density, the record must note the corrective step applied—initially additional compaction and, if that fails, the sequence of loosening, remoistening, reshaping and recompacting the entire low‑density area, or ultimately removal and replacement with a new mix when required density cannot be obtained. Under Section 405 for longitudinal joint density, inspection records must identify each longitudinal joint core by lot and sublot number, location, placement date, mixture type and designate it as an acceptance sample (Sample Class AS), while also providing the daily theoretical maximum specific gravity for each side of the joint; sampling procedures require one Representative‑selected 6‑inch core per sublot according to PTM 1 and PTM 729 plus a possible companion QC core, and on the first day five random 6‑inch cores are taken for quality‑control density testing; test results from PTM 715 or PTM 716 are submitted within 24 hours and used to calculate percent compaction and the Percent Within Limits, which must meet the lower specification limit of 91.0 %; if densities are inadequate, the Contractor may adjust mixture design or placement and compaction operations and must submit any revisions that affect the field quality‑control plan or job‑mix formula for Department review, and damaged cores are replaced from within 12 inches of the original location as directed by the Representative. Under Section 520 for pervious concrete pavement system, In‑Place Density records shall contain unit‑weight test results for every truck performed to ASTM C1688, core sampling data (three 4‑inch diameter cores per 5,000 sq ft with documented average core length of 3/8‑inch + 3/4‑inch and any individual length not less than –½‑inch), oven‑dry density results to ASTM C1754 together with void‑content values that must be within ±5 % of the average voids from the accepted test sections, and infiltration testing on three areas per 10,000 sq ft according to ASTM C1701 where the average rate must exceed 200 inches per hour; any concrete failing the infiltration requirement shall be removed and replaced, rain‑induced mortar loss shall be repaired or replaced at the Representative’s discretion, while the hardened density from the two test sections is also recorded as the project basis with a tolerance of ±5 pounds per cubic foot and not to exceed 130 pounds per cubic foot. Under Section 502 for roller‑compacted concrete acceptance, the inspection record must capture the compaction percentages for each of the five equal sublots that make up a lot and compute both the individual test values and the final average; density is accepted only when the final average is not less than 98 % of the maximum wet density and no single test is less than 96 % of the maximum wet density; if any density value falls below these limits, the area must be cored and the core tested for compressive strength according to AASHTO T‑24, with the core hole subsequently filled with RCC material or a rapid‑set concrete patching material, and if the lot also fails the compressive‑strength requirement it is deemed defective and must be removed and replaced as directed; documentation therefore includes the lot and sublot identifiers, recorded compaction percentages, calculated averages, any cores taken, the resulting compressive‑strength test results, and the corrective actions performed. Under Section 413 for courses with density acceptance by lots, inspection records shall capture the sublot depth measurements taken by the Inspector using PTM No. 737 density acceptance samples for courses with density acceptance by lots, as well as any mass‑per‑square‑meter (weight per square yard) calculations performed to verify yield for courses accepted by non‑movement or optimum rolling pattern; when yield indicates insufficient depth the inspector must log each 6‑inch diameter core drilled (one per 500 tons placed) and note any depth deficiency that exceeds more than ¼ inch, or more than ⅛ inch in three or more adjacent core locations, which triggers a defective‑work determination; the record should also detail corrective actions taken, such as backfilling, compacting and sealing the core holes with the original mixture and removing or replacing any pavement identified as defective. Under Section 419 for Stone Matrix Asphalt mixture design, the project file must contain the paving operation QC Plan (Form CS‑413) and pre‑placement meeting notes that record the agreed density acceptance criteria, a core‑sampling log showing that three pavement cores were taken from the first 100 tons placed for the trial demonstration, and the corresponding density test results together with the laboratory values for air voids at Ndesign Va and the mixture’s maximum specific gravity; plant testing records for the three loose samples (asphalt content, gradation and draindown) are also required, along with a copy of the AASHTO re:source On‑Site Laboratory Assessment that certifies the designated laboratory and any corrective‑action documentation submitted when test results failed to meet specification limits or when deficiencies were noted on the assessment. Under Section 344 for Full Depth Reclamation, In‑Place Density documentation should capture equipment calibration logs verified by the test strip, operator records of water and additive quantities and application times, the density results obtained for each 3,000 sq yd lot as determined per PTM No. 402 after the test strip, and any corrective actions such as additional test strips or adjustments made when non‑conforming conditions were identified. Under Section 412 for thin asphalt overlay courses, In‑Place Density documentation must record the density test results used for “Density Acceptance by Non‑Movement, Optimum‑Rolling Pattern” or “Density Acceptance by Optimum Rolling Pattern,” together with the associated payment factor percentages and any certification that the mixture meets specification; it must also capture the locations of original box samples and, if a retest is required, the 24‑inch offset sample locations, core drilling logs (including two 6‑inch diameter cores per location), rinsing, backfill actions, traffic control measures, and the test method employed (PTM 757 or PTM 702 unless changed by written approval). When a lot is deemed defective—i.e., when the payment factor for asphalt content and percent passing the 75 µm sieve are both 85 %—the contractor must submit a written retest request within three weeks along with quality‑control test results, control charts, companion sample data, trend evaluation, and any other pertinent information; these records constitute the corrective actions required for In‑Place Density compliance. For field technicians handling stone matrix asphalt the quality records must be identified on TR‑447 forms with project number, lot and sublot, station/offset, date and mixture type, then submitted to the testing laboratory; the Representative reviews the density, Gmm and asphalt‑content test results against the tolerances in Tables F, I and D, applying extreme‑value analysis and requiring at least three valid density results per lot, and verifies contractor daily Gmm values only when a randomly selected Gmm sample agrees within ±0.030 of the contractor’s value; acceptance is granted when the density meets the 100 % payment factor for consecutive lots or when the statistical review shows compliance, while any failure triggers a stop‑paving order, documentation of corrective measures and a review before work can resume, providing a documented basis for future performance evaluation. During full‑depth reclamation the contractor must record nuclear density readings taken after each roller pass on the compaction control strip and, after finishing, perform at least ten random in‑place density tests per 3 000‑square yard lot as required by PTM No. 1; these results are submitted to the Representative for verification against the minimum density of at least 95 % of the laboratory compacted maximum density at optimum moisture content specified in Section 344.3(e)3, and the recorded densities are reviewed together with the control‑strip data to confirm that each lot meets the acceptance criterion; any strip whose average falls below the required minimum triggers additional compaction or the construction of a new control strip which must achieve at least 98 % of the original strip density. Accepted density records become part of the permanent quality file and are used to demonstrate compliance for current acceptance and to provide baseline performance data for future evaluations of pavement durability. For thin asphalt overlay courses the contractor must record density readings obtained with an approved nuclear gauge or electrical impedance gauge in accordance with PTM 402 or PTM No 403, include the control‑strip data that established the optimum rolling pattern, attach the current nuclear‑gauge calibration certificate and operator training documentation, complete the forms supplied in PTM 402 and deliver those forms to the Representative; the Department accepts the mat density only when the mixture shows non‑movement under compaction equipment and the readings confirm the optimum rolling pattern as verified by the Representative during the control‑strip procedure, retaining the calibrated gauge certificates, training files and completed density forms provides a traceable data set that supports both immediate acceptance decisions and later performance assessments. For longitudinal‑joint asphalt pavement the contractor must test core samples in accordance with PTM 715 or PTM 716 and submit the results to the Representative within 24 hours together with identification of lot, sublot, location, placement dates, mixture type and the daily theoretical maximum specific gravity for each side of the joint; the Representative reviews all received results, computes the percent‑within‑limits and the average in‑place density applying a lower specification limit of 91 % with no upper limit, and the documented densities and PWL values become permanent quality records that may be referenced for future performance evaluation or any required adjustments to mixture design or compaction practices. For pervious concrete pavement the contractor must compile quality records for each test section and submit them to the Representative, including measured thickness that is not more than 1/4‑inch less than the design thickness, void content as defined by ASTM C1754 with a target of 20 % ± 5 %, and hardened density as defined by ASTM C1754 with a tolerance of ± 5 lb/ft³ and not to exceed 130 lb/ft³; three 4‑inch cores per 5 000 ft² must be removed, sent for oven‑dry density testing and void‑content analysis, and the average results compared against the accepted test‑section values (void content ± 5 % of the average voids); infiltration testing on three areas per 10 000 ft² shall be performed according to ASTM C1701; any location that does not meet a minimum average infiltration rate of 200 in/hr must be removed and replaced, with the documented average infiltration retained for future maintenance evaluation. For extended‑season superpave paving operations the contractor must submit all in‑place density data and related measurements on Form CS‑413EQC to the Representative within 24 hours of completion; the Representative reviews the submission together with the Extended‑Season Paving Plan to verify that the required density acceptance method—either pavement cores or optimum‑rolling pattern as specified—has been satisfied, and the Department later uses the submitted documentation in a spring performance survey that evaluates cracking, rutting and other distress indicators to determine whether the work is accepted or remedial action is required, and will withhold the Final Acceptance Certificate until any needed repairs are completed. For stone matrix asphalt trial demonstrations the contractor must obtain three pavement cores from a demonstration strip of at least 100 tons placed as identified on the approved paving operation QC Plan, test those cores for density and submit the results to the Representative; the records are reviewed during the pre‑placement meeting where the Representative checks the reported densities against the specification limits, acceptance is granted only when the recorded core densities satisfy those limits, and if any test fails either single or multiple sample tolerances another demonstration and new records must be provided before work may continue, thereby creating a documented basis for future performance evaluation. For full‑depth reclamation operators of water and additive applicators must keep proper records of material amounts and application times; these records become part of a QC Plan that is submitted at least two weeks before work begins, all equipment used to place and compact the material must be calibrated and the calibration verified through a 300‑foot test strip that demonstrates achievement of the minimum density required by 344.3(e)3.a, after the test strip confirms compliance n = 1 density is determined for each 3 000‑square‑yard lot in accordance with PTM No 402; if any aspect of the work is not adequately controlled additional test strips are constructed until the necessary control is established, providing documented corrective action that supports acceptance of full production and creates a baseline for future performance evaluation. When a retest of in‑place density is requested the contractor must include the quality‑control test results, control charts, companion sample data if available and a trend evaluation in a written request delivered to the District Executive within three weeks of the LTS test release; the Department reviews the submission and may allow retesting only when the information provides a reasonable basis that the original results do not represent the in‑place material, after evaluating repeatability the density test values used for the final payment factor percentage are either the original (if repeatable) or the retest values (if non‑repeatable), and accepted density values are then used to calculate the density payment factor percentage applied to the contract unit price and thus serve as both an acceptance criterion and a performance metric for future evaluation. Relationship to long-term performance

In‑place density measurements serve as the primary acceptance criterion that predicts a pavement’s ability to resist distress and achieve its intended service life. Under SECTION 206, moisture‑density acceptance tests require the in‑place dry density to reach at least 97 % of maximum dry density, except for the top three feet where 100 % of maximum dry density is required; when these targets are met the material is deemed stable with no rutting, displacement or shear wave, which prevents premature distress that would otherwise reduce durability. If the required density is not obtained, the specification mandates reshaping, re‑compacting and retesting because any permanent deformation (rutting) greater than ½ inch is classified as unstable and must be corrected before further work. SECTION 208 specifies that after each compaction pass, testing according to AASHTO T 191 or T 310 verifies that the material reaches the required density—97 % of maximum dry density for embankment foundations and 100 % of maximum dry density for structure foundations, subgrades, or material within three feet of the proposed subgrade elevation. When measured moisture‑density values do not increase by more than 1 % over three consecutive passes and the surface does not appear stable, the material is deemed too wet and must be dried and recompacted before special rolling; otherwise proceeding indicates that the achieved density will limit permanent deflection or rutting greater than ½ inch for existing subgrade or greater than 1 inch for embankment foundations, conditions associated with a higher likelihood of distress and reduced service life. Thus meeting these thresholds predicts adequate resistance to deformation and contributes to long‑term durability. SECTION 419 requires in‑place density verification by core testing according to PTM No. 715 (or PTM No. 716) to obtain percent compaction, with results required to fall within the limits set in Table F; samples no more than 2.0 % below the minimum or above the maximum still earn a reduced payment, while values outside those bounds are deemed defective work. The specifications further require that if density for two consecutive lots or a total of three lots does not meet the density‑payment factor percentage of 100, paving must be stopped and corrective action taken. Achieving the required compaction eliminates roller marks, flushing, displacement, shoving, cracking and aggregate breakage—distress mechanisms that would otherwise diminish pavement durability and service life. Under the stone matrix asphalt specifications, when a density acceptance lot is deemed defective the contractor may also submit a written request within three weeks for retesting; the LTS again employs the original cores—unless any were paraffin‑wax coated—to repeat bulk‑density testing per PTM No. 715 and PTM No. 716 and, where required, to determine theoretical maximum density, selecting one core with the highest bulk density from each production or placement date for Gmm testing according to AASHTO T 209 as modified in Bulletin 27; the laboratory compares repeatability of the original and retest results and uses the original numbers if they are repeatable, otherwise the retest numbers are used for the final payment factor. For full‑depth reclamation a minimum of ten random nuclear density measurements must be made on each compaction control strip after the reclaimed material has been compacted; the average in‑place density must equal at least 95 % of the laboratory maximum density, and if the readings fall below this threshold additional compaction is required, with a new control strip constructed and tested with another ten random measurements when the original strip does not achieve the target, the new strip being required to reach at least 98 % of the original control‑strip density. In addition, after construction a follow‑up verification is required in which the n=1 in‑place density of the reclaimed pavement is determined according to PTM No. 402 for each 3,000 square‑yard lot once the test strip has demonstrated that the minimum density specified in section 344.3(e)3.a can be achieved. Following placement of a cold‑recycled asphalt base course the material must be cured for at least two weeks when an emulsified‑asphalt stabilizing agent is used, or until the average moisture content drops below 2 % with a foamed‑asphalt stabilizer; before installing the wearing or binder course the contractor extracts a minimum of three randomly selected samples of at least 1,200 g each from throughout the project and determines their moisture content in accordance with PTM No. 749, backfilling and compacting the core holes thereafter. After a pervious concrete pavement has cured additional in‑place monitoring is performed by extracting three four‑inch diameter cores for every 5,000 sq ft and sending them to the laboratory for oven‑dry density testing per ASTM C1754 together with void‑content analysis; the core void content is accepted within ± 5 % of the average voids measured on the test sections, and the cores must also satisfy length criteria of an average of 3/8‑inch plus 3/4‑inch and no individual measurement less than –1/2‑inch.
