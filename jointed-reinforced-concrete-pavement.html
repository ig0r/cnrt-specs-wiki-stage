Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement

Part of: Rigid Pavement is-subtopic-of
Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement is a topic under Rigid Pavement.
reinforced-cement-concretereinforced-cement-concrete-pavementsreinforced-concrete-pavementsjointed-reinforced-concrete-pavement
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-28

Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement (JRCP) is a full‑width reconstruction technique for Portland cement concrete slabs that extends at least five hundred feet, designed to remedy deteriorated joints and restore adequate transverse load transfer across the pavement [1][2]. It accomplishes this by installing dowelled joints whose dowel bars are coated and bonded along their entire embedded length, using perpendicular joints spaced per RC‑21M (typically thirty‑foot spacing) and sealing them with appropriate expansion material or cellular polystyrene to reestablish joint integrity [1]. Although reinforcement is placed in the slab, it does not add structural strength; performance depends on concrete quality, correct joint geometry, and the prescribed load‑transfer devices and repair actions such as removal of unsound concrete and treatment of exposed dowels [1][2].

Purpose and design objectives

Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement is intended to remedy the problem of deteriorated joints and loss of load transfer that occur in existing concrete pavements when reconstructing all adjacent traffic lanes over a length of 500 feet or more, thereby providing a joint system capable of adequate transverse load transfer for proper pavement performance. The design also seeks to address the engineering issue of concrete delamination at transverse or longitudinal joints, which can compromise slab continuity and serviceability, by specifying repair actions that remove unsound concrete, create a rough bonded surface, protect or treat exposed dowel bars, and reestablish the joint with appropriate expansion material or cellular polystyrene to restore joint integrity. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–89); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 427–428)

The design of Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement must achieve adequate long‑term performance by providing effective load transfer at transverse joints, which is accomplished through dowelled joints that are bonded along their entire embedded length and sealed with coated dowel bars; joint spacing must follow the standards—30‑foot spacing for JRCP and 15‑foot spacing for JPCP—with perpendicular orientation and appropriate seals. When reconstruction exceeds ±500 feet or when patches leave ten feet or less of original slab between them, a full‑depth replacement with an appropriately increased slab thickness is required, and tie bars are only needed for patches longer than 65 feet. The pavement must avoid high early strength concrete because of its potential for excessive shrinkage cracking, and any accelerated concrete used for early opening must meet the minimum compressive strength specified in Publication 408 Section 501. Together these criteria ensure functional load transfer, structural integrity, durability against cracking, safety during construction and traffic reopening, and serviceability through uniform support and joint performance. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–89)

Scope and applicability

Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement (JRCP) is appropriate for a full‑width reconstruction of all adjacent traffic lanes when the work extends at least 500 feet and the pavement is to be placed with Portland cement concrete; under those conditions the design must follow Publication 72M Roadway Construction Standards RC‑21M, which prescribes 30‑foot joint spacing, perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals, and the requirement for JRCP must be identified during preliminary engineering because the reinforcement does not provide additional strength compared with plain concrete. JRCP is also appropriate when a widening or lane‑addition project involves an existing rigid pavement that was originally constructed as JRCP; in such cases the new slab may be placed with reinforced concrete or plain cement concrete and shall conform to the same thickness and joint‑spacing criteria applied to the original pavement. For full lane additions that are at least 10 feet wide, the design shall use the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic conditions dictate a different design, while for partial lane widenings of less than 10 feet the new work shall match the existing pavement type and depth; transverse joints shall be aligned with the original spacing and, when the original joint spacing exceeds 20 feet, shall be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96)

In section 4.2 Concrete Pavement Patching, when a roadway segment of roughly ±500 feet requires many intermittent patches leaving only ten feet or less of original slab between them, the section should be removed and reconstructed in full rather than using jointed reinforced concrete pavement. In the same section, for a full‑width reconstruction of all lanes that exceeds 500 feet, the district may select either jointed plain concrete pavement (JPCP) or jointed reinforced concrete pavement (JRCP), but the reinforcement in JRCP is noted as not adding any additional strength, making JPCP an acceptable alternative. The use of high early‑strength concrete should be avoided because of its potential for excessive shrinkage cracking, and if rapid traffic opening is required the specifications direct designers to consider accelerated concrete or precast concrete paving instead of a conventional reinforced slab. In section 4.8 Widening and Lane Additions, when an existing jointed reinforced concrete pavement is being widened or a lane added, the guidelines permit using plain cement concrete instead of reinforced concrete; this option is explicitly allowed for JRCP widenings and becomes mandatory when the original transverse joint spacing exceeds 20 feet, at which point the widening must be done with JPCP and joint spacing must be adjusted to between 10 feet and 17 feet. For partial lane widenings that are less than 10 feet, the new section shall match the existing pavement type and depth, so a change to another material is not required unless other criteria dictate it. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96)

Design inputs and requirements

The design and construction of jointed reinforced concrete pavement are governed by PennDOT’s Roadway Construction Standards Publication 72M, specifically RC‑21M, which prescribes a 30‑foot joint spacing, perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals [1]. It is also subject to the State Specifications in Publication 408, including Sections 516 and 519 that define how full‑depth patches and new joints are to be designed, constructed and documented [1]. When reconstructing all adjacent traffic lanes for 500 feet or more with Portland cement concrete, the design must follow RC‑21M (or RC‑27M for jointed plain concrete pavement) and the associated pay items must be identified during preliminary engineering [1]. Accelerated‑setting patch work shall meet the minimum compressive strength and timing requirements set out in Publication 408 Section 501 [1]. The use of precast or high‑performance dowel bars shall comply with the relevant sections of Publication 408 such as Section 704 for Class AA cement concrete [1]. Widening or lane addition projects for jointed reinforced concrete pavement are required to use Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted, a policy that applies to all uniform‑depth rigid pavements built since 1945 [1]. Full lane additions that are at least 10 feet wide shall be designed to the same thickness as the existing pavement unless heavier traffic loads dictate otherwise, in which case design shall reflect the actual traffic [1]. Partial widenings under 10 feet must match the existing pavement type and depth [1]. Widening of jointed plain concrete pavement shall be constructed with plain cement concrete, while jointed reinforced concrete pavement may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete [1]. For new sections, transverse joints must align with those on the original pavement; when the original joint spacing is 20 feet or less the same slab length is used, and if it exceeds 20 feet the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet [1]. All lane additions and widenings shall be tied to the existing pavement and any deviation from these criteria requires an engineering‑judgment exemption with supporting documentation [1]. The design and construction of jointed reinforced concrete pavement repairs are governed by the Rigid Pavements specifications in Section 525 of Publication 408 [2]. A quality‑control plan approved under Section 106.03(a)2, covering all phases from removal to curing, is required for partial‑depth repair work [2]. Repair zones must be rectangular with minimum dimensions of 15 inches long, 10 inches wide and 2 inches deep, and any areas less than 24 inches apart are combined into a single repair zone [2]. Concrete removal shall not be shallower than 2 inches nor deeper than half the slab thickness [2]. For Type 1 repairs a perimeter saw cut of at least 2 inches depth is required; milling heads must be V‑head, flat or rounded, and chipping hammers may not exceed 35 pounds [2]. Joint treatment in repairs mandates installing ½‑inch expansion joint material in transverse joints and ¼‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene in longitudinal joints, each extending at least ½ inch below the repair bottom, with sealing as specified in Sections 512.3 and 521.3 [2].

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 427–428)

Design methods and criteria

In section 4.2 concerning concrete pavement patching, when full‑width reconstruction of all adjacent lanes exceeds about 500 feet, designers must follow Publication 72M Roadway Construction Standards RC‑21M for jointed reinforced concrete pavement (JRCP). That specification calls for 30‑foot joint spacing, perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals. A pavement design analysis may be required to check whether a thicker slab depth is needed, and the reinforcement in JRCP does not add any additional strength. In section 4.8 dealing with widening and lane additions, the design follows the procedures set out in Chapter 4 Section 4.8. The work shall be designed using Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted; full lane additions that are ten feet wide or more shall be given the same slab thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic requires a different design, while partial widenings narrower than ten feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth. Transverse joints in the new section must be aligned with those on the existing pavement, using the original joint spacing when it is twenty feet or less, and otherwise using joint lengths between ten feet and seventeen feet with intermediate joints as needed to keep slab lengths approximately equal. The addition shall be tied to the existing pavement and sound engineering judgment must be exercised, and any deviation from these criteria requires an exemption request supported by documentation. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96)

For projects that require full‑width reconstruction of all adjacent lanes for at least 500 feet, the jointed reinforced concrete pavement shall be designed to Publication 72M RC‑21M, which calls for 30‑foot joint spacing, joints that are perpendicular, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals; the reinforcement in JRCP is assumed not to add any additional strength. If the same length of pavement is rebuilt as jointed plain concrete pavement, RC‑27M requires 15‑foot joint spacing but otherwise identical joint orientation, dowel coating and sealing requirements. The District may choose either a JPCP or JRCP pavement, but it should be noted that the reinforcement in JRCP does not add any additional strength. Widening or lane additions for Jointed Reinforced Cement Concrete Pavement must follow the Class AA cement concrete policy unless an exemption is granted. Full lane additions (≥ 10 feet) shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic requires a different design, and partial lane widenings (< 10 feet) shall match existing pavement type and depth. JRCP may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete, while transverse joints must align with the original pavement; when the original joint spacing is 20 feet or less the same slab length is used, but when it exceeds 20 feet the joint spacing in the widening shall be limited to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet. Under Section 525.3 Construction for partial‑depth repair of jointed reinforced concrete pavement, the repair area must be rectangular with minimum dimensions of 15 inches in length by 10 inches in width by 2 inches in depth and any two repairs spaced less than 24 inches apart shall be combined into a single repair. The unsound concrete is to be removed to at least 2 inches depth but not deeper than one‑half of the slab thickness, and removal tools are limited to a chipping hammer weighing no more than 35 pounds operated at a 30–60° angle to avoid additional damage. Prior to placing repair material the surface must be cleaned with compressed air free of contaminants at a pressure of at least 100 psi measured at the source, and when the repair abuts an existing joint the appropriate joint filler—1/2‑inch expansion joint material for transverse joints or 1/4‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene for longitudinal joints—shall be installed extending at least 1/2 inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 427–428)

Structural section and dimensions

In section 4.2 Concrete Pavement Patching, the design standard RC‑21M for Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement requires a 30‑foot joint spacing with perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals; no other pavement, base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab, shoulder, edge‑support or reinforcement dimensions are given in this section. In section 4.8 Widening and Lane Additions, a full lane addition defined as ≥ 10 feet shall be designed with the same thickness as the existing pavement unless heavier traffic requires otherwise; a partial lane widening of < 10 feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth. For Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement widenings, transverse joint spacing must follow the original pattern: if the original spacing is 20 feet or less, slabs are constructed with the same length as on the original pavement; if it exceeds 20 feet, the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet. Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96)

For jointed reinforced concrete pavement, reconstruction of all lanes over 500 feet must follow Publication 72M RC‑21M, which calls for 30‑foot transverse joint spacing, perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals; a pavement design analysis shall be performed to decide if a thicker slab depth is required. When only one lane is reconstructed over 500 feet the new joints must align with existing joints and an intermediate joint shall be added wherever existing spacing exceeds 20 feet to satisfy RC‑27M requirements. Tie bars or tie bolts are required only for concrete patches longer than 65 feet, and cross‑slope after installing precast slabs is restored by diamond grinding because the delivered slab elevation is set at least ¼ inch higher than the surrounding pavement. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–89)

Materials and mixture design

For widening and lane addition projects that involve Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement, the specification shall require the use of Class AA cement concrete unless an exemption is granted; any widening of jointed plain concrete pavement (JPCP) must be constructed with plain cement concrete, while Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement (JRCP) may be widened with either reinforced or plain cement concrete. For partial‑depth repair of Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement the specification must call for installing a ½‑inch expansion joint material in any transverse joint that the repair abuts and a ¼‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene filler in any longitudinal joint, each extending at least ½ inch below the bottom of the repair when possible. The repair mix shall be placed using either volumetrically mixed concrete as defined in Section 704.3 or hand‑batched mixtures prepared in a power‑driven revolving and tilting drum mixer or a suitable revolving paddle mixer capable of thoroughly blending the material, and when such mixers are used the batch size must fall within the mixer’s recommended minimum and maximum limits. Any exposed dowel bars shall receive a bond‑breaking material on all surfaces, and the final cleaning of the repair area must be performed with a compressed‑air stream that is free of oil, moisture, and other contaminants and provides at least 100 psi measured at the source. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 95–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 427–428)

Jointed reinforced concrete pavement (JRCP) should be placed using the standard Portland cement concrete mix called for in Publication 408 Section 704, employing an optimized aggregate gradation to meet structural and durability goals; however, designers must recognize that the reinforcement in JRCP does not add any additional strength, so the concrete quality governs performance. High‑early‑strength concrete is discouraged because it can cause excessive shrinkage cracking, and when a rapid opening to traffic is required the mix may be switched to an accelerated concrete that meets the minimum compressive‑strength timing requirements of Publication 408 Section 501. For reconstruction projects longer than 500 ft a pavement design analysis may require a thicker slab depth, while joint spacing for JRCP follows the 30‑foot requirement with perpendicular joints and coated dowel bars as specified in RC‑21M. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–89)

Structural details and interfaces

During reconstruction of a jointed reinforced concrete pavement that extends five hundred feet or more, the design must follow RC‑21M, which requires thirty‑foot joint spacing, perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals. Reinforcement is provided in the slab but does not add any additional strength. Load transfer across the transverse patch joints is achieved by dowelling all patch joints and securely anchoring the dowels to the existing pavement with epoxy, polyester or vinylester binders so that the dowels are bonded throughout their entire embedded length. If only a single lane is reconstructed for five hundred feet or more, new joints must be aligned with existing joints and an intermediate joint must be placed when the existing spacing exceeds twenty feet. When widening or adding lanes to a jointed reinforced concrete pavement, the transverse joints shall be aligned with those of the existing pavement. If the original joint spacing is twenty feet or less, the new slabs shall match the original slab length; if the original spacing exceeds twenty feet, the joint spacing in the widened area shall be limited to a minimum of ten feet and a maximum of seventeen feet, with intermediate joints added as needed. The widening may be constructed with either reinforced or plain cement concrete, and widening of JPCP shall be constructed with plain cement concrete. Any lane addition or widening shall be tied to the existing pavement. In a partial‑depth repair the joint treatment follows the existing joint type: if the repair abuts an existing transverse joint, install one‑half‑inch expansion joint material in the joint and the material should extend at least one‑half inch below the bottom of the repair. If the repair abuts an existing longitudinal joint, install one‑quarter‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene in the joint with the same minimum extension of at least one‑half inch below the bottom of the repair. Cut and remove any reinforcement in jointed reinforced concrete pavement exposed after removal of delaminated concrete. If dowel bars in the transverse joint are exposed and show little sign of corrosion they should receive a bond‑breaking material on all exposed surfaces. Longitudinal joints are sealed as specified in Section 512.3 and transverse joints as specified in Section 521.3. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 427–428)

In Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement (JRCP) the designer shall provide transverse load‑transfer devices by dowelling all patch joints and securely anchoring the dowels to the existing pavement with epoxy, polyester or vinylester binders so that the dowel is bonded along its entire embedded length. New pavement joints shall follow Publication 72M; for JRCP the joint spacing shall be 30 feet, joints shall be perpendicular, dowel bars shall be coated and appropriate seals shall be provided, while Jointed Plain Concrete Pavement (JPCP) calls for a 15‑foot joint spacing with the same details. When reconstruction involves all lanes over 500 feet these spacings apply to every new joint; when only one lane is rebuilt the new joints shall be aligned with existing joints and an intermediate joint shall be placed if the existing joint spacing exceeds 20 feet. The design also requires specifying skewed or non‑skewed joint baskets as shown on RC‑26M and including longitudinal tie bars or bolts for patches longer than 65 feet. For widening or lane additions, full lane additions that are at least 10 feet shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic loads require design for actual traffic, and partial lane widenings that are less than 10 feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth. Transverse joints on the widening or lane addition shall be aligned to match those on the original pavement; when the original transverse joint spacing is 20 feet or less slabs of the same length as on the original pavement shall be used, and when it exceeds 20 feet the joint spacing shall be adjusted to a minimum length of 10 feet and a maximum length of 17 feet with intermediate joints so that slab lengths are approximately equal. For partial‑depth repairs in JRCP that meet an existing transverse joint the design calls for placing a half‑inch expansion joint material in the joint, and when meeting a longitudinal joint a quarter‑inch preformed cellular polystyrene shall be inserted; both fillers should extend at least a half‑inch below the bottom of the repair whenever possible. If the repair mix can be sawed without damage the joint is re‑established by sawing through the full depth of the repair, and the finished joint is then sealed according to the procedures in Section 512.3 for longitudinal joints and Section 521.3 for transverse joints. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 427–428)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

Under the requirements of section 4.2 Concrete Pavement Patching, a jointed reinforced concrete pavement shall be designed in accordance with the geometric criteria set out in Publication 72M. For RC‑21M the geometry calls for 30‑foot joint spacing, perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals; for RC‑27M the geometry calls for 15‑foot joint spacing while retaining perpendicular joints, coated dowel bars and appropriate seals. When only one lane is reconstructed for a length of 500 feet or more, the new joints should be aligned with the existing joints and an intermediate joint must be placed if the existing spacing exceeds 20 feet; longitudinal tie bars are required only in patches longer than 65 feet. Early opening to traffic may be achieved by using accelerated concrete rather than high‑early‑strength mixes, which are discouraged because of shrinkage cracking.

In addition, section 4.8 Widening and Lane Additions specifies that any widening or new lane on a jointed reinforced concrete pavement shall follow the geometric criteria for rigid pavements. Full lane additions that are at least 10 feet wide shall be designed to the same thickness as the original pavement unless heavier traffic requires a different design, while partial widenings under 10 feet shall match the existing pavement type and depth. Transverse joints in the new section shall be aligned with those on the existing pavement; when the original joint spacing is 20 feet or less the same slab length may be used, but if it exceeds 20 feet the joint spacing must be adjusted to a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 17 feet with intermediate joints added so that slab lengths are approximately equal. The widening may use plain cement concrete for JPCP and either reinforced or plain cement concrete for JRCP, and the new lane shall be tied to the existing pavement. [1]

[1] — Ch. 4 (pp. 84–96)
