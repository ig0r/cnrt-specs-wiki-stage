Mixing Plant

Part of: Asphalt Concrete (HMA) is-subtopic-of
Mixing Plant is a topic under Asphalt Concrete (HMA).
mixing-plantcentral-mix-plant
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

A mixing plant is a fully‑automated, inspected and listed facility—Bulletin 42 for cement‑concrete and Bulletin 41 for asphalt—that provides separate covered compartments for cement, fine and coarse aggregates, water and additives, each supplied by calibrated, individually controlled gates or meters[2]. By proportioning ingredients to tight tolerances (cement 0 to +4 %, fine and coarse aggregate ±2 %, admixtures ±3 % and water ±2 %) and keeping the drive‑shaft speed within 50 rpm of its set point, the auger‑type or twin‑shaft pug mill delivers a uniform blend without segregation while allowing real‑time monitoring of proportions[2][1]. The plant also controls mix temperature (50–90 °F for general concrete, 50–80 °F for bridge decks), air‑content variation (≤1.5 %), and delivery time (discharge within 1½ h of mixing) to satisfy the precise proportioning and performance requirements of pavement structures such as concrete, roller‑compacted concrete, stone matrix asphalt, Superpave and cold‑recycled mixes[2].

Purpose and applicability

The mixing plant solves the problem of producing concrete with precisely proportioned ingredients and uniform consistency required for pavement structures (Section 704.3) by providing separate covered compartments, a calibrated feeder system with individually controlled gates and an auger‑type mixer that discharges without segregation; this arrangement lets the operator monitor proportions in real time and keeps each batch within the weight tolerances of 0 to +4 % for cement, ±2 % for fine aggregate, ±2 % for coarse aggregate, ±3 % for admixtures and ±2 % for water, while maintaining drive‑shaft speed within 50 rpm of the operating speed and limiting batch intervals to no more than a half hour. For plant‑mixed cement concrete (Section 704.2) it addresses the need to control air content, temperature and delivery time so that air content does not vary by more than 1.5 %, concrete temperature after mixing remains between 50°F and 90°F for general mixes or between 50°F and 80°F for bridge‑deck mixes, and discharge occurs within 1 ½ hours of mixing (or within 1 hour when the concrete temperature is 80°F or above). For stone matrix asphalt mixtures (Section 419.3) it provides a fully automated, recorded plant listed in Bulletin 41 that ensures both aggregates and the finished mixture are free of unburned fuel oil and excess moisture and can be modified to incorporate warm‑mix technology additives or modifiers as directed by the Technical Representative, thereby delivering a uniform blend that complies with SMA specifications. For Superpave mixtures (Section 413.3) it likewise requires a fully automated, recorded facility listed in Bulletin 41; when recycled material is used the plant must be altered to weigh RAP/RAS before entering the pug mill, employ specially designed bins, conveyors and chutes that prevent segregation or sticking, dry and heat virgin aggregate and RAP/RAS so that the finished mix falls within the temperature range set out in Table A, and guarantee the aggregate is free of unburned fuel oil and excess moisture as required by Section 413.2(e)1.d.1. For roller‑compacted concrete (Section 502.3) it solves the problem of supplying a mixture that conforms exactly to the approved mix design by producing the RCC pavement mixture in the defined proportions and within the specified tolerances, and the plant must be able to produce a uniform mixture at a rate compatible with the placement equipment. For crushed‑aggregate base courses (Section 312.3) it meets the need for correct proportioning and uniform blending by requiring acceptance of the processing and mixing plant before work begins, measuring coarse and fine material separately by volume or weight, feeding them in proper proportions at a rate that ensures correct blending, accurately metering added water, and using automatic cutoff devices that stop feed to the mixer if delivery of any component stops, thus preventing segregation or incorrect ratios. For SMA mixtures (Section 419.1 f) it resolves the problem of meeting design‑specified volumetric, gradation, moisture‑susceptibility and coating requirements by integrating the approved warm‑mix additive during production, performing daily draindown testing according to AASHTO T 305 with a 1‑hour reading, checking particle coating to achieve at least 95.0 % coated particles, and increasing mixing time or making other adjustments when the coating target is not met. For cold‑recycled asphalt base courses (Section 342.3) it meets the need for a homogeneous mixture without binder stripping by using equipment that automatically meters liquids with a variation of not more than +2.0 % by weight and positive‑displacement pumps capable of accurately metering emulsified‑asphalt additives down to 4 gallons per minute, while accurate water metering maintains sufficient moisture for thorough mixing and compaction. For cold‑recycled asphalt base course, in‑place (Section 341.3) it solves the problem of delivering and blending liquids accurately during construction by using a pugmill mixer and liquid‑additive system that automatically meters liquids within a ±2.0 % variation by weight and maintains adequate total liquids to ensure thorough mixing of reclaimed material, aggregate and emulsified or foamed asphalt binder. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502.2) it addresses the need to produce RCC that consistently meets the prescribed mix design and quality tolerances by providing a controlled environment for complete and uniform blending of cement, aggregates, water, admixtures and other ingredients, while not exceeding the manufacturer’s rated volume capacity for dry concrete in the mixing chamber, keeping the mixer interior free of hardened material, monitoring blade wear, and supplying daily production records so that plant calibration can be verified against the tolerances set in Section 704.1(e). For polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt micro‑surfacing (Section 483.3) it solves the problem of producing a uniformly blended mixture with precisely proportioned ingredients and continuous flow, thereby preventing segregation, premature setting, lumps or side build‑up; it achieves this by using a self‑propelled front‑feed continuous‑loading mixer linked to a chain‑dragged conveyor belt and a positive displacement water‑jacketed gear pump for accurate proportioning, a twin‑shafted multi‑bladed pug mill at least 50 inches long, and calibrated material control devices that allow the inspector to verify amounts in real time, while introducing polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt beyond the first quarter point of the mixer to ensure thorough mixing before addition. For an asphalt treated permeable base course (Section 360.3) it addresses the need to create a permeable base that can be properly coated, bound and placed without material segregation or excessive cooling by drying the aggregate as necessary, heating the asphalt so that combining with aggregate produces a completed mixture at a temperature below 320 °F that also provides suitable viscosity for adequate coating of aggregate particles and does not cause segregation during transportation, and requiring placement within 8 hours from when it is made to avoid stockpiling. For an asphalt seal coat using precoated aggregate (Section 471.2) it solves the problem of producing a free‑flowing, uniformly coated pre‑coated aggregate that meets strict pavement specifications by drying the aggregate in the plant and pre‑coating it with 0.6 % to 1.2 % residual asphalt binder, mixing at a temperature not exceeding 350 °F, achieving a thin brownish film that covers at least 90 % of the visible surface area while limiting stockpile height to no more than 4 feet, and ensuring that uncoated material passing the 75 µm sieve does not exceed 0.5 %. In the Pavement Policy Manual, a mobile mixing plant is appropriate when producing FB‑1 wearing and binder courses because those mixes are cold; the preferred practice is to mix and place them with a mobile plant, although they may also be mixed in a stationary plant if needed, and this approach is recommended for low‑volume roads that have highly flexible existing pavement structures. By contrast, FB‑2 specifications require mixing at a stationary mixing plant and the hot‑mix material must be placed while hot using PG 58S-28 or PG 64S-22 asphalt cement, so a stationary mixing plant should be used for FB‑2 hot mixes. For cold‑recycled asphalt base courses, central‑plant mixing is appropriate when surplus millings are available or the existing asphalt pavement must be removed to permit stabilization, grade adjustment, or other treatment of underlying materials because the material can be produced off‑site and placed as required. Conversely, for projects that are far from a central mix plant—such as collector or local access highways—or where economic advantage is sought, an in‑place cold‑recycling method should be used instead of central‑plant mixing. Under Section 704.1 of the Pennsylvania specifications, a Plant Mixed Cement Concrete method using a stationary commercial, central or near‑site plant is appropriate when the concrete must be proportioned and mixed at such a plant and then delivered by truck, agitator truck or mixer truck. If the project calls for dry batching at a proportioning plant with water added only after the ingredients are placed in the truck drum, the Truck Mixed Cement Concrete method should be used instead and mixing may not occur en route or on site. When on‑site proportioning and immediate discharge are required, a Volumetric Mixed Cement Concrete method using a mobile truck‑mounted mixer is preferred over a fixed plant. A volumetric mixing plant that is inspected and listed in Bulletin 42 may be employed for concrete needed for endwalls, inlets, manholes, end anchors, signposts or other miscellaneous structures that require small quantities, and it may also be used for pavement patching only if written permission is obtained from the District Executive; when the work does not fall within those listed items or lacks such written approval, a volumetric mixing plant is not appropriate and another concrete production method must be selected. For cold‑recycled asphalt base courses under Section 342.3, a central‑plant mixed system is appropriate whenever a CPMCRABC is required, provided the plant includes the specified RAP feed hopper, conveyor belts, twin‑shaft pugmill, metered emulsified or foamed asphalt system, cement and water additive systems, pumps, spray bars and an accepted continuous‑flow or batch mixer, with equipment capable of automatically metering liquids within a variation of not more than + 2.0 % by weight, automatic shut‑off interlock, calibrated meters for water and additive flow, and foamed‑asphalt sampling capability. Placement of CPMCRABC is prohibited from September 1 to April 30 in Districts 1‑0, 2‑0, 3‑0, 4‑0, 9‑0 and 10‑0 and from October 1 to April 30 in all other districts, and it may proceed only when no freezing temperatures have occurred for 24 hours before paving and the ambient air temperature is 45 °F and rising; under those conditions another construction method must be used. For micro‑surfacing under Section 483.3, a self‑propelled front‑feed continuous‑loading mixing machine is appropriate when the job area is less than twenty thousand square yards and the site does not involve limited‑access highways or municipal projects; in larger or restricted situations a different production method such as a stationary plant must be used instead. For asphalt seal coat using pre‑coated aggregate under Section 471.2, a mixing plant is appropriate when the pre‑coating is applied with asphalt cement; in that case the contractor dries the aggregate in a batch or continuous type asphalt plant conforming to Bulletin 27, mixes at a temperature not exceeding 350 °F, provides a residual binder of 0.6 % to 1.2 % by weight, stocks the material on a suitable base no higher than four feet while ensuring at least ninety percent of the aggregate surface is covered and that any uncoated fraction passing the No. 200 sieve does not exceed 0.5 %. When damp aggregate is used with cut‑back asphalts, the contractor may instead employ a stabilization plant, apply a pre‑coat containing 0.4 % to 1.0 % residual binder, keep the aggregate temperature at or below 150 °F and cure the material before use. Required inputs

A cement‑concrete mixing plant must be inspected and listed under Bulletin 42 and provide separate covered compartments for cement, fine aggregate, coarse aggregate, water and additives, and aggregate bins may not be charged more than four hours before mixing. The plant must proportion, measure and batch ingredients by a weight‑equivalent method so that cement is within zero to plus 4 % of the design weight, each fine and coarse aggregate is within plus or minus 2 %, admixtures are within plus or minus 3 %, and water is within plus or minus 2 %. During mixing the drive‑shaft speed shall be kept within 50 rpm of the operating speed and batches must be placed continuously with no more than a half‑hour gap. The mixing time for normal strength mixes shall be at least 50 seconds but not exceed 90 seconds, and for high‑early‑strength mixes at least 70 seconds. Concrete temperature after mixing shall be kept between 50 °F and 90 °F for general concrete or between 50 °F and 80 °F for bridge deck concrete, and the air‑content of sampled batches may not vary by more than 1.5 % before correction. The plant must deliver mixed concrete to the work site within one and a half hours of mixing, or up to two hours when a retarding admixture is used; in hot weather or when concrete temperature reaches 80 °F the time between mixing completion and discharge may not exceed one hour unless an approved retarder is added. Placement of the mixed material shall be discontinued when ambient air temperature falls below 40 °F, when the surface is frozen, or when freezing temperatures are forecast within the next 24 hours. For roller‑compacted concrete the plant must produce the mixture in the proportions defined by the approved mix design and within the specified tolerances, delivering a uniform mix at a rate compatible with placement equipment; batch‑type plants shall meet the requirements of Sections 704.2 and 704.3 while continuous plants shall comply with AASHTO M 241. RCC material supplied to the plant may not contain frost, lumps or crusts of hardened material and must be kept within a temperature range of 50 °F to 90 °F during placement; placement must be discontinued when ambient air temperature away from artificial heat falls to 40 °F or when the RCC mixture temperature exceeds 90 °F after mixing, and concrete may not be placed if the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 lb/ft²·hr unless mitigation such as windbreaks or fog misting is used. A Stone Matrix Asphalt (SMA) plant must be a fully‑automated facility listed in Bulletin 41 and provide at least 120 ft² of office space, and both aggregates and the finished mix shall be free of unburned fuel oil and excess moisture. Any warm‑mix asphalt technology additives or modifiers must be introduced according to the Technical Representative’s directions so that a uniform blend is achieved. The stabilizer (fiber) supply system must meter the fibers by weight within plus or minus 10 % of the required amount, include low‑level and no‑flow indicators, a feed‑rate printout in pounds per minute, and a transparent pipe for visual flow verification, and the plant must ship SMA material to the job site within two hours of mixing. Placement of the SMA mix is prohibited when surfaces are wet or when air or surface temperature is 50 °F or lower, and it may not be placed during seasonal windows specified for each district without written permission from the District Executive. A Superpave asphalt plant must also be fully‑automated and recorded in Bulletin 41; for recycled mixtures the plant must be modified to weigh reclaimed asphalt pavement (RAP) or reclaimed asphalt shingle (RAS) before it enters the pug mill, use cold‑feed bins and conveyors that prevent segregation or sticking, dry virgin aggregate and RAP/RAS, and heat both aggregates so that after adding RAP/RAS the finished mix falls within the temperature range set out in Table A for the material class. All aggregates and the completed mixture must be free of unburned fuel oil and excess moisture, and any warm‑mix asphalt technology additives or modifiers must be incorporated according to the technical representative’s directions to achieve a uniform blend. Placement may only occur within the seasonal windows—April 1 to October 15 for PG 64E‑22 wearing courses (or other shallow depths) and April 1 to October 31 for all other courses—and never when surfaces are wet or air/surface temperature is 40 °F or lower; when ambient temperature is below 50 °F from October 1 through April 30, hauled loads must be transported in tightly sealed, insulated (or double‑walled/heated) trucks and a three‑eighths‑inch hole near the top of each truck side must be provided for temperature checks. For crushed‑aggregate base courses the plant must be accepted before work and be capable of measuring coarse and fine aggregates separately by volume or weight, feeding them in proper proportions, and metering water by weight, volume, or a metering device for uniform distribution. When directed calcium chloride may be added before water at the rate of seven pounds per ton for Type 1 and 5.7 pounds per ton for Type 2, and the plant should include automatic cutoff devices that stop feed if any component stops. Materials containing frost or frozen pieces are prohibited, and placement must not occur on a frozen surface, when air temperature is below 40 °F, or when freezing temperatures are forecast within the next 24 hours. A central mixing plant used for cold‑recycled asphalt base courses shall be equipped with a RAP feed hopper, conveyor belts, a twin‑shaft pugmill, metered emulsified or foamed asphalt additive, metered cement additive and water additive systems. The liquid‑metering system shall maintain the flow rate of liquids within a variation of not more than plus 2.0 % by weight, and water is added by calibrated meter as needed for compaction. Placement of the cold‑recycled mix is prohibited from September 1 to April 30 in Districts 1‑0 through 4‑0, 9‑0 and 10‑0, and from October 1 to April 30 elsewhere; when placement is allowed no freezing temperatures may have occurred for at least 24 hours and the ambient air temperature must be 45 °F and rising. Cold‑recycled base mixes produced at a mixing plant require reclaimed asphalt pavement together with virgin coarse aggregate (No. 8, 57, or 67) and, when higher traffic is anticipated, polymer‑modified emulsions; the mixtures have lower initial structural strength, are less resistant to abrasion, possess higher void content that makes a wearing surface imperative, and must be compacted to a depth no less than 3 inches and no more than 5 inches. A minimum of one week of curing under low humidity‑high temperature is required before placing the wearing course. Suitable projects are those where the existing road has at least 2 inches of asphalt material (or can be supplemented with reclaimed or virgin aggregate to meet the 3‑inch minimum), traffic can be controlled or detoured during base construction and curing, and projects carrying significantly heavy truck traffic (i.e., 200 or more daily ESALs) should not be selected for cold recycling. For FB‑2 surface courses the mix design calls for PG 58S‑28 or PG 64S‑22 asphalt cement and must be mixed hot at a stationary mixing plant; the resulting mix is highly flexible because it has a high void content and uses binder that is softer than PG 64S‑22. FB‑1 mixes are “cold” blends that may be produced by a mobile plant or a stationary plant and also exhibit a high void content and greater flexibility due to the use of softer asphalt materials. After any FB surface is placed, a minimum of 3 months of warm‑weather traffic densification is recommended before applying a seal coat or surface treatment. A polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt micro‑surfacing plant must be equipped with a scalping screen having a 3/8‑inch maximum square opening for Type A material and a 1/2‑inch maximum square opening for Types B and RF, and aggregates are to be coated with cement before entering the mixer. Water, additive, and polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt are proportioned by a positive‑displacement gear pump and introduced into a continuous‑flow twin‑shafted multi‑bladed pug mill at least 50 inches long, with the polymer‑modified asphalt added after the first quarter point of the mixer. All material‑control devices must be calibrated before each mix type and a water‑pressure spray system must be ready to wet the aggregate as it drops into the pug mill. Placement may only occur when atmospheric temperature is at least 50 °F during the entire placement, rain is not imminent, and no freezing temperatures are expected within 24 hours; micro‑surfacing work is prohibited from September 16 to April 30 in Districts 1‑0, 2‑0, 3‑0, 4‑0, 10‑0 and 5‑0, and from October 1 to April 30 in Districts 6‑0, 8‑0, 9‑0, 11‑0, 12‑0 and 5‑0. The plant producing an asphalt‑treated permeable base course must first dry the aggregate as necessary, then heat the asphalt material so that when combined it coats the aggregate with an adequate film thickness; the ATPBC shall be produced at a temperature below 320 °F, providing sufficient viscosity for proper coating and avoiding segregation during transport, and must be placed within eight hours of production. Placement is prohibited on surfaces that are unstable, frozen, or below a temperature of 35 °F, and work may not proceed when air temperature is below 35 °F or during rain. For plant‑mixed 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlay courses the mixing plant shall be either a batch plant or a drum‑mixer plant as required by Section 413.3(c), and the produced mixture must meet the acceptance criteria for asphalt content and the percent passing the No. 200 sieve, and its mat density must satisfy the non‑movement and optimum‑rolling‑pattern requirements of Table E using approved nuclear or electrical impedance gauges. Placement is only permitted when site conditions comply with weather limitations – surfaces must be dry, surface temperature may not be 50 °F or lower (or 40 °F if a written exception is granted), and paving cannot occur during the prohibited date ranges listed for each district. The plant used for an asphalt seal coat with precoated aggregate must handle asphalt cement or other asphalt materials together with the specified coarse aggregate, applying a residual binder content of 0.6 % to 1.2 % by weight when asphalt cement is used and 0.4 % to 1.0 % when other asphalt materials are employed; the batch or continuous plant must dry the aggregate and pre‑coat it in a pug mill at a mixing temperature not exceeding 350 °F, while any cut‑back asphalt on damp aggregate must keep the aggregate temperature below 150 °F. Air content of samples taken at the beginning, midpoint and end of the batch is tested, and use is discontinued if variation exceeds 1.5 %, also per Section 704.2. Before any work begins the mixing plant must be accepted and its operation verified; aggregates are stockpiled in a way that prevents segregation or degradation, and the plant’s separate bins ensure proper proportioning. Quality control and acceptance

Prior to any batching the mixing plant and all associated equipment must be inspected and accepted in accordance with Bulletin 42, and a yearly calibration of cement meters and aggregate gate settings must be performed in the presence of Department representatives. The calibrated plant must then produce concrete whose ingredient proportions fall within the tolerances specified for cement (0 to +4 %), fine aggregates (±2 %), coarse aggregates (±2 %), admixtures (±3 %) and water (±2 %). During mixing the drive‑shaft speed shall be maintained within 50 rpm of the operating speed. Each batch shall be verified by slump and air‑content tests in accordance with PTM No. 601, as well as unit weight, concrete uniformity and output‑meter calibration tests according to AASHTO T 121, ASTM C136, AASHTO M 157 and PTM No. 626 before release to the Inspector‑in‑Charge. If directed air content of individual mixed‑concrete samples taken approximately at the beginning, midpoint and end of the batch shall be tested and any variation greater than 1.5 % shall require discontinuing the mixer or agitator until corrected. Concrete temperature after mixing must be maintained between 50°F and 90°F for general concrete and between 50°F and 80°F for bridge‑deck concrete. When mixing in a plant the mixing time shall not be less than 50 seconds nor more than 90 seconds for normal strength concrete, and shall be at least 70 seconds for HES concrete. For truck‑drum mixers the batch must receive between 70 and 125 revolutions at a speed not less than 6 rpm and not more than 18 rpm, then reduced to 2–6 rpm without exceeding a total of 300 revolutions. Mixed concrete must be delivered and discharged within one and a half hours after mixing, or within one hour if the temperature reaches 80°F or above. Material densities (except admixtures) shall be verified using bulk specific gravity on a saturated‑surface‑dry basis as listed in Bulletin 14 or by SSD testing; lightweight aggregate requires confirmation of its SSD value similarly. The batch weight of each aggregate shall be adjusted for free water based on tests of representative stockpile samples, and if slag is used it shall be tested daily to determine loose‑struck unit weight, solid volume per cubic yard and bulk specific gravity factor, using those values to maintain established proportions. Cement and aggregates must be measured by weight in accordance with AASHTO M 157; the water‑measuring system shall discharge the total measured water into the mixer drum in a time not greater than one‑fourth of the specified mixing time, shall be equipped with interlocks to prevent discharging during the charge cycle and charging during the discharge cycle, and shall be calibrated to within ±3 %. Plant temperature control checks must confirm that aggregates are free of frozen lumps and are at a temperature not less than 40°F or more than 100°F when charged, that mixing water never exceeds 150°F, and that water hotter than 90°F may not contact cement until the cement has been mixed with the aggregates. The plant shall be capable of producing an RCC pavement mixture in the proportions defined by the approved mix design and within the specified tolerances, shall deliver a uniform mix at a rate compatible with the placement equipment, and shall comply with applicable plant type standards – batch‑type plants must meet the requirements of Sections 704.2 and 704.3 while continuous‑mix plants must satisfy AASHTO M 241. The mixing plant shall be a fully automated and recorded facility listed in Bulletin 41 and shall provide an on‑site office of at least 120 square feet; both aggregates and the finished mix shall be free of unburned fuel oil and excess moisture as required by Section 413.2(e)1.d.1. Any stabilizer or fiber addition system must use interlocked proportioning devices calibrated to ±10 % of the required fiber weight, equipped with low‑level and no‑flow indicators, a feed‑rate printout in pounds per minute and a transparent pipe section for visual flow verification. Hot‑mixture storage shall be limited to shipping the material within two hours of mixing and any stored SMA that does not consistently match the quality of material discharged directly into haul trucks shall be rejected. Warm‑mix additives, modifiers or foaming processes must be installed according to the WMA Technology Technical Representative’s recommendations to achieve a uniform blend. Batch plants are required to weigh RAP/RAS before it enters the pug mill and shall have cold‑feed bins, conveyors, charging chutes and special bins designed so that RAP/RAS does not segregate or stick. Virgin aggregate and reclaimed material must be dried and heated so that after RAP/RAS is added the completed mixture temperature falls within the range specified in Table A for the class and type of material; both the aggregate and the finished mix shall be free of unburned fuel oil and excess moisture, and drum mixers shall be modified to keep RAP/RAS from contacting the burner flame or overheating while still meeting the same temperature limits. Before any work begins the processing and mixing plant must receive acceptance of its operation, and its proportioning and measuring devices for coarse and fine aggregates as well as water must be checked and verified for accuracy; the plant shall be equipped with automatic cutoff devices that stop feed if delivery of any mix component ceases, and mass‑proportioning plants should have separate bins for each aggregate size to prevent segregation. In a stone matrix asphalt mixing plant the operator must verify aggregate gradation by testing the percent passing the 4.75 mm (No. 4), 2.36 mm (No. 8) and 75 µm (No. 200) sieves as well as the asphalt content, and these single‑sample results shall fall within the tolerances of Table D. Volumetric properties such as air voids and VMA shall be determined on compacted specimens in accordance with AASHTO T 312 and must meet the limits of Table E. A draindown test performed according to AASHTO T 305 with a one‑hour reading shall be required each day and its result shall satisfy the tolerances of Table D. The degree of particle coating shall be measured by AASHTO T 195 and the mixture must achieve percent coated particles ≥ 95.0 %, any failure triggering corrective actions. For cold‑recycled asphalt base courses the plant inspection shall confirm that liquid‑metering devices keep variation within +2.0 % by weight, that a positive‑displacement pump can meter emulsified asphalt down to four gallons per minute, and that a positive interlock automatically shuts off the pump when material is not in the mixing chamber; the foamed‑asphalt system shall be checked for a functional test nozzle and continuous temperature gauges on the hot‑asphalt supply tankers, and the mixer shall be capable of producing a homogeneous blend without stripping the binder while meeting the mix‑design requirements. When the plant is used for cold‑in‑place applications the liquid metering system shall automatically meter liquids with variation not more than ± 2.0 % by weight, the pugmill mixer’s spray bar shall provide volumetric control of additive based on milling depth, width and speed, separate computerized controls and injection bars shall exist for foamed asphalt and water, a test nozzle shall be available to obtain field samples of foamed asphalt, hot‑asphalt supply tankers shall be equipped with gauges that continuously display the temperature of the asphalt, and water added at the mill head shall be measured with a calibrated meter. In roller‑compacted concrete production the plant must be inspected to verify that the mix design and materials remain consistent throughout the project and that moisture content is optimized according to AASHTO T 180 Method D; inspectors must assure complete and uniform mixing, confirm that the dry‑concrete volume does not exceed the manufacturer’s rated capacity for the mixing chamber, keep the mixer sides and blade surfaces free of hardened concrete, check the blades frequently for wear and replace them when wear could cause inadequate mixing, and the produced RCC shall meet the ingredient tolerances set out in Section 704.1(e) with daily plant records of material quantities supplied to the Representative as a calibration check. For slab‑stabilization grout plants the mixing machine shall be capable of operating at a minimum speed of 800 rpm and a maximum speed of 2000 rpm, its performance shall be verified by making flowability measurements at least two times during each work shift, mixed material shall not be held in the mixer or injection‑pump sump for more than one hour after mixing, and pressure in the grout line shall be monitored to ensure it does not exceed a maximum pressure of 200 psi unless otherwise directed. In polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt micro‑surfacing the plant shall use a self‑propelled front‑feed continuous‑loading mixer equipped with a chain‑dragged conveyor belt and a positive displacement water‑jacketed gear pump to proportion ingredients; filler feed shall be positioned so that cement coats the aggregate before entering the mixer; the pug mill shall be a continuous‑flow twin‑shafted multi‑bladed unit at least 50 inches long with blade size and side clearance as recommended by the manufacturer; polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt shall be introduced beyond the first quarter point of the mixer to ensure thorough blending; all material control devices shall be readily accessible to the Inspector and calibrated before each mix type is produced, and the machine shall be equipped with a water‑pressure system and nozzle spray bar as required. For asphalt‑treated permeable base courses the plant must generate the mix at a temperature below 320 °F, ensuring that the asphalt has sufficient viscosity to form an adequate coating film on the dried aggregate and that no segregation of asphalt and aggregate occurs during transport; the aggregate shall be dried as needed and the asphalt material heated so that combination yields a completed mixture, and the finished ATPBC may not be stockpiled and must be placed within eight hours after production. Cooling or curing, protection, and handoff

Cold‑recycled mixtures exhibit lower structural strength when first placed and gain strength as they cure over several weeks; a minimum of one week of curing under favorable weather conditions—characterized by low humidity and high temperature—is required before the wearing course may be installed [1]. For asphalt paving mixtures produced at the mixing plant, haulage trucks shall provide covers of sufficient size and quality to protect the entire load under all conditions, and when air temperature is below 50 °F from October 1 to April 30 the truck body must be insulated on all sides, double‑walled, or heated; placement shall not occur when surfaces are wet or when the air or surface temperature is 40 °F or lower, and paving is limited to the seasonal window of April 1 through October 15, thereby protecting the freshly laid pavement until it cools and cures under field conditions [2]. Roller‑compacted concrete (RCC) transported from the plant shall be covered during transit to shield the mixture from inclement weather and reduce surface moisture loss; if ambient temperature near placement rises to 85 °F, temperature readings of the plastic RCC mix must be taken at half‑hour intervals, and when the mix temperature approaches 90 °F cooling water or aggregates shall be applied so that the plastic mix remains within 50 °F to 90 °F during placement, with placement stopped if it exceeds 90 °F. Placement shall also be halted when the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 lb/ft²·h unless mitigation measures such as windbreaks or fog misting are employed. After final rolling and in‑field density testing, the RCC surface shall be kept moist for a minimum of 72 hours by water curing, polyethylene sheeting, curing compound, or cold‑weather methods until a compressive strength of at least 3,000 psi is achieved [2]. For conventional concrete placed from the plant, the aggregate must be free of frozen lumps and charged into the mixer at a temperature not less than 40 °F and not more than 100 °F; heated mixing water may be used but shall not exceed 150 °F, and in hot weather the aggregates and mixing water shall be cooled as necessary so that concrete temperature at placement stays within 50 °F to 90 °F (or within 50 °F to 80 °F for bridge decks) [2]. Crushed‑aggregate base courses shall not be laid on a frozen surface when air temperature is below 40 °F or when freezing temperatures are forecast within the next 24 hours; they shall be kept moist until covered by the overlying layer and thereafter protected by limiting traffic under the Representative’s authority, with exposed portions protected from marring, distortion, and other damage caused by construction equipment [2]. After a cold‑recycled asphalt base course has been placed, shaped, graded and compacted, any exposed sections shall be protected from marring or distortion; emulsified‑asphalt stabilized CRABC must cure for at least two weeks before a wearing or binder course is placed, or until the average moisture content falls below 2 %, and foamed‑asphalt stabilized CRABC shall also remain in cure until the average moisture content is less than 2 % [2]. For slab stabilization, mixed cement grout shall not be held in the mixer or injection pump sump for more than one hour after mixing; repaired drill holes shall be filled with a rapid‑set, non‑shrink concrete patching material struck flush with the surrounding pavement and allowed to cure before traffic returns, with a minimum opening time of 12 hours unless the approved field QC plan or manufacturer’s recommendations specify otherwise [2]. Polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt micro‑surfacing may be placed only when the entire surface can achieve satisfactory penetration and adhesion and atmospheric temperature is at least 50 °F for the whole operation; the mixture shall not be applied if rain is imminent or freezing temperatures are expected within 24 hours after application, and any rained‑on mix must be removed and replaced before it sets [2]. Asphalt‑treated permeable base course (ATPBC) produced at the plant shall be placed within eight hours and shall not be stockpiled; after spreading, the mat shall be allowed to cool to 100 °F before subsequent layers are laid, and rolling shall commence only after the mat has cooled sufficiently to prevent shoving or lateral movement. At the end of each workday—or whenever a pause longer than thirty minutes occurs—a temporary vertical bulkhead (or sawed joint) shall be installed to protect the transverse construction joint until paving resumes [2]. The mixing operation for asphalt seal coat using precoated aggregate shall be conducted at a temperature not exceeding 350 °F, and when cut‑back asphalts are used the aggregate temperature shall not exceed 150 °F; after mixing, the material shall be stocked on a suitable base area no higher than four feet to allow it to cool and be protected, and if damp aggregate with cut‑back asphalts is used the pre‑coated aggregate shall be sufficiently cured before use to prevent pickup, with the coating covering at least ninety percent of the visible surface area to protect the particles [2].

Expected performance and maintenance

Under SECTION 704—CEMENT CONCRETE, proportion errors may arise when calibrated gates or meters drift beyond the tolerances of 0 to +4 % for cement weight, ±2 % for fine and coarse aggregates, ±3 % for admixtures and ±2 % for water, or when the drive‑shaft speed deviates more than 50 rpm from its set point; failure to recalibrate after a source change or to perform the required annual calibration can also produce inaccurate batching, and charging aggregate bins more than 4 hours before mixing may further degrade mix quality. Also in SECTION 704, neglect of daily inspection and cleaning can lead to contamination of aggregate bins, moisture ingress into cement bins, or mortar build‑up on the auger screw, which may cause segregation and off‑spec concrete. In SECTION 502—ROLLER COMPACTED CONCRETE PAVEMENTS, concrete must be maintained between 50°F and 90°F for general mixes (or between 50°F and 80°F for bridge decks) and any batch that exceeds these ranges must not be shipped; in hot weather when the mix temperature reaches 80°F or above, the time from mixing to discharge must not exceed 1 hour. In SECTION 704—CEMENT CONCRETE, mixing time is limited to at least 50 seconds but no more than 90 seconds for normal‑strength concrete, and an air‑content variation greater than 1.5 % requires stopping the mixer or agitator until corrected. The stabilizer/fiber supply system in SECTION 483—POLYMER‑MODIFIED EMULSIFIED ASPHALT PAVING SYSTEM must proportion fibers within ±10 % of the required weight, and the appearance of fiber clumps at the discharge chute or fibers in the bag‑house indicates that mixing time/intensity should be adjusted or the lines relocated to prevent loss. In SECTION 419—STONE MATRIX ASPHALT MIXTURE DESIGN, stored SMA material that does not consistently meet the same quality as material discharged directly into hauling vehicles will be rejected, and SMA material held longer than two hours may also be rejected. Defective material or workmanship arising from mixing‑plant processes can manifest as fatigue cracking, transverse and miscellaneous cracking, raveling/weathering, rutting, flushing, potholing, joint and edge deterioration, and loss of bond/delamination. When proportioning and measuring devices are inaccurate, the blend can be off‑ratio, leading to segregation or improper moisture content; automatic cutoff devices that fail to stop feed when any component delivery ceases can cause mix interruptions. Improper blending or moisture control often results in layers that do not achieve the required 100 % maximum density, necessitating additional compaction, remoistening, reshaping, or full replacement, and surface irregularities greater than 1/2‑inch are also a sign of mixing or placement distress. QC test results that fall outside the tolerances listed in Table D or Table E—such as single‑sample sieve‑passing percentages for the 4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, or 75 µm sieves, average multiple‑sample sieve or asphalt‑content values, or draindown test results—trigger a hold on production; percent coated particles must be at least 95.0 %, and if this limit is not met the plant must increase mixing time or make other adjustments. The sides of the mixer and mixer blade surfaces must be kept free of hardened concrete and other debris, worn blades must be replaced when wear is sufficient to cause inadequate mixing, and the manufacturer’s rated volume capacity for dry concrete in the mixing chamber must not be exceeded; failure to maintain turbulence and proper sealing in a continuous‑loading mixing machine can allow the mix to set up in the box, develop excessive side build‑up, or form lumps. Spreading equipment that does not prevent loss of mixture on super‑elevated curves can produce skips, tears or a non‑uniform consistency that compromises skid resistance. In SECTION 360—ASPHALT TREATED PERMEABLE BASE COURSE, heating the asphalt above 320°F may cause segregation of asphalt and aggregate, so production must occur below 320°F; material must be placed within eight hours of manufacture because stockpiling is prohibited, and placement on surfaces that are unstable, frozen, or below 35°F—or when rain occurs—can result in inadequate binding or surface defects. Under SECTION 704—CEMENT CONCRETE, a daily visual examination of the auger‑type mixer screw shall be performed and the screw cleaned as necessary to prevent mortar or concrete buildup; any observed wear on the screw or feeder gates should be repaired promptly. All gauges, dials and flow meters must remain fully visible so that operators can read or adjust them during production, and the sides of the mixer and blade surfaces shall be kept free of hardened concrete and other material, with frequent inspection of blades for wear and replacement when wear is sufficient to cause inadequate mixing. Calibration of cement meters, aggregate gates and other measuring devices shall be performed once a year in the presence of Department representatives and shall be repeated whenever the source of fine or coarse aggregate or cement changes, when directed, or before producing any mix type, with discrepancies in cement count limited to no more than two counts per 60 seconds. The drive‑shaft speed indicated by the tachometer shall be maintained within 50 rpm of the rated speed, and successive batches shall be placed without a gap exceeding one‑half hour. For normal strength concrete the mixing time shall be not less than 50 seconds and not more than 90 seconds, and for high‑early‑strength concrete it shall be not less than 70 seconds; truck‑drum mixers must operate between 70 and 125 revolutions at a speed of not less than 6 rpm and not more than 18 rpm, with an overall limit of 300 revolutions. Air content of each mixed concrete batch shall be tested at the beginning, midpoint and end; if it varies by more than 1.5 % the use of the mixer or agitator must be discontinued until corrected. Concrete temperature after mixing shall be maintained between 50°F and 90°F for general concrete and between 50°F and 80°F for bridge‑deck concrete, and any mix outside those ranges may not be shipped. For stone matrix asphalt production the plant shall remain fully automated and listed in Bulletin 41; proportioning devices for fibers shall be interlocked with the plant and controlled to ±10 % of the required fiber weight, shall include low‑level and no‑flow indicators, and shall provide a feed‑rate printout in pounds per minute, with a transparent pipe section for visual flow checks. Stabilizer material shall be stored dry and hot‑mix material shipped within two hours of mixing; any SMA that does not meet direct‑discharge quality shall be rejected. Annual verification of each Job‑Mix Formula shall be performed before initial production and whenever a new JMF is issued, with daily sampling of the completed mixture for draindown testing, gradation and volumetric analysis per PTM No. 1, and determination of particle coating to achieve at least 95.0 % coated particles; if this or other quality‑control results fall outside tolerances in Tables D or E, corrective actions such as increasing plant mixing time shall be taken immediately and production suspended until a Representative approves the solution. All equipment, including the mixing plant, shall be maintained according to Section 108.05(c). Liquid‑metering devices must stay within a variation of not more than ±2.0 % by weight, and emulsified‑asphalt pumps shall be capable of delivering flow rates as low as 4 gallons per minute; a positive interlock system shall automatically shut off the pump when no material is present in the mixing chamber. Hot‑asphalt supply tankers shall be equipped with gauges that continuously display asphalt temperature. The plant shall never exceed the manufacturer’s rated volume capacity for dry concrete in the mixing chamber, and daily plant records of production and material quantities shall be supplied to the Representative for calibration checks. Under SECTION 679—SLAB STABILIZATION, the mixing machine shall be operated within a speed range of at least 800 rpm and not more than 2000 rpm; mixed material may not remain in the mixer or injection‑pump sump for more than one hour, and flowability measurements shall be made at least two times each work shift to monitor mix quality. Pumping operations shall be stopped whenever any vertical movement is noted, and upward pavement movement greater than 0.05 inch shall not be allowed; pressure gauges shall be protected and positive‑cutoff/bypass valve provisions maintained. If the slab surface rises above the 0.05‑inch allowable tolerance it must be ground to correct grade, and after grouting all drill holes shall be patched with a rapid‑set, non‑shrink concrete patching material. Preventive upkeep of the mixing plant centers on routine calibration and inspection; each material‑control device shall be calibrated before producing any mix type. The filler feed shall be positioned so that cement properly coats the aggregate prior to charging the mixer. Blade size and side clearance shall meet the manufacturer’s recommendations, and the water‑pressure system with its nozzle spray bar shall be functional. A continuous‑flow, twin‑shafted, multi‑bladed pug mill at least 50 inches long shall be provided. [2]
