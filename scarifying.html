Scarifying

Part of: Rehabilitation is-subtopic-of
Scarifying is a topic under Rehabilitation.
scarifyingscarifies
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

Scarifying is a surface‑preparation technique used when a thin, distressed layer of high‑type paved shoulder or existing pavement must be removed or roughened to provide a sound base for new Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S, or 6‑SP shoulders, Hot In‑Place Recycled (HIR) asphalt overlays, or bonded concrete overlays. The method employs a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier that cuts at least 1½ inches deep—after indirect propane‑heated radiant heating to roughly 300 °F for HIR work—or directly on the existing layer, producing a clean, uniformly roughened substrate free of loose reinforcement and debris, which is essential for achieving required bond strength and interlayer adhesion. Equipment constraints limit treatment to lane widths between 10 ft and 14½ ft and, unless otherwise specified, scarify depths are capped at four inches below the existing grade, ensuring controlled removal while preserving surrounding structures. [1][2]

Purpose and applicability

Scarifying is applied to high‑type paved shoulders that show distress only in the first couple feet adjacent to the travel lane, where removing or roughening that thin damaged layer creates a sound base for installing a new Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S, or 6‑SP shoulder and allows provision of a minimum 3‑feet wide section, thereby satisfying the requirement that excavation or scarifying be performed when rehabilitation is necessary. In the Hot In‑Place Recycled asphalt process the scarifier cuts a thin layer at least 1 ½ inches deep so that the material can be mixed with a recycling agent and rejuvenated in place, eliminating the need to mill the pavement and ship material off‑site and improving interlayer bonding. For bonded concrete overlay projects scarifying is used to eliminate a thin, deteriorated layer of existing pavement and, when required, lower the pavement elevation by removing material to the depth specified in Section 591.3, which uniformly removes any exposed or loose reinforcement so that only sound substrate remains for the overlay and provides a clean, roughened surface that will achieve the required bond strength. In special rolling operations scarifying is employed when the soil or aggregate being prepared for compaction contains moisture above the optimum level; the material must be dried by disking, scarifying, or other means until the moisture content meets the required limits before compaction can proceed, thereby ensuring that the subsequent compaction can achieve the specified density targets. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 61–62), Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 168–170), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Scarifying is appropriate when a project on an Interstate or other limited‑access freeway or arterial requires a Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S or 6‑SP shoulder and the existing shoulder must be excavated or scarified; it may also be used if the existing high‑type paved shoulder is only distressed in the first couple feet adjacent to the pavement, allowing a 3 feet minimum width of those shoulder types while the remaining shoulder is resurfaced with the same material as the new shoulder. [1] When only cleaning and patching of an existing paved shoulder is necessary, a Type 7 shoulder should be provided instead, and for flexible pavements on collectors and local roads the guidance calls for shoulders of the same surface material or Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S or 1‑SP rather than scarifying. [1] Under the bonded concrete overlay specifications, scarifying is appropriate when the overlay design requires lowering the pavement elevation or a uniform removal of the existing surface to a specified depth, such as cutting off exposed reinforcement that is loose, and a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier must be used to achieve the required depth. [2] If the objective is only to eliminate loose concrete, rust or other debris without changing elevation, shot blasting, sand blasting or water blasting equipment should be employed instead of scarifying. [2] In special‑rolling operations where the in‑place moisture of a subgrade or embankment layer exceeds the optimum moisture content, scarifying may be used as one of the drying actions to bring the material into the moisture range between minus 3 % of optimum moisture content and optimum moisture content; if scarifying is not feasible, disking or any other suitable means may be used to achieve the required moisture condition before compaction. [2]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 61–62); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 168–170), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Required inputs

Scarifying in the Hot In‑Place Recycled (HIR) process requires indirect propane‑heated radiant heating that raises the existing pavement to approximately 300 °F ± 25 °F before the scarifier removes a minimum of 1½ inches of asphalt, after which a predetermined amount of recycling agent is blended with the reclaimed material and the rejuvenated mix is placed by a heated screed [1]. The scarifying equipment must be capable of treating lane widths from at least 10 ft to no more than 14½ ft [1]. When the subsequent surface treatment is a chip seal, micro‑surfacing, or slurry seal, a four‑week waiting period after scarification is required; no waiting period is needed for hot‑mix asphalt (HMA), warm‑mix asphalt (WMA) or ultra‑thin bonded overlays, and an overlay must be placed before winter [1]. Selection of the overlay should consider the existing pavement condition, pavement type, and average daily traffic [1].

For a bonded concrete overlay, scarifying calls for a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier that uniformly removes the old surface to the depth specified in Section 591.3, followed by shot blasting, sand blasting or water blasting to eliminate loose concrete and rust [2]. The prepared surface must be free of all debris, oil, grease and standing water, protected from contaminants, and joint backing or other approved joint‑filling material must be installed so that its top is within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface to prevent displacement during paving [2]. A final cleaning with a compressed‑air stream that contains no oil or moisture and provides at least 100 psi measured at the source must occur no more than 30 minutes before placement of the concrete overlay [2]. The surface temperature at that time must be less than 120 °F, while the fresh concrete temperature during placement must be maintained between 65 °F and 90 °F; fog‑misting or protective covers may be used as needed to keep these limits [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

The Pavement Policy Manual specifies that when scarifying an existing shoulder on Interstate or other limited‑access freeways and arterials, the design calls for the use of Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S or 6‑SP shoulders wherever excavation or scarifying is required. If the high‑type paved shoulder (stabilized excluded) shows distress only in the first few feet adjacent to the pavement, a minimum width of three feet of the new Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S or 6‑SP shoulder may be provided and the remainder of the shoulder is resurfaced to match the new material. In a Hot In‑Place Recycled asphalt process, the scarifying step follows heating of the pavement to approximately 300 °F with an allowable variation of plus or minus 25 °F by radiant heat, and the scarifier must cut the existing surface to a depth of at least 1½ inches. The equipment used for this operation may treat lanes as narrow as ten feet and as wide as 14½ feet, which defines the width limits for the scarifying activity. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications require the contractor to provide a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier capable of uniformly removing the existing surface to the depth called for in Section 591.3, cutting off and removing any exposed reinforcement, and then shot‑blasting or water‑blasting the pavement so that a roughened surface is obtained while standing water is drained as directed; final cleaning of the surface immediately ahead of the paver must occur no more than 30 minutes before overlay placement. If a compressed‑air stream is used for cleaning, it must be free of oil, moisture or other contaminants and must have a minimum pressure of 100 pounds per square inch measured at the source. Joint backing or any other approved joint‑filling material shall be installed to within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface in all existing transverse joints before the overlay is placed. Prior to placing the concrete overlay, the prepared surface must undergo bond‑strength testing at seven days in accordance with ASTM C1583; for each day’s lot not exceeding 3 000 square yards, five random locations shall be tested and the results accepted only if the average of three test results is at least 175 psi and each individual result is a minimum of 160 psi. In Section 654.3 governing paved shoulders type 4, scarifying after removal of surplus material is performed at the plan‑indicated locations and is limited to a maximum depth of four inches below the existing grade unless a different depth is specifically directed; this four‑inch limit constitutes the sole dimensional control for scarifying in that section. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 61–62), Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 574)

Equipment and personnel

The HIR scarifying operation relies on a small fleet of heater trucks; typically two or three trucks equipped with propane‑heated firebrick units provide indirect radiant heating to about 300 °F. The final truck in the train is fitted with a scarifier that cuts at least 1½ inches deep, then immediately adds the prescribed recycling agent and mixes it with the loosened material on board, after which a heated paving screed spreads the rejuvenated mix for placement. No other equipment such as hauling vehicles, separate compactors, finishers, curing systems or testing devices is described in this section. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 131)

Work sequence

Before scarifying can be undertaken, several preparatory actions are required depending on the pavement treatment and specification. In a Hot In‑Place Recycled (HIR) asphalt process the existing pavement must first be heated with heater trucks that employ propane‑heated firebrick to raise the surface temperature to approximately 300 °F ± 25 °F by radiant heat; only after this heating step does scarifying commence [1]. For bonded‑concrete overlay work under Section 545.3(b), the contractor must first provide a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier capable of uniformly removing the old surface to the depth called for in Section 591.3, and must also have shot‑blasting, sand‑blasting or water‑blasting equipment available to remove any loose concrete and rust from exposed reinforcement before scarifying begins [2]. When treating paved shoulders as specified in Section 654.3, all surplus material on the existing shoulder must be removed down to the bottom of the proposed base course or to a directed depth; if a guide rail is to remain, the shoulder must be excavated around the rail without disturbing it and the material behind the rail shaped as indicated before scarifying may proceed at the designated locations, limited to a maximum depth of four inches below existing grade or the directed depth [2]. Finally, under Section 208.3 for special rolling, the pavement material must first be identified as having moisture content above the optimum and then dried by disking, scarifying, or another method until the moisture‑content requirements are satisfied prior to any compaction [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 168–170), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 574)

In the hot‑in‑place recycled asphalt (HIR) process described in the Pavement Policy Manual, a train of two or three heater trucks first uses propane‑heated firebrick to indirectly raise the pavement temperature to approximately 300 °F ± 25 °F by radiant heat; the last heater truck then scarifies the surface to a depth of at least 1 ½ in., adds a predetermined amount of recycling agent, mixes the agent with the scarified material, spreads the rejuvenated mix using a heated paving screed, and finally compacts it into place [1]. In the bonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements specified in the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications, scarifying begins by mobilizing a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier that uniformly removes the existing surface to the depth indicated, while cutting off and removing any exposed or loose reinforcement and any deteriorated concrete or asphalt below the scarified level; if transverse joints contain steel plates, those plates are removed next [2]. The freshly scarified slab is then shot‑blasted or water‑blasted to achieve a roughened surface, standing water is drained, all loose debris is completely cleaned, and joint backing material is placed within ±¼ inch of the pavement surface; final cleaning in front of the paver must occur no more than 30 minutes before overlay placement and the surface temperature must be less than 120°F at that time [2]. For paved shoulders of type 4, surplus shoulder material is first cleared down to the bottom of the proposed base course or to a directed depth without disturbing any existing guide rail; scarifying is then carried out at the indicated locations to a maximum depth of 4 inches below the existing grade or as otherwise directed, after which the surface is shaped and compacted with graded material or aggregate base to the bottom elevation of the proposed base course using the rollers specified in Section 108.05(c)3.a, (c)3.b, (c)3.e, or (c)4, and the material is considered satisfactorily compacted when it does not move under the compaction equipment [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 574)

Following the scarifying operation in a Hot In‑Place Recycled (HIR) asphalt treatment, a four week waiting period must be observed before placing chip seals, micro surfacing, or slurry seal surface treatments; however, when the subsequent overlay is hot mix asphalt, warm mix asphalt, or an ultra‑thin bonded overlay, no waiting period is required. After scarifying, shot‑blasting or water‑blasting, and removal of all loose debris in a concrete‑surfaced pavement, the final cleaning must be completed no more than 30 minutes before the concrete overlay or inlay is placed. Curing materials for the concrete overlay must be applied within 30 minutes after bleed water has dissipated following finishing operations. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Staging and logistics

Scarifying operations are constrained by the physical dimensions of the equipment and the geometry of the work site, requiring that both space and access be adequate for the machinery involved [1][2]. The mechanical scarifier used in a Hot In‑Place Recycled (HIR) system can treat pavements only when the available width lies between a minimum of 10 feet and a maximum of 14 ½ feet, so the site must provide a clear corridor that accommodates that range [1]. Likewise, the self‑propelled scarifier required for bonded concrete overlay projects must be able to travel the full cutting path, which demands sufficient clearance and unobstructed access to every portion of the pavement surface [2]. Weather conditions impose additional limits: an HIR overlay must be placed before winter arrives, and a four‑week waiting period is mandated before any chip seal, micro‑surfacing, or slurry seal can be applied after scarification [1]. The temperature of the prepared surface must remain below 120 °F immediately prior to concrete placement, which may require scheduling work during cooler periods or employing protective measures such as fog‑misting or covers to keep the temperature within limits [2]. Finally, moisture and contaminant control are required; any standing water on the scarified surface must be drained or removed, and oil or grease drippings from equipment must be prevented so that the substrate stays dry before overlay placement [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Quality control and acceptance

In Hot In‑Place Recycled asphalt treatment, after heating to roughly 300 °F, the final heater truck scarifies and the resulting scarified layer must be inspected to verify that it reaches a depth of at least 1 ½ inches, which is the acceptance criterion for this step. [1] For concrete‑surfaced pavements, the inspector must verify that a self‑propelled mechanical scarifier is employed and that it removes the existing concrete uniformly over the full cutting width to the depth specified in Section 591.3. [2] Any exposed or loose reinforcement and deteriorated material below the scarified level must be completely removed, after which the surface shall be shot‑blasted or water‑blasted to produce a roughened texture, all standing water and debris cleared, and the surface protected from oil or grease before overlay placement. [2] Prior to placing the concrete overlay, bond‑strength testing of the scarified surface shall be performed in the presence of the inspector at seven days according to ASTM C1583, using five random locations per 3,000 sq yd lot; the area is considered defective if the average of three test results is below 175 psi or any individual result is less than 160 psi. [2] The surface temperature must also be verified to be less than 120°F before overlay placement. [2] When scarifying paved shoulders, the resulting surface must be inspected to confirm that the cut does not exceed a maximum depth of 4 inches below the existing grade unless a different depth is specifically directed, and acceptance is achieved when the measured scarify depth meets this limit. [2] For scarifying required for special rolling, the only characteristic that must be inspected or tested is the in‑place moisture content; the material must be measured to confirm it lies between minus 3 % of the optimum moisture content and the optimum moisture content, and any material found above the optimum must be dried by scarifying (or other means) until it meets those moisture‑content limits before compaction can proceed. [2]

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 131); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 168–170), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469), SECTION 600 INCIDENTAL CONSTRU… (p. 574)

When material is above optimum moisture content it must be dried by disking, scarifying, or other means until the material meets the moisture content requirements before compaction. If moisture‑density test data are not available a test strip approach is used: after each pass of the compaction equipment, in‑place moisture and density are checked at several pre‑determined random locations according to AASHTO T 191 or AASHTO T 310. Measurements continue after every pass until the measured moisture‑density increase does not exceed 1 % over three consecutive passes, at which point special rolling may proceed. [2]

[2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 168–170)

In Section 545—Bonded Concrete Overlay of Concrete‑Surfac ed Pavements, the acceptance of scarifying work is tied to a seven‑day bond‑strength test performed in accordance with ASTM C1583. The area is considered defective when the average strength of the three tests falls below 175 psi or when any individual result does not reach at least 160 psi. When defectiveness is identified—whether because the scarified depth, air content, bond strength, or surface condition fails to meet the specified limits—the overlay must be removed and replaced, and the replacement must extend a minimum of six feet between transverse joints. In Section 208—Special Rolling, scarifying may be used only after the in‑place moisture has been reduced to a range from minus 3 % of optimum moisture content up to the optimum value; any material that remains above the optimum must be dried by disking, scarifying, or other approved means until it satisfies the moisture requirement before compaction. After the drying step, the compacted layer is required to reach at least 97 % of maximum dry density for embankment foundations—or for existing embankments located more than three feet below the proposed subgrade elevation—and must attain 100 % of maximum dry density for structure foundations, subgrades, or material situated within three feet of the proposed subgrade elevation. If either the moisture‑content range or the prescribed density is not achieved, the work is classified as non‑conforming and shall be re‑scarified (or otherwise dried) and recom‑pacted. Should the Representative determine that subgrade stability remains unsatisfactory, evidenced by a permanent deflection greater than ½ inch for existing subgrade or greater than 1 inch for embankments, stabilization is required by undercutting or excavating the unsuitable material and replacing it in accordance with the prescribed undercut depths. [2]

[2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 168–170), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Cooling or curing, protection, and handoff

After scarifying and final cleaning, the prepared pavement must be protected from oil, grease, and other contaminants until the concrete overlay is placed; fog‑misting, protective covers or blankets may be used to keep the surface temperature below 120F immediately before placement. Curing material shall be applied within 30 minutes after bleed water dissipates and remain in place until the pavement attains the strength required for traffic opening. If the forecast air temperature is 45F or less, insulating blankets shall be provided to prevent rapid heat loss and may be removed only when the air temperature exceeds 45F or when the average compressive strength exceeds 3,600 pounds per square inch, ensuring that surface temperature change does not exceed 40F within any 1‑hour period. The curing compound shall be applied uniformly to form a solid white opaque film at a minimum rate of 1 gallon (± 0.1 gallon) per 125 square feet. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Work completed under scarifying may carry construction traffic or be opened to users only after the overlaid concrete reaches the minimum required compressive strength of 3,600 psi (verified by cylinder testing per PTM No. 604) and the bond strength meets the minimum acceptable level set out in Section 545.3(e), at which point the Representative may permit traffic. [2]

[2] — SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (pp. 466–469)

Expected performance and maintenance

In SECTION 545—BONDED CONCRETE OVERLAY OF CONCRETE‑SURFACED PAVEMENTS, after scarifying the pavement surface must be inspected and any loose material, exposed reinforcement, or deteriorated concrete or asphalt that extends below the scarified level must be removed. Only minimal minor repairs of surface defects may be addressed by placing an overlay. The adequacy of the preparation is monitored by a 7‑day bond strength test performed in accordance with ASTM C1583; an area is considered defective if the average of three tests is below 175 pounds per square inch or any individual test has a minimum strength of 160 pounds per square inch. In SECTION 654—PAVED SHOULDERS, TYPE 4, after scarifying the work must be inspected to confirm that the scarify depth does not exceed the maximum of 4 inches below the existing grade unless a different depth is directed. Any guide rail that has been damaged or destroyed during the operation must be satisfactorily repaired as directed. Ongoing monitoring consists of checking scarify depth and the condition of guide rails before proceeding to subsequent shaping, compacting, or backfill steps. [2]
