Lift

Part of: Pavement Structure is-subtopic-of
Lift is a topic under Pavement Structure.
liftliftslift-thicknesseslift-thickness
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-30

Lift is a construction technique in which material—such as roller‑compacted concrete, thin asphalt overlay, pervious mix, embankment fill, foamed glass aggregate, Geofoam capping, or manual patching—is placed and compacted in a series of controlled‑thickness layers rather than in a single pour. By limiting each layer to a prescribed maximum depth (for example 4–9 in for RCC, ≤4 in for pervious asphalt, ≤3 in for manual patches) and achieving the required wet‑ or dry‑density targets before the next layer is added, the lift method provides the necessary structural depth while preserving performance characteristics such as strength, porosity, and stability without using forms, reinforcement, or dowel bars. The sequential placement enables precise moisture control, timely curing, and continuous quality‑control testing so that each lift meets dimensional tolerances and compaction criteria before subsequent lifts are installed [1][2].

PennDOT publications covering “Lift” also include the following subtopics: Paver.

The Paver deposits the material in controlled widths and depths while its built-in compaction devices achieve initial density targets before rollers are employed. [1][2] The machine incorporates automatic line, grade, and edge-control systems that limit slumping and halt vibration when forward motion stops to maintain alignment. [2] For specialized applications such as ultra-thin bonded wearing courses or stone-matrix asphalt, the Paver can be fitted with a spray bar ahead of a variable-width heated screed and a safety-edge extrusion device. [1][2]

Purpose and applicability

In roller‑compacted concrete pavement the lift concept solves the problem of obtaining the required structural depth when a very dry mix is placed by paver and compacted with rollers, allowing the designer to build up the specified thickness without using forms, steel reinforcement or dowel bars while preserving RCC performance characteristics and providing a controlled placement thickness that enables contractors to achieve the necessary compaction for thick sections[1][2]. For thin‑asphalt overlays the lift technique solves the problem of delivering a durable, low‑profile preservation treatment that extends service life and improves ride quality more effectively than emulsified asphalt alternatives, by placing the 6.3 mm Superpave Thin Asphalt Overlay in thin lifts that satisfy the minimum and maximum placement depths and structural coefficient requirements, thereby providing an effective thin preservation layer without resorting to thicker conventional overlays[1]. In pervious asphalt pavement systems the lift method solves the difficulty of achieving the designed pavement depth and structural integrity while preserving porosity, by limiting each compacted lift to a maximum of 4 inches, keeping the compacted thickness within ¼‑inch of the design thickness, restricting compaction to no more than four passes, and placing detention‑basin coarse aggregate in lifts not exceeding 8 inches[2]. For embankments and fills the lift approach solves the problem of creating a uniformly stable layer by requiring uniform material from a single borrow source for each horizontal placement, placing soil or granular material to specified compacted depths (6 inches for Type 1, 8 inches for Type 2), operating compaction equipment at speeds not exceeding 1.5 feet per second, and attaining at least 97 % of the maximum dry density (100 % in the top three feet)[2]. In asphalt‑grout stabilization lift monitoring solves the problem of excessive upward slab movement by employing vertical‑movement testing capable of detecting lift to 0.001 inch and limiting pavement rise to no more than 0.05 inch[2]. When Geofoam lightweight fill is encapsulated, the lift procedure solves the problem of protecting the blocks, geomembrane or geotextile from damage and providing a stable base for subsequent soil placement by placing a minimum 6‑inch compacted lift of capping material over the geotextile after blading an 8‑inch loose lift[2]. In manual asphalt patching of depressions deeper than three inches, the lift technique solves the problem of achieving uniform density and grade conformity by limiting each uncompacted lift to no more than three inches, leveling and compacting each layer with an approved mechanical tamper before adding the next lift, and finishing the final lift with a steel‑wheeled roller or other approved equipment[2]. For pipe underdrain and pavement base drain construction the lift method solves the problem of ensuring complete contact between backfill and geotextile and eliminating voids that could impair drainage performance by placing the first fine‑aggregate backfill lift simultaneously with the drain and then backfilling in hydraulically compacted lifts not exceeding 9 inches[2]. In flexible base replacement the lift concept solves the problem of segregation of the asphalt base course by requiring placement to the indicated lift thickness and compaction to the specified density with approved equipment, thereby meeting structural and performance criteria[2]. For Superpave asphalt base courses where the required compacted depth exceeds six inches for a 25 mm mix or eight inches for a 37.5 mm mix, the lift approach solves the inability to achieve proper density in a single placement by dividing the material into two or more layers of approximately equal compacted depth (no layer less than 3 inches nor more than 6 inches for the 25 mm mix; no layer less than 4 inches nor more than 8 inches for the 37.5 mm mix), with top‑lift inspection by full‑depth cores and a defect determination if depth deficiency of one‑half inch or more cannot be corrected[2]. In rubblizing of concrete pavements the lift procedure solves the problem of obtaining a stable, uniformly compacted backfill after rubblizing by limiting each placed layer to a maximum six‑inch thickness that can be effectively rolled and compacted until nonmovement is achieved, thereby preventing shoving or wave motion under the roller[2]. For geosynthetic stabilized bridge approaches the lift method solves the problem of damaging the underlying separation geosynthetic while still providing a uniform firm surface by placing each required lift of coarse aggregate in a controlled, nondestructive manner (hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading), using two six‑inch compacted lifts up to the elevation of the next stabilization layer and barring vehicle traffic until at least six inches of loose backfill cover the geosynthetic[2]. When concrete pavements are constructed on an aggregate subbase with low grade, the lift technique solves the problem of achieving the required elevation before concrete placement by adding additional subbase in one‑half‑inch compacted lifts until the surface reaches the needed height[2].

When a preservation treatment calls for Superpave 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlay on a sound pavement surface, the material may be placed in thin lifts as permitted by the design tables; this is appropriate when the overlay is intended as a scratch or leveling course and the existing pavement meets the soundness criteria. If the objective is to correct an erratic longitudinal profile or re‑establish the proper cross‑section, a separate leveling course (minimum 1‑inch thickness) should be used rather than combining a binder layer with a lift, and where the base is stable and removal will not affect performance, heater planing or milling may be considered as alternative methods.

For roller‑compacted concrete pavements a lift is appropriate when the RCC lift thickness falls within the range defined in Section 502.3(g)—at least four inches and up to nine inches—and the paver type and compaction capability meet the required wet‑density thresholds; lifts under six inches require a conventional or high‑density paver that can achieve at least 85 % of maximum wet density before rollers, while lifts from six inches to less than nine inches require a high‑density paver capable of at least 90 % before rollers. If the planned lift exceeds nine inches, the pavement must be placed in multiple equal‑thickness lifts, each satisfying the same compaction requirements prior to rolling, and when equipment cannot provide the specified compaction or the lift thickness is outside these limits an alternate paving method such as multiple‑lift placement or approved alternative equipment should be employed. When placing foamed glass aggregate a lift is appropriate only if the uncompacted thickness stays within the limits set for the equipment; tracked equipment may place FGA in lifts up to 24 inches thick, whereas areas that cannot be reached by tracked equipment require lifts no more than 12 inches thick and compact the material with a plate compactor weighing between 110 lb and 220 lb, and compaction is considered complete after four full passes that provide 100 % coverage of the lift. For pervious asphalt mixtures a lift is appropriate when the mixture is placed in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts; the detention basin coarse aggregate associated with that system shall be installed in lifts not exceeding eight inches and each lift must be compacted in accordance with Section 360.3. However, pervious pavement mixtures shall not be placed when the air or surface temperature is lower than 50 °F, a tack coat shall not be applied between lifts of any pervious asphalt, and pervious pavement beds shall not be used for storage of equipment, materials, or soils; under those conditions an alternative method must be employed. When placing embankment or fill material a full lift—using uniform material from a single borrow source and laid in horizontal layers to a compacted six‑inch depth for soil (or the equivalent thickness for granular types)—is appropriate when the work can be performed over the full width with standard full‑scale compaction equipment. In confined situations such as pipe trenches, areas adjacent to structures, or any space where standard equipment is prohibited, may cause damage, or is impractical, the lift should be reduced to a four‑inch compacted layer and compacted with the smaller equipment specified by the Representative. For encapsulated Geofoam block fill a minimum six‑inch compacted lift of capping material must be placed over the geotextile before any embankment is built; this lift is created by end dumping the material just off the blocks, blading an approximately eight‑inch loose layer across the surface with pneumatic‑tire equipment and then compacting it with a smooth drum roller in static mode. Once that capping lift is in place additional soil embankment lifts are placed and compacted to reach the pavement subgrade elevation, each meeting the same minimum six‑inch compacted thickness, and direct end dumping of capping material onto the blocks or stockpiling material on earlier lifts is prohibited, so the lift method is required whenever cover or embankment layers are added. For manual asphalt patching a pothole or abrupt depression three inches and less in depth is treated as Type A work using only an asphalt wearing course and no lift placement; for depressions over three inches the binder course must be placed in lifts that do not exceed three inches in uncompacted depth, each lift being leveled and compacted with an approved mechanical tamper before the topmost wearing‑course layer is added. When constructing a Superpave asphalt base course a lift is required whenever the design calls for a compacted depth that exceeds the single‑layer limits; for a 25.0 mm base, if the indicated compacted depth is more than six inches the contractor must place the course in two or more lifts of roughly equal compacted depth, each lift no less than three inches and no greater than six inches, and for a 37.5 mm base, if the indicated compacted depth is more than eight inches the same rule applies with each lift between four inches and eight inches; if the design depth is within those limits a single placement without additional lifts may be used. For an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course the mixture is spread, struck off and placed in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts, allowed to cool to 100 °F before the next layer, and compacted with steel‑wheel rollers weighing eight to ten tons; this approach may be used only when the surface is not unstable, frozen, or below a temperature of 35 °F and rain is not occurring, and if any of those thresholds are exceeded the specification requires that placement be halted or an alternative work‑pause procedure be employed rather than attempting a lift. In pervious concrete pavement systems the coarse aggregate for the detention basin is installed in lifts that do not exceed eight inches and each lift must be compacted as specified in Section 360.3, which constitutes appropriate use of a lift; the pavement slab itself, however, is placed by forming, pouring and finishing with equipment such as a form‑riding paving machine, roller screed or vibrating screed rather than building up material in lifts, so another method should be used for those layers. For plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements built on an aggregate subbase, when the existing grade is below the required level the specification permits raising the base by adding additional subbase in half‑inch compacted lifts, which is appropriate use of a lift; conversely, if the subbase grade exceeds the design elevation the prescribed alternative is to cut the subbase to the required grade and then recompact rather than using lifts. Required inputs

In roller‑compacted concrete (RCC) lifts the material must consist of cement, aggregate and water, with a water‑to‑cement ratio as low as 0.30 but typically 0.40, and may use a dense aggregate gradation whose maximum size is less than 1 inch; the mixture is dry, no‑slump and behaves like damp gravel, while air entrainment is optional and admixture use is limited [1]. The RCC mix must conform to the approved design, be placed at ambient temperatures between 50 °F and 90 °F, and be moisture‑adjusted as required for the equipment; the subgrade or subbase shall be clean, free of foreign material, ponded water, frost or frozen material before placement, and concrete may not be placed when the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 lb/ft²·h unless mitigation such as windbreaks or fog misting is used [2]. Lift thickness for RCC must be at least 4 in., with single lifts permitted from 4 in. up to ≤9 in.; thicker sections require multiple lifts of equal thickness, and compaction behind the paver shall reach a minimum of 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts under 6 in. or 90 % for lifts ≥6 in. before final rolling [2].

For an ultra‑thin bonded wearing course (UTWC) lift the material consists of a polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane applied directly in front of the screed and an overlying hot‑mix asphalt concrete placed in a single pass; three gradations are available—Type A, B or C—with nominal maximum aggregate sizes of 6.3 mm, 9.5 mm and 12.5 mm respectively, and the lift thickness is set to one to 1.5 times the diameter of the coarsest aggregate, producing a surface treatment about ½ inch thick for Type A and about ¾ inch thick for Types B and C [1].

In the thin‑asphalt overlay context a 6.3 mm lift must be constructed with the Superpave 6.3 mm wearing‑course mix and PG 64E‑22 binder, may only be placed on sound pavement surfaces, and requires good surface drainage—or an upgrade to good—and good subsurface drainage—or an upgrade to good—while no structural upgrade is required [1].

Foamed‑glass‑aggregate (FGA) lifts are placed over a Class 4, Type A geotextile after removal of standing water deeper than six inches and ice; each truckload shall be tested for moist loose bulk density before use, and the lift may be up to 24 in. uncompacted when compacted with tracked equipment provided the compactor’s ground pressure stays between 625 psf and 1,025 psf, while areas inaccessible to tracks require lifts no more than 12 in. uncompacted and a plate compactor weighing between 110 lb and 220 lb; four full passes are required for each lift, after which a capping material of at least six inches compacted thickness must be placed within two weeks with the geotextile protected during blading and rolling [2].

Pervious‑asphalt lifts are placed as a compacted layer no thicker than four inches; each lift may be laid only when air or surface temperature is at least 50 °F, the mixture must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before the next lift is installed, and compaction is performed with a smooth steel‑wheeled static roller that the surface can resist at ten tons, limited to four passes [2].

Embankment and fill lifts require uniform material from a single borrow source; soil lifts are placed in six‑inch compacted layers and compacted with a padfoot roller to at least 97 % of maximum dry density (100 % in the top three feet) while keeping moisture within –3 % of optimum; Type 1 granular lifts may not exceed a six‑inch compacted thickness and are compacted with a smooth drum vibratory roller to at least 97 % (100 % in the top three feet) with moisture kept within ±2 % of optimum; Type 2 granular lifts may be up to eight inches thick and need only achieve non‑movement under the compaction equipment while being surface damp but not wet; rock lifts are placed in loose layers no deeper than the average rock size, limited to a maximum of 36 in., and must be compacted with a smooth drum vibratory roller until non‑movement is observed; shale lifts are limited to six‑inch compacted layers; confined areas material larger than two inches or lifts thicker than four inches are prohibited, frozen material may not be placed, any wet or unstable foundation must be dried or treated so that moisture is within the stated tolerances before compaction, and equipment speed shall never exceed 1.5 ft/s (1 mph) [2].

Asphalt‑grout stabilization lifts require hot‑mix asphalt heated to between 380 °F and 450 °F (any batch above 500 °F is rejected), pumped at a pressure of 25–45 psi into vertical grout injection holes no larger than 1½ in. in diameter; water shall be placed around each hole, a splash shield of at least 15 ft² erected before injection, and compressed air blown through the hole for 5‑15 seconds as the drill is withdrawn; lift movement shall be monitored with equipment capable of detecting vertical movement to 0.001 in., upward movement may not exceed 0.05 in., and traffic may not be reopened until a minimum of 30 minutes after grouting or per the approved QC plan [2].

Geofoam lightweight‑fill lifts require a minimum six‑inch compacted layer of capping material that satisfies the criteria in Section 219.2(f) placed on top of the Class 4, Type A geotextile within two weeks after the geotextile is installed; the material is end‑dumped just off the blocks and then bladed into an eight‑inch‑thick loose lift with pneumatic‑tire equipment before being compacted with a smooth drum roller operating in static (non‑vibratory) mode, no stockpiling on previously placed capping material is allowed, the blocks, geomembrane and geotextile must be protected from damage, de‑icing salts are prohibited, and the lift must achieve the specified minimum thickness without exceeding placement tolerances [2].

Manual asphalt patching lifts consist of binder‑course lifts with an uncompacted thickness that does not exceed three inches and a final wearing‑course lift limited to the topmost 1½ inches; the lifts are built from the specified asphalt binder‑course material and asphalt wearing‑course material with a tack coat applied to the bottom and vertical surfaces before any lift is placed, the pothole must be excavated to sound, stable material, all loose debris removed, and the remaining surfaces thoroughly dried so that the tack coat can cure properly, and each lift shall be compacted with an approved mechanical tamper (the top wearing‑course lift with a steel‑wheeled roller) until the patch surface matches the grade of the surrounding pavement [2].

Pipe underdrain and pavement‑base‑drain lifts use fine aggregate classified as Type A placed in hydraulically compacted lifts that do not exceed nine inches in thickness; every lift shall be sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot to obtain proper moisture before compaction, the first lift shall be installed immediately after the drain and geotextile so full contact is achieved, the trench must extend at least two inches into the subgrade while any open portion deeper than two inches shall not be left overnight, and Class 1 geotextile is required beneath the lift [2].

Thin wearing‑course lifts (Section 412) employ plant‑mixed dense‑graded asphalt concrete with a nominal maximum aggregate size of 6.3 mm placed on a prepared surface; before placing the lift the existing subgrade or subbase must be compacted and any disturbance re‑compacted with vibratory compactors to restore the original grade, vertical faces shall be cleaned and uniformly coated with PG 64S‑22, and the asphalt base course shall be placed without causing segregation to the indicated lift thickness and compacted to the density specified by use of approved compaction equipment [2].

Superpave base‑course lifts (Section 313) use Superpave asphalt mixes identified by nominal aggregate size—either a 25.0 mm or a 37.5 mm asphalt base course; placement may only occur on dry prepared surfaces and when air or surface temperature is above 35 °F, wet conditions or temperatures at 35 °F or lower are prohibited, and if the indicated compacted depth exceeds 6 inches for the 25.0 mm mix (or 8 inches for the 37.5 mm mix) it shall be laid in two or more layers of approximately equal compacted depth, each layer no less than 3 inches and no more than 6 inches (or no less than 4 inches and no more than 8 inches respectively); the finished lift must meet surface tolerances of less than ¼ inch irregularity and depth tolerances that do not exceed a ½‑inch deficiency from the design depth [2].

Cement‑treated permeable base‑course lifts consist of a cement‑aggregate blend that must remain free draining and not become crushed; rubblized pavement or subbase shall be thoroughly wetted to a moist condition before the CTPBC is laid, water may be added to the aggregate and cement no more than one hour before compaction unless a retarding admixture extends the window to 1½ hours, and compaction shall be achieved with steel‑wheel power rollers weighing from eight tons to ten tons, with at least one pass but never to the point of loss of drainage; immediately after compaction the surface shall be cured with a white membrane forming compound applied at roughly one gallon per 100 ft² (±10 %) and cure must be maintained for twenty‑four hours, extending another twenty‑four hours if temperature falls below forty °F, lift removal and replacement is required if temperature drops to thirty‑two °F within seventy‑two hours, construction traffic shall be prohibited on the fresh lift for at least three days, and overlying pavement may not be placed during that period [2].

Asphalt‑treated permeable base‑course lifts (Section 360) require dry aggregate coated with heated asphalt material mixed at a temperature below 320 °F to provide the required film thickness and viscosity without segregation; the lift shall be placed within eight hours of production, limited to a maximum compacted thickness of four inches, must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed, and placement is prohibited on unstable, frozen surfaces, when air temperature is below 35 °F, or during rain [2].

Geosynthetic stabilized bridge‑approach lifts (Section 224) specify coarse aggregate—PennDOT No. 2A when it forms the top layer—placed as two 6‑inch compacted layers up to the elevation of the next geosynthetic layer; the aggregate may only be placed in a non‑destructive manner such as hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading, and it shall never be dumped directly from a haul truck onto exposed geosynthetics [2].

Asphalt interlayer system lifts (Section 466) must be placed on a dry, clean, drainable pavement surface that is free of dirt, gravel, vegetation, oil and loose debris, with any cracks sealed and potholes repaired as specified; the asphalt material used for the tack coat shall be kept at a temperature no lower than 290 °F and shall not exceed 320 °F to maintain a uniform spray pattern, when a fiberglass‑mesh grid is incorporated the pavement surface temperature must be between 40 °F and 140 °F, and the first lift of the asphalt overlay shall achieve a minimum compacted thickness that will not be less than 1½ inches in areas where an interlayer system is installed [2].

Plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements placed on an aggregate subbase (Section 501) require additional subbase added in ½‑inch compacted lifts when the existing grade is low; the lift material shall be the same aggregate subbase used for the base course and shall be placed and compacted to a uniform ½‑inch thickness before the forms are set, and prior to placing the concrete the base course should be thoroughly sprinkled with water as needed to ensure a moist surface [2].

Roller‑compacted concrete (RCC) lifts are governed by a set of dimensional and performance criteria. The design thickness for an RCC pavement may range from four inches to eighteen inches and the placement width and depth of each lift are controlled by the dimensions of the paver used on site [1]. A lift shall be at least four inches thick; a single lift may be placed up to nine inches, while any lift thicker than nine inches must be divided into multiple equal‑thickness lifts, each required to achieve at least eighty‑five percent of the maximum wet density before rollers are applied [2]. Lifts thinner than six inches must reach no less than eighty‑five percent of the maximum wet density prior to roller use, and lifts from six to nine inches must attain at least ninety percent before rollers; final compaction for all lifts shall exceed ninety‑six percent of the maximum wet density [2]. Each subsequent RCC lift shall be placed within sixty minutes of the preceding lift or a cold joint must be prepared as specified, and field density testing in accordance with ASTM C1040 shall be performed no later than thirty minutes after rolling [2]. A minimum fifty‑foot test section shall be constructed before paving to verify mix consistency, equipment performance, roller combination and curing procedures, and a field operation quality‑control plan must be approved prior to work commencement [2]. When individual RCC lifts are less than eight inches thick, control joints shall be spaced between fifteen feet and twenty feet; for lifts that are eight inches or greater, joint spacing is calculated as the pavement thickness multiplied by 2.5 feet [1].

The ultra‑thin bonded wearing course (UTWC) is placed in a single lift using a self‑priming paver that first lays a polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane and immediately follows with hot‑mix asphalt concrete; the lift shall be placed to achieve a final thickness equal to one to one‑and‑half times the diameter of the coarsest aggregate, which results in a surface thickness of one‑half inch for Type A (6.3 mm nominal maximum) and three‑quarters inch for Types B (9.5 mm) and C (12.5 mm) [1].

Foamed glass aggregate lifts may be placed with a maximum uncompacted thickness of twenty‑four inches when compacted with tracked equipment or twelve inches where only plate compactors are used; the tracked equipment must operate within ground pressures of six hundred twenty‑five to one thousand twenty‑five pounds per square foot, while plate compactors shall have a static weight between one hundred ten and two hundred twenty pounds, and documentation of equipment make, model and corresponding ground pressure shall be submitted for verification [2]. Standing water deeper than six inches shall be removed, compaction is considered complete after four full passes that provide one‑hundred percent coverage of the lift, and each truckload of delivered foamed glass aggregate shall be tested for moist loose bulk density in the presence of the Representative [2].

In pervious asphalt pavement systems the wearing‑course lifts are limited to a maximum compacted thickness of four inches, whereas coarse aggregate placed in detention basins may be laid in lifts not exceeding eight inches; the finished compacted thickness of any lift may not be more than one‑quarter inch less than the design thickness [2]. Test sections must achieve an average infiltration rate greater than two hundred inches per hour before acceptance, lifts shall only be placed when air or surface temperature is at least fifty degrees Fahrenheit, and compaction shall be performed with a static steel‑wheeled roller limited to four passes per spot [2].

Embankment and fill construction requires lifts to be placed in uniform horizontal layers whose maximum compacted thickness depends on material type: soil, Type 1 granular and shale are limited to six inches, Type 2 granular to eight inches, and rock depth shall not exceed the average size of the larger rock but is capped at thirty‑six inches; lifts must be built from uniform material sourced from the same borrow unless directed otherwise, and a compaction control quality‑control plan on Form CS‑206 shall be submitted at least fourteen calendar days before placement [2]. Moisture‑density testing using a nuclear gauge calibrated per AASHTO T310 (or PTM No. 418 for Troxler gauges) is required for soils and Type 1 granular material with target densities of ninety‑seven percent of maximum dry density below the top three feet and one hundred percent within that zone [2].

Slab stabilization work is preceded by a vertical movement test capable of detecting slab lift to measurements of 0.001 inch while simultaneously monitoring edge or corner points; any upward movement of the pavement greater than zero point zero five inch is not allowed, so lift shall be stopped before this limit is reached, and monitoring is performed while injecting asphalt through drilled holes that may not exceed one and a half inches in diameter and are plugged with cylindrical plugs driven no more than two inches deep [2].

Geofom lightweight fill requires a minimum six‑inch compacted lift of capping material to be placed over the fully encapsulated blocks and spread within two weeks of geotextile placement; the underlying leveling course shall consist of a six‑inch loose bedding material graded parallel to the roadway profile, vertical joints between adjacent longitudinal rows of blocks must be offset a minimum of two feet and may not exceed a gap of 0.1 foot, and the top finished surface is required within a tolerance of one half inch over any ten‑foot interval of the design longitudinal profile and within 0.1 foot of the block design grade under pavement sections (0.2 foot on side slopes) [2]. Shop drawings showing block layout, bedding thickness, capping thickness, embankment depth and cover shall be submitted for Representative review, and an on‑site scale calibrated after setup shall verify each truck load’s block density; any random block failing the density requirement shall cause rejection of that load, while blocks may be field‑cut only with a hot wire cutter to within 0.5 inch of the required dimensions and damaged blocks exceeding volumetric damage of more than 0.5 % or surface damage of more than five percent must be replaced [2].

Manual asphalt patching specifies that the binder course shall be placed in lifts not to exceed three inches in uncompacted depth, each lift being leveled then compacted with an approved mechanical tamper before the next lift is added; the final wearing‑course layer is limited to a topmost one and a half inch lift, which after compaction must match the grade of the surrounding pavement, and all lifts shall be compacted – binder lifts with a mechanical tamper and the finishing wearing‑course lift with a steel‑wheeled roller or other approved equipment – before work is completed for the day, except that the one‑and‑half‑inch wearing course may be placed on the following day [2]. The sequence also mandates that a tack coat be applied to the prepared surface and allowed to cure prior to placing any lift [2].

Pipe underdrain and pavement base drain work calls for hydraulic compaction in lifts that do not exceed nine inches in thickness, each lift being sprayed with water at a rate of one gallon per linear foot; the first lift is required to be placed simultaneously with the drainage pipe to guarantee full contact with the geotextile and trench wall, and trenches may not remain open more than two inches deep overnight, ensuring that lift placement proceeds under controlled exposure conditions [2].

A common set of lift controls applies to flexible‑base replacement, Superpave base courses, geocell load distribution mats, asphalt concrete pavement warranty work, cement‑treated permeable bases, rubblizing of concrete pavements, asphalt‑treated permeable bases and geosynthetic‑stabilized bridge approaches. The lift shall be placed to the thickness shown on the standard drawings after the existing subgrade or subbase has been compacted to its original grade; any disturbance caused by saw cutting or milling must be re‑compacted with vibratory compactors, and if the remaining subgrade/subbase is not at original grade additional subbase shall be placed and re‑compacted [2]. Vertical faces of the prepared area shall be cleaned and coated uniformly with PG 64S‑22 before the asphalt base course is laid in a manner that avoids segregation, and the newly placed lift shall be compacted to the density required by the approved compaction equipment referenced in Section 108.05(c); lifts may only be placed when the prepared surface is dry and the air or surface temperature exceeds thirty‑five degrees Fahrenheit [2]. If the indicated compacted depth of a Superpave 25.0 mm asphalt base course is more than six inches, it shall be laid in two or more layers each no less than three inches and no more than six inches; if the indicated compacted depth of a Superpave 37.5 mm asphalt base course is more than eight inches, the layers shall be between four inches and eight inches [2]. Surface irregularities greater than one‑quarter inch render the pavement defective, and depth control requires maintaining loose depth within tolerance and drilling full‑depth cores at one random location per three thousand square yards in the presence of the Inspector; any lift deficient in depth by one‑half inch or more that cannot be satisfactorily corrected shall also be classified as defective [2]. For lifts placed in geocell cells the compacted lift thickness shall be one to two inches greater than the cell height when the cell height exceeds six inches, with compaction performed as specified in Section 350; the top of the compacted subbase must be at least one inch above the geocell top to protect it during paving, and the drop height of the subbase material may not exceed a maximum of three feet [2]. After final compaction of each asphalt concrete lift a core at least four inches in diameter shall be drilled through all lifts and courses for every two thousand linear feet of lane within twenty‑four hours and measured against PTM No. 737; any single core showing a depth deficiency greater than one‑quarter inch that cannot be corrected or a uniform deficiency greater than one‑eighth inch across three consecutive cores that cannot be corrected shall render the area defective, and corrective work shall bring the repair zone within one‑quarter inch of the required depth [2].

Pervious concrete pavement systems and asphalt interlayer installations also prescribe lift controls. In the detention‑basin portion of a pervious concrete system coarse aggregate shall be placed in lifts that do not exceed eight inches thickness, each lift being compacted according to the requirements of Section 360.3 before the next layer is added; the test‑section thickness for pervious concrete shall not be more than one‑quarter inch less than the design thickness shown on Section 501.3(s), and the average hardened density of the two test sections shall serve as the acceptance basis with a tolerance of plus or minus five pounds per cubic foot and an upper limit of one hundred thirty pounds per cubic foot [2]. For the first lift of an asphalt overlay over an interlayer system the compacted thickness shall not be less than one and a half inches, the liquid asphalt binder temperature shall be kept at or above two hundred ninety degrees Fahrenheit and shall never exceed three hundred twenty degrees Fahrenheit during application, and the tack coat applied to the geotextile paving fabric shall be delivered at a rate of 0.22 to 0.28 gallons per square yard covering the fabric width plus six inches on each side with overlaps of at least one inch; prior to placement a pre‑placement meeting with the manufacturer’s technical representative shall review material type, application width and rate, mixture acceptance, surface cleaning and sequencing of work, ensuring that the pavement surface is dry, clean and free of debris [2].

Equipment and personnel

In roller‑compacted concrete pavement lifts (Section 502) the preparation step calls for a clean, foreign‑material‑free subgrade or subbase that is uniformly moistened, typically using at least one water truck equipped with a fine‑mist distribution system. Placement of each lift depends on a mixing plant that meets AASHTO M 241 and covered haul trucks that keep the RCC mixture within the 50 °F–90 °F temperature range; a conventional or high‑density paver with built‑in compaction devices is used to achieve at least 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts less than six inches thick or 90 % for lifts greater than or equal to six inches before rollers are employed. Primary and final compaction are provided by self‑propelled smooth dual steel‑drum rollers of minimum eight tons weight (or walk‑behind vibratory rollers/plate tampers where access is limited) together with optional rubber‑tired rollers to remove equipment marks as required, and curing is maintained with the same water truck delivering a uniform mist. Testing is performed by a concrete technician and a field testing technician using ASTM C1040 direct‑transmission density equipment within thirty minutes of rolling to verify greater than 96 % compaction acceptance.

For foamed glass aggregate lifts (Section 218) any standing water deeper than six inches and all ice must be removed before placement, after which a Class 4, Type A geotextile is installed and the FGA is placed. Compaction for lifts up to twenty‑four inches thick is done with a tracked excavator or dozer that provides ground pressures between 625 pounds per square foot and 1,025 pounds per square foot, making four full passes; areas inaccessible to tracks are compacted with a plate compactor weighing between 110 lb and 220 lb, also completing four full passes. Testing consists of bulk‑density testing each truckload for moist loose density.

In pervious asphalt pavement lifts (Section 420) subgrade preparation calls for light equipment such as a spring‑tooth rake or equivalent operated by a light tractor to scarify the soil while avoiding heavy haul trucks. The mixture is spread and finished with either a track paver (per Section 413.3(e)) or a mechanical spreader that uses fully automatic sensors to control profile and grade, and each lift is limited to four‑inch compacted thickness. Compaction requires smooth steel‑wheeled rollers operated in static mode—either a ten‑ton steel‑wheeled static roller or a vibratory roller used statically—with no more than four passes to preserve porosity; pneumatic‑tire rollers are prohibited. Test sections are later evaluated by infiltration testing according to ASTM C1701.

For embankment and fill lifts (Section 206) preparation begins with backfilling depressions and then proof‑rolling the foundation using a loaded triaxle dump truck of 72,000 lb GVW at uniform tire pressures and no more than one mile per hour. Placement calls for uniform material from the same borrow source but does not require special equipment beyond standard hauling. Compaction depends on the material: soil is compacted with a padfoot roller operated in static mode; Type 1 and Type 2 granular materials are compacted with a smooth‑drum vibratory roller or other dynamic/vibratory equipment as listed in Sections 108.05(c)3.d or 108.05(c)4, and confined areas may require hand‑operated rollers; rock is also compacted with a smooth‑drum vibratory roller to achieve non‑movement. Finishing includes fine grading and sealing the surface at the end of each workday by rolling with a smooth‑drum roller. Testing requires a calibrated nuclear density/moisture gauge (AASHTO T 310 or PTM 418) operated by a certified Nuclear Equipment Operator, with acceptance testing recorded on Form TR‑4276AC and verification testing performed with an independent gauge recorded on Form TR‑4276AD.

Gefoam lightweight fill lifts (Section 219) require that the embankment foundation be prepared with a six‑inch loose bedding course graded parallel to the longitudinal profile and kept free of debris and water. Blocks are positioned by hand, although transport or lifting devices may be used only if they do not dent or otherwise damage the blocks, and an on‑site scale capable of weighing each block is required to verify block density. A six‑inch minimum compacted lift of capping material that caps the blocks is spread with pneumatic‑tire equipment and then consolidated using a smooth drum roller operating in static (non‑vibratory) mode; testing of block density relies on the calibrated scale recorded at the time of measurement.

During manual asphalt patching (Section 450) preparation may employ a milling or planing machine, while placement of the asphalt wearing‑course is done by hand. Each lift in a Type B repair is compacted with an approved mechanical tamper, and the final lift—whether wearing‑course or binder—must be compacted with a steel‑wheeled roller or other approved compaction equipment; no specific finishing, curing, or testing equipment is mentioned.

Flexible base replacement lifts (Section 316) are prepared by first marking the perimeter and then using saw‑cutting or milling equipment to define the edges before removing all material within that line. The existing subgrade or subbase is compacted with vibratory compactors prior to placing the asphalt base course, which is laid without segregation to the indicated lift thickness. Final consolidation of the lift is achieved by operating the approved compaction equipment referenced in Section 108.05(c).

For geocell load distribution mat lifts (Section 222) the subgrade must be compacted to a uniform surface using standard compaction equipment before any geocell work begins. Collapsed geocell panels are positioned and expanded by driving anchors until they extend at least twelve inches below the geocell, or an installation frame may be used where anchoring is impractical; adjacent panels are joined with mechanical fasteners per manufacturer recommendation. Subbase material is placed into the expanded cells ensuring that drop height does not exceed three feet and overfilling enough to leave at least one inch above the geocell, after which compaction equipment specified in Section 350 is employed to achieve the required density; no guidance on finishing, curing, or testing equipment is provided.

In cement‑treated permeable base course lifts (Section 303) preparation requires thoroughly sprinkling water onto the rubblized pavement or subbase to achieve a moist condition. Placement is performed with an approved asphalt paver or mechanical spreader equipped with a screed, plate vibrator and fully automated sensors for profile and transverse grade control. Consolidation is achieved by passing steel‑wheel power rollers certified at a metal weight between 8 tons and 10 tons, with at least one roller pass required; the finished surface is checked with a 10‑foot straightedge. Curing begins immediately after compaction either by applying a white membrane forming curing compound at a rate of 1 gallon per 100 square feet ± 10 % or by covering the course with white polyethylene sheeting for twenty‑four hours, extended if temperature falls below 40 °F.

For asphalt‑treated permeable base course lifts (Section 360) preparation uses an asphalt mixing plant to heat the asphalt material and may dry aggregate as needed, producing the mix at a temperature below 320 °F. Placement is done with a slip‑form paver or mechanical spreader that spreads the mixture into compacted lifts not exceeding four inches, with screed assemblies adjusted and fully automated sensors controlling profile and transverse grade. Compaction employs steel‑wheel power rollers certified at 8–10 tons (or a vibratory roller in static mode) applying four passes after the mat has cooled to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed; surface tolerance testing uses a 10‑foot straightedge and depth verification requires drilling one six‑inch diameter test hole per three thousand square yards of completed base course.

In pervious concrete pavement system lifts (Section 520) subgrade preparation is performed with light equipment such as a light tractor and a spring‑tooth rake to scarify at least six inches; forms are set, aligned, braced and treated with a form‑release agent before placement. Mixing and hauling employ truck mixers operating at a minimum of 70 to 100 revolutions per minute of the drum, and concrete must be placed within ninety minutes of final water addition. Placement may use a form‑riding paving machine, roller screed, vibrating screed or asphalt paver with compaction tampers where access permits. Consolidation is achieved with smooth steel‑wheeled rollers in static mode for one to four passes (no pneumatic‑tire rollers) using a maximum two‑ton roller to eliminate seams. Final surface finish is applied with steel finishing equipment or a motorized pan finisher provided the voids remain open. Curing begins within twenty minutes of discharge by completely covering the surface with polyethylene sheeting secured at all edges for at least seven days. Testing includes unit‑weight testing on every truck per ASTM C1688, extraction of three four‑inch diameter cores per five thousand square feet for oven‑dry density and void content per ASTM C1754, and infiltration testing on designated areas per ASTM C1701.

For asphalt paving interlayer system lifts (Section 466) preparation requires the existing pavement to be dry and clean of dirt, gravel, vegetation and oil. Asphalt material is applied with a calibrated distributor truck spray bar, with hand spraying, squeegee or brush permitted only where the truck cannot reach. Placement uses mechanical or manual lay‑down equipment capable of providing a smooth installation with minimal wrinkling or folding. Consolidation is obtained by brooming or rubber‑tire rolling (or both); no specific finishing, curing or testing equipment is prescribed.

In plain or reinforced cement concrete pavement lifts (Section 501) preparation calls for thoroughly sprinkling water onto the base course and, when evaporation exceeds 0.20 lb/ft²·h, employing windbreaks or fog‑mist equipment to keep the rate below that threshold. Steel forms (or approved wood forms) secured with pins, shims and bracing provide the placement framework while sufficient hauling units are scheduled for continuous concrete delivery. Consolidation is achieved with vibrators capable of transmitting at least 100 impulses per second, applied no longer than five seconds in one spot. Finishing includes strike‑off tools and straightedge testing as outlined in the quality‑control plan. Curing follows the prescribed application methods and temperature monitoring. Testing comprises aggregate sieve analysis, non‑destructive depth testing using verified targets and measuring devices, and concrete joint wipe tests. [2]

Work sequence

For roller‑compacted concrete lifts, before placement the project must have a prepared subgrade and subbase identified in design and shown as separate construction items, capable of supporting compaction [1]. In addition, per Specification Section 502 the contractor must prepare a paving operation QC Plan for Representative review, provide a qualified concrete technician and a concrete field testing technician, construct a minimum 50‑foot test section with project equipment to verify mix consistency, placement capability, roller selection and curing procedures, have all paving, hauling, paver, compactor and water‑truck equipment on site and approved by the Representative, and clean the subgrade or subbase of foreign material, ponded water, frost or frozen material and uniformly moisten it in accordance with the plans [2].

For an ultra‑thin bonded wearing course overlay, before the single lift of hot‑mix asphalt concrete can be placed the polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane must first be laid by a self‑priming paver directly ahead of the paving screed; only after that membrane is in place may the ultra‑thin bonded wearing course be overlaid in one lift [1]. Moreover, before any overlay lift is placed, pre‑overlay surface preparation must be completed, requiring either a scratch course to fill wheel ruts and small depressions or a leveling course at least 1 inch thick to provide a uniform working platform and correct the pavement profile [1].

For foamed glass aggregate lifts, standing water deeper than six inches and all ice must be removed, a Class 4, Type A geotextile must be installed as shown on the drawings, and documentation of make, model and ground pressure of the compaction or tracked equipment must be submitted to the Representative for verification and acceptance, allowing ten working days for review [2].

For pervious‑asphalt pavement system lifts, two test sections must be produced using the approved JMF and placement and finishing operations, the average infiltration value of both sections must exceed 200 inches per hour, the compacted thickness may not be more than one‑quarter inch less than design, the subgrade must be excavated to undisturbed soil without compaction, scarified where damaged, repaired as needed and left permeable, and the Representative must inspect and accept the subgrade work before any further pavement courses are installed; placement may not occur when air or surface temperature is lower than 50 °F, and lifts may not exceed four inches compacted [2].

For embankment and fill lifts, the foundation must be prepared in accordance with Section 201, including back‑filling depressions such as gullies, old stream channels, stump holes and areas of undercutting to the adjacent ground elevation using suitable material; the entire foundation must then be proof‑rolled with a loaded triaxle dump truck at tire pressures between 100 and 110 psi and at speeds not greater than 1.5 ft/s, any rutting greater than one‑half inch must be excavated, dried and re‑compacted or replaced, and a compaction control QC Plan (Form CS‑206) must be submitted at the pre‑construction conference or at least 14 calendar days before placement and compaction is scheduled to commence [2].

For slab stabilization using asphalt grout lifts, the grout must be heated to a temperature between 380 °F and 450 °F, asphalt that exceeds 500 °F must be rejected, and only after these preparation steps are verified may lift monitoring and injection proceed [2].

For geofoam lightweight fill lifts, shop drawings showing block layout must be submitted to the Representative for review and acceptance; the embankment foundation area at or below original ground must be prepared in accordance with Section 206.3(a); a leveling course of a six‑inch loose bedding material meeting Section 219.2(e) must be placed, graded parallel to the roadway profile and free of debris and water, and a manufacturer’s representative must be on site for any period deemed necessary before block placement [2].

For Type B manual asphalt patch binder‑course lifts, the marked area must be cut out and excavated to sound, stable material with each patch at least one square foot, all debris must be removed, the surface and surrounding pavement must be thoroughly cleaned of loose or foreign material and completely dried, a tack coat must be applied to the bottom and vertical faces as specified in Section 460.3(b) and allowed to cure, and only after this cured tack coat can the asphalt binder course be placed in lifts not exceeding three inches uncompacted depth [2].

For pipe under‑drain and pavement base drain backfill lifts, the trench must be excavated to the width shown on the Standard Drawings and to a depth extending at least two inches into the subgrade without disturbing the pavement structure or subbase; the prefabricated pavement base drain then has to be positioned vertically in that trench as illustrated, and only after the drain is set may the first lift of backfill material be placed simultaneously with the drain installation [2].

For plant‑mixed 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlay courses, a prepared surface must be ready to receive the plant‑mixed, dense‑graded asphalt concrete with a 6.3 mm nominal maximum aggregate size before the thin lift wearing course can be placed [2].

For flexible base replacement lifts, the contractor must first mark the perimeter of the area to be replaced, saw cut or mill that perimeter, remove all material within the saw‑cut or milled area to the depth indicated, then compact the existing subgrade or subbase; if removal has disturbed the surface a re‑compaction with vibratory compactors is required along with any grade correction by adding and recompacting subbase to original grade, unsuitable subgrade or subbase must be removed and backfilled as specified in Section 203.1(b) or (c) when directed, and all vertical faces of the patch area must be cleaned and uniformly coated with PG 64S‑22 before the lift is placed [2].

For standard construction base course lifts, the crew must verify that the prepared surface is not wet and that both the air temperature and the surface temperature are above 35 °F; placement is prohibited if either condition is not met [2].

For geocell load distribution mat lifts, the expanded geocell must already be positioned on a prepared, compacted subgrade with geotextile installed, the cells must be filled with loose subbase that is overfilled so the top of the compacted material lies at least one inch above the geocell, equipment may not be operated on any unfilled geocell sections until this over‑filling is complete, and the drop height of the placed subbase shall not exceed three feet to avoid damaging the cell walls [2].

For cement treated permeable base course lifts, the existing rubblized pavement or subbase must first be conditioned by thoroughly sprinkling water to achieve a moist condition; the water should be applied in an amount the material readily absorbs and well ahead of placement [2].

For rubblizing of concrete pavements prior to backfill lifts, the pavement must first be cleared of any overlaying asphalt as required in Section 490.3, and the concrete surface must be prepared by rubblizing, which includes excavating two full‑depth test sections within the first half‑mile to verify that the specified piece dimensions are being achieved; only after those excavation and verification steps are completed may subbase No. 2A be placed in backfill lifts no thicker than six inches, each compacted by rolling until nonmovement is achieved [2].

For asphalt treated permeable base course lifts, the surface must be stable, unfrozen and at least 35 °F; the aggregate must be dried as necessary, the asphalt material heated and the ATPBC produced at a temperature below 320 °F with suitable viscosity, and the finished mix then has to be placed within eight hours of production before spreading and compaction begin [2].

For geosynthetic stabilized bridge approach lifts, the project must first complete the material submittals at least thirty days prior to the start of GSBA construction, finish full foundation preparation – excavating to the required lines and grade, removing unsuitable soils, proof‑rolling to achieve a uniform firm surface and repairing any unstable areas, and the prepared foundation then must be inspected and approved by the Representative before any fill or geosynthetics are placed [2].

For asphalt paving interlayer system lifts, a pre‑placement meeting with the manufacturer’s technical representative must be held and the existing pavement surface must be fully prepared; it must be dry, free of dirt, gravel, vegetation, oil and loose debris, all cracks sealed and allowed to cure, and any potholes or other distresses repaired as identified by the Representative, after which a tack coat of asphalt material must be applied uniformly with a calibrated distributor truck at a temperature no lower than 290 °F and not exceeding 320 °F before the interlayer fabric or grid is laid [2].

For plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements, a comprehensive paving operation QC Plan must be submitted at least two weeks before the start of paving operations and paving may not begin until the plan is accepted; steel forms that are not less than ten feet in length, with form depth and base width equal to pavement depth, must be cleaned, oiled and secured so they do not deflect beyond tolerance; the base course must be prepared and, if necessary, sprinkled with water to keep it moist and its surface temperature below 115 °F; concrete may not be placed when the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 lb/ft²·hr unless mitigation measures such as windbreaks or fog misting are employed; applicable cold‑ and hot‑weather temperature restrictions must be observed, and adequate equipment, trained personnel and sufficient hauling units must be ensured to maintain continuity of placement [2].

Roller‑compacted concrete pavement lifts are initiated by preparing a paving operation quality‑control plan, providing the required technicians, and constructing a minimum 50‑foot test section to verify mix consistency, equipment performance, roller combination, density testing procedures, curing methods and compressive strength; after approval the subgrade or subbase is cleaned, uniformly moistened and all approved equipment—including mixing plant, covered haul trucks, a suitable paver equipped with compaction devices, rollers and a water truck—is positioned on site while observing weather limits such as air temperatures above 40 °F and avoiding hard rain. The RCC mixture, maintained at a temperature of 50 °F to 90 °F, is hauled to the paver and placed in lifts of at least 4 inches (single lifts may be up to 9 inches), with the paver achieving a minimum compaction of 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts under 6 inches or 90 % for lifts 6 inches or greater before any roller passes, after which rollers complete primary and final compaction to exceed 96 % and field‑density tests are performed within 30 minutes, while the surface is kept continuously moist using water trucks, misting equipment or curing compounds for a minimum of 72 hours. According to the Pavement Policy Manual, in roller‑compacted concrete pavement each lift is built by first placing the very dry RCC mix with a conventional or high‑density asphalt paver and then compacting it with rollers to obtain the required density; because RCC does not require forms, steel reinforcement, or dowel bars, these two actions constitute the primary sequence for building up pavement thickness, which may be achieved by placing RCC in single or multiple lifts. Ultra‑thin bonded wearing course lifts are installed by a self‑priming paver that first lays the polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane directly ahead of the screed and then immediately overlays this membrane with hot‑mix asphalt concrete as it passes, creating one continuous layer that is compacted to a final thickness equal to 1 to 1.5 times the diameter of the coarsest aggregate, corresponding to ½ inch for Type A and ¾ inch for Types B and C. Foamed glass aggregate lifts are placed after the contractor follows the manufacturer’s delivery, storage and handling recommendations, removes standing water deeper than six inches and all ice, submits equipment make, model and ground‑pressure data for a ten‑working‑day review, installs Class 4 Type A geotextile with overlaps of at least twelve inches, then places the aggregate in the lifts shown on the drawings; when tracked equipment is used each uncompacted lift may be up to twenty‑four inches thick (or between twelve and twenty‑four inches) and must be compacted with a tracked excavator or dozer applying ground pressures of between 625 lb/ft² and 1,025 lb/ft², raising the blade or bucket to track over the layer and making four full passes defined as one hundred percent coverage, whereas where tracked equipment cannot reach lifts are limited to twelve inches thick and compacted with a plate compactor weighing between 110 lb and 220 lb, also completing four full passes; after compaction rollers are not allowed on the FGA, storm water must be prevented from accumulating until sufficient over‑burden is placed, and any rutting deeper than four inches must be repaired by excavating to five inches below the rut, refilling and compacting with four plate‑compactor passes. Pervious asphalt pavement system lifts are placed by spreading or striking off the mixture with a track paver or mechanical spreader, adjusting the screed to achieve the required cross‑section and depth and constructing the profile to the design grade line while limiting each placement to maximum four‑inch compacted lifts; after placement the mixture must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is laid, then the lift is compacted with a ten‑ton steel‑wheeled static roller or a vibratory roller operated in static mode, and compaction is limited to not more than four passes so that porosity is retained; for multiple‑lift construction the second lift may be placed and rolled perpendicular to the direction of placement and rolling of the first lift. Manual asphalt patching lifts are installed after completing the preparation step and allowing the tack coat to cure, by placing the asphalt binder course in successive lifts that shall not exceed three inches in uncompacted depth, each lift being leveled and compacted with an approved mechanical tamper before the next lift is installed; once the binder lifts are finished the topmost one‑and‑a‑half inch of asphalt wearing‑course material is laid, the surface is verified to conform to the grade of the surrounding pavement, and the final lift is compacted with a steel‑wheeled roller or other approved compaction equipment. In pipe underdrain and pavement base drain installation, after excavating a trench that does not disturb the pavement structure or subbase, the prefabricated pavement base drain is placed vertically in the trench and the first lift of backfill material is placed simultaneously to achieve full contact with the geotextile and trench wall, any tears in the geotextile being repaired before proceeding; subsequent lifts consist of fine‑aggregate Type A placed in lifts not exceeding nine inches thick, each lift being sprayed with water at a rate of one gallon per linear foot and then hydraulically compacted before the next lift is added, while maintaining a continuous sequence of excavation, drain placement and backfill operations and keeping trench exposure to a minimum. Flexible base replacement begins by marking the perimeter of the area to be replaced and then saw‑cut or mill along that line, removing all material inside the cut to the required depth; after the existing subgrade or subbase is compacted (and re‑compacted if disturbed) vertical faces are cleaned and uniformly coated with PG 64S‑22 before the asphalt base course is placed without segregation to the specified lift thickness, and finally the lift is compacted to the density called for using approved compaction equipment as referenced in Section 108.05(c). Construction of a Superpave asphalt lift starts by verifying that the prepared surface is dry and that air temperature exceeds 35 °F before placing any base course; the mix is then spread in layers whose compacted thickness obeys the limits— for a 25.0 mm mix with an indicated depth over six inches use two or more layers each no less than three inches and no more than six inches, and similarly for a 37.5 mm mix over eight inches use layers between four and eight inches—while controlling the loose depth of each layer to achieve the specified compacted depth within tolerance; after the top lift is completed the Inspector directs drilling of full‑depth cores at one random location per three thousand square yards of completed base course and at any suspect spots, measures core depth according to PTM No. 737 and declares the pavement defective if irregularities exceed a quarter inch or depth deficiency is half inch or more and cannot be corrected, then backfills, compacts and seals each core hole with the same mixture and immediately corrects deficient areas longitudinally and transversely until the depth shortfall is less than half inch; within twenty‑four hours after final compaction of all lifts an inspector‑present coring operation using a four‑inch (or larger) drill is performed through every lift to the existing base, any single core showing a deficiency greater than a quarter inch that cannot be satisfactorily corrected or three consecutive cores uniformly low by more than one eighth inch deem the area defective, after which the holes are backfilled, compacted and sealed with the same wearing‑course mixture and deficient depth is immediately corrected by adding material until the repaired zone is within a quarter inch of the required depth, with additional cores drilled as needed. Cement‑treated permeable base course lifts are placed after thoroughly sprinkling water on the rubblized pavement or subbase until it readily absorbs moisture to create a moist condition; the CTPBC is then placed using an approved asphalt paver or mechanical spreader equipped with a screed, plate vibrator and automated sensors to control profile and transverse grade, and compacted with steel‑wheel power rollers of eight to ten tons weight, making at least one roller pass while ensuring the material remains free draining and the aggregate is not crushed; compaction must not exceed one hour from the time water is added to the aggregate and cement (or up to one and a half hours if a retarding admixture is used), after which surface tolerances are checked with a ten‑foot straightedge at intervals no greater than five feet and any irregularities exceeding one‑half inch are corrected by loosening, adding or removing CTPBC without grinding or milling; curing begins immediately after compaction using a white membrane forming curing compound applied at a rate of one gallon per hundred square feet ± 10 % (or alternatively covering with white polyethylene sheeting) and is maintained for twenty‑four hours, extending another twenty‑four hours if the temperature falls below forty °F, and the CTPBC must be removed and replaced at no cost to the Department if the temperature drops to thirty‑two °F within seventy‑two hours of placement; construction traffic is prohibited on the fresh CTPBC for at least three days, overlying pavement may not be placed during this period, and after three days only equipment needed for the next layer or adjacent lift may enter, with any damaged areas repaired at no cost. Rubblizing work requires that before any lift work begins a rubblizing plan and equipment list be approved, existing asphalt over concrete is removed, the pavement is mechanically or sonically rubblized to the required fragment size, test excavations of two three‑foot by twelve‑foot sections are made early in the operation, all excavated voids are backfilled with subbase No. 2A placed in maximum six‑inch lifts, each lift being compacted by rolling until nonmovement is achieved and shoving or wave motion under the roller is avoided; after backfilling a fifty‑ton pneumatic‑tired roller seats the rubblized material using a pattern that contacts every point of the surface in no more than four passes, and final seating is accepted only when vertical deflection under the roller is less than three‑quarters inch; the surface is then swept clean, traffic is prohibited until the prime coat (if an asphalt leveling course is required) is applied, and the overlay must be placed within sixty calendar days, with all damage to utilities or bridge elements repaired at no cost to the Department. Asphalt‑treated permeable base course lifts are prepared by drying the aggregate, heating the asphalt and coating the aggregate, then producing the mix at a temperature below three hundred twenty degrees Fahrenheit and placing it no later than eight hours after production because stockpiling is prohibited; the material is spread with a slip‑form paver or mechanical spreader, struck off, and the screed adjusted to achieve the required cross‑section while constructing the profile to the design grade line, each lift being limited to a maximum four‑inch compacted thickness and the mix being allowed to cool to one hundred degrees Fahrenheit before the next lift is placed; rolling begins as soon as the mat has cooled sufficiently, using an eight‑ton to ten‑ton steel‑wheel roller in static mode and applying exactly four passes, with any extra passes permitted only to remove surface irregularities, and longitudinal joints are overlapped one to two inches while transverse joints are formed at day’s end or after a thirty‑minute interruption by a temporary bulkhead or saw cut. Geosynthetic stabilized bridge approach lifts begin with grading the embankment subgrade and lay‑back slope to the required profile and proof‑rolling, then laying a continuous Class 4, Type A separation geosynthetic flat, pulled tight and anchored over the prepared surface; aggregate for each lift is placed in a non‑destructive manner—hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading—rather than being dumped directly from a haul truck onto the exposed geosynthetic, and two six‑inch compacted layers of the specified coarse aggregate are built up to the elevation of the next stabilization geosynthetic while keeping the geosynthetic within plus‑minus half inch of plan elevation; after each lift the surface is graded without wrinkling the geosynthetic and vehicle traffic is prohibited until at least six inches of loose backfill cover the geosynthetic, the process being repeated, overlapping adjacent geosynthetic strips a minimum of twelve inches, until the subgrade elevation is reached. Staging and logistics

In Foamed Glass Aggregate lifts, material delivery and storage must follow the manufacturer’s recommendations and be kept in a stockpile area that is not prone to flooding. Haul roads may only be used after an evaluation of the capping thickness—at least six inches compacted—is submitted for Department approval. Before placement any standing water deeper than six inches or ice must be removed, and equipment ground‑pressure data must be provided for verification; tracked equipment can work on lifts up to 24 inches thick if its ground pressure stays between 625 and 1,025 pounds per square foot and it makes four full passes covering the lift. Where tracked equipment cannot access, lifts are limited to 12 inches and must be compacted with a plate compactor weighing between 110 and 220 pounds, also requiring four full passes. Traffic on exposed or compacted FGA is minimized until a six‑inch compacted capping layer is placed, and any rutting deeper than four inches must be repaired by excavating to five inches below the rut and reinstalling new material. In pervious asphalt pavement systems, work zones are protected by prohibiting haul trucks and other heavy equipment from driving through the installation area and by limiting traffic on fresh binder courses. Material delivery is staged so that equipment does not traverse unprotected subgrade before geotextile and stone‑bed placement, and any temporary sediment control is removed once the site stabilizes. Stockpiles of equipment, materials, or soils are not allowed to be placed on pervious pavement beds during construction. Access routes remain clear of heavy traffic until the subgrade is prepared, and traffic on the binder course is avoided when practical to prevent contamination. Equipment movement uses smooth steel‑wheeled rollers in static mode with one to four passes, and pneumatic‑tire rollers are prohibited. For each lift of embankment or fill, the contractor must provide uniform material from the same borrow source for the entire lift being placed, place it in full‑width horizontal layers, and compact it with the equipment specified for that material while keeping compaction speed at or below 1.5 feet per second (1 mile per hour). Moisture content is kept within the tolerances given for soil, Type 1 and Type 2 granular materials, and each layer is fine‑graded and rolled at the end of the day to maintain drainage and stability. In Geofoam lightweight fill lifts, material delivery must follow the manufacturer’s recommendations, and each truck load is verified on‑site with a scale sized no more than two and one half times the weight of the heaviest block and calibrated before use. Stockpiles are placed in a secure area away from heat sources, off the ground, protected from standing water, direct sunlight, and wind‑induced movement by sandbags; vehicle or equipment traversal over the blocks is prohibited and foot traffic is kept to a minimum. Equipment may only transport or lift blocks if it does not dent or puncture them; during placement all handling is done by hand and no wheeled, tracked, or sharp‑turning pneumatic‑tire equipment is allowed on block surfaces, with capping material applied by end dumping just off the blocks and blading an 8‑inch‑thick loose lift using pneumatic‑tire equipment. Traffic through the work zone is therefore confined to designated delivery routes that avoid the stored blocks, and any single triaxle truck load may be dumped onto previously compacted layers but must not be driven directly on the blocks. For plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements, work zones are defined by constructing the pavement in one or two traffic lane widths as shown on the Standard Drawings, and a one‑lane layout is required whenever a two‑lane layout would interfere with traffic. For projects exceeding 15,000 square yards of mainline concrete, aggregate gradations must be monitored using plant stockpiled aggregate by performing sieve analysis before daily production and each time bins are restocked, but not more than once per day. Prior to placement the contractor must have adequate equipment, trained personnel, and enough hauling units scheduled to keep placement continuous, while travel on a freshly prepared surface is limited to the immediate area needed for depositing concrete ahead of the paver and the surface may not be used for general hauling. Quality control and acceptance

For roller‑compacted concrete lifts (Section 502) the inspector must verify a minimum thickness of 4 inches and, when placed as a single lift, the thickness shall be between 4 inches and 9 inches. Compaction is inspected by in‑place wet‑density tests performed according to ASTM C1040 no later than 30 minutes after rolling; at least five measurements are taken directly behind the paver and at least five after final compaction for each 500 cubic yards. The required density is a minimum of 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts less than 6 inches thick, or 90 % for lifts greater than or equal to 6 inches, and after final rolling the lift must achieve more than 96 % of maximum wet density. The RCC mixture temperature shall be maintained within 50 °F to 90 °F during placement, and successive lifts must be placed within 60 minutes of one another to avoid cold joints. For foamed‑glass‑aggregate lifts (Section 218) the lift may not exceed an uncompacted thickness of 24 inches; when the lift thickness is between 12 and 24 inches it must be compacted with tracked equipment whose ground pressure lies between 625 and 1,025 pounds per square foot. Compaction is considered complete only after four full passes covering the entire lift, and every truckload that forms a lift must be tested for moist loose bulk density in the presence of the Representative to verify compliance. Pervious‑asphalt pavement system lifts (Section 420) may not exceed 4 inches in compacted thickness and the final compacted thickness shall be no more than ¼ inch less than the design thickness. The surface temperature must be allowed to drop to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed, and compaction shall be limited to no more than four passes so that porosity is retained. Test sections of at least 225 square feet each are constructed and infiltrated in accordance with ASTM C1701; the average infiltration rate from both test sections must exceed 200 inches per hour. Embankment or fill lifts (Section 206) are inspected for moisture content and in‑place density using a calibrated nuclear gauge in accordance with PTM No. 402, with the frequency of acceptance testing following the Representative’s direction. Soil lifts except the top three feet must achieve not less than 97 % of maximum dry density, while the upper three feet must reach 100 %; Type 1 granular material must be placed within ±2 % of optimum moisture content and compacted to at least 97 % of maximum dry density, with the top three feet required to meet 100 %. Type 2 material is to be surface‑damp but not wet. Stability is verified by confirming that no rutting, displacement or shear wave occurs under the compaction equipment, and verification testing with an independent gauge must agree within ±2.0 lb/ft³ for wet density and within ±0.8 w for moisture. Slab‑stabilization lifts (Section 679) require vertical‑movement testing using equipment that can simultaneously record the lift at two outside slab corners adjacent to a joint and the adjoining shoulder; the device must be capable of detecting slab lift to a resolution of 0.001 inch, and any upward movement greater than 0.05 inch is not allowed. Geofoam lightweight‑fill lifts (Section 219) have their density verified by weighing a randomly selected block from every truckload on a calibrated onsite scale accurate to one‑tenth of a pound, recording dimensions, weight and calculated density; any block whose density falls outside the required range causes rejection of the whole load. Blocks may not exhibit damage exceeding 0.5 % volumetric loss, more than 5 % surface loss on the load‑bearing area or total surface, or continuous damage longer than 20 % of any side, and damaged units must be replaced. The capping material placed as a lift shall be at least a 6‑inch minimum compacted layer, the finished block surface of each lift must be within a tolerance of ½ inch over any 10‑foot interval with vertical joint gaps no greater than 0.1 foot; overall cover over the blocks must be between a minimum of 4 feet and not exceed 6 feet. Manual asphalt‑patching lifts (Section 450) for Type B binder‑course may not exceed an uncompacted depth of 3 inches; each lift is leveled and then compacted with an approved mechanical tamper, while the final wearing‑course lift is compacted with a steel‑wheeled roller, after which the surface must conform to the grade of the surrounding pavement. Flexible‑base replacement lifts (Section 316) must be placed without segregation to the thickness shown on the plans and compacted to the density prescribed in Section 108.05(c) using approved compaction equipment; lift thickness and achieved density are the characteristics that must be verified during construction. Base‑course lifts (Section 313) are considered defective if surface irregularities exceed ¼ inch, and the compacted depth of each layer is controlled to the design depth within the specified tolerance; on the top lift the inspector drills a full‑depth core at one random location per 3,000 square yards selected according to PTM No. 1 and measures that core’s depth according to PTM No. 737, and when the measured depth shows a deficiency of ½ inch or more that cannot be satisfactorily corrected the pavement is declared defective. Binder‑course and wearing‑course lifts under the 60‑month warranty (Section 496) must be inspected for thickness by drilling a core at least 4 inches in diameter within 24 hours after final compaction while the Inspector is present; the core depth is measured according to PTM No. 737 and any single measurement that exceeds the required depth by more than ¼ inch, or a uniform excess of more than ⅛ inch over three consecutive cores, constitutes defective work unless it can be corrected. The Department may require informational samples: one loose‑mix box sample taken behind the paver before compaction and one 6‑inch diameter pavement core for each 1,000 tons placed, both identified, packaged (no larger than 240 cubic inches for the loose mix), and delivered to the Inspector within the times specified. Cement‑treated permeable base course lifts (Section CTPBC) are tested for surface evenness with a 10‑foot straightedge at suspected irregular locations and across transverse joints, moving the straightedge no more than 5 feet between stages; any irregularity of more than ½ inch requires correction. Compaction is verified by ensuring at least one pass of a steel‑wheel power roller weighing from 8 tons to 10 tons, without over‑compacting to the point that drainage is lost or aggregate is crushed. Curing is checked by applying the white membrane curing compound at a rate of 1 gallon per 100 square feet ± 10 % (or using approved polyethylene sheeting) and maintaining it for 24 hours, extending the period if temperature falls below 40 °F. Protection is confirmed by prohibiting construction traffic on the lift for at least three days after placement and limiting post‑cure traffic to only equipment needed for the next layer. Rubblizing backfill lifts (Section 526) must be installed in lifts no thicker than six inches and each lift is required to be compacted by rolling until nonmovement is achieved, with shoving or wave motion prohibited under the roller. The final seating of the rubblized pavement is judged acceptable when vertical deflection measured under the 50‑ton pneumatic‑tired roller is less than three‑quarters inch; any area not meeting this limit must be undercut and unsuitable material removed. Asphalt‑treated permeable base course lifts (Section ATPBC) shall be placed in compacted lifts no thicker than 4 inches, allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is laid, and compacted with exactly four roller passes using an 8‑ton to 10‑ton steel‑wheel roller (additional passes only to remove surface irregularities). After compaction the surface must be inspected with a 10‑foot straightedge; any irregularity exceeding ½ inch requires loosening or adding material, and the lift is considered defective if such defects remain. Depth control is verified by drilling one 6‑inch diameter test hole to full depth for each 3,000 square yards (or less) of completed base course, with the Representative measuring the depth and requiring correction where the depth is short by ½ inch or more. The first lift of an asphalt overlay placed over an interlayer system must be compacted to at least 1½ inches; this thickness is a key acceptance criterion that inspectors verify before proceeding. The lift also must be installed on a dry surface, wet pavement is prohibited, and any damaged interlayer material observed during placement must be removed and replaced. Inspectors must check that no wrinkles larger than one inch remain—wrinkles of that size are to be sliced and overlapped flat—and that all fabric or grid overlaps provide at least a one‑inch lap with asphalt tack coat applied between them. The temperature of the underlying asphalt material used to bond the interlayer must stay within the specified range (not below 290 °F and not above 320 °F). [2]

Safety and environmental controls

During an asphalt grout lift, workers are exposed to burn hazards because the asphalt must be heated to between 380 °F and 450 °F and must not contact an open flame; uncontrolled spraying of this hot material can also injure nearby personnel or passing traffic. A splash shield of at least 15 sq ft is required between the nozzle and traffic, and the area must remain closed to vehicles for a minimum of 30 minutes after grouting before it is safe for the public. During placement of a Geofoam lightweight fill lift, workers must guard against physical injury and structural failure caused by block damage; blocks may not be lifted or transported in ways that create dents or holes, and vehicles, equipment, and foot traffic on stored blocks must be kept to a minimum. Heat sources, smoking, or open flames are prohibited in the storage area to prevent fire hazards, and blocks must be protected from vapors of organic solvents and petroleum fuels which present chemical exposure risks. When wind could move the blocks, they must be secured with sandbags or other soft weights that do not dent them, otherwise falling or shifting material could endanger both workers and nearby public users. During installation of prefabricated pavement base drains, the first back‑fill lift is placed simultaneously with the drain and each subsequent lift is compacted hydraulically to a maximum 9‑inch thickness while being sprayed with water; however, open trenches created for these lifts present collapse or falling material hazards to workers and the public. Consequently, trenches must not be left open during precipitation or overnight and must be barricaded at all times according to Chapter 203 of the Pennsylvania Code, limiting any open trench depth greater than 2 inches after back‑filling. During placement of each lift in a geosynthetic stabilized bridge approach, workers must guard against geosynthetic damage caused by high heat, sunlight and stray debris; therefore all geosynthetics are required to be protected from temperatures greater than 140 °F, direct exposure to sunlight, and debris that may damage the material. Aggregate may not be dumped directly from a haul truck onto the exposed geosynthetic. Vehicles pose a public safety hazard because no vehicles are allowed on the geosynthetic until a minimum of 6 inches of loose backfill has been placed, and sudden braking or sharp turning of any vehicle, including tracked equipment, on stabilized backfill is prohibited. During placement of the first lift of an asphalt overlay, workers and the public are exposed to heat and traffic hazards because the asphalt material must be kept at a minimum temperature of 290 °F and must not exceed 320 °F, and no traffic is permitted on the fresh material until it has cured. The interlayer system must be stored elevated and covered to keep it away from damage, ultraviolet light, precipitation, chemicals, and sparks, which could create fire or chemical exposure risks. Curing, protection, and handoff

The ultra‑thin bonded wearing course, placed in a single lift, acts as a seal that reduces oxidation and weathering of the surface, thereby helping the pavement remain resilient to fatigue and low‑temperature cracking, and no traffic restrictions are imposed on the paver‑placed surface treatment [1]. After the final rolling pass and in‑field density testing for roller‑compacted concrete lifts, the surface is kept moist by a water‑truck misting system, soaking hoses, sprinkler system or by applying a curing compound immediately after rolling at a rate that produces a uniform solid opaque coverage; the selected method is maintained for at least 72 hours and until a compressive strength of at least 3,000 psi is achieved, with cold‑weather procedures permitted as allowed [2]. Foamed glass aggregate material is stored away from flood‑prone areas, traffic is minimized until a geotextile is installed and an adequate capping layer of at least six inches compacted thickness is placed within two weeks, storm water is not allowed to accumulate on the lift until the ballast‑providing thickness is achieved, rollers are prohibited directly on the FGA, and any damage to the geotextile or rutting shall be repaired before further fill [2]. For pervious asphalt lifts a tack coat may not be applied between lifts, the mixture shall not be placed when air or surface temperature is below 50 °F, and after each compacted lift the mix must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer or wearing course is placed, with compaction limited to no more than four roller passes [2]. After each embankment or fill lift is placed and compacted, the surface shall be fine‑graded to eliminate low spots and then sealed at the end of the workday by rolling with a smooth drum roller to promote continuous positive drainage and reduce water infiltration; layers are shaped to drain during construction and furrows that would block surface water flow are prohibited [2]. For asphalt grout lifts water is applied around each drilled hole immediately after injection, a splash shield of at least 15 sq ft is erected between the nozzle and passing traffic, upward movement greater than 0.05 inch is not allowed, and the pavement must remain closed to traffic for a minimum of 30 minutes or longer in accordance with the contractor’s approved field QC plan or manufacturer’s recommendations [2]. Geofoam blocks are fully encapsulated in a continuous hydrocarbon‑resistant geomembrane that is field‑welded with hot‑wedge seams overlapping at least three inches, then covered with a Class 4, Type A geotextile overlapped a minimum of twelve inches and sealed with spray adhesive applied to at least six inches of each overlap; within two weeks of the geotextile installation a compacted lift of capping material at least six inches thick is placed over the geotextile using a smooth drum roller in static mode, and the blocks are protected from heat sources, direct sunlight, UV rays, organic‑solvent vapors and mechanical damage by using soft weights such as sandbags, avoiding vehicle traffic on block surfaces, and not using de‑icing salts [2]. For pipe underdrain and base drain lifts each backfill lift is compacted to a maximum depth of nine inches and sprayed with water at one gallon per linear foot; the trench is never left open during precipitation or overnight, openings greater than two inches in depth are prohibited after placement, barricades are used as required, the upstream end of the pipe underdrain or base drain is sealed to keep foreign material out, and all joints and fittings are protected from damage throughout construction [2]. Prior to placing a flexible‑base replacement asphalt lift the vertical faces of the prepared area are cleaned and coated with a uniform layer of PG 64S‑22 as a protective coating; the asphalt base course is then placed to the specified lift thickness without segregation and compacted with approved equipment to achieve the density required by Section 108.05(c) [2]. After a geocell is expanded and anchored, loose subbase is placed in the cells and over‑filled so that the compacted material extends at least one inch above the geocell; this cover permits fine grading and protects the underlying lift from construction traffic and heat during paving, and equipment may not be operated on the area until the overfill is achieved [2]. Within 24 hours after a lift is placed and compacted, a core of at least four‑inch diameter is drilled through all lifts in the presence of the Inspector; once depth measurements are completed the holes are backfilled, compacted, and sealed with the same mixture used to construct the wearing course, thereby protecting the newly placed lift while the core samples may be retained for quality‑control testing [2]. Cement‑treated permeable base course lifts are cured immediately after the roller pass by applying a white membrane‑forming curing compound at a rate of one gallon per 100 square feet ± 10 % (or an alternative white polyethylene sheet may be used) and are left in place for twenty‑four hours, with the cure period extended another twenty‑four hours if surface temperature drops below forty degrees Fahrenheit; construction traffic is not allowed on the CTPBC for at least three days and no overlying pavement may be placed during that interval [2]. Asphalt treated permeable base course lifts shall not be placed on unstable or frozen surfaces, nor when air temperature is below 35°F or during rain; after spreading the mixture is allowed to cool to 100°F before any subsequent layer is placed, rolling is performed as soon as the mat has cooled sufficiently to avoid shoving or lateral movement, and when work stops for more than a 30‑minute interruption or at the end of the day a temporary vertical bulkhead is installed to form a straight transverse construction joint that must be the full depth and width of the ATPBC [2]. Pervious concrete lifts are cured within 20 minutes of discharge and the surface is completely covered with polyethylene sheeting that is thoroughly secured at all exterior edges and interior laps to resist wind removal; the curing blanket remains in place for a minimum of seven days, during which rollers are prohibited from bridging the newly placed concrete for seven days or until the Representative confirms sufficient strength [2]. After an asphalt paving interlayer system lift is installed, all traffic except construction vehicles required for placement is prohibited and the surface is kept dry until the overlay is placed; any rain that falls on the interlayer must be allowed to dry before the asphalt overlay is applied, crack‑filling material used in surface preparation shall be cured before the interlayer is placed, the asphalt material shall not exceed 320°F, and the asphalt material shall be fully cured before allowing any construction traffic over the surface including paving [2].

Expected performance and maintenance

In SECTION 502—ROLLER COMPACTED CONCRETE PAVEMENTS inadequate compaction is a frequent distress; the specification requires at least 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts under six inches and 90 % of maximum wet density for thicker lifts directly behind the paver, with final rolling needing more than 96 % to be acceptable, and if the paver cannot achieve the minimum 95 % field density a self‑propelled smooth dual steel drum roller operating in static or vibratory mode having a minimum weight of 8 tons is used to achieve primary compaction before final compaction. When the interval between successive lifts exceeds sixty minutes the joint is classified as a cold joint and is prepared by cutting, cleaning and moistening the face to preserve bond, and improper paver operation that tears, shoves, gouges or causes segregation also creates permanent surface blemishes that are common lift distresses. In SECTION 218—FOAMED GLASS AGGREGATE the most common distresses are surface rutting that exceeds four inches in depth, crushing or alteration of the FGA gradation caused by excessive compaction pressure, damage to the overlying geotextile during handling or placement, and accumulation of storm water on the uncapped lift before a sufficient capping thickness of six inches compacted is provided. In SECTION 420—PERVIOUS ASPHALT PAVEMENT SYSTEM over‑compacting a pervious asphalt lift with a steel‑wheeled static roller beyond four passes can reduce surface porosity and crush the aggregate, making the layer no longer free‑draining, and the specification also requires that the compacted thickness of each lift not be more than 1/4‑inch less than the design thickness for test sections; exceeding this limit is a failure condition. In SECTION 206—PLACEMENT AND COMPACTION OF EMBANKMENT AND FILL permanent deformation (rutting) greater than ½ inch is considered unstable and requires excavation, drying and recompaction, and any lift in which the compactor observes rutting, displacement or a shear wave under the equipment also fails to meet the required stable condition and must be dried, reshaped, re‑compacted and retested for moisture and density; lifts placed with moisture outside the specified limits (soil within ‑3 % of optimum, Type 1 granular ±2 %, Type 2 surface damp) can become unstable, triggering the same corrective actions. In SECTION 679—SLAB STABILIZATION (ASPHALT GROUT STABILIZATION) upward movement of the pavement greater than 0.05 inch is not allowed and lift must be monitored with equipment capable of detecting changes as small as 0.001 inch; premature asphalt seepage from joints or cracks before undersealing is complete mandates an immediate stop to pumping until the material congeals. In SECTION 219—GEOFOAM LIGHTWEIGHT FILL the lift can suffer physical damage to its Geofoam blocks if they are dented, cracked, or otherwise compromised during manufacture, shipment, storage, or placement; such damage is considered unacceptable when volumetric loss exceeds 0.5 % of a block’s volume, surface damage exceeds 5 % of either the load‑bearing area or the total block surface, or continuous damage runs longer than 20 % of any side length, and damaged blocks must be replaced at no expense to the Department while any truckload containing out‑of‑tolerance blocks is rejected until density verification confirms compliance. In SECTION 496—ASPHALT CONCRETE PAVEMENT (60‑MONTH WARRANTY) the pavement is defective if irregularities are more than ¼ inch, and pavement deficient in depth by ½ inch or more that cannot be satisfactorily corrected is defective; the specifications prohibit placing a base course on prepared surfaces that are wet or if the temperature of the air or the prepared surface is 35°F or lower, conditions that can lead to inadequate compaction and subsequent distress. In SECTION 303—CEMENT TREATED PERMEABLE BASE COURSE a lift can fail when surface irregularities exceed more than ½ inch, when over‑compaction makes the layer not free draining or crushes the aggregate, when the temperature drops below 32°F within 72 hours of placement requiring removal and replacement, or when construction traffic or other contamination damages the material before the required protection period of at least three days has elapsed; any remaining irregularities after final compaction also render the area defective. In SECTION 360—ASPHALT TREATED PERMEABLE BASE COURSE the lift can develop surface irregularities greater than ½ inch if the ATPBC is not properly compacted or if compaction is continued until the mix loses its free‑draining condition and the aggregate becomes crushed; premature rolling before the mat has cooled to about 100°F can cause shoving or lateral movement of the lift, so rolling with an 8‑ton to 10‑ton steel‑wheel roller is limited to four passes unless additional passes are needed to eliminate creases. Depth deficiencies of ½ inch or more are considered defective and require removal and replacement of the affected section, and all irregularities that remain after final compaction render the area defective. In SECTION 224—GEOSYNTHETIC STABILIZED BRIDGE APPROACH damage can arise if the aggregate is dumped directly from a haul truck onto the exposed fabric; instead the lift must be built by hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading to avoid tearing, and no vehicle may travel on it until at least six inches of loose backfill cover is in place, otherwise sudden braking or sharp turning can cause disturbance or distortion that must be corrected. In SECTION 466—ASPHALT PAVING INTERLAYER SYSTEM any damaged or poorly adhered interlayer must be removed and replaced because failure to achieve full contact can lead to delamination; wrinkles larger than 1 inch must be sliced and overlapped flat, and all fabric or grid overlaps must contain at least a 1‑inch asphalt coating or be trimmed to that length. The first lift of overlay must have a minimum compacted thickness not less than 1 ½ inches over the interlayer, and the asphalt material temperature may not exceed 320°F (and must stay above 290°F for performance‑grade binders) to avoid thermal damage to the interlayer; placement on wet pavement or allowing traffic before the tack coat cures is prohibited, as moisture or premature loading can cause loss of adhesion and subsequent cracking or pothole formation. Damage to the newly placed pavement before it is opened to traffic is another common distress, so procedures to eliminate such damage are required. [2]

Cold joints are prevented or repaired by cleaning loose material, re‑moistening the joint face and, when required, applying cement slurry before placing the next lift; any random cracking that appears before saw cutting must be repaired as directed. For foamed glass aggregate lifts, traffic on exposed compacted FGA should be minimized and ongoing monitoring must watch for rutting exceeding four inches in depth; if such rutting occurs, repair requires excavating and removing FGA to a depth of five inches below the bottom of the rut, placing new FGA material and compacting it with four full passes of a plate compactor. For embankment or fill lifts, continuous monitoring is required by proof‑rolling the newly placed material at no more than one point five feet per second and checking for permanent deformation; any rutting greater than one‑half inch is deemed unstable and must be treated by excavating to a depth of one foot, drying, and recompacting or replacing with suitable compacted material. Moisture and density are controlled through a QC plan that uses nuclear‑gauge testing to keep soil within minus three percent of optimum moisture and not less than ninety‑seven percent of maximum dry density (top three feet to one hundred percent), and Type 1 granular material within plus or minus two percent of optimum moisture and not less than ninety‑seven percent; if acceptance testing shows the required density is not achieved, the lift must be reshaped, recompacted, or removed and replaced, followed by retesting, with verification tests performed by an independent gauge agreeing within plus or minus two point zero pcf wet density and plus or minus zero point eight w moisture. Preventive maintenance includes maintaining equipment speed limits, keeping rollers clean, and ensuring gauges are calibrated annually. For asphalt grout‑stabilization lifts, the lift of the slab must be continuously monitored with vertical‑movement testing equipment capable of detecting slab lift to zero point zero zero one inch; upward movement greater than zero point zero five inch is not allowed, so injection pressure and rate are regulated to control rise within this limit, and no other preventive maintenance or repair actions are specified. For Geofoam block lifts, preventive measures require careful delivery, storage and handling—blocks must be kept off the ground, protected from heat sources, direct sunlight, solvents, wind and vehicle traffic, and de‑icing salts may not be used because they can cause degradation; monitoring is performed by providing an on‑site calibrated scale to weigh a random block from each truckload and recording date, time, dimensions, weight and calculated density. Repairs are required when damage exceeds the limits of Section 219.3(d): volumetric damage greater than zero point five percent of the volume, surface damage greater than five percent of the load‑bearing area or total surface, or continuous damage greater than twenty percent of a side length; damaged blocks are replaced at no expense to the Department and acceptable damaged bearing surfaces may be filled with dry fine sand, while geomembrane damage is repaired by welding patches extending at least six inches beyond the damaged area and geotextile damage is removed and replaced with a lap extending at least twelve inches in all directions secured with the specified adhesive. For the top lift of a Superpave asphalt base course, after placement the Inspector must take full‑depth cores at one random location per three thousand square yards; any pavement deficient in depth by one‑half inch or more that cannot be satisfactorily corrected is deemed defective and immediate correction must begin at that location and continue longitudinally and transversely until the deficiency is reduced to less than one‑half inch. Surface irregularities exceeding one‑quarter inch also constitute a defect. For asphalt concrete pavement covered by the sixty‑month warranty, within twenty‑four hours after final compaction the Inspector must drill a four‑inch (or larger) core through all lifts at intervals of one core per two thousand linear feet and measure each depth; if any single core shows a deficiency greater than one‑quarter inch that cannot be satisfactorily corrected, or if three consecutive cores show a uniform deficiency greater than one‑eighth inch that cannot be satisfactorily corrected, the area is deemed defective and must be repaired by adding material at the point of deficiency and extending longitudinally and transversely until the depth throughout the repair zone is within one‑quarter inch of the required depth, with additional cores taken to confirm the start and end of the defect. The Inspector may also direct collection of loose‑mixture box samples behind the paver and six‑inch core samples at a rate of one per one thousand tons for informational testing. For cement‑treated permeable base‑course lifts, surface tolerance is tested with a ten‑foot straightedge moving no more than five feet between test stations; any irregularity exceeding more than one‑half inch is corrected by loosening the surface mixture and removing or adding CTPBC rather than grinding. Curing begins immediately after compaction using a membrane curing compound applied at a rate of one gallon per one hundred square feet plus or minus ten percent (or white polyethylene sheeting) and the curing material is maintained for twenty‑four hours, with an additional twenty‑four‑hour extension if temperature falls below forty degrees Fahrenheit; if temperature drops to thirty‑two degrees Fahrenheit within seventy‑two hours the CTPBC is removed and replaced. Construction traffic is not allowed on the CTPBC for at least three days after placement, and any area that becomes damaged or contaminated is removed and replaced at no cost to the Department. For rubblizing of concrete pavements, backfill shall be placed in maximum six‑inch lifts and compacted by rolling until nonmovement is achieved; shoving or wave motion under the roller is prohibited, providing a direct check on lift quality. After seating with a fifty‑ton pneumatic‑tired roller, any damage to underground utilities, drainage facilities, bridge approach slabs, bridge decks, or to rubblized and seated pavement caused by construction traffic must be repaired at no cost to the Department. For asphalt treated permeable base course lifts, when the mixture temperature is below three hundred twenty degrees Fahrenheit and the air temperature is at least thirty‑five degrees Fahrenheit, an ATPBC lift shall be placed as a maximum four‑inch compacted lift and the roller crew should allow the surface to cool to one hundred degrees Fahrenheit before installing the next lift. After each lift the finished surface shall be inspected with a ten‑foot straightedge; any irregularity greater than one‑half inch must be corrected by loosening the surface mixture and adding or removing ATPBC. Depth monitoring requires one six‑inch diameter test hole for every three thousand square yards of completed base, and sections found deficient in depth by one‑half inch or more shall be removed and replaced.
