Lift

Part of: Pavement Structure is-subtopic-of
Lift is a topic under Pavement Structure.
liftliftslift-thicknesseslift-thickness
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-29

Lift is the placement of pavement or fill material in one or more controlled‑thickness layers—called lifts—to achieve the required structural depth, density and performance for a range of treatments such as roller‑compacted concrete, thin Superpave asphalt overlays, pervious asphalt, geofoam capping, and manual patching [1]. Each lift is laid on a prepared subgrade with the specified mix, compacted by the paver to at least 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts under six inches or 90 % for lifts six inches or greater, then finished with rollers until a final density exceeding 96 % is reached; when multiple lifts are required the subsequent lift must be placed within sixty minutes to avoid a cold joint [2]. The defining characteristics of a lift include prescribed minimum and maximum compacted thicknesses (e.g., a minimum of four inches for RCC and a maximum of four inches for pervious asphalt), strict temperature limits on both mix and ambient conditions, and mandatory quality‑control inspections such as field‑density testing and core sampling that verify thickness tolerances before acceptance [2].

Purpose and applicability

In roller‑compacted concrete pavement, Lift addresses the need to obtain the required structural depth when a very dry mix is placed by paver and compacted with rollers; by placing the material in single or multiple lifts the designer can achieve the desired thickness without forms, steel reinforcement, or dowel bars, thus maintaining RCC’s intended performance characteristics [1]. In thin‑asphalt overlay work, Lift is employed to meet the need for a durable, low‑profile preservation treatment that extends service life and improves ride quality better than emulsified asphalt alternatives; by placing the 6.3 mm Superpave Thin Asphalt Overlay in thin lifts the pavement receives a longer‑lasting surface while complying with the minimum and maximum placement depths and structural coefficient requirements set out in Tables 9.3, 9.5, 10.4 and 10.5 [1]. Lift solves the problem of achieving required density for pavement sections that exceed the thickness practical for a single placement by allowing the material to be placed in multiple lifts of equal thickness so each lift can meet compaction criteria before rolling, and by requiring the second lift to be placed within 60 minutes to prevent the formation of a cold joint [2]. In pervious asphalt pavement construction Lift provides a way to place the mixture in thin, controlled layers so that each layer can be compacted without crushing the aggregate or eliminating drainage pathways; limiting each lift to a maximum 4‑inch compacted thickness and restricting compaction to not more than four passes prevents a reduction in surface porosity and ensures the pavement remains free draining while still meeting the required design thickness [2]. Lift solves the problem of protecting encapsulated blocks, geomembrane and geotextile during subsequent fill operations by requiring a minimum 6‑inch compacted lift of capping material placed over the geotextile after the blocks have been fully encapsulated, thereby providing a protective base that prevents damage [2]. Lift addresses the difficulty of uniformly compacting deep potholes by specifying placement in lifts not to exceed three inches in uncompacted depth; each lift is leveled and then compacted with an approved mechanical tamper before adding the next lift so that adequate density is achieved and the final surface conforms to the grade of the surrounding pavement [2]. Lift resolves inadequate contact between geotextile, trench wall and the installed drain by placing the first lift of backfill material simultaneously with the drain installation to achieve continuous contact, and by limiting subsequent lifts to a maximum 9‑inch thickness, compacting them hydraulically and spraying each lift with water at 1.0 gallon per linear foot to meet moisture and compaction criteria [2]. Lift is used to control the loose depth of each layer so that the compacted depth matches the design value within the specified tolerance; by drilling full‑depth cores on the top lift under inspector supervision and comparing core depth to design depth, any pavement deficient by ½ inch or more can be identified as defective and corrected, thereby solving excessive depth variation in a newly placed layer [2]. Under the 60‑month warranty for asphalt concrete pavement Lift solves the problem of undetected thickness deficiencies by inspecting each placed course with a 4‑inch (or larger) core drilled through all lifts within 24 hours of final compaction while an inspector is present, and by defining acceptance criteria that any single core showing a depth shortfall of more than ¼ inch or a uniform shortfall of more than ⅛ inch across three consecutive cores must be corrected or the area is deemed defective work [2]. Lift resolves the difficulty of obtaining a uniformly compacted backfill in rubblized concrete pavements by requiring placement in maximum 6‑inch lifts and rolling each increment until it does not move, thereby preventing shoving or wave motion under the roller and delivering a stable base for subsequent seating [2]. Lift solves the problem of protecting the exposed geosynthetic in bridge approach construction by placing the required lift thickness of aggregate gently—avoiding direct dumping from a haul truck and using hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading—so that the separation membrane is not damaged, distorted or wrinkled while still permitting proper backfill compaction [2]. Lift addresses insufficient subbase elevation in plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements by adding additional subbase in ½‑inch compacted lifts, thereby raising the grade to the design level so that the concrete slab can be placed on a uniform, properly supported surface [2].

When a preservation treatment specifies a Superpave 6.3 mm thin‑asphalt overlay on a sound pavement surface, the material may be placed in thin lifts as permitted by the design tables; this is appropriate when the overlay is intended only as a scratch or leveling course and the existing pavement satisfies the soundness criteria. If the purpose is to correct an erratic longitudinal profile or to re‑establish the proper cross‑section, a separate leveling course of at least 1 inch thickness should be used rather than combining a binder layer with a lift, and where the base is stable and removal will not affect performance heater planing or milling may be considered as alternative methods. For roller‑compacted concrete pavements whose required thickness lies between four inches and nine inches, a single‑lift placement is appropriate; lifts less than six inches shall be placed with either a conventional or high‑density paver equipped with compaction devices capable of producing at least 85 % of the maximum wet density before rollers are used, while lifts from six up to nine inches require a high‑density paver that can achieve at least 90 % compaction prior to rolling. When the design calls for a thickness greater than nine inches, the pavement must be placed in multiple equal‑thickness lifts, each lift using a conventional or high‑density paver with compaction devices capable of producing at least 85 % of the maximum wet density before rollers and the next lift must be placed within sixty minutes to avoid a cold joint; if these thickness limits or the required pre‑roller compaction cannot be satisfied with the specified equipment, an alternate paving method must be submitted for approval. In pervious‑asphalt pavement construction the mix is placed in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts and each lift must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed, while the coarse‑aggregate base of a detention basin is installed in eight‑inch maximum lifts and compacted as required. Activities such as subgrade preparation, test‑section construction, or surface conditioning are not called for lift placement and therefore are performed by the methods described elsewhere. For pervious‑concrete pavement systems a lift is appropriate only when installing the coarse aggregate base of a detention basin, which shall be installed in eight‑inch maximum lifts and compacted each lift as required; placement of the pervious concrete itself does not use lifts but instead requires equipment such as a form‑riding paving machine, roller screed, vibrating screed or an asphalt paver to strike off and finish the surface, so another method should be used. When blocks have been fully encapsulated with geomembrane and geotextile, a six‑inch minimum compacted lift of capping material must be placed on top of the geotextile before embankment placement; the capping material is placed by end dumping just off the blocks and then blading an eight‑inch thick loose lift across the blocks with pneumatic‑tire equipment, followed by static smooth‑drum compaction, and direct end dumping onto the blocks is prohibited, requiring a different placement method. For potholes or abrupt depressions that exceed three inches in depth the repair shall be performed as Type B manual asphalt patching and the binder course is placed in lifts that shall not exceed three inches in uncompacted depth; each lift is leveled, compacted with an approved mechanical tamper, and finally topped with a wearing‑course layer of about one‑and‑a‑half inches that is compacted with a steel‑wheeled roller so the surface conforms to the surrounding grade. Depressions three inches or less (Type A) are performed with only the asphalt wearing course in a single placement and compacted directly, without using lifts. In rigid concrete pavement over an aggregate subbase a lift is appropriate only for low‑grade sections; the specification calls for adding additional subbase in one‑half‑inch compacted lifts to bring the surface up to the design elevation, and if the existing subbase is too high the procedure requires cutting the subbase to the required grade and recompacting rather than using lifts, with the same trimming approach applied when the pavement rests on cement‑treated or asphalt‑treated base courses. Required inputs

In the roller‑compacted concrete (RCC) context described in section 4.11 of the Pavement Policy Manual, each lift must be made from cement, aggregate and water, with a water‑to‑cement ratio as low as 0.30 but typically 0.40; a dense aggregate gradation with a maximum size less than 1 inch may be used, air entrainment is optional, admixture use is limited, and the resulting mixture is very dry with no slump, behaving like damp gravel, and it is placed with a paver on a prepared subgrade and subbase designed to support compaction [1]. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania specifications require that an RCC lift conform to the approved mix design, be kept between 50 °F and 90 °F, and be placed on a clean, unfrozen, uniformly moist subgrade or subbase; placement is prohibited when ambient temperature falls to 40 °F, exceeds 85 °F without controlling mixture temperature, evaporation rates exceed 0.20 lb/ft²·h unless mitigated, or during hard rain that may damage the surface [2]. Each single RCC lift must be at least 4 inches thick and not more than 9 inches thick, with lifts greater than 9 inches placed in multiple equal‑thickness lifts, and the paver must achieve a minimum compaction of 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts under 6 inches or 90 % for lifts 6 inches or more before rollers are used [2]. For an ultra‑thin bonded wearing course, the lift consists of a polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane applied directly in front of the screed and an overlying hot‑mix asphalt concrete placed in a single pass; the hot‑mix may be Type A, B, or C with nominal maximum aggregate sizes of 6.3 mm, 9.5 mm, and 12.5 mm respectively, and the lift thickness is set to one to 1.5 times the diameter of the coarsest aggregate, resulting in a surface treatment about ½ inch thick for Type A and about ¾ inch thick for Types B and C [1]. In the overlay context of section 5.12, a 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlay lift must be constructed with the Superpave 6.3 mm wearing‑course mix and PG 64E‑22 binder, may only be placed on sound pavement surfaces, and requires good surface drainage or an upgrade to good as well as good subsurface drainage or an upgrade to good; no structural upgrade is required [1]. The Superpave specifications further define the lift as a plant‑mixed dense‑graded asphalt concrete with nominal maximum aggregate size of 6.3 mm placed on a prepared surface before compaction, and acceptance depends on certification of the mix and density acceptance by a non‑movement optimum‑rolling pattern meeting the specified asphalt content and percent passing the 75 µm sieve; the pavement is considered defective if the payment factor for both asphalt content and percent passing that sieve are 85 % [2]. The pervious asphalt pavement system requires that the mixture be placed in compacted lifts no thicker than four inches, each lift being allowed to cool to about 100 °F before the next layer is laid; placement may only occur when air or surface temperatures are at least 50 °F, and the pervious mix must attain an infiltration rate greater than 200 inches per hour, with a compacted thickness not more than ¼‑inch less than the design thickness, while compaction is limited to a maximum of four passes with a 10‑ton steel‑wheeled static roller so that porosity is retained [2]. For Geofoam lightweight fill, a lift begins with a six‑inch loose layer of bedding material meeting the requirements of Section 219.2(e); the surface of this leveling course must be free of debris and water before block placement; after the geomembrane and geotextile are in place, a minimum six‑inch compacted lift of capping material satisfying Section 219.2(f) is placed within two weeks using end dumping off the blocks followed by rapid blading of an eight‑inch loose layer with pneumatic‑tire equipment and then compacted with a smooth drum roller operating in static (non‑vibratory) mode, and throughout lift construction no wheeled or tracked equipment may travel on the block surfaces and standing water, snow, ice, or debris must be avoided before subsequent lifts are placed [2]. For a Type B manual asphalt patch repair, the pothole shall be excavated to sound, stable material, cleaned of all loose and foreign material and thoroughly dried before any lift is placed; after a cured tack coat, an asphalt binder course may be laid in lifts whose uncompacted depth does not exceed three inches, each lift being leveled and compacted with an approved mechanical tamper, and the final wearing‑course layer is limited to the topmost one‑and‑half inches and is compacted with a steel‑wheeled roller so that the patch surface matches the surrounding grade [2]. In pipe underdrain and pavement base drain construction, a Class 1 geotextile is placed first, followed immediately by an initial lift of fine‑aggregate Type A to achieve full contact with the geotextile and trench wall; each subsequent backfill lift may be no more than nine inches thick, must be hydraulically compacted, and should be sprayed with water at a rate of one gallon per linear foot, while the trench depth shall extend at least two inches into the subgrade and remain protected from precipitation or overnight exposure [2]. For flexible base replacement, the existing subgrade or subbase must be compacted before placing the lift, any low‑grade areas corrected by adding and recompacting subbase to the original grade, vertical faces of the prepared area cleaned and uniformly coated with PG 64S‑22, and an asphalt base course placed in a manner that does not cause segregation and to the lift thickness shown on the drawings, the newly placed material being compacted to the density required by the approved compaction equipment [2]. The cement‑treated permeable base course requires a moist aggregate‑cement mix that is water‑sprinkled until it readily absorbs moisture, with water added no more than one hour before compaction unless a retarding admixture extends this to 1½ hours; placement must be performed with steel‑wheel power rollers weighing eight to ten tons, avoiding crushing of aggregate or loss of drainage, and using an approved asphalt paver or mechanical spreader equipped with screed, plate vibrator and automated sensors, after which immediate curing is achieved by applying a white membrane forming compound at a rate of 1 gallon per 100 ft² (±10 %) or covering with white polyethylene for twenty‑four hours, the cure period being extended an additional twenty‑four hours if temperature falls below 40 °F, and construction traffic is prohibited for at least three days; if the CTPBC temperature drops to 32 °F within seventy‑two hours it must be removed and replaced [2]. In rubblized concrete pavement, a backfill lift shall be made from subbase No. 2A, placed in lifts that do not exceed six inches, compacted by rolling until nonmovement is achieved, with shoving or wave motion under the roller prohibited; the seated surface is considered satisfactory only when vertical deflection under a fifty‑ton pneumatic‑tired roller is less than three‑quarters of an inch, and the lift must be protected from freezing conditions before the overlay is placed [2]. For geosynthetic stabilized bridge approaches, clean aggregate is placed to the specified thickness by non‑destructive methods such as hand placement, lightly raining from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading, and direct dumping from haul trucks onto the exposed geosynthetic is prohibited; the underlying foundation must be prepared free of deleterious or unsuitable soils and proof rolled to provide a uniform, firm surface [2]. The asphalt‑treated permeable base course shall not be placed on surfaces that are unstable, frozen, or below thirty‑five °F; the mixture must be produced at a temperature below three hundred twenty °F with sufficient viscosity to coat aggregate particles without segregation during transport, and it must be placed within eight hours of production, in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts allowed to cool to one hundred °F before subsequent layers, with compaction performed by steel‑wheel power rollers weighing eight to ten tons applying four roller passes without crushing aggregate or eliminating drainage [2]. Finally, for asphalt paving interlayer systems the existing pavement surface must be dry, clean, and drainable, free of dirt, gravel, vegetation, oil and any loose debris, with cracks sealed and potholes repaired before placement; the asphalt material used to saturate the reinforcement must be maintained at a minimum temperature of two hundred ninety °F and must not exceed three hundred twenty °F, applied at rates of 0.22‑0.28 gal/yd² for geotextile fabric, 0.02‑0.05 gal/yd² for fiberglass mesh grid, or 0.19‑0.28 gal/yd² for composite reinforcing grid, and the first lift of overlay over any interlayer system may not be less than one‑and‑half inches compacted thickness [2].

In Roller Compacted Concrete (RCC) work the design thickness of the pavement may range from 4 inches to 18 inches and is achieved by placing RCC in one or more lifts whose placement width and depth are governed by the paver dimensions [1]. When an individual RCC lift is less than 8 inches thick, control joints shall be spaced between 15 feet and 20 feet; for lifts that are 8 inches or greater, joint spacing is calculated as the pavement thickness multiplied by 2.5 feet [1]. The RCC mix shall use a water‑to‑cement ratio as low as 0.30 but typically 0.40, producing a mixture similar to damp gravel with no slump, and the subgrade and subbase must be prepared to support compaction of the lifts during construction [1].

Section 502 of the Pennsylvania specifications requires that each RCC lift have a minimum thickness of 4 inches; single lifts may be placed from 4 inches up to 9 inches, while any pavement thicker than 9 inches must be placed in multiple equal‑thickness lifts and the second lift must be placed within 60 minutes of the first [2]. For each lift the compaction device on the paver must achieve at least 85 % of the maximum wet density before rollers are used when the lift is less than 6 inches, and at least 90 % when the lift is from 6 to 9 inches; after final rolling every lift must be compacted to greater than 96 % [2]. An 8‑ton or heavier steel‑drum roller shall be used when paver compaction is below 95 %, and a test section at least 50 feet long, built to plan thickness and width with the proposed equipment, must be constructed prior to full production to verify mix consistency, equipment performance, roller combination, curing procedures and compressive strength; a field‑operation QC plan together with a concrete technician and testing technician must be approved [2].

For ultra‑thin bonded wearing courses (UTWC) the surface treatment is placed in a single lift using a self‑priming paver that lays a polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane ahead of the screed and immediately follows with hot‑mix asphalt concrete; the lift shall achieve a final thickness equal to 1 to 1.5 times the diameter of the coarsest aggregate, resulting in a surface thickness of ½ inch for Type A (6.3 mm nominal maximum aggregate) and ¾ inch for Types B (9.5 mm) and C (12.5 mm) [1].

Section 420 governs asphalt‑treated permeable base courses, requiring that test sections on the prepared subgrade be at least 225 square feet in area and that the average infiltration measured in accordance with ASTM C1701 exceed 200 inches per hour; the compacted thickness may not be more than ¼‑inch less than the design thickness and the mixture must be placed in maximum 4‑inch compacted lifts [2]. Profile is controlled by automatic sensors to the design grade line, the surface must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent lift is placed, and compaction shall be performed with smooth steel‑wheeled rollers operated in static mode, limited to no more than four passes per spot; the roller must be capable of resisting a 10‑ton load and pneumatic‑tire rollers are not permitted [2].

In geosynthetic stabilized bridge approaches (Section 224) proof‑rolling of the subgrade shall confirm a uniform, firm surface before any lift is placed, the Class 4 separation geosynthetic shall be positioned within ±½ inch of the plan elevations, and each aggregate lift shall be installed at the required thickness using non‑destructive methods such as hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading rather than direct truck dumping; no vehicles may travel on the newly placed geosynthetic until a minimum of six inches of loose backfill covers it, and any disturbance shall be corrected at no additional cost [2].

Section 219 requires a six‑inch minimum compacted layer of capping material to be placed on the geotextile within two weeks after placement of the geotextile; the finished surface of the block fill that supports the lift must be built to a tolerance of ½ inch over a 10‑foot interval along the longitudinal profile and transverse slope, vertical joint gaps may not exceed 0.1 foot, side‑slope surfaces must be within 0.2 foot of design grade, and all lifts are compacted using a smooth drum roller in static (non‑vibratory) mode [2].

For manual asphalt patching (Section 450) the binder course is placed in lifts that shall not exceed three inches in uncompacted depth; each lift is leveled then compacted with an approved mechanical tamper, and the topmost wearing‑course material is limited to the upper one‑and‑half inches of the patch and is finished with a steel‑wheeled roller or other approved compaction equipment [2]. Prior to placing any lifts the outlined area must be cut vertically, excavated to sound material, cleaned, dried and tack‑coated on the bottom and vertical surfaces as required by Section 460.3(b), and after each lift is compacted the surface of the patch shall conform to the grade of the surrounding pavement [2].

In pipe underdrain and base drain work (Section 610) backfill is placed in fine‑aggregate Type A lifts no deeper than 9 inches maximum; each lift must be hydraulically compacted then sprayed with water at a rate of 1.0 gallon per linear foot, the first lift is placed concurrently with the drain to guarantee full contact with the geotextile and trench wall, any tears in the geotextile are repaired before subsequent lifts are added, and backfilling may not begin until the pipe has been inspected and approved by the Inspector‑in‑Charge [2].

The thin 6.3 mm asphalt wearing course (Section 412) is measured by square yard or ton; acceptance is governed by certification of the mixture together with density verification using an optimum‑rolling pattern, and preconstruction testing includes asphalt‑content analysis and sieve‑size grading performed according to PTM No. 757 or PTM No. 702 [2]. Any lot that fails requires a written retest request within three weeks, core sampling offset 24 inches from the original locations, two 6‑inch diameter cores rinsed and backfilled within 24 hours, and repeat testing under the same methods to determine final payment factors; the pavement will be considered defective when the payment factor for both asphalt content and the percent passing the 75 µm (No. 200) sieve reaches 85 % [2].

Section 313 specifies that each lift be placed to the compacted depth shown on the plans and kept within the tolerance specified for that layer; for the top lift, while the Inspector is present, a full‑depth core is drilled at one random location chosen by the Inspector for every 3,000 square yards of completed work (or wherever the Inspector suspects a deficiency) and the core depth is measured according to PTM No. 737; any pavement that measures ½ inch or more below the design depth and cannot be satisfactorily corrected is deemed defective, with corrective compaction continuing until the depth is less than ½ inch short of the design depth [2].

In flexible base replacement (Section 316) the lift is placed after the perimeter is marked, the existing material is saw‑cut or milled and removed to the indicated depth, and the subgrade or subbase is compacted (or re‑compacted if disturbed) before any new base course is laid; any shortfall in grade is corrected by adding and recompacting subbase to the original grade, vertical faces are cleaned and coated with PG 64S‑22, the asphalt base course is placed without segregation to the lift thickness indicated and compacted to the density specified using approved compaction equipment per Section 108.05(c) [2].

Section 496 mandates that each lift shall be checked by coring within twenty‑four hours after final compaction while the Inspector is present; a core of at least four inches in diameter shall be drilled through all lifts and courses for every two thousand feet of lane and its depth measured according to PTM No. 737, and a single core that shows a deficiency of more than ¼ inch, or a uniform deficiency of more than ⅛ inch in three consecutive cores when not correctable, renders the area defective, with corrective placement continuing until the depth is within ¼ inch of the required value [2]. When directed the Contractor shall also provide one loose‑mix box sample sized no larger than two hundred forty cubic inches from behind the paver before compaction and one six‑inch diameter pavement core for each one thousand tons placed, both identified and delivered to the Inspector within the specified time frames [2].

Cement‑treated permeable base course lifts (Section 303) shall be placed with an approved asphalt paver or mechanical spreader equipped with a screed, plate vibrator and fully automated sensors to control profile and transverse grade; compaction shall be performed with steel‑wheel power rollers whose certified metal weight is between eight tons and ten tons, making at least one pass but never crushing the aggregate or eliminating drainage, and compaction must be completed within one hour of adding water to the aggregate and cement unless a retarding admixture extends that window to one and a half hours [2]. Surface tolerances shall be checked with a ten‑foot straightedge moved no more than five feet per stage, and any irregularity greater than ½ inch shall be corrected by loosening or adding material without grinding or milling; curing shall begin immediately after compaction using a white membrane forming compound applied at a rate of one gallon per one hundred square feet plus or minus ten percent, or alternatively the surface may be covered with white polyethylene sheeting, the cure period shall be extended twenty‑four hours if temperature falls below forty degrees Fahrenheit, and if temperature drops to thirty‑two degrees Fahrenheit within seventy‑two hours the lift must be removed and replaced at no cost to the Department; construction traffic shall be prohibited on the lift for at least three days after placement, with only equipment needed for the next layer permitted to enter thereafter [2].

Back‑fill lifts associated with rubblizing of concrete pavements (Section 526) are limited to a maximum thickness of six inches and each lift shall be compacted by rolling until no movement occurs; prior to any lifting the Contractor shall submit a pavement rubblizing plan together with a list of proposed equipment for Department approval, which submission serves as the pre‑construction test of compliance [2]. Additionally two full‑depth test sections measuring three feet by twelve feet shall be excavated early in the rubblizing sequence to verify that the broken concrete meets the required dimensions, and any change in equipment or breaking force shall trigger a repeat of this verification [2].

In asphalt‑treated permeable base course work (Section 360) lifts shall be placed in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts and shall not be overlaid until the surface has cooled to one hundred degrees Fahrenheit; compaction shall be performed with steel‑wheel power rollers having a certified metal weight of eight tons to ten tons, applying four roller passes per spot, and any surface irregularity greater than ½ inch shall be corrected before proceeding [2]. Depth control shall require a six‑inch diameter test hole for each three thousand square yards of completed base, and the finished surface shall be checked with a ten‑foot straightedge at intervals not exceeding five feet to verify compliance [2].

Pervious concrete pavement systems (Section 520) require that coarse aggregate be installed in lifts that do not exceed eight inches, and each lift shall be compacted as specified in Section 360.3 [2].

For asphalt overlay placed over an interlayer system (Section 466) the first lift shall have a minimum compacted thickness of not less than 1.5 inches, with the overall overlay thickness meeting the contract drawings; prior to placement a pre‑placement meeting with the manufacturer’s technical representative shall be held to review material type, application width, rate, mixture acceptance, surface cleaning and sequencing, which meeting serves as the preconstruction test for lift installation [2]. The asphalt tack coat applied before the lift shall be sprayed at a temperature of at least two hundred ninety degrees Fahrenheit but shall not exceed three hundred twenty degrees Fahrenheit, the application rate shall be controlled to saturate the specific interlayer (for example between 0.22 and 0.28 gallons per square yard) with a target width equal to the fabric width plus six inches on each side, overlaps of the interlayer shall be at least one inch and any wrinkles larger than one inch shall be trimmed and overlapped, ensuring dimensional tolerances are met before the lift is placed, and all surface preparation, including cleaning, crack sealing and repair of potholes as specified in Section 450, shall be completed and verified before the lift can be installed [2].

In plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements (Section 501) any low spot in an aggregate subbase shall be corrected by adding additional subbase in one‑half inch compacted lifts, shims used to keep forms vertical may not exceed a height of one inch, and a comprehensive paving operation QC Plan shall be submitted at least two weeks before the start of paving operations; the plan includes procedures for non‑destructive depth testing, which requires that targets be placed and verified daily according to PTM No. 605 in the presence of the Representative [2].

Equipment and personnel

For roller‑compacted concrete lifts, the subgrade/subbase is cleaned and uniformly moistened, typically using at least one water truck before placement. The mix is produced in a batch‑type or continuous mixing plant that meets the approved design and hauled in covered trucks to a conventional or high‑density paver equipped with compaction devices that must achieve a minimum compaction of 85 % of the maximum wet density for lifts less than six inches and a minimum compaction of 90 % for lifts six inches or greater before rollers are employed. If a minimum 95 % field density is not achieved with the paver, a self‑propelled smooth dual steel drum roller having a minimum weight of eight tons is used for primary compaction, followed by static steel‑drum or rubber‑tired rollers (or walk‑behind vibratory rollers where access is limited) for final consolidation and surface finishing. Curing is maintained with at least one water truck equipped with fog nozzles (or alternative methods such as polyethylene sheeting or curing compound), while a concrete technician and a concrete field testing technician conduct in‑place density tests per ASTM C1040 and later compressive‑strength checks as required by the QC plan. For pervious asphalt lifts, subgrade preparation is performed with light equipment such as a spring‑tooth rake and a light tractor that scarify the soil to at least six inches depth while avoiding heavy haul trucks. Placement and finishing of each lift are accomplished with a track paver or mechanical spreader equipped with adjustable screed assemblies, laying the mixture in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts. Compaction is performed with smooth steel‑wheeled rollers operated in static mode (or a ten‑ton steel‑wheeled static roller or vibratory roller) limited to no more than four passes to preserve porosity. Testing of test sections includes infiltration testing according to ASTM C1701. For geofoam lightweight fill lifts, Geofoam blocks are positioned manually after a six‑inch loose bedding layer is graded and cleared, with any lifting or transport performed by equipment that does not damage the blocks and without wheeled, tracked, or other machines on the block surface. Capping material is end‑dumped just off the blocks and bladed into an eight‑inch thick loose lift using pneumatic‑tire equipment, then each lift is compacted with a smooth drum roller operating in static (non‑vibratory) mode to achieve the required minimum six‑inch compacted thickness. Embankment fill is delivered by single triaxle truck loads that may be dumped onto previously placed and compacted capping or embankment layers, followed by rapid spreading to protect the underlying geomembrane and geotextile. For manual asphalt patching lifts, preparation may employ a milling or planing machine, all edges are cut vertically before debris is removed and the surface dried, and placement of the asphalt wearing or binder course is performed manually. Compaction is achieved with a steel‑wheeled roller or other approved compaction equipment, and for each lift in Type B a mechanical tamper is used. For cement‑treated permeable base course lifts, the rubblized pavement or subbase is conditioned by thoroughly sprinkling water to achieve a moist condition before placement. Placement then requires an approved asphalt paver or mechanical spreader equipped with a screed, plate vibrator and fully automated sensors to control profile and transverse grade. Compaction is achieved with steel‑wheel power rollers that have a certified metal weight from eight tons to ten tons, and handwork may be performed as directed, but the vibratory paver screed alone may not be used for compaction. Surface tolerances are checked by testing the finished surface with a 10‑foot straightedge at suspect locations and transverse joints. Curing begins immediately after compaction and is done either by applying white membrane forming curing compound at a rate of 1 gallon per 100 square feet ± 10 % or by covering the course with white polyethylene sheeting for 24 hours, extending the period if temperature falls below 40 °F. For asphalt‑treated permeable base course lifts, preparation begins at the plant where the aggregate is dried as needed and the asphalt binder is heated so that the mixture can be produced below 320 °F, then delivered to the site within eight hours for placement. Placement and finishing are accomplished with a slip‑form paver or mechanical spreader equipped with screed assemblies and automated sensors that lay the material in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts and allow it to cool to 100 °F before the next layer. Compaction is achieved by seating the lift with steel‑wheel power rollers of eight tons to ten tons (or a vibratory roller operated in static mode) applying four roller passes, while any surface irregularities greater than ½ inch are corrected after compaction without grinding. Surface tolerance is verified using a 10‑foot straightedge moved no more than five feet at a time, and depth verification requires drilling a six‑inch diameter test hole for each 3,000 square yards of completed base. For pervious concrete lifts, the subgrade is scarified with a light tractor and spring‑tooth rake, forms are set and braced with release agent, and the mix is kept in truck mixers rotating at the manufacturer’s speed before being deposited by a form‑riding paving machine, roller screed or vibrating screed. Each lift is then compacted with smooth steel‑wheeled rollers operated in static mode for one to four passes while avoiding pneumatic tire rollers, finished with steel finishing equipment or a motorized pan finisher that leaves the surface voids open, cured by covering the surface with polyethylene sheeting within 20 minutes of discharge and maintaining the cover for a minimum seven days, and finally tested by performing unit weight testing on every truck per ASTM C1688, extracting three 4‑inch cores per 5,000 ft² for oven‑dry density and void content per ASTM C1754, and conducting infiltration tests on three areas per 10,000 ft² to verify an average rate exceeding 200 inches per hour. For plain or reinforced cement concrete lifts, preparation requires wetting the base course with enough water to achieve a moist surface without creating puddles, and when the evaporation rate exceeds 0.20 pounds per square foot per hour windbreaks or fog‑mist atomizing equipment must be on hand; steel forms (or approved wood for tight curves) equipped with pins, stake pockets and proper bracing are set to line and grade before placement, while a continuous supply of concrete from an approved mixing plant is delivered by hauling units and premolded expansion joint filler is installed as required. Consolidation is achieved with vibrators that transmit at least 100 impulses per second, operated around but not on joints and limited to no more than five seconds in one spot to avoid segregation. Finishing includes strike‑off and straightedge testing as outlined in the QC plan, and curing is protected by the prescribed curing application and temperature monitoring methods. Non‑destructive depth testing uses calibrated measuring devices and daily‑verified targets placed according to PTM No. 605. [2]

Work sequence

Before any roller‑compacted concrete lift is placed, the project must have a prepared subgrade and subbase course that were identified during the design phase and shown as separate construction items; these courses must be capable of supporting the compaction of the RCC pavement during construction. Before the single lift of hot‑mix asphalt concrete for an ultra‑thin bonded wearing course can be placed, the polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane must first be laid by a self‑priming paver directly ahead of the paving screed, and only after that membrane is in place does the ultra‑thin bonded wearing course get overlaid in one lift. Before any lift of an overlay is placed, the pre‑overlay surface preparation must be completed, which requires installing either a scratch course to fill wheel ruts and small depressions or a leveling course that is at least 1 inch thick to provide a uniform working platform and correct the pavement profile. In the Roller Compacted Concrete specifications, the contractor must prepare a field‑operation quality‑control plan that the Representative reviews and approves, provide the required concrete technician and concrete field testing technician, construct a minimum 50‑foot long test section to verify mix consistency, equipment performance, roller combination, density testing procedure, curing method and compressive strength, have all required equipment on hand and approved by the Representative, and ensure the subgrade/subbase is cleaned of foreign material, ponded water or frost and uniformly moistened before RCC placement. For a pervious asphalt pavement system, the subgrade must be excavated to undisturbed soil, left as permeable as possible, scarified where eroded, repaired to the Representative’s satisfaction and inspected and accepted; a detention basin then must be built with at least 2 inches of fine‑aggregate underlay, a geotextile wrap, and coarse aggregate placed in lifts not exceeding 8 inch and compacted per Section 360.3, and any required asphalt‑treated permeable base course from Section 360.3 must be installed, while the air or surface temperature must be at least 50 °F, existing surfaces must be conditioned—including removal of contaminated binder and application of a tack coat to vertical elements (but not between pervious lifts)—and smooth steel‑wheeled static rollers must be ready for use. For Geofoam lightweight fill, the blocks must already be fully encapsulated; the hydrocarbon‑resistant geomembrane must be welded around all blocks in a continuous layer and then a Class 4, Type A geotextile must be laid over that geomembrane with at least twelve‑inch overlaps and adhesive secured before the six‑inch minimum compacted capping‑material lift may be placed. For manual asphalt patching, the outlined portion must be cut out and excavated to sound, stable material with vertical edges, all debris removed, the surface and surrounding pavement thoroughly cleaned and dried, a tack coat applied to the bottom and vertical faces, and the tack coat allowed to cure before the lift is placed. For pipe underdrain installation, fine grading must be completed prior to installing the pipe; for pavement base drain, the adjacent roadway pavement must be constructed first, the trench excavated to the dimensions shown on the Standard Drawings without disturbing the subbase, the prefabricated drainage core set in the trench, and only after the trench is ready and the drain positioned may the first lift of back‑fill material be placed. For plant‑mixed 6.3 mm thin asphalt overlay courses, the underlying pavement must be prepared before the dense‑graded asphalt concrete with a nominal maximum aggregate size of 6.3 mm is laid on that prepared surface. For flexible base replacement, the contractor must first mark the perimeter of the replacement area, saw cut or mill that perimeter, remove all material within the cut to the indicated depth, compact the existing subgrade or subbase (re‑compacting if disturbed), and, when directed, remove unsuitable material and backfill as specified; vertical faces of the patch area must be cleaned and uniformly coated with PG 64S‑22 before the lift is placed. For a geosynthetic stabilized bridge approach, the foundation subgrade must be excavated to the required lines and grade, all deleterious soils removed, and the surface proof‑rolled to achieve a uniform, firm base; once those criteria are met, a continuous Class 4, Type A separation geosynthetic is laid over the entire prepared subgrade and layback slope, positioned within ±½ inch of the design elevations, pulled tight, anchored, and spaced vertically as required. For an asphalt‑treated permeable base course, the surface must be stable, unfrozen and above 35 °F with no rain; the aggregate must be dried as necessary, the asphalt binder heated so it coats the aggregate, the mixture produced at a temperature below 320 °F and placed within eight hours of production, without stockpiling, using a slip‑form paver or mechanical spreader. For a pervious concrete pavement system, the subgrade must be excavated to undisturbed soil, cleaned of fines, scarified to a minimum depth of six inches where erosion has occurred, repaired as needed, and the Representative notified for final inspection and acceptance; only after that acceptance may basin and choker course installation proceed. For an asphalt paving interlayer system, the project must supply the manufacturer’s installation instructions to the Representative, ensure a trained technical representative is present for all phases, hold a pre‑placement meeting to review material type, acceptance criteria, surface cleaning, curve handling, removal of non‑adhered product, slope identification and paving sequence; the interlayer material must be kept dry, wrapped and stored elevated and covered until use, placement may not proceed unless weather conditions meet the manufacturer’s recommendations, and the existing pavement surface must be dried and cleaned of dirt, gravel, vegetation and oil, loose debris removed, cracks sealed and allowed to cure, potholes repaired as specified, and any depressions eliminated so that a drainable, non‑wet surface is ready. For plain or reinforced cement concrete pavements, a comprehensive paving operation quality‑control plan must be submitted at least two weeks prior to the start of paving operations and accepted; paving may not begin until that acceptance occurs, the contractor must verify adequate equipment, trained personnel and sufficient hauling units are scheduled to maintain continuity in placement, and the base course must be prepared according to outlined procedures, including any required water sprinkling or shimming, so that subgrade and subbase control measures are in place prior to lift placement. In roller‑compacted concrete pavement (section 4.11) each lift is built by first placing the very dry RCC mix with a conventional or high‑density asphalt paver and then compacting it with rollers to obtain the required density; these two actions constitute the primary sequence for building up pavement thickness, which may be achieved in single or multiple lifts [1]. Prior to placement the contractor prepares a field‑operation quality‑control plan for representative review, assigns a concrete technician and a concrete field‑testing technician, and constructs a minimum 50‑foot test section to verify mix consistency, equipment performance and curing procedures; with all equipment approved the subgrade or subbase is cleaned, free of water and uniformly moistened before the RCC mixture is hauled in covered trucks, deposited into the paver hopper and placed at a temperature between 50 °F and 90 °F while the paver speed is regulated to avoid segregation, producing lifts that are at least 4 inches thick (single lifts up to 9 inches) or multiple lifts of equal thickness for greater depths [2]. The paver must achieve a minimum compaction of 85 % of maximum wet density for lifts under 6 inches and 90 % for lifts 6 inches or more, after which rollers are used to reach a final compaction greater than 96 % of maximum wet density with field density tests performed no later than 30 minutes after rolling; when multiple lifts are required the second lift is placed within 60 minutes of the first lift or else the interface shall be considered a cold joint [2]. For pervious asphalt pavement (section 420.3) the subgrade is prepared, any detention basin installed and any required asphalt‑treated permeable base course placed; the pervious asphalt mixture is spread with a track paver or mechanical spreader and laid in maximum 4‑inch compacted lifts while the screed is adjusted to meet the specified cross‑section and grade, each lift being allowed to cool to 100 °F before the next lift is placed, no tack coat being applied between lifts, and rolling performed with smooth steel‑wheeled static rollers limited to one to four passes per spot so that porosity is not reduced [2]. In a geosynthetic stabilized bridge approach (section 224.3) after excavation the foundation is proof rolled to a uniform and firm surface and inspected and approved before any fill or geosynthetic placement; a Class 4, Type A separation geosynthetic is then laid flat over the entire area, overlapping adjacent rolls a minimum of 12 inches and positioned within ±½‑inch of the plan elevations, after which the aggregate lift is placed in the required thickness using non‑destructive methods such as hand placement, lightly raining from an excavator bucket, or blading out gradually rather than dumping from a haul truck, with the surface graded without wrinkling the geosynthetic; no vehicles are allowed on the geosynthetic until a minimum of 6 inches of loose backfill has been placed [2]. For cement‑treated permeable base course (section 303.3) the rubblized pavement or subbase is thoroughly sprinkled with enough water to achieve a moist condition, after which the CTPBC is placed using an approved asphalt paver or mechanical spreader equipped with a screed, plate vibrator and automated sensors that set profile and grade; fresh CTPBC is compacted with steel‑wheel power rollers weighing from 8 tons to 10 tons making at least one pass and completing compaction within 1 hour of water addition (or up to 1½ hours if a retarding admixture is used) while avoiding over‑compaction that would impair drainage or crush aggregate, surface tolerance being checked in stages with a 10‑foot straightedge moved no more than 5 feet per stage and any irregularities greater than ½ inch corrected by loosening, adding or removing CTPBC without grinding or milling; curing begins immediately after compaction using a white membrane forming compound applied at a rate of 1 gallon per 100 sq ft ±10% (or covering with white polyethylene), the membrane being maintained for 24 hours and extended if temperature falls below 40 °F, replacement required if temperature drops to 32 °F within 72 hours, and construction traffic is prohibited for at least 3 days after placement [2]. For asphalt‑treated permeable base course (section 360.3) the contractor first dries the aggregate as needed, heats the asphalt and coats the aggregate to produce ATPBC at a temperature below 320 °F; the ATPBC must be placed within 8 hours from when it is made provided the surface is not unstable, frozen or below 35 °F, the mixture being spread with a slip‑form paver or mechanical spreader, struck off and placed in maximum 4‑inch compacted lifts while adjusting the screed to provide the indicated cross‑section and depth; each lift is allowed to cool to 100 °F before placing subsequent layers or pavement courses, after which rolling begins as soon as cooling is sufficient using steel‑wheel power rollers with a manufacturer’s certified metal weight of 8 tons to 10 tons (or a vibratory roller operated in static mode) and four roller passes are applied, additional passes being used only to eliminate surface irregularities while avoiding compaction that would make the material not free draining or crush the aggregate; longitudinal joints are overlapped 1‑2 inches, transverse joints are formed at the end of each day’s work or when more than a 30‑minute interruption occurs by installing a temporary vertical bulkhead or by sawing as directed, and the finished surface is tested with a 10‑foot straightedge with any irregularity greater than ½ inch corrected by loosening or adding ATPBC without grinding or milling; depth is verified by digging one 6‑inch diameter test hole to full depth for each 3,000 sq yd of completed base and sections deficient by ½ inch or more are removed and replaced [2].

For pipe underdrain and pavement base drain the first lift of backfill shall be placed simultaneously with the drain to ensure continuous contact with the geotextile and trench wall; each subsequent fine‑aggregate lift, which may not exceed nine inches in height, must be hydraulically compacted before the next lift is added, trenches deeper than two inches may not be left open overnight, and the subsurface drain outlet pipe and fittings shall be installed so that positive drainage is achieved within 24 hours of beginning trenching for that section of base drain [2]. For cement‑treated permeable base course compaction must be completed within no more than one hour after water is added to the aggregate and cement; a retarding admixture may be used to increase this period to 1½ hours, curing begins immediately after compaction with the curing material remaining in place for at least 24 hours (an additional 24‑hour extension applies if temperature falls below 40 °F), and construction traffic is prohibited on the newly placed CTPBC for a minimum of three days before any overlying pavement can be installed [2]. The rubblizing of concrete pavements specifies that the overlay must be placed within 60 calendar days after the rubblizing and seating operations; no other time limits are prescribed between critical steps [2]. For asphalt‑treated permeable base course the mixture must be placed within eight hours of production, stockpiling is not permitted, and after spreading the mixture must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer or pavement course is placed; rolling should be performed as soon as the mat has cooled sufficiently to avoid shoving, and when a paving interruption exceeds a 30‑minute interval the specification calls for installing a temporary vertical bulkhead at the end of that day (or the contractor may saw a construction joint) to create a full‑depth transverse joint [2]. In pervious concrete pavement systems the concrete should be placed within ninety minutes after the final addition of mix water, curing must be started within twenty minutes of concrete discharge with a minimum curing period of seven days; rollers may not bridge previously placed and cured concrete for seven days or until the Representative confirms sufficient strength, and vehicular traffic may not be allowed onto the pavement until the concrete has cured for at least seven days while ambient temperature has exceeded 55 °F during any time of the day [2]. For asphalt paving interlayer systems crack‑filling material must be fully cured before placement of the interlayer, and the interlayer should be laid while the tack coat or asphalt cement is still tacky—that is, before it cools and loses its tackiness; once the interlayer is installed all sections must receive the final asphalt overlay on the same day, and if rainfall wets the interlayer before the overlay the surface must be allowed to dry first [2].

Quality control and acceptance

In roller‑compacted concrete lifts (Section 502) the inspector verifies that the lift thickness is at least 4 inches; single lifts may be from 4 inches up to ≤9 inches and multiple‑lift construction must use equal‑thickness lifts. Compaction is checked by field density testing in accordance with ASTM C1040, requiring a minimum of 85 % of the maximum wet density for lifts under 6 inches and a minimum of 90 % for lifts 6 inches or greater when measured directly behind the paver, and after final rolling a compaction greater than 96 % of the maximum wet density is required for all thicknesses. The second lift must be placed within 60 minutes of completing the first to avoid a cold joint, and surface evenness is inspected with a straightedge; any variation exceeding ¼ inch is deemed defective. For pervious‑asphalt lifts (Section 420) each compacted thickness may not be more than ¼ inch below the design thickness and lifts are limited to a maximum compacted depth of 4 inches. The surface must cool to at least 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed, compaction is restricted to no more than four passes with a smooth steel‑wheel static roller (pneumatic rollers prohibited), and test sections built with the same material are required to be infiltrated per ASTM C1701 with an average rate exceeding 200 inches per hour. Placement shall not occur when air or surface temperature is below 50 °F. For capping layers (Section 219) a minimum compacted lift of six inches of the specified capping material must be placed on top of the geotextile within two weeks; the material is spread by end dumping just off the blocks and bladed into an eight‑inch thick loose lift with pneumatic‑tire equipment, then compacted using a smooth drum roller in static (non‑vibratory) mode. Any damage observed during placement must be repaired before proceeding, vertical joints shall not exceed 0.1 foot, and the top surface must be within a tolerance of ½ inch over any ten‑foot interval of the design profile. In Type B manual asphalt patching (Section 450) binder‑course lifts may not exceed three inches in uncompacted depth, and after each lift is compacted the surface of the patch must be inspected to confirm it conforms to the grade of the surrounding pavement. For thin 6.3 mm asphalt wearing courses (Section 412) the mixture’s asphalt content, its gradation expressed as percent passing the 75‑µm (No. 200) sieve, and the achieved density using the optimum‑rolling pattern are inspected; the pavement is considered defective when both the asphalt‑content payment factor and the 75‑µm sieve payment factor equal 85 %. Base‑course lifts (Section 313) require control of the loose depth of each layer and verification that the compacted depth meets design tolerance. On the top lift, in the presence of the inspector, a full‑depth core is drilled at one random location per 3,000 square yards as selected according to PTM No. 1 and measured according to PTM No. 737; any pavement deficient in depth by ½ inch or more that cannot be satisfactorily corrected is deemed defective. For flexible‑base replacement lifts (Section 316) the inspector must verify that the asphalt base course does not cause segregation, that its thickness conforms to the lift thickness shown on the plans, and that compaction achieves the specified density using approved equipment as required by Section 108.05(c). Under the 60‑month warranty (Section 496) each lift is inspected for installed thickness by drilling a core at least 4 inches in diameter through all lifts within 24 hours after final compaction with the inspector present; depth is measured according to PTM No. 737 and any single core showing a deficiency greater than ¼ inch that cannot be corrected, or a uniform deficiency greater than ⅛ inch over three consecutive cores, renders the pavement area defective. When directed, the contractor must also collect one loose‑mixture box sample from directly behind the paver before compaction and one 6‑inch diameter core sample for each 1,000 tons placed, label each sample with location, date, mixture type and class IF, package the loose samples in cardboard boxes no larger than 240 cubic inches, and deliver them to the inspector within the specified time frames. In cement‑treated permeable base courses (Section 303) surface tolerance is tested by running a ten‑foot straightedge along the finished surface at suspect locations, transverse joints and paving notches, moving the straightedge no more than five feet between stages; any irregularities greater than one‑half inch must be corrected, and compaction must include at least one pass with a steel‑wheel power roller to achieve the required density without crushing aggregate or impairing drainage. For rubblized concrete pavements (Section 526) each backfill layer is placed in lifts no thicker than six inches and compacted by rolling until there is no movement; the inspector confirms that shoving or wave motion does not occur under the roller, a fifty‑ton pneumatic‑tired roller is used for seating, and final seating is acceptable only when vertical deflection measured under the roller is less than three‑quarters of an inch, any area failing this limit must be undercut and re‑compacted. In asphalt‑treated permeable base courses (Section 360) each lift may not exceed four inches in thickness and must be produced at a temperature below 320 °F; the mix is allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed. Inspection requires exactly four roller passes with an eight‑to‑ten‑ton steel‑wheel roller, checking surface evenness with a ten‑foot straightedge and correcting irregularities greater than one‑half inch, and verifying depth by drilling a six‑inch diameter test hole for every three thousand square yards of completed base; the measured depth must be within one‑half inch of the design requirement. For asphalt paving interlayer systems (Section 466) the inspector verifies that the first lift placed over the interlayer achieves a compacted thickness not less than 1.5 inches, that the asphalt material is applied at a temperature no lower than 290 °F and never exceeds 320 °F, and that the underlying pavement surface has been left dry and clean of dirt, gravel, vegetation and oil with all cracks sealed and cured before placement; the application rate must be sufficient to saturate the specified interlayer fabric or grid (for example 0.22‑0.28 gal/yd² for geotextile) and overlaps must receive at least a one‑inch asphalt coating, no traffic is permitted on the interlayer system until the overlay lift is placed, and any damaged sections must be removed and replaced. Safety and environmental controls

When placing a lift of aggregate over a geosynthetic layer, workers must avoid dumping material directly from a haul truck onto the exposed fabric because it can damage the geosynthetic and create unsafe conditions; instead they should use hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading. Vehicles are not permitted on the geosynthetic until at least six inches of loose backfill has been placed, and sudden braking or sharp turning of any vehicle, including tracked equipment, on stabilized backfill is prohibited to prevent loss of control and injury to workers or the public. Geosynthetic handling also requires protection from temperatures greater than 140°F, direct sunlight, and debris, with storage elevated from the ground and rejection of any material showing tears, punctures, or other defects. For the lift of ATPBC, the material is produced at temperatures up to 320 °F and must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any further work, requiring workers to avoid contact with the hot mix; steel‑wheel power rollers weighing 8 tons to 10 tons are used for compaction, presenting a crushing risk if the mat has not cooled sufficiently. Placement is barred on unstable, frozen surfaces or when air temperature is below 35 °F or during rain, which mitigates slip and equipment instability hazards. Traffic is prohibited on the freshly placed ATPBC except for the trucks and equipment required to place the next layer, protecting the public from exposure to hot material and heavy rolling equipment. For the first lift of an asphalt overlay in an interlayer system, the binder temperature must be kept between a minimum of 290 °F and not above 320 °F, using calibrated distributor trucks and limiting hand‑spraying to avoid uneven application; the interlayer material must be stored dry, wrapped, elevated and shielded from sparks, chemicals, UV light and precipitation, with any damaged wrap discarded before use. Traffic is prohibited on the fresh lift – only construction vehicles may travel over it until the asphalt has cured and a minimum compacted thickness of at least 1.5 inches for the first lift is achieved – thereby protecting workers from hot material handling hazards and the public from vehicle‑related exposure. Curing, protection, and handoff

The ultra‑thin bonded wearing course is placed in a single lift, the paver first laying a polymer‑modified emulsified asphalt membrane and then immediately overlaying it with hot‑mix asphalt concrete to a finished thickness of 1/2 inch for Type A or 3/4 inch for Types B and C; once in place the layer acts as a seal that reduces oxidation and weathering of the surface, and no traffic restrictions are imposed on the paver‑placed surface treatment [1]. For roller‑compacted concrete lifts, after final rolling and in‑field density testing the pavement surface must be kept continuously moist by water misting, white polyethylene sheeting, or a curing compound, this moisture protection being maintained for at least 72 hours while the concrete develops a compressive strength of not less than 3,000 psi and vehicular traffic must be prohibited for the duration of the curing period [2]. In pervious asphalt lifts the freshly placed mixture must be allowed to cool to 100°F before any subsequent layer or pavement course is installed; a tack coat shall not be used between lifts and the wearing course shall be placed as soon as practical after the binder course in order to protect the fresh surface [2]. Geofoam block lifts require that the blocks be secured with sandbags or other similar soft weights that do not dent until the soil cover is placed, equipment shall be prohibited from traveling on the block surface, and the entire mass must be encapsulated by a hydrocarbon‑resistant geomembrane welded with hot‑wedge seams having a minimum three‑inch overlap; the geomembrane is then covered with Class 4 Type A geotextile overlapped at least twelve inches and bonded with spray adhesive, a six‑inch minimum compacted lift of capping material is placed on top of the geotextile and compacted with a smooth drum roller in static mode, after which soil embankment provides a minimum four‑foot vertical and horizontal cover over any exterior face of the blocks [2]. For fine‑aggregate base drain lifts each hydraulically compacted lift may not exceed nine inches, each lift is immediately sprayed with water at a rate of one gallon per linear foot, trenches deeper than two inches shall not be left open overnight, and the work area shall be barricaded at all times in accordance with 67 PA Code, Chapter 203 [2]. When a top lift of a base course reveals a depth deficiency of one‑half inch or more, the deficient areas must be corrected; after inspection the core holes are backfilled, compacted, and sealed with the same mixture used to construct the course [2]. Cement‑treated permeable base‑course lifts must begin curing immediately; a white membrane‑forming curing compound is applied at a rate of one gallon per 100 square feet ±10 % or alternatively white polyethylene sheeting may be used, the material being kept in place for 24 hours and an additional 24 hours if temperature falls below 40°F, construction traffic is prohibited for at least three days and no overlying pavement may be installed during that interval; after the three‑day period only trucks and equipment needed for the next lift may travel and any damaged or contaminated areas shall be removed and replaced [2]. Rubblized concrete pavements are swept clean after rubblizing and seating, traffic is prohibited, disturbed areas must be repaired at no cost to the Department, an overlay must be placed within 60 calendar days of rubblizing and seating, and the pavement shall not be left exposed to freezing conditions [2]. In geosynthetic‑stabilized bridge‑approach lifts the installed geosynthetic layer is protected from temperatures greater than 140°F, direct sunlight, and debris; it is stored elevated from the ground and during placement of the aggregate lift the material is placed in a non‑destructive manner such as hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading, and vehicle traffic is prohibited until at least six inches of loose backfill has been placed [2]. Asphalt‑treated permeable base‑course lifts must be allowed to cool to 100°F before subsequent layers are installed; rolling begins once the mat has cooled sufficiently using an eight‑ton to ten‑ton steel‑wheel roller or a vibratory roller operated in static mode only, traffic is not permitted except for trucks and equipment required to place the next layer, damaged or contaminated areas are replaced at no cost, the material may be re‑compacted if necessary before subsequent paving, and the surface is protected from damage before and during the concrete paving process [2]. Pervious concrete lifts require that curing start within 20 minutes after discharge; the entire surface must be completely covered with polyethylene sheeting that is thoroughly secured at every exterior edge and interior lap so wind cannot remove it, this protective cover is maintained for a minimum of seven days before traffic is allowed and rollers shall not be permitted to bridge the newly placed concrete for seven days or until the Representative confirms sufficient strength [2]. Asphalt paving interlayer systems prohibit all traffic except construction vehicles needed for placement, keep the surface dry until the overlay is installed and any rain that falls before the overlay must be allowed to dry, the asphalt material used to saturate the fabric or grid is maintained at a temperature not less than 290°F and not exceeding 320°F, the system must be fully cured before any construction traffic may drive on it, lifts that include a fiberglass mesh grid also require full cure before paving traffic, and the first lift of asphalt overlay over the interlayer system shall achieve a minimum compacted thickness of not less than 1.5 inches [2]. Plain or reinforced cement concrete lifts are protected from rapid moisture loss by keeping the evaporation rate at or below 0.20 lb/ft²·h; if that limit is exceeded windbreaks or fog‑misting systems shall be employed, fog misting equipment must atomize droplets without leaving water deposits on the surface, and the contractor’s quality‑control plan shall include procedures to ensure proper curing application and temperature monitoring after placement [2].

Expected performance and maintenance

In roller‑compacted concrete pavements (Section 502—ROLLER COMPACTED CONCRETE PAVEMENTS) a lift may develop a cold joint when more than 60 minutes elapse between successive lifts; such joints must be cut and moistened as required by Section 502.3(i)2. Compaction deficiencies constitute another distress: lifts thinner than 6 inches must reach at least 85 % of the maximum wet density, lifts six inches or thicker must attain at least 90 %, and after final rolling every lift must exceed 96 % of the maximum wet density. Surface tearing, cracking or uneven marks can arise when rollers operate within 12 to 18 inches of a fresh lane edge or when direction is reversed, and the use of pavers that tear, shove, gouge the mix or cause tracks, indented areas, segregation or other permanent surface blemishes may also generate distress. In pervious‑asphalt pavement systems (Section 420—PERVIOUS ASPHALT PAVEMENT SYSTEM) a lift is considered failed if the compacted thickness is more than 1/4‑inch less than the design thickness, and over‑compaction is a distress when more than four passes are applied or when compaction reduces the material’s free‑draining condition or crushes aggregate, thereby decreasing surface porosity. In lifts constructed with Geofoam lightweight fill blocks (Section 219—GEOFOAM LIGHTWEIGHT FILL) damage to individual blocks is a common distress: volumetric damage exceeding 0.5 % of a block’s volume, surface damage exceeding 5 % of the load‑bearing area, or continuous side damage exceeding 20 % of any side length all require replacement of the affected blocks. Water ponding on lifts—such as when crowned blocks are not placed crown upward and standing water, snow, ice or debris remain before the next layer is placed—can degrade performance, and de‑icing salts including sodium chloride must not be applied because they cause degradation. Joint distress also occurs if vertical joint gaps exceed 0.1 ft; such joints should not be filled with sand, and equipment riding on the block surface may dent or otherwise damage the blocks. Any breach of the encapsulating geomembrane or geotextile during placement, welding or compaction must be repaired to preserve lift integrity. In thin‑asphalt wearing lifts of 6.3 mm thickness (Section 412—SUPERPAVE MIXTURE DESIGN, CONSTRUCTION OF PLANT‑MIXED 6.3 MM THIN ASPHALT OVERLAY COURSES) the lift is classified as defective when the payment factor for both asphalt content and the percent passing the 75 µm (No. 200) sieve each falls to 85 %; in that case the lot must be removed and replaced, and a retest request must be submitted within three weeks of the original test results or retesting will not be allowed. For standard superpave base‑course lifts (Section 313—SUPERPAVE ASPHALT MIXTURE DESIGN, STANDARD CONSTRUCTION, BASE COURSE) a lift is deemed defective when full‑depth core measurements show a compacted depth that is short by ½ inch or more and cannot be satisfactorily corrected; the construction procedure therefore requires controlling the loose depth of each layer so that the compacted depth falls within the specified tolerance. Under the 60‑month warranty for asphalt‑concrete pavements (Section 496—ASPHALT CONCRETE PAVEMENT, 60‑MONTH WARRANTY) a lift fails when a core taken within 24 hours after final compaction indicates a depth deficiency of more than ¼ inch that cannot be satisfactorily corrected, and also when three consecutive cores reveal a uniform deficiency of more than ⅛ inch that likewise cannot be corrected; either condition renders the work defective. In cement‑treated permeable base course lifts (Section 303—CEMENT TREATED PERMEABLE BASE COURSE) surface irregularities exceeding ½ inch that remain after final compaction constitute a defect, over‑compacting to the point where the material is not free draining or the aggregate is crushed also represents a failure, premature exposure to construction traffic before at least three days of protection can damage or contaminate the lift requiring removal and replacement, and if temperature drops to 32 °F within 72 hours of placement the lift must be removed and replaced. In asphalt‑treated permeable base course lifts (Section 360—ASPHALT TREATED PERMEABLE BASE COURSE) surface irregularities greater than ½ inch after compaction are defective and must be corrected by loosening or adding material; any depth deficiency of ½ inch or more also requires removal and replacement. Over‑compacting with an 8‑ to 10‑ton steel‑wheel roller that eliminates the free‑draining condition or crushes aggregate leads to drainage loss, traffic not required for placement of the next layer is prohibited until the lift has cooled to at least 100 °F, and limiting compacted lifts to a maximum thickness of 4 inches helps prevent shoving or lateral movement that manifests as surface distress. When a lift is placed as part of an asphalt paving interlayer system (Section 466—ASPHALT PAVING INTERLAYER SYSTEM) it can be compromised if the interlayer system is damaged during placement; common distresses include wrinkles that are 1 inch or larger, which must be sliced and overlapped, folds or other damage that require removal and replacement with the same type of interlayer system at no additional cost, loss of tackiness when placed on wet pavement, and damage caused by abrupt starts and stops, which should be avoided. The roller‑compacted concrete (RCC) lift must be placed at a minimum thickness of 4 inches, and for single lifts the thickness shall be kept between four inches and nine inches; when more than one lift is required the second lift shall be placed within 60 minutes of completion of the first to avoid a cold joint, otherwise the joint must be prepared as a cold joint. Surface quality is checked with a straightedge and any deviation exceeding 1/4 inch triggers removal of high points by grinding the surface with a self‑propelled diamond grinder; cracks that appear before saw cutting must be repaired as directed. During service each geofoam lift shall be inspected for block damage, and any block showing volumetric damage of more than 0.5 % of its volume or surface damage of more than 5 % of the load‑bearing area shall be replaced with the damaged area filled with dry fine sand as specified; a cracked geomembrane shall be repaired by welding patches that extend a minimum of 6 inches beyond each damaged edge, and any torn geotextile shall be removed and replaced with a new lap overlapping a minimum of 12 inches in all directions secured with the prescribed seaming spray adhesive. The thin‑lift 6.3 mm asphalt wearing course is monitored through mixture acceptance testing of density, asphalt content and the percent passing the No. 200 sieve; if both the asphalt‑content and sieve‑passing payment factors drop to 85 % the lift is deemed defective and must be removed and replaced, with the contractor permitted to request a retest in writing within three weeks of the LTS test release; when a retest is granted the inspector takes offset cores, rinses them, and backfills the holes within 24 hours using the same JMF asphalt mixture or the mix used for subsequent courses at no cost to the department. Under the 60‑month warranty each lift must be monitored by coring within 24 hours after final compaction in the presence of the inspector; a 4‑inch (or larger) core taken through all lifts and courses at intervals of one per 2,000 ft is measured according to PTM No. 737, and any single core showing a deficiency greater than ¼ inch or three consecutive cores uniformly low by more than ⅛ inch trigger a defective‑work determination that requires immediate repair by adding material at the identified point and extending longitudinally and transversely until the depth is within ¼ inch of the required value, after which the core holes are backfilled, compacted and sealed with the same mixture. During construction of a cement‑treated permeable base course the finished surface shall be monitored with a 10‑foot straightedge at suspect locations and transverse joints, advancing the tool not more than 5 feet per stage; any irregularity exceeding more than ½ inch must be corrected by loosening the surface mixture and adding or removing CTPBC without grinding or milling, and the lift shall be protected by prohibiting construction traffic for at least three days after placement, then limiting traffic to equipment needed for the next layer; areas that become damaged or contaminated shall be removed and replaced at no cost to the department. For a geosynthetic‑stabilized bridge‑approach lift all vehicles are kept off the exposed geosynthetic until a minimum of 6 inches of loose backfill is in place, sudden braking or sharp turning on the stabilized backfill is prohibited, and aggregate for the lift must be placed in a non‑destructive manner such as hand placement, light rain from an excavator bucket, or gradual blading to avoid damaging the geosynthetic; any disturbance or distortion of the geosynthetic caused by equipment shall be corrected promptly at no additional cost. The asphalt‑treated permeable base course lift shall be protected from traffic except for placement equipment, work must stop when air temperature falls below 35 °F or rain occurs, and after each lift the mixture is allowed to cool to 100 °F before the next layer; ongoing monitoring consists of straight‑edge checks for surface irregularities and periodic six‑inch test holes—one per 3,000 square yards of completed base—to verify that depth remains within ½ inch of design, with any irregularity exceeding ½ inch repaired by loosening the surface and adding or removing ATPBC (grinding or milling prohibited), and any section found deficient in depth by ½ inch or more removed and replaced until tolerance is met, with damaged or contaminated areas also replaced and re‑compacted before subsequent paving. In the pervious‑concrete system the coarse‑aggregate base is placed in lifts that shall not exceed eight inches in thickness, each lift being compacted as required by Section 360.3; during service infiltration testing shall be performed on three locations per 10,000 sq ft of pervious concrete and coring at a rate of three four‑inch cores for each 5,000 sq ft to verify hardened density, void content within ± 5 % and core‑length tolerances, and if the average infiltration falls below the required 200 inches per hour or the void content exceeds its tolerance the affected lift may need to be removed and replaced.
