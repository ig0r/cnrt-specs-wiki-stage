Slurry Seal

Part of: Pavement Preservation is-subtopic-of
Slurry Seal is a topic under Pavement Preservation.
slurry-sealslurry-sealsslurry-surfacing
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

Slurry seal is an emulsified‑asphalt surface treatment applied to existing pavements to correct minor surface deficiencies such as slight rutting, cracking, and loss of fine aggregate, thereby restoring the functional wearing condition without providing structural upgrading [1] [2]. The mix—comprising a Class SS‑1h or CSS‑1h asphalt emulsion, fine aggregate meeting SRL specifications, filler and water free of harmful salts—is designed per ASTM D3910 and placed at 50 °F–125 °F with a mechanically‑operated squeegee distributor after conditioning cracks, joints and removing paint, forming a thin wearing layer that may serve as a minimum or moderate surface depending on the selected Type I, II, or III [2]. Because it is limited to non‑Interstate roads where the pavement structure does not require upgrading, slurry seal is only appropriate when these minor‑defect criteria are met and must be avoided for more extensive rehabilitation needs [1].

Purpose and treatment objectives

Slurry seal is applied to existing pavements to correct surface deficiencies such as minor rutting, minor cracking, and loss of fine aggregate, and it may be used only when the pavement does not require structural upgrading [1]. The treatment is also specified for pavements that exhibit cracking, open voids, and surface erosion; in more severe erosion conditions a minimum wearing layer can be provided, or a new moderate wearing surface or crown can be built up [2]. Different slurry‑seal types are defined to target these conditions: Type I addresses cracks, voids and general surface erosion, Type II is intended for filling voids, correcting severe erosion and supplying at least a minimal wearing surface, while Type III supplies a fresh moderate wearing surface or adds crown buildup [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (p. 333)

According to the Pavement Policy Manual, the objective of applying a slurry seal is to correct surface deficiencies such as minor rutting, minor cracking, and loss of fine aggregate, thereby restoring the pavement’s functional condition rather than providing structural upgrading; this aligns with repairing damage and regaining functionality [1]. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications state that the slurry seal treatment is applied primarily to repair damage in existing pavements by sealing cracks, filling voids, and correcting surface erosion, thereby restoring the functional wearing surface, and that depending on the selected type it can provide a minimum wearing layer (Type II) or create a new moderate wearing surface or crown (Type III), which contributes to extending the pavement’s service life [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (p. 333)

Applicability and treatment selection

Slurry seal is appropriate only on non‑Interstate roads where the pavement exhibits surface‑level defects such as minor rutting, minor cracking, or loss of fine aggregate, and where the structure does not require upgrading; it should be applied after determining suitability according to Table 5.1 for the specific traffic and site conditions. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103)

Slurry seal should be avoided on any Interstate roadway, and it is also inappropriate when the pavement exhibits conditions that require structural upgrading rather than merely minor rutting, minor cracking, or loss of fine aggregate; in such cases a more substantial rehabilitation or repair method must be selected. For other roads the suitability of slurry seal is decided according to Table 5.1. [1]

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103)

Condition assessment and timing

Slurry seal is appropriate only for pavements that exhibit minor surface deficiencies such as minor rutting, minor cracking, or loss of fine aggregate, and it shall not be placed on a pavement in need of structural upgrading; the treatment is also prohibited on Interstate highways (source: Pavement Policy Manual) [1]. The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications state that slurry seal may be applied when the pavement exhibits cracking, voids, or surface erosion; a Type I mix is used to seal cracks, fill voids, and correct surface erosion, a Type II mix is selected to fill surface voids, correct severe surface erosion, and provide at least a minimum wearing surface, and a Type III mix is employed when a new moderate wearing surface or crown is needed (source: Commonwealth of Pennsylvania DOT Publication 408/2026) [2].

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (p. 333)

Materials and repair details

Slurry‑seal treatment is performed by constructing an emulsified asphalt slurry seal course as described in Section 482.1, using the designated emulsified asphalt mix for the surface layer. Slurry seal requires an emulsified asphalt material classified as Class SS‑1h or CSS‑1h (Section 702), fine aggregate that meets the SRL designation called for in the contract and is suitable for wearing courses, filler per Section 703.1(c)1, and water free of harmful soluble salts whose hardness must not exceed 150 ppm (9 grains) unless a softener is added. The mixture is to be designed and tested according to ASTM D3910 using fine aggregate, asphalt emulsion, water and filler in the weight‑percentage ranges within Table A. For Type I—sealing cracks, filling voids and correcting surface erosion—the dry‑aggregate rate shall be from 6 pounds per square yard to 10 pounds per square yard; for Type II—filling surface voids, correcting severe erosion and providing a minimum wearing surface—the rate shall be from 10 to 15 pounds per square yard; and for Type III—providing a moderate wearing surface or building a crown—the rate shall be 15 pounds per square yard or more. Prior to placement the existing pavement surface must be conditioned by sealing open joints and cracks with an acceptable crack sealer or emulsion, removing heavy paint buildup, and protecting or replacing raised pavement markers. During placement the slurry temperature must be kept between 50 °F and 125 °F. Application requires a mechanically‑operated, squeegee‑type distributor that is integrally assembled with the slurry mixer; the equipment includes a flexible‑lined strike‑off, vertical adjustment for grade and cross slope, and a pressure system equipped with a fog‑type spray bar capable of applying a complete fog coat of water at no more than 0.05 gallon per square yard immediately before spreading. Auxiliary hand equipment such as squeegees, shovels and surface‑cleaning machines must also be provided. Transverse joints are formed either by overlapping cured slurry with ten feet to fifteen feet of fresh slurry or by lightly wetting the area where the spreader box contacts an uncured mix; longitudinal joints require pulling a burlap bag (or similar) over the seam when the slurry is uncured and may be wetted with the spray bar if fully cured. Traffic is prohibited until the seal has fully cured to a firm condition that will prevent pick‑up of the mixture. [2]

[2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 333–335)

Slurry seals are restricted to non‑Interstate roads and should only be applied where the pavement exhibits minor surface defects such as slight rutting, cracking, or loss of fine aggregate; they shall not be placed on a pavement that requires structural upgrading because using them on structurally deficient pavements would compromise performance. [1] The emulsified asphalt used in the slurry must meet the Class SS‑1h or CSS‑1h specification, the fine aggregate must carry the SRL designation required by the contract item, and filler is provided as called for in Section 703.1(c)1. [2] Water incorporated into the mix shall be free of harmful soluble salts and its hardness may not exceed 150 ppm (9 grains); if the hardness exceeds that limit it must be lowered below 150 ppm (9 grains) by adding a water softener before mixing. [2] The mixture is designed and tested in accordance with ASTM D3910 using the weight‑percentage limits given in Table A, and the selected type of seal—Type I for crack sealing, void filling and minor surface erosion, Type II for severe surface erosion and a minimum wearing surface, or Type III for a moderate new wearing surface or crown—depends on the existing pavement distress. [2] Open joints and cracks must be sealed with an acceptable crack sealer or emulsion before slurry placement, heavy paint buildup must be removed, and any raised‑pavement markers must be covered and satisfactorily protected or replaced. [2] Proper joint construction also affects integrity; fresh slurry should overlap transverse joints, and appropriate wetting techniques should be used on longitudinal joints. [2] The temperature of the components of the completed mixture must be controlled so that the application temperature of the slurry seal stays within the designated range but not less than 50 °F or more than 125 °F. [2]

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 333–335)

Treatment procedures

The existing pavement must first be conditioned by sealing open joints and cracks with an acceptable crack sealer or emulsion, removing any heavy paint buildup, and protecting or replacing raised pavement markers as required. Immediately before spreading, a fog coat of water is applied using the spreader’s pressure system and spray bar at no more than 0.05 gallon per square yard, then the slurry—kept between 50°F and 125°F—is placed with the integrated squeegee distributor while controlling grade and cross‑slope; transverse joints are formed by overlapping fresh over cured or by lightly wetting the area, and longitudinal joints are treated by pulling a burlap bag when the mix is uncured or by wetting if cured. After placement the surface is allowed to cure without traffic until it reaches a firm condition. [2]

[2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 334–335)

Slurry seal applications must not be used on the Interstate system, so any project that includes this treatment has to avoid those roadways. For all other roads the eligibility of slurry seal is determined in accordance with Table 5.1, although no guidance is provided on equipment, sequencing, or environmental conditions. Implementation requires a mechanically‑operated squeegee‑type distributor that is integrated with the mixer and equipped with a flexible‑lined strike‑off, vertical adjustment, and a fog‑type spray bar capable of applying up to 0.05 gallon per square yard immediately before spreading; auxiliary hand tools such as hand squeegees, shovels and surface‑cleaning machines are also supplied. Prior to placement the existing pavement must be conditioned by sealing open joints and cracks, removing heavy paint buildup, and covering or replacing raised pavement markers, after which a fog coat is applied and the slurry temperature is maintained not less than 50 °F and not more than 125 °F. Transverse joints are formed either by overlapping previously cured slurry with ten‑to‑fifteen feet of fresh slurry or by lightly wetting the area while the mix remains semi‑fluid, whereas longitudinal joints are handled by pulling a burlap bag when uncured or by wetting with the spray bar when cured. Traffic is prohibited until directed to allow the seal to fully cure to a firm condition that prevents pick‑up of the mixture. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103); [2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 334–335)

Quality and workmanship

When inspecting a slurry seal, the inspector should verify that the emulsified asphalt used is Class SS-1h or CSS-1h, that the fine aggregate supplied carries the SRL designation required by the contract and originates from an acceptable source, that any filler meets the specification, and that the mixing water is free of harmful soluble salts and does not exceed 150 ppm (9 grains) hardness unless softened. The mixture shall be designed and tested in accordance with ASTM D3910 and its weight‑percentage composition must fall within the limits of Table A for the selected type. The installed dry‑aggregate layer must be placed at the rate prescribed for the chosen type—between 6 pounds per square yard and 10 pounds per square yard for Type I, between 10 pounds per square yard and 15 pounds per square yard for Type II, or 15 pounds per square yard or more for Type III. The spreading equipment shall be a mechanically‑operated squeegee‑type distributor that is integrally mounted to the mixer, equipped with a flexible‑lined strike‑off, vertical adjustment for grade and cross‑slope, and a pressure system plus fog spray bar capable of applying up to 0.05 gallon per square yard of water; auxiliary hand tools such as squeegees, shovels and surface‑cleaning machines must be present. The existing pavement surface shall be examined to confirm that open joints and cracks have been sealed with an acceptable crack sealer or emulsion, heavy paint buildup has been removed, and raised markers are protected or replaced as needed. During placement the temperature of all mixture components must be controlled so that the application temperature stays within the specified range, not less than 50 °F and not more than 125 °F. Joints should be constructed according to the prescribed methods for transverse and longitudinal seams, and traffic must be withheld until the seal has fully cured to a firm condition. [2]

[2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 333–335)

For construction under Section 482.3, a satisfactory slurry seal requires the use of a mechanically‑operated squeegee‑type distributor that is integrally assembled with the mixer, includes a flexible‑lined strike‑off, provides vertical adjustment for grade and cross‑slope, and incorporates a pressure system with a fog‑type spray bar capable of applying no more than 0.05 gallon per square yard of water immediately before spreading. The mixed slurry must be placed at a temperature that is not less than 50 °F and not more than 125 °F. Surface preparation must seal open joints and cracks, remove heavy paint buildup, and protect or replace raised pavement markers; joints are to be constructed according to the prescribed overlapping or wetting methods, and traffic may not be allowed until the seal has fully cured to a firm condition. For material selection under Section 482.2, a satisfactory slurry seal requires the emulsified asphalt to meet Class SS‑1h or CSS‑1h as specified in Section 702; the fine aggregate must carry the SRL designation called for in the contract (or be an equal‑or‑better blend mixed by an approved method); and the water hardness must not exceed 150 ppm (9 grains) unless a softener is used to reduce it below that limit. The mixture must be designed and tested per ASTM D3910, and the proportions of fine aggregate, emulsion, water and any filler must fall within the weight‑percentage limits shown in Table A. The dry‑aggregate application rate must correspond to the selected type: 6–10 lb/yd² for Type I, 10–15 lb/yd² for Type II, or at least 15 lb/yd² for Type III. [2]

Monitoring and follow-up

A slurry seal can address only minor rutting, minor cracking, and loss of fine aggregate; if the pavement exhibits conditions that require structural upgrading—meaning distress beyond those minor deficiencies—the treated area should be considered for additional maintenance, repair, or rehabilitation rather than another surface treatment. [1] — Ch. 5 (p. 103)
