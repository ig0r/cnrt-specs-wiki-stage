Structural Number

Part of: Flexible Pavement Design is-subtopic-of
Structural Number is a topic under Flexible Pavement Design.
structural-numbersn-effsn-fsn-fsn-for-future-traffic
Created 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-30

Structural Number (SN) is a dimensionless measure of the structural strength required for full‑depth flexible pavements, used to ensure that a pavement or overlay can sustain anticipated traffic loads throughout its design life [1]. In design it quantifies both the effective existing capacity (SN_eff) and the future‑traffic requirement (SN_f), and an overlay is deemed adequate only when the calculated SN of the proposed structure exceeds the required Design SN, thereby bridging any gap between existing and needed strength [1]. The SN is derived by summing the products of material‑specific structural coefficients and layer thicknesses—each multiplied by a drainage coefficient set to 1.0—and then translating that total into specific layer depths while respecting minimum and maximum course limits and cost considerations [1].

PennDOT publications covering “Structural Number” also include the following subtopics: Structural Layer Coefficient.

The Structural Layer Coefficient supplies the material-specific capacity values required by the structural-number equation to translate a Design Structural Number into specific layer thicknesses. [1] Coefficients account for variations in stabilization methods and recycled materials, ensuring that each pavement course contributes appropriately to the overall load-bearing performance of the structure. [1]

Structural Layer Coefficient

Purpose and design objectives

Structural Number is used to quantify the required structural strength of a full‑depth flexible pavement so that the pavement can be structurally adequate under anticipated traffic loads. In overlay design it provides a dimensionless measure of the existing effective structural capacity (SN_eff) that can be compared with the structural capacity required for future traffic (SN_f); by quantifying this difference engineers ensure that an overlay will supply enough strength to meet anticipated loading and maintain performance over its design life. The design must bridge the gap between the SN required for future traffic and the effective existing SN, and the calculated Structural Number of the proposed pavement or overlay must be greater than the corresponding Design Structural Number for the structure to be considered structurally adequate. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173), Ch. 10 (pp. 177–180)

For an AC overlay the design must achieve a calculated Structural Number that exceeds the required Overlay (Design) SN, thereby satisfying the structural adequacy objective; this is ensured by using the appropriate future‑traffic SN inputs and effective existing SN methods while omitting subbase contributions when its condition is uncertain or poor, as specified for Interstates and certain other roadways. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (p. 180)

Scope and applicability

For full‑depth flexible pavement designs the design structural number is applied, representing the required strength and later converted to layer thicknesses with the standard equation; it is appropriate when the approved structure is a flexible pavement that includes either an aggregate/cement or an aggregate/lime/pozzolan base course, which must be offered as alternatives in the project bid, and the design follows the specified or optimized thickness methods while observing the minimum and maximum course depth limits. For overlay design the Structural Number is used only for flexible pavements; the existing effective structural capacity is expressed as SN_eff and the capacity required for future traffic as SNf, and applying a Structural Number is appropriate when the underlying pavement is a flexible (asphalt) structure rather than a rigid or composite one. When designing an asphalt‑concrete overlay over an existing AC pavement the Structural Number is employed to calculate both the Future Traffic SN and the Effective Existing SN; the Component Method or the Non‑Destructive Testing Method should be used, with layer depths and structural coefficients entered as described, and data obtained from pavement cores or, if cores are unavailable, from the Roadway Management System; on Interstates the existing subbase is excluded from the structural evaluation, while on non‑Interstate roads the subbase may also be omitted when the design 18‑kip ESALs exceed 5,000,000 unless a specific investigation confirms its condition and drainability. The Structural Number approach is also appropriate for designing an asphalt concrete overlay over a fractured Portland cement concrete slab, specifically for cracked‑and‑seated or rubblized Jointed Plain Concrete Pavement or Jointed Reinforced Concrete Pavement; in this application the designer first computes the SN required for future traffic and then determines the Existing Effective SN using the Component Method, following the guidance of Section 10.2 for subbase inclusion, and flexible‑pavement average initial truck factors from Table 7.1 are used in the ESAL calculations that feed the SN values. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173), Ch. 10 (pp. 177–181)

In the full‑depth flexible pavement design, the required Structural Number must be satisfied but the layer thicknesses are obtained only with the Specified Thickness Design method or the Optimized Thickness Design method; the Layered Analysis Thickness Design method is not permitted, particularly when minimum and maximum course depth limits from Tables 9.4 and 9.5 constrain the solution or when total pavement cost must be minimized. All drainage coefficients shall be set to 1.0 and structural coefficients taken from Table 9.3. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173)

Design inputs and requirements

Designing an AC overlay requires site‑specific existing pavement data—layer thicknesses obtained from cores or, if cores are unavailable, from the Roadway Management System—and information on subbase condition and drainability when the design 18‑kip ESALs exceed 5,000,000. Traffic inputs consist of future traffic loads expressed as ESALs using the flexible pavement Average Initial Truck Factors and the appropriate values listed in Table 10.7. Material data include the structural coefficients for each layer (Table 9.3) entered with drainage coefficients set to 1.0, while subbase may be excluded on Interstates or when it is missing on non‑Interstates unless its quality has been verified. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (p. 180)

In a full‑depth flexible pavement design (section 9.8) the required Design Structural Number is governed by project constraints and specifications. The Calculated SN from the thickness design must be greater than the Design SN to be structurally adequate. Only the Specified Thickness Design or Optimized Thickness Design methods available in DARWin may be used, and do not use the Layered Analysis Thickness Design method. Structural coefficients (a_i) are obtained from Table 9.3 and all layer drainage coefficients (m_i) shall be set to 1.0. The allowable layer thicknesses are limited by the minimum and maximum course depths given in Tables 9.4 and 9.5, with cost considerations driving a least‑cost configuration. When a flexible pavement includes an aggregate/cement or aggregate/lime/pozzolan base, those two base materials must be offered as alternatives in the bid proposal. For an asphalt concrete overlay (section 10.2) the Structural Number is governed by several project specifications. Input values for the future‑traffic SN are taken from Table 10.7 and ESAL calculations shall use the flexible pavement Average Initial Truck Factors listed in Table 7.1. When the Component Method is employed, the depths of all pavement layers and their corresponding structural coefficients from Table 9.3 are entered into DARWin, with all drainage coefficients set to 1.0, to calculate the effective SN. The subbase may be omitted on Interstates, and on other roadways it shall be excluded when the design 18‑kip ESALs exceed 5,000,000 unless a subbase investigation has verified its quality and drainability. Finally, the overlay is only acceptable if the Calculated SN is greater than the Overlay (Design) SN. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173), Ch. 10 (p. 180)

Design methods and criteria

In full‑depth flexible pavement design the Structural Number is obtained by entering the required variables into DARWin and clicking the calculate button; the resulting Design SN is then related to layer thicknesses through the equation SN = a1 d1 + a2 d2 m2 + a3 d3 m3 … an dn mn, where the structural coefficients ai are taken from Table 9.3 and each drainage coefficient mi shall be set to 1.0. To be structurally adequate the calculated SN from a thickness design must exceed the Design SN, and this is achieved by using either the Specified Thickness Design method or the Optimized Thickness Design method available in DARWin, while the Layered Analysis Thickness Design method is not permitted. In overlay design the effective structural number for flexible pavements (SN_eff) is obtained by applying one of three basic evaluation methods: a condition survey or component analysis that considers pavement distress and layer properties; non‑destructive deflection testing that uses Falling Weight Deflectometer data to assess in‑situ subgrade and pavement stiffness; or a remaining‑life approach that projects service life from historic traffic and current condition; the resulting SN_eff is then compared with the future‑traffic requirement SN_f. For an asphalt concrete (AC) overlay of an existing AC pavement the Future Traffic SN is first calculated in DARWin using the input guidance of Table 10.7 together with flexible pavement Average Initial Truck Factors from Table 7.1 for ESAL estimation; the Effective Existing SN is then determined by either the Component Method or the Non‑Destructive Testing Method (the Remaining Life Method is also available but not recommended) by entering each layer’s depth and its structural coefficient from Table 9.3 into the structural number equation of Section 9.8 with drainage coefficients set to 1.0; the Component Method or Non‑Destructive Testing Method should be used for AC Overlay of AC Pavement design, and the overlay SN is taken as the difference between these two values, after which a thickness design is performed in DARWin using either the specified or optimized thickness design methods. For an AC overlay of a fractured Portland cement concrete (PCC) slab the procedure follows the same sequence: the SN for Future Traffic is calculated, the Existing Effective SN is determined using the Component Method with subbase inclusion guidance from Section 10.2, and the Overlay SN is computed before performing a thickness design that references Table 10.7 and Section 10.2; ESAL values required for these calculations are taken from the flexible pavement Average Initial Truck Factors listed in Table 7.1. [1]

In the full‑depth flexible pavement design covered by section 9.8, the calculated structural number obtained from the chosen thickness method must be greater than the required Design SN for the pavement to be considered structurally adequate; only the Specified Thickness Design or Optimized Thickness Design methods may be used and the Layered Analysis Thickness Design method is prohibited. For AC overlay of an existing AC pavement as described in section 10.2, the calculated SN from DARWin must be greater than the overlay (design) SN to ensure structural adequacy; the effective existing SN shall be obtained with either the Component Method or the Non‑Destructive Testing Method using layer depths and the structural coefficients of Table 9.3 while all drainage coefficients are set to 1.0. Subbase material may be omitted on Interstate projects, and it also may be omitted on non‑Interstate roads when the design 18‑kip ESALs exceed 5,000,000 unless a subbase investigation has been performed that verifies its quality and drainability. Input values for future traffic SN follow Table 10.7 and ESAL calculations shall use the flexible pavement Average Initial Truck Factors from Table 7.1. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173), Ch. 10 (p. 180)

Materials and mixture design

In section 9.8 DESIGN STRUCTURAL NUMBER, the design Structural Number is obtained by summing, for each pavement layer, the product of the material‑dependent structural coefficient (taken from Table 9.3) and the layer thickness, with a drainage factor that shall be set to 1.0; consequently, the SN must specify only the structural coefficient and the thickness for every layer, and no other mixture proportions are required, while a flexible pavement that includes a base course must present both aggregate/cement and aggregate/lime/pozzolan options as allowable materials in the bid. In section 10.2 AC OVERLAY OF AC PAVEMENT, when the Structural Number is calculated by the Component Method for an asphalt concrete overlay, the designer must provide the thickness of every pavement layer together with the structural coefficient assigned to that material (as listed in Table 9.3), and all drainage coefficients must be set to 1.0; any subbase judged to be in poor condition may be omitted from this evaluation under the conditions described for Interstates and high‑ESAL roadways. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173), Ch. 10 (p. 180)

To satisfy the required structural strength indicated by the calculated Design Structural Number, the SN must be translated into specific layer thicknesses using the equation SN = a1 d1 + a2 d2 m2 + a3 d3 m3 + …, where the structural coefficients ai are taken from Table 9.3 and all drainage coefficients mi shall be set to 1.0; the resulting thicknesses must meet or exceed the Design SN for structural adequacy. The pavement designer should employ either the Specified Thickness Design method or the Optimized Thickness Design method in DARWin, while observing the minimum and maximum course depths given in Tables 9.4 and 9.5 and selecting layer thicknesses that minimize total pavement cost by considering surface, base, and subbase unit costs along with their structural coefficients. When a flexible pavement includes an aggregate/cement or aggregate/lime/pozzolan base course, both base material options must be presented as alternatives in the project bid. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

In the full‑depth flexible pavement design (section 9.8) all layer drainage coefficients, mi, shall be set to 1.0, and in the AC‑overlay design (section 10.2) all drainage coefficients should equal to 1.0, so no adjustment for drainage conditions is applied in the Structural Number calculation. The source does not give any additional requirements for moisture, climate, or other environmental conditions in the structural number computation. Because the existing subbase may be in poor condition, do not include it in the structural evaluation of the existing pavement for overlay design on Interstates. Do not include subbase when the design 18‑kip ESALs exceed 5,000,000 on all other roadways unless subbase investigation has been performed to verify its quality and drainability. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173), Ch. 10 (p. 180)

Verification and expected performance

After calculating the Design Structural Number, the engineer must convert it to layer thicknesses using the SN = a_i d_i m_i equation and verify that the resulting Calculated SN from those thicknesses is greater than the Design SN, which confirms structural adequacy. The verification shall be performed with either the Specified Thickness Design method or the Optimized Thickness Design method in DARWin, while the Layered Analysis Thickness Design method must not be used, and all drainage coefficients mi shall be set to 1.0. Compliance also requires that the proposed layer depths respect the minimum and maximum course depths given in Tables 9.4 and 9.5, and that any cost‑effective adjustments consider surface, base and subbase unit costs as well as structural coefficients. When a flexible pavement with an aggregate/cement or aggregate/lime/pozzolan base is approved, those two base materials must be offered as alternatives in the project bid to satisfy constructability and project‑requirement consistency. [1]

[1] — Ch. 9 (pp. 172–173)
