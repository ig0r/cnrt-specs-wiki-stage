Separation Layer

Part of: Unbonded Concrete Overlay is-subtopic-of
Separation Layer is a topic under Unbonded Concrete Overlay.
separation-layerasphalt-separation-layerlayer-separationgeotextile-interlayer
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-28

The separation layer is a thin interlayer placed between pavement courses to keep adjacent materials from mixing, thereby preserving each course’s intended structural function and aiding control of water movement and erosion within the pavement system [1][2]. It may be provided either as a fine‑graded asphalt film at least 1 inch thick or as a Class 4 Type A (Layer‑Separation) geotextile that must extend across the full lane width (minimum eight feet), be overlapped on ends and edges, and remain uncovered no longer than two weeks to ensure drainage and prevent moisture accumulation [1][2]. By isolating the layers while allowing independent slab movement and providing a drainable path—often daylighted or tied to a subdrain—the separation layer enables uniform support of new overlays, mitigates faulting of unstable slabs, and contributes to long‑term durability of the pavement structure [1][2].

Purpose and design objectives

The separation layer is placed between pavement courses to prevent the adjacent materials from intermixing, thereby preserving each course’s intended function and assisting in the control of water movement and erosion within the pavement structure [1][2]. In unbonded concrete overlays on deteriorated pavements, a fine‑graded asphalt separation layer at least 1 inch thick is installed between the existing concrete slab and the new overlay so that the two slabs do not bond; this allows independent slab movement, provides uniform support, and mitigates faulting of unstable or visibly rocking slabs [1]. When mill joints exhibit vertical displacement greater than ¼ inch, a geotextile interlayer is required to maintain layer separation and to avoid non‑uniform support of the concrete overlay; if the joint fault exceeds ½ inch, an asphalt interlayer must be placed after a scratch course or milling to reduce the fault before the overlay is placed [1][2]. For treatments that involve strengthening subgrade soils, a Class 4‑Type A geotextile is specified as the separation layer; its function is to act as a barrier that keeps the subgrade and subbase materials distinct so that each retains or improves its structural performance [1]. The geotextile separator must be rolled over the prepared surface, overlapped at least 6 inches on ends and edges, covered within two weeks, and must not impede free drainage, thereby preventing water accumulation at the interface and contributing to erosion control [2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 62–63), Ch. 4 (pp. 96–98), Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150), Ch. 10 (p. 185); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–174), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (p. 471)

In unbonded concrete overlay rehabilitation the separation layer must be a fine‑graded asphalt material that is at least 1 inch thick or an equivalent non‑woven geotextile placed on the existing concrete pavement to isolate the old slab from the new overlay so that the overlay can function as an independent structural element and restore capacity without relying on bond with the underlying pavement. In a Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach the separation layer must functionally cover the entire prepared subgrade and excavated layback slope as a continuous Class 4 Type A geosynthetic to prevent intermixing of adjacent pavement layers and to control erosion. For general geotextile applications the Separation Layer must act as a continuous barrier that prevents intermixing of adjacent pavement layers and controls erosion, which requires placement on a prepared surface free of puncturing objects. The separation layer shall be placed across the entire lane width—at least eight feet—for all completed subgrades as stipulated in Publication 408 Section 210.3(b). In an unbonded concrete overlay the layer must extend through any reconstructed shoulder and be tied to the mainline. When specifying a geotextile interlayer, joints with greater than 1/4‑inch faulting must be milled; if faulting exceeds 1/2 inch a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) or milling/grinding of the joint is required. In Section 224 the layer must be laid flat, pulled tight, anchored, kept within ±1/2‑inch of plan elevation, and maintain required vertical spacing to other layers. Adjacent sheets in that section must overlap a minimum of 12 inches with no splicing. In Section 212 the fabric must be rolled out loosely without stretching, folds or wrinkles and overlapped at least 6 inches on roll ends and edges; general overlaps are 1.5 ft and underwater overlaps are 3 ft. Securing pins may be used at a maximum spacing of six feet on un‑overlapped portions or eliminated on slopes flatter than 6:1. In Section 548 the geotextile shall be placed flat, its edges overlapped by 8 inches ± 2 inches, extended at least 4 inches beyond the pavement edge, and terminated in a drainable layer or daylighted location so that free drainage is not impaired; no more than three layers of geotextile may overlay in any location. In Section 224 the material must be protected from temperatures greater than 140 °F, direct sunlight and debris, stored elevated, and any geosynthetic showing defects, tears or damage must be rejected and replaced at no additional cost. The fabric in Section 212 must be covered with backfill or subbase promptly so that exposure does not exceed two weeks, and the cover material must be placed and compacted as specified to avoid slippage and protect the fabric from damage such as rocks dropped from heights greater than one foot. In Section 548 the fabric must be kept clean and free of loose debris, dampened but not saturated, and its surface temperature may not exceed 120 °F before the concrete overlay is placed. By maintaining a positive slope for drainage, the layer contributes to long‑term serviceability and durability of the pavement structure. In an unbonded PCC overlay the separation layer must provide a drainage path achieved by daylighting the interlayer or installing a vertical subdrain. Section 224 ensures safety by prohibiting vehicle traffic on the exposed layer until a minimum of 6 inches of loose backfill is placed, and by requiring avoidance of sudden braking or sharp turning on stabilized backfill. Grading aggregate must be performed without wrinkling the geosynthetic, and any disturbance or distortion caused by equipment must be corrected. [1][2]

Scope and applicability

In PennDOT experimental pavement construction (section 2.9), a separation layer is appropriate when a geotextile is employed for drainage, layer separation, erosion control, sediment control, or to reinforce soft subgrade soil and aggregate layers; these uses are treated as standard practice [1]. If the geotextile is intended to add structural reinforcement beyond those functions it must be classified as an experimental construction item and a project‑specific design shall be prepared using Publication 408, FHWA’s Geosynthetic Design and Construction Guidelines, NTPEP data, and the manufacturer’s product information [1]. For unbonded concrete overlays that are used to restore structural capacity of moderately to significantly deteriorated pavements (section 4.9), a separation layer between the existing concrete slab and the new overlay is required and may be provided by placing a fine‑graded asphalt layer at least 1 inch thick, or alternatively by using a non‑woven geotextile interlayer so that the overlay functions as a new pavement on the stable existing platform [1]. Where subgrade‑strengthening treatments such as cement or lime stabilization or rock capping are applied (section 6.2), a separation layer supplied by a Class 4 Type A geotextile is required and must be placed between the treated subgrade and any overlying aggregate material; it also applies to all completed subgrades that span an entire lane width of eight feet or greater [1]. In addition, a Class 4 Type C geotextile may serve as a separation‑and‑stabilization layer on subgrades whose CBR value is less than three, thereby supporting full‑depth flexible pavement designs and any patching work longer than twenty feet where the subbase is removed for drainage or other concerns [1]. For unbonded concrete overlays on concrete‑surfaced rigid pavements, a separation layer is used when existing mill joints exhibit greater than 1/4‑inch faulting, or when joint faulting exceeds 1/2 inch and a scratch course or milling is provided [1][2]. When a geotextile interlayer is selected, the fabric is laid flat over the repaired and cleaned pavement, overlapped by 8 inches ± 2 inches, limited to no more than three layers in any location, and extended at least 4 inches beyond the pavement edge before being terminated in a drainable layer; the geotextile must be kept clean, damp but not saturated, and its surface temperature may not exceed 120°F [2]. When an asphalt interlayer is selected, a nominal depth of 1 inch of Superpave mix design is placed over joints that have been reduced to acceptable faulting, with surface temperature also limited to 120°F before overlay placement [1][2]. When a geotextile interlayer is specified, a nominal depth of 1 inch of a Superpave Wearing Course, 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix is the standard thickness, and if joint faulting exceeds 1/2 inch a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch), 9.5 mm mix should be placed at the joint or the joints milled/grinded to reduce faulting [1]. The interlayer must have proper drainage by daylighting it or installing a vertical subdrain, and it must extend through any reconstructed shoulder and be tied to the mainline [1]. In Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach (GSBA) projects, a Class 4 Type A geotextile is used to separate the prepared subgrade and excavated lay‑back slope from the overlying aggregate backfill; it may be applied for both new bridge construction and retrofits where a GSBA is specified, provided the foundation has been cleared of deleterious soils and proof‑rolled, and when the subgrade consists of AASHTO No. 1 or No. 3 coarse aggregate, R‑rock or shot rock fill [2]. The layer must extend over the entire prepared surface before backfill placement [2]. The separation‑layer method is also prescribed under Class 4 – Layer Separation (Type A) geotextile applications where a prepared surface free of puncturing objects receives a loose, unstretched fabric that is overlapped at least six inches on roll ends and edges; the fabric must be covered with subbase or other required cover material within two weeks to function as a layer separator between subgrade and overlying aggregate [2]. This approach is appropriate for projects that call for layer separation (and optionally erosion control) using Type A geotextile, with the specified handling, overlap, and exposure limits [2].

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 62–63), Ch. 4 (pp. 96–98), Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150), Ch. 10 (p. 185); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–204), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (p. 471)

When an unbonded concrete structural overlay is required, the guidance prohibits using an asphalt separation layer as a leveling course and mandates that all grade corrections be performed with the concrete overlay itself, making a concrete‑only approach preferable. Geotextiles or geogrids intended to act as soil reinforcement are treated as experimental items; because such applications demand project‑specific design parameters drawn from Publication 408, FHWA’s Geosynthetic Design and Construction Guidelines, NTPEP data, manufacturer data, and other technical references, a non‑experimental alternative is preferred. If the existing concrete pavement is in relatively good condition, the subgrade is stable, and drainage functions properly, a bonded JPCP overlay that relies on structural bonding is favored over an unbonded system that would require a fine‑graded minimum 1‑inch asphalt separation layer or a non‑woven geotextile interlayer. Conversely, significant structural deterioration, unstable support, stripping of the asphalt, or unresolved drainage problems make the separated‑layer approach appropriate, so the presence of good condition limits the use of a separation layer. For subgrades with a CBR value less than three, or where sufficient undercut depth cannot be achieved, a dual‑function stabilization geotextile (Class 4 Type C) is preferred over a simple Class 4 Layer Separation (Type A); likewise, if lane width is less than eight feet the standard Class 4 Layer Separation cannot be applied without a special provision, requiring an alternative treatment. Geotextile interlayers are limited to joints faulting no more than ¼‑inch; when joint faulting exceeds ½‑inch the specification calls for placing a Superpave Wearing Course scratch mix or milling/grinding the joints rather than relying on an asphalt or geotextile separation layer, making those alternatives preferable. Additional constraints such as keeping the surface temperature of the fabric or pavement below 120 °F and avoiding construction traffic on the geotextile also limit its use; when these limits cannot be met, alternative preparation or design measures must be employed instead of a separation layer. Design inputs and requirements

The design of a separation layer for an unbonded PCC overlay requires traffic input in the form of rigid‑pavement Average Initial Truck Factors for ESAL calculations, material specifications that the interlayer be a geotextile or asphalt concrete and that a nominal 1 inch Superpave Wearing Course (9.5 mm Fine‑Graded) be placed over milled joints with faulting greater than ¼‑inch (or a Scratch mix if faulting exceeds ½‑inch), existing‑condition data such as joint faulting measurements and the presence of visibly rocking slabs, and environmental/drainage provisions that the interlayer be daylighted or provided with a vertical subdrain and extend through any reconstructed shoulder; the existing pavement must also be capable of serving as a relatively uniform support subbase. [1]

[1] — Ch. 10 (p. 185)

In PennDOT projects a separation layer that is provided by a geotextile shall be designed in accordance with Publication 408 (Specifications) and the FHWA Geosynthetic Design and Construction Guidelines, and the designer should reference the National Transportation Product Evaluation Program, the geotextile manufacturer’s published product data, and other appropriate technical references when determining the geotextile properties, design specifications, product selection, and construction procedures. When a geotextile is employed as an experimental item—such as for soil reinforcement beyond its standard drainage or layer‑separation functions—the work must be coordinated through the BOCM Materials Division New Products and Innovations Section, an Experimental Item Work Plan must be prepared and approved before the PS&E package submittal, and approval must be obtained from FHWA on Federal‑oversight projects or from the appropriate Bureau Director on non‑federal projects, with FHWA notification when federal funds are used. Both Publication 408 Section 210.3(b) and Section 224.3(e) require a Class 4, Type A separation geosynthetic to cover the entire prepared subgrade (and excavated lay‑back slope for bridge approaches) across the full lane width; when the underlying subgrade has a CBR value less than three, a Class 4, Type C geotextile may be used to provide both separation and reinforcement. Design of a Separation Layer is first governed by the material submittal and approval requirements of Section 106.03(b)3, which require an electronic submission of manufacturer data for all geosynthetics at least 30 days before construction. The geosynthetic must be stored elevated, protected from temperatures greater than 140°F, direct sunlight and debris, and any damaged material must be rejected and replaced at no cost. Placement tolerances are limited to ±1/2 inch of plan elevation; splicing is prohibited, a minimum 12‑inch overlap on parallel roll edges is required, overlaps shall be limited to within 4 feet of wrapped edges when placed perpendicular to the roadway, and adjacent roll‑ends and edges shall overlap a minimum of 6 inches. The fabric shall be rolled out loosely, unstretched and without folds or wrinkles, and adjacent roll‑ends and edges shall overlap a minimum of 6 inches; steel pins, as specified in Section 212.2, may be used to secure the fabric before cover placement, but the fabric must not be left exposed for more than 2 weeks before it is covered with subbase or other required cover material. Cover material shall be placed by back dumping and spreading ahead of all equipment, and shall be compacted as specified in Section 206.3. For unbonded concrete overlays the separation layer may be provided either by a fine‑graded asphalt interlayer of at least 1 inch nominal depth, specified as a 9.5 mm Superpave mix, or by a non‑woven geotextile interlayer; when an asphalt interlayer is used the existing pavement must be repaired, cleaned and swept, the surface temperature must not exceed 120°F before placement, the mix shall be compacted with a rubber‑tire roller, any joint faulting greater than ½ inch must be corrected by a scratch course or milling, and the Superpave wearing course shall have a nominal depth of 1 inch as referenced in Section 410.3. When a geotextile interlayer is specified for an unbonded concrete overlay the fabric shall be laid flat with edges overlapped 8 inches ± 2 inches, shall not exceed three layers in any location, shall extend at least 4 inches beyond the pavement edge, shall terminate in a drainable layer, shall be kept clean and dampened but not saturated, and shall be protected from construction traffic while its surface temperature does not exceed 120°F. Proper drainage of the interlayer shall be achieved by daylighting it or installing a vertical subdrain, and the interlayer must extend through any shoulder reconstruction and be tied to the mainline. Design methods and criteria

The design of a Separation Layer that employs geotextiles begins with establishing project‑specific geotextile design parameters and then consulting FHWA’s Geosynthetic Design and Construction Guidelines (Publication 408), the National Transportation Product Evaluation Program, the manufacturer’s published product data, and other appropriate technical references to define material properties, design specifications, product selection, and construction procedures. For unbonded concrete overlays the separation function may be provided either by placing a fine‑graded minimum one‑inch asphalt separation layer on the existing concrete pavement or by using a non‑woven geotextile interlayer as specified in Publication 408 Section 548. When a geotextile is selected, the required product is a Class 4—Layer Separation (Type A) fabric that separates two dissimilar materials such as subbase and subgrade. The geotextile must extend across the entire lane width, which shall be eight feet or greater according to Publication 408 Section 210.3(b); any road‑widening project that is less than eight feet requires a special provision. In patching situations where the subbase is removed for drainage or other reasons, the geotextile must be placed in sections longer than twenty feet and at least eight feet wide as required by Publication 72M RC‑26M. The construction procedure for a Class 4, Type A separation geosynthetic over a prepared subgrade or excavated lay‑back slope requires the sheet to be laid flat, pulled tight, anchored, and positioned within ±½ inch of the plan elevation while maintaining the required vertical spacing to adjacent geosynthetic layers; splicing is prohibited, and each roll edge parallel to the roadway must overlap a minimum of twelve inches with no overlapping allowed within four feet of a wrapped edge. For installation under Section 212—Geotextiles, the surface shall be prepared and cleared of puncturing objects, after which the geotextile is rolled out flat in an unstretched condition without folds or wrinkles; roll‑ends and edges are overlapped at least six inches with adjacent material, overlaps are secured with steel pins placed on a maximum six‑foot spacing on unlapped portions, and the fabric must be covered with the designated subbase or other required cover material within two weeks of exposure, after which the cover material is compacted in accordance with Section 206.3. In the unbonded concrete overlay construction sequence (Section 548), surface preparation includes repairing unstable slabs and filling defects larger than four square inches and one inch deep; for a geotextile interlayer, joints exhibiting more than one‑quarter‑inch faulting are milled, the fabric is laid flat on the cleaned surface with an eight‑inch ± two‑inch overlap, limited to three layers in any location, extended at least four inches beyond the pavement edge, kept clean and damp but not saturated, protected from traffic, and its surface temperature must not exceed 120°F while maintaining free drainage. When an asphalt interlayer is used instead of a geotextile, a nominal one‑inch Superpave mix design is placed, and if joint faulting exceeds one‑half inch a scratch course or milling is required before rolling with a rubber‑tire roller, also keeping the pavement surface temperature below 120°F. The overlay thickness design follows the Unbonded Concrete Overlay module, using rigid‑pavement Average Initial Truck Factors from Table 7.1 to compute equivalent single axle loads (ESALs), then determining the future‑traffic pavement thickness from Table 10.8 and the existing effective pavement thickness from Table 10.9 so that the required overlay thickness can be derived and checked against the minimum values in Table 10.10; a geotextile interlayer is specified when milled joints have greater than one‑quarter‑inch faulting, with a standard one‑inch Superpave Wearing Course of 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix applied, and if joint faulting exceeds one‑half inch the Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) 9.5 mm mix is placed at the joint or the joints are milled/grinded to reduce faulting. Backfill placement over a geotextile in bridge‑approach applications must be performed in a non‑destructive manner such as hand placement or light rain from an excavator bucket, and vehicles are barred until at least six inches of loose backfill cover the sheet. Anchor the fabric in place by securing pins or other acceptable methods along overlaps, with pin spacing shown in Table A, and ensure that any exposed geotextile is not left uncovered for more than two weeks. [1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 62–63), Ch. 4 (pp. 96–98), Ch. 6 (pp. 143–150), Ch. 10 (p. 185); [2] — SECTION 200 EARTHWORK (pp. 173–204), SECTION 500—RIGID PAVEMENTS (p. 471)

In experimental pavement construction, when a geotextile is selected as the separation layer, its design parameters must be established on a project‑by‑project basis by consulting Publication 408 specifications, FHWA’s Geosynthetic Design and Construction Guidelines, National Transportation Product Evaluation Program data, the manufacturer’s published product information, and any other appropriate technical references; the source does not prescribe generic numeric thresholds, safety factors, or reliability values. In rehabilitation of unbonded concrete overlays, the separation layer shall be a fine‑graded asphalt placed on the existing concrete pavement with a minimum thickness of 1 inch, although a non‑woven geotextile interlayer may also be used in lieu of an asphalt separation layer as permitted by Publication 408 Section 548. In subgrade work, the separation layer shall be a Class 4 Layer‑Separation geotextile (Type A) installed between subgrade and subbase, and it must extend across the entire lane width of at least eight feet as required by Publication 408 Section 210.3(b); a special provision is required when road widening is less than eight feet, and for patching where the subbase is removed the geotextile must be placed in sections longer than 20 feet and at minimum eight feet wide per Publication 72M RC‑26M. The separation layer shall be a Class 4, Type A geotextile that completely covers the prepared subgrade and excavated lay‑back slope; adjacent sheets must overlap a minimum of 12 inches along parallel roll edges, splicing is prohibited, and the layer must be placed within ±½ inch of the plan elevation while preserving the required vertical spacing to any overlying geosynthetic layers. The Class 4, Type A separation geotextile is required to be rolled out over a prepared surface in a loose, unstretched condition without folds or wrinkles; overlaps of roll ends and edges shall be at least 6 inches with adjacent material, side edges shall overlap by at least 1.5 feet (or 3 feet for underwater placement), adjacent roll ends shall be offset by at least 10 feet, and the fabric may not remain exposed for more than 2 weeks before being covered with subbase or other required cover material. In unbonded PCC overlay of PCC pavement, the separation layer shall be provided either by a geotextile interlayer or by asphalt concrete, and it is required when existing concrete joints exhibit faulting greater than 1/4 inch; if joint faulting exceeds 1/2 inch a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) 9.5 mm mix must be placed at the joint or the joint must be milled/grinded to reduce faulting, and the standard thickness of the asphalt concrete interlayer is a nominal depth of 1 inch of a Superpave Wearing Course, 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix. Proper drainage of the interlayer shall be ensured by daylighting the interlayer or installing a vertical subdrain, and the interlayer must extend through any shoulder that is constructed or reconstructed and be tied to the mainline. For unbonded concrete overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements, the separation layer must be installed on a repaired, clean surface and may be placed only after milling joints that exhibit greater than 1/4‑inch faulting; geotextile sheets shall overlap edges by 8 inches ± 2 inches, no more than three layers may occur in any location, the free edge must extend at least 4 inches beyond the pavement edge while maintaining free drainage, the fabric must be kept clean, dampened but not saturated, and its surface temperature may not exceed 120°F before concrete placement, and construction traffic should be avoided. If an asphalt interlayer is used, a nominal depth of 1 inch of Superpave mix shall be placed, and joints with faulting greater than 1/2‑inch require a scratch course or milling to reduce fault. Structural section and dimensions

According to the Pavement Policy Manual, in Section 4.9 Rehabilitation for unbonded concrete overlays the separation layer shall be a fine‑graded asphalt material having a minimum thickness of 1 inch; alternatively a non‑woven geotextile interlayer may be used in lieu of the asphalt layer, and the manual provides no dimensional requirements for pavement, base, subbase, treated‑soil, slab, shoulder, edge‑support, or reinforcement [1]. The same Manual, in Section 6.2 Subgrade Soils, requires that a Class 4‑Type A geotextile be installed as the separation layer across the entire lane width, which is defined as eight feet or greater; for patching situations the geotextile must extend for greater than 20 feet in length and a minimum of eight feet in width, and it is required as a separation layer in all subgrade‑strengthening treatments [1]. The Department of Transportation Specification states that when a geotextile separation layer is used it shall be placed flat on the repaired surface with its edges overlapped by 8 inches ± 2 inches and the free edge extending at least 4 inches beyond the pavement edge, and no more than three layers of geotextile may overlay in any location [2]. When an asphalt interlayer is used as the separation layer, a nominal depth of 1 inch of Superpave mix design shall be provided [2].

Materials and mixture design

For unbonded concrete overlays addressed in the Rehabilitation section of the Pavement Policy Manual, the separation layer shall consist of a fine‑graded asphalt placed on the existing concrete pavement with a minimum thickness of 1 inch, or may be substituted by a non‑woven geotextile interlayer [1]. In the same manual’s Unbonded PCC Overlay of PCC Pavement section, when a geotextile is selected it must be a Class 4 Layer‑Separation Type A fabric and is required where joint faulting exceeds ¼ inch; the layer shall be installed with a nominal depth of 1 inch of a Superpave Wearing Course comprising a 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix, while joints with faulting greater than ½ inch require placement of a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) of the same 9.5 mm mix or milling/grinding to reduce faulting [1]. The Subgrade Soils section further mandates that the separation layer be a Class 4 Layer‑Separation geotextile Type A applied across the entire lane width, which must be at least eight feet wide [1]. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications for a Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach require the separation layer to be a Class 4 Type A separation geosynthetic that fully covers the prepared subgrade and excavated lay‑back slope, placed within ±½ inch of the design elevations, with prescribed vertical spacing to other geosynthetic layers, no splices, at least 12 inches overlap between adjacent sheets, protection from temperatures above 140°F, direct sunlight and debris, storage elevated off the ground, and replacement of any defective material at no cost [2]. For general geotextile applications in Section 212, the separation layer shall be a Type A fabric laid loose and unstretched without folds, with roll‑ends and edges overlapped a minimum of six inches, and promptly covered with subbase or other required cover material [2]. In the Unbonded Concrete Overlay section of the Pennsylvania specifications, if a geotextile interlayer is used it shall be placed flat on the repaired surface, overlapped 8 inches ± 2 inches, limited to no more than three layers in any location, with its free edge extending at least four inches beyond the pavement edge, must not impair drainage, and its temperature may not exceed 120°F before overlay placement [2]. If an asphalt interlayer is selected for the same unbonded concrete overlay, a Superpave mix design shall be placed to a nominal depth of 1 inch as specified in Section 410.3; when joint faulting exceeds ½ inch a scratch course or milling must be provided, and the pavement surface temperature may not exceed 120°F before placement [2].

Structural details and interfaces

In the Pavement Policy Manual, section 4.9 Rehabilitation, the separation layer for an unbonded concrete overlay shall be a fine‑graded asphalt film having a minimum thickness of 1 inch, although a non‑woven geotextile interlayer may be substituted. The same manual states that joints in the unbonded concrete overlay are to be installed in the same manner as joints in a new concrete pavement and need not align with existing transverse joints; joint spacing is determined by the type and thickness of the concrete placed. Still within section 10.7 Unbonded PCC Overlay, when a geotextile interlayer is specified the contractor must mill any existing concrete joint that exhibits faulting greater than 1/4‑inch, and if faulting exceeds 1/2‑inch either a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) 9.5 mm mix shall be placed at the joint or the joint shall be milled/grinded to reduce the faulting. The same section prescribes a nominal depth of 1 inch of a Superpave Wearing Course, 9.5 mm fine‑graded mix as the standard thickness for the interlayer and requires that proper drainage of the interlayer be provided either by daylighting it or by installing a vertical subdrain; the interlayer must also extend through any shoulder that is constructed or reconstructed and be tied to the mainline. In section 6.2 Subgrade Soils, the manual mandates the use of a Class 4—Layer Separation (Type A) geotextile placed between the subgrade and the overlying subbase; this fabric must extend across the entire lane width, defined as 8 feet or greater, and is required in all subgrade‑strengthening treatments. For a Separation Layer required by Section 224—Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach, a continuous Class 4 Type A geotextile shall be installed with no splices; seams or connections are prohibited and each roll must overlap adjacent parallel edges by at least 12 inches. When the material is wrapped at an edge, it must remain continuous with no overlap within 4 feet of that edge, and any partial rolls placed perpendicular to traffic must extend a minimum of 4 feet from the wrapped face before an overlap is permitted; full‑width strips may run parallel with the same 12‑inch overlap. The geosynthetic shall be laid flat, pulled tight and anchored in place until backfill is placed, while maintaining the prescribed vertical spacing between layers and an elevation tolerance of ±½ inch. In the layer‑separation application described in Section 212—Geotextiles, each roll end and side edge shall be overlapped by at least six inches with the adjacent piece, with the upslope sheet lying over the downslope sheet; steel pins may be used to anchor the geotextile before back‑filling, and on any un‑overlapped perimeter the pins shall not be spaced more than six feet apart. The installed fabric must be covered with subbase or other cover material within two weeks of exposure, and the cover is to be compacted in accordance with Section 206.3 to prevent movement that could open the joints. For erosion‑control installations the required overlap increases to a minimum of 1½ feet (or three feet underwater) and roll ends are offset at least ten feet. Under Section 548—Unbonded Concrete Overlay, existing pavement joints must be milled when they have faulting greater than 1/4‑inch, and if joint faulting exceeds 1/2‑inch an asphalt interlayer requires a scratch course at the joint or milling/grinding of the joint to reduce faulting. Geotextile interlayers for this overlay are placed flat with edges overlapped by 8 inches ± 2 inches, limited to three layers in any location, and the free edge must extend a minimum of 4 inches beyond the pavement edge before terminating in a drainable or daylighted location. In experimental pavement construction, when a Separation Layer is provided by a geotextile, its interface with adjacent pavement layers must be designed on a project‑by‑project basis using the criteria set forth in Publication 408, the FHWA Geosynthetic Design and Construction Guidelines, NTPEP data, the manufacturer’s product data, and any other appropriate technical references; these sources are to define geotextile properties, design specifications, product selection, and construction procedures because the use of geotextiles is limited to drainage, layer separation, erosion control, sediment control, and reinforcement of soft subgrade or aggregate layers [1]. For unbonded concrete overlays, the interface is deliberately separated by installing a fine‑graded asphalt separation layer that is at least 1 inch thick on the existing concrete pavement, or alternatively a non‑woven geotextile interlayer may be used in lieu of the asphalt layer; this separation prevents bonding between the old and new concrete so that the overlay can act as a new pavement with joints designed according to standard new‑pavement criteria [1]. When a geotextile is employed for unbonded PCC overlays, concrete joints with greater than ¼‑inch faulting must be milled, and if joint faulting exceeds ½ inch a Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) 9.5 mm mix of nominal depth 1 inch should be placed at the joint or the joint should be milled/grinded to reduce faulting; proper drainage of the interlayer must be achieved by daylighting it or installing a vertical subdrain, and the interlayer must extend through any shoulder that is constructed or reconstructed and be tied into the mainline [1]. At the subgrade‑subbase interface, the Separation Layer is provided by a Class 4—Layer Separation (Type A) geotextile that must extend across the entire lane width of at least eight feet as required by Publication 408 Section 210.3(b); if the project involves road widening that is less than eight feet wide a special provision is required, and when the geotextile is used in patching situations where the subbase is removed for drainage or other reasons it must be installed in lengths greater than 20 feet and with a minimum width of eight feet as specified in Publication 72M RC‑26M [1]. The separation layer for a Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach, described in Section 212—Geotextiles, is installed by laying a Class 4, Type A geotextile over the entire prepared subgrade and excavated lay‑back slope, with the material positioned within ±½ inch of the plan elevation and the required vertical spacing to any overlying geosynthetic layers preserved [2]. Splicing of the geotextile is prohibited; adjacent sheets are overlapped a minimum of 12 inches along parallel edges, and when the sheet is wrapped at an edge no overlap may occur within 4 feet of that wrap, while continuous lengths placed perpendicular to traffic must extend at least 4 feet from the wrapped face before any overlap [2]. All layers are extended in uninterrupted strips whenever feasible, and subsurface drainage systems are provided at the abutment and at the far end of the fill as shown on the standard drawing [2]. For general layer‑separation applications the geotextile is rolled out over a clean, prepared surface free of objects that could puncture it and must lie loose and unstretched without folds or wrinkles; the fabric edges and roll ends are overlapped at least 6 inches with adjacent material, then covered promptly so that exposure does not exceed two weeks, and the cover material is placed and compacted as specified in Section 206.3 [2]. Overlaps should be arranged with the upslope fabric over the downslope fabric, and steel pins may be used to secure the geotextile before covering if wind lift is a concern [2]. After repairing and cleaning the existing pavement, a separation layer is placed directly on the surface; when a geotextile interlayer is used the fabric must be laid flat, overlapped by eight inches ± two inches on each edge, limited to no more than three layers in any spot, extended at least four inches beyond the pavement edge, and terminated into a drainable layer or daylighted location without impairing drainage [2]. The geotextile must be secured per the manufacturer’s instructions, kept clean, dampened but not saturated, protected from construction traffic, and its surface temperature may not exceed 120 °F before the concrete overlay is placed [2].

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

According to the Pavement Policy Manual, the Separation Layer can be a Class 4 geotextile identified as a Layer‑Separation (Type A) fabric that isolates the subbase from the subgrade across the full lane width, preserving each material’s integrity and limiting moisture migration; when placed on low‑CBR subgrades the Stabilization (Type C) version may be used, which also requires maintaining a positive slope for drainage [1]. The design should incorporate pavement base drains or other appropriate drains to remove water that could contribute to frost heave, because any solution to frost action must reduce or eliminate excess moisture and freezing temperatures, thereby mitigating climate‑related effects such as differential heaving and thaw‑weakening of the subgrade [1]. In Section 224—Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach, the separation geosynthetic layer must be protected from temperatures greater than 140 °F and direct sunlight during delivery, storage, and handling to prevent degradation [2]. The design also must provide subsurface drainage systems at the abutment and at the far end of the GSBA fill to control moisture and ensure proper environmental performance [2]. Section 212—Geotextiles requires that the placement surface be cleared of objects that could puncture the fabric, and that the geotextile be laid flat in a loose, unstretched condition with overlaps of at least 6 inches on roll ends and edges; the fabric must be secured, for example with steel pins, to prevent wind uplift [2]. The geotextile may not remain exposed for more than two weeks before being covered with subbase or other specified cover material, which limits moisture intrusion and protects the layer from climatic exposure, and a positive slope is maintained during placement to provide drainage away from the separation interface [2]. For unbonded concrete overlays, Section 548 specifies that the geotextile separation layer shall be placed flat on the repaired and cleaned pavement surface, overlapped by about eight inches and extended at least four inches beyond the edge of the pavement; the interlayer shall be terminated in a drainable layer or at a daylighted location so that free drainage is not impaired [2]. The geotextile may be dampened but must not be saturated before the concrete overlay, and its surface temperature must not exceed 120 °F prior to placement [2].

The separation layer identified as Class 4—Layer Separation (Type A) geotextile shall be installed on all completed subgrades and must extend across the entire lane width, which is defined as eight feet or greater [1]. In patching situations it must be at least eight feet wide and applied over a length greater than twenty feet [1]. When a geotextile interlayer is used in an unbonded PCC overlay, existing concrete joints shall be milled if they exhibit faulting greater than ¼‑inch; if faulting exceeds ½‑inch a 9.5 mm Superpave Wearing Course (Scratch) mix shall be placed at the joint or the joint must be milled/grinded to reduce the faulting [1]. The interlayer shall have a nominal depth of one inch of a 9.5 mm Fine‑Graded Superpave Wearing Course and shall provide geometric continuity across the pavement structure [1]. Proper drainage shall be ensured by daylighting the interlayer or installing a vertical subdrain, and the interlayer shall extend through any reconstructed shoulder and be tied into the mainline [1]. For Section 224—Geosynthetic Stabilized Bridge Approach, the separation layer must be installed as a continuous Class 4, Type A geosynthetic covering the entire prepared subgrade and excavated layback slope, with required vertical spacing between geosynthetic layers [2]. Any adjacent rolls shall overlap at least 12 inches along parallel edges and the layer must be placed within ±½‑inch of the plan elevation [2]. No vehicles are allowed on the geosynthetic until a minimum of six inches of loose backfill has been placed, and sudden braking or sharp turning on the stabilized surface is prohibited [2]. For Section 212—Geotextiles, the Separation Layer must be installed as a loose, unstretched geotextile without folds or wrinkles and protected from direct traffic or construction equipment until it is fully covered [2]. Roll‑ends and edges shall overlap at least 6 inches, and the fabric may be secured with steel pins before back‑dumping the cover material [2]. Abrupt turns with tracked equipment that could pull or tension the fabric are prohibited, exposure of the geotextile must not exceed two weeks before it is covered by subbase or other required material, and rocks may not be dropped onto the fabric from a height greater than one foot [2]. For Section 548—Unbonded Concrete Overlay of concrete‑surfaced pavements, the geotextile separation layer shall be placed only after mill joints that exceed ¼‑inch faulting have been prepared, laid flat on the repaired surface and overlapped by eight inches ± 2 inches on each side [2]. Its free edge must extend a minimum of four inches beyond the pavement margin and be terminated in a drainable or daylighted location so drainage is not impaired [2]. No more than three layers may overlay at any point, the fabric must be kept clean, damp but not saturated, secured per the manufacturer’s instructions, and its surface temperature may not exceed 120 °F before overlay placement, while construction traffic should be avoided on the geotextile and sharp turns or braking minimized during installation [2].

Verification and expected performance

The completed Separation Layer is verified by confirming that a Class 4—Layer Separation (Type A) geotextile has been placed over the full lane width required (eight feet or greater) in accordance with Publication 408 Section 210.3(b), that any road‑widening projects narrower than eight feet include the mandated special provision, and that the material and its installation meet the product and placement criteria set out in the relevant Publication 408 specification sections such as Sections 212 and 735 which define the acceptable class, type, and performance of the separation fabric. The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Transportation specifications require the Representative to inspect the completed separation layer after foundation preparation and before backfill to confirm that a continuous Class 4 Type A geotextile fully covers the prepared subgrade and lay‑back slope, that splicing is prohibited, that adjacent rolls overlap at least 12 inches along parallel edges, and that the material is placed within ±1/2‑inch of the plan elevation. The inspection also verifies that required vertical spacing to any overlying geosynthetic layers is maintained and that the geotextile has been protected from temperatures greater than 140 °F, direct sunlight and debris during delivery, storage and handling. In Section 212 of Publication 408, compliance of the completed separation layer is further verified by confirming that the geotextile was rolled out over the prepared surface in a loose, unstretched condition without folds or wrinkles, that roll‑ends and edges overlap at least 6 inches, that any steel pins permitted by Section 212.2 are installed to prevent wind lift, that the fabric was covered with the required cover material within two weeks of placement and compacted as directed in Section 206.3, and that no traffic or construction equipment contacted the fabric before it was fully covered. For unbonded concrete overlay projects (Section 548), verification requires confirming that the geotextile has been laid flat, overlapped by eight inches ± 2 inches on each side, limited to no more than three layers in any location, extended at least four inches beyond the pavement edge with its free edge terminated into a drainable layer so drainage is not impaired; it must be secured per the manufacturer’s instructions, kept clean, damp but not saturated, protected from construction traffic while ensuring its surface temperature does not exceed 120 °F before concrete placement. The same section also mandates checking the asphalt interlayer by confirming a nominal one‑inch depth of the specified Superpave mix, that any joint faulting greater than ½ inch has been reduced by a scratch course or milling, that a rubber‑tire roller was used, and that the pavement surface temperature at placement did not exceed 120 °F, with temperature control performed by an approved method.
