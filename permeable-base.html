Permeable Base

Part of: Subsurface Drainage is-subtopic-of
Permeable Base is a topic under Subsurface Drainage.
treated-permeable-basecement-treated-permeable-base-courseasphalt-treated-permeable-base-coursetreated-permeable-base-courseopen-graded-drainage-layers
Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-28

A Permeable Base is a treated asphalt or cement layer placed over a 2A subbase in rigid Interstate and Expressway pavement designs, serving as the designated drainage tier when a positive outlet can be provided [1]. Its purpose is to create a free‑draining matrix that prevents water accumulation, erosion, and loss of support so that the effective modulus of subgrade reaction is maintained, with design values of 15 000 psi for spring‑thaw, 30 000 psi for normal conditions, and 50 000 psi when frozen, and a drainage coefficient of Cd = 1.1 for new construction or reconstruction projects that incorporate an underdrain system [1]. The base functions by allowing storm‑water to infiltrate through its porous aggregate at rates exceeding 200 in/hr while compaction is limited to four passes with steel‑wheel rollers, the mix is produced below 320 °F and placed within eight hours on stable, unfrozen surfaces (air temperature ≥35 °F for ATPB/CTPB or ≥50 °F for pervious systems), thereby preserving porosity and drainage performance [2].

Purpose and design objectives

In rigid pavement designs for Interstate and Expressway projects, the engineering problem addressed by a Treated Permeable Base is loss of support caused by water‑induced erosion or pumping that can reduce the effective modulus of subgrade reaction k (pounds per square inch) [1]. The base is intended to provide a free‑draining layer beneath the pavement so that water does not accumulate and cause moisture‑related distress, and in pervious asphalt systems it ensures storm‑water can pass through the pavement structure rather than pond on or within it [2]. To prevent that degradation, all such designs should have an Asphalt or Cement Treated Permeable Base (ATPB or CTPB) placed over a 2A Subbase so that a dedicated drainage layer provides positive outlet drainage and maintains the effective k‑value [1]. The primary performance need identified is adequate drainage beneath the rigid pavement, reflected by using a drainage coefficient C_d = 1.1 for new construction or reconstruction projects [1]. Under the asphalt treated permeable base course specification, compaction shall be limited; rollers may make four passes but the material must not be compacted to the point where it ceases to be free draining or the aggregate becomes crushed [2]. Test sections for pervious asphalt systems must demonstrate an average infiltration rate exceeding 200 inches per hour, and subgrade excavation shall be to undisturbed soil without compaction so that the subgrade remains as permeable as possible [2].

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–163); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

For Interstate and Expressway rigid pavement designs, the permeable base—provided as either an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base (ATPB) or a Cement Treated Permeable Base (CTPB)—may be used as an alternate layer whose primary function is drainage, but it shall be placed only where a positive outlet for water can be achieved. [1] When the treated permeable base is incorporated into a subbase that rests on unbound aggregate and an underdrain system providing positive drainage, the design must achieve an overall drainage coefficient of C_d = 1.1, which satisfies the functional requirement for effective water removal to protect pavement life; in rehabilitation projects that do not incorporate significant drainage improvements the required drainage coefficient reverts to C_d = 1.0. [1] The permeable base must also achieve a functional drainage performance in which test sections produce an average infiltration rate exceeding 200 inches per hour. [2] For evaluating structural performance the contribution of this layer to the effective modulus of subgrade reaction shall employ the prescribed base elastic moduli of 15 000 psi in spring‑thaw conditions, 30 000 psi under normal conditions, and 50 000 psi when frozen, and the combined subbase‑to‑soil resilient‑modulus ratio may not exceed 4. [1] The ATPBC shall be placed only when the surface is stable and the air temperature is at least 35 °F; placement on unstable, frozen or colder surfaces is prohibited. [2] The mixture shall be produced below 320 °F and allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed so that the binding film achieves the required viscosity without segregation. [2] Structural integrity for this course is obtained by using steel‑wheel power rollers weighing 8 tons to 10 tons, applying exactly four roller passes, overlapping longitudinal joints by 1–2 inches, and verifying depth with a 6‑inch diameter test hole so that the finished base is within ½ inch of the design elevation. [2] The compacted thickness may not be more than ¼‑inch less than the design thickness and compaction shall be limited to no more than four passes to preserve porosity. [2] Loss‑of‑support factors for each layer are applied in a weighted average to reflect potential erosion or pumping, thereby addressing durability concerns. [1] Traffic on the fresh ATPBC shall be prohibited except for placement equipment, protecting the surface from damage, and any damaged or contaminated areas shall be replaced at no cost to the Department. [2] Durability requirements prohibit placement when air or surface temperature is lower than 50 °F and forbid over‑compacting that would eliminate free drainage or crush aggregate. [2] Safety objectives include preparing an undisturbed, permeable subgrade without heavy equipment traffic, controlling sediment from entering detention beds, and prohibiting the use of pervious beds for storage of equipment, materials, or soils during construction. [2] Serviceability is maintained by ensuring that the base remains freely draining throughout construction and after compaction, limiting surface irregularities to less than ½ inch, correcting any deviations by loosening or adding material, and confirming profile tolerances with a 10‑foot straightedge and automated sensors. [2]

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–163); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

Scope and applicability

For rigid pavements on Interstate and Expressway projects, a permeable base—either Asphalt Treated Permeable Base (ATPB) or Cement Treated Permeable Base (CTPB)—shall be placed as the base layer over a 2A Subbase [1]. The same type of permeable base may also be used in 3R and other CPR projects when the existing pavement was constructed on an Open‑graded subbase, ATPB, or CTPB and the design team approves its use [1]. Drainage layers employing these permeable bases shall only be specified where a positive outlet drainage can be achieved; otherwise a dense base with longitudinal edge drains is required [1]. Because Open Graded Subbase is prohibited for concrete pavement projects, ATPB or CTPB are the sole permitted drainage layer materials for such projects [1]. For new construction or reconstruction projects that incorporate a subbase of treated permeable base (or dense asphalt base) placed over unbound aggregate and provide an underdrain system with positive drainage, the design calls for a drainage coefficient Cd = 1.1, indicating suitability under those conditions [1]. For pervious asphalt pavement systems where bus or light‑truck traffic is expected, the treated permeable base is called for under Section 360.3 [2]. The material may be placed only on surfaces that are stable and not frozen, and the air temperature must be at least 35 °F; placement is prohibited during rain [2]. The mixture shall be produced at a temperature below 320 °F, must provide suitable viscosity for adequate coating of aggregate particles, avoid segregation during transportation, may not be stockpiled, and must be installed within eight hours of production [2]. Compaction shall be performed with steel‑wheel rollers having a certified metal weight of eight to ten tons in four‑inch lifts, and the material may not be compacted so much that it ceases to be free draining or that the aggregate is crushed; no more than four passes with smooth steel‑wheeled static rollers are permitted to preserve drainage [2]. For pervious asphalt pavement systems the subgrade shall be scarified at least six inches deep and left as undisturbed and permeable as possible, and test sections of at least 225 ft² must show an average infiltration rate exceeding 200 in/hr; the compacted thickness may not be more than a quarter‑inch less than the design thickness [2]. Installation is limited to air or surface temperatures of fifty degrees Fahrenheit or higher [2].

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–163); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

When a site cannot provide appropriate positive outlet drainage, a dense base with longitudinal edge drains is recommended instead of an Asphalt or Cement Treated Permeable Base [1]. If the ratio of the subbase modulus to the roadbed soil resilient modulus would exceed 4, the permeable‑base option should be avoided and a different base type considered [1]. Placement of an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course is prohibited when the subgrade is unstable, frozen, when the ambient temperature is below 35 °F, or when placement would occur during rain; under any of those circumstances a different base design must be used [2]. The mix must be produced at a temperature below 320 °F and placed within eight hours of production, so projects unable to meet either the temperature limit or the eight‑hour placement window should select an alternative treatment [2]. If the required steel‑wheel power rollers with a certified metal weight of eight to ten tons are not available, if compacted lifts cannot be limited to four inches, or if the surface cannot be cooled to 100 °F before the next layer, another design approach should be chosen [2]. For pervious asphalt pavement systems a permeable base is limited when the air or surface temperature is below 50 °F, when infiltration testing of each required 225 sq ft test section does not yield an average exceeding 200 inches per hour, when the subgrade cannot be left undisturbed and permeable because of erosion, fine‑material accumulation, or heavy equipment traffic, or when compaction would require more than four passes, in any such case another design approach should be selected [2].

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–162); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

Design inputs and requirements

Design of a permeable base must be based on site investigations that confirm an undisturbed, minimally compacted subgrade with any eroded or fine‑material zones scarified at least six inches and repaired to the Representative’s satisfaction before basin installation; material selection is verified by constructing two test sections using the approved JMF where each section is at least 225 ft², the compacted thickness may not be more than ¼‑inch less than design thickness, and the average infiltration rate must exceed 200 in/hr; environmental constraints require that placement of the pervious mixture be avoided when air or surface temperature is lower than 50 °F, and construction traffic is limited to light equipment with no heavy haul trucks on the installation area and no traffic on the binder course until it is cleaned or replaced as directed by the Representative; existing‑condition preparation includes coating vertical surfaces with an emulsified asphalt tack coat and correcting any irregularities in the binder course before placing the wearing layer. [2]

[2] — SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

According to the Pavement Policy Manual (Publication 242 Change No.10) for Interstate and Expressway rigid‑pavement projects, the base layer shall be an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base or a Cement Treated Permeable Base placed over a 2A subbase, with ATPB and CTPB required to be bid as alternates and Open Graded Subbase prohibited; use of these permeable bases is permitted only where a positive outlet drainage can be achieved. The same manual specifies that when the effective modulus of subgrade reaction is calculated, the base elastic modulus shall be taken as 15,000 psi for “spring thaw” conditions, 30,000 psi for normal (summer and fall) conditions, and 50,000 psi for frozen conditions, and the ratio of the subbase to the roadbed soil resilient modulus shall not exceed 4. For new construction or reconstruction projects that employ a treated permeable‑base subbase over unbound aggregate together with an underdrain system and positive drainage, the overall drainage coefficient C_d shall be 1.1, whereas rigid pavements on unbound aggregate only or rehabilitation projects without significant drainage improvements retain a coefficient of 1.0. The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation Specification 408/2020 governs the design and construction of an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course in Section 360.3, which imposes weather limits that prohibit placement on unstable or frozen surfaces, when air temperature is below 35 °F, or during rain; if work stops for weather, a Representative may allow limited en‑route placement. The same specification requires the mix to be produced at a temperature below 320 °F, to use dried aggregate as needed, and to be placed within eight hours of production with no stockpiling permitted. Placement shall occur in compacted lifts not exceeding four inches and must be performed with a slip‑form paver or mechanical spreader; compaction shall use steel‑wheel rollers weighing eight to ten tons for exactly four passes only until the surface remains free draining, after which the layer must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is placed. Joints require one‑to‑two inch longitudinal overlaps and a full‑depth transverse bulkhead after any interruption longer than thirty minutes, while surface tolerances are verified with a ten‑foot straightedge and depth confirmed by a six‑inch test hole for each three thousand square yards of completed base; traffic on the ATPBC is prohibited except for trucks and equipment required to place the next layer. For treated permeable bases used within pervious asphalt pavement systems, Section 420.3 mandates construction of two test sections of at least 225 ft² each on the approved JMF and testing in accordance with ASTM C 1701, with the average infiltration of both sections required to exceed 200 in/hr; the compacted thickness may not be more than ¼‑inch less than the design thickness. Placement of these pervious systems is limited to air or surface temperatures of at least 50 °F, must employ smooth steel‑wheeled static rollers (pneumatic‑tire rollers are prohibited), and may not exceed four roller passes so that the material remains free draining; over‑compaction that crushes aggregate is prohibited. Where buses or light trucks are anticipated, the asphalt treated permeable base course follows the requirements of Section 360.3, and all vertical surfaces in contact with the asphalt must be coated with an AASHTO‑specified emulsified asphalt (SS‑1, CSS‑1, SS‑1h, CSS‑1h, or TACK). [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–163); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

Design methods and criteria

In rigid pavement designs that employ a Cement Treated Permeable Base (CTPB), the effective modulus of subgrade reaction is obtained by combining the CTPB layer with the underlying subbase and applying a weighted‑average loss of support calculation using the equation LS1D1/(D1+D2)+LS2D2/(D1+D2). [1] The base elastic modulus to be used in that k‑value computation is 15,000 psi for spring‑thaw conditions, 30,000 psi for normal (summer and fall) conditions, or 50,000 psi for frozen conditions, and these values may be adjusted so that the ratio of subbase to roadbed soil resilient modulus does not exceed 4. [1] Each distinct subbase type and depth must have its own effective k‑value, and CTPB must be bid as an alternate base layer on a 2A Subbase for Interstate and Expressway projects. [1] For drainage performance the treated permeable base is represented by the overall drainage coefficient Cd, an empirical factor that adjusts predicted pavement life for drainage quality; when a subbase of treated permeable base (or dense asphalt base) over unbound aggregate is used together with an underdrain system and positive drainage in new construction or reconstruction projects, the recommended value of Cd is 1.1, whereas when only unbound aggregate forms the base or rehabilitation work does not include significant drainage improvements, the coefficient is taken as 1.0. [1] Placement of a Treated Permeable Base is prohibited on surfaces that are unstable, frozen, or when air temperature is below 35 °F (Section 360) and also prohibited when air or surface temperature is lower than 50 °F under Section 420. [2] The mixture must be produced at a temperature below 320 °F to provide suitable viscosity for adequate coating of aggregate particles, and the material must be placed within eight hours of production. [2] Spreading and finishing are performed with a slip‑form paver or mechanical spreader in compacted lifts not exceeding four inches while automated sensors control profile and grade; surface irregularities greater than one‑half inch are corrected by loosening or adding material rather than grinding. [2] Compaction is achieved with steel‑wheel power rollers weighing eight to ten tons applying exactly four roller passes after the mat has cooled sufficiently, and smooth steel‑wheeled static rollers may be used for one to four passes as permitted in Section 420.3. [2] Depth verification requires drilling a six‑inch diameter test hole to the full base depth for each 3,000 square yards of completed course, and any deficiency of half an inch or more must be remedied by removal and replacement. [2] Under Section 420 two test sections are produced on the prepared subgrade and base, each having an area of at least 225 square feet; infiltration testing of both sections is performed in accordance with ASTM C 1701 and the average infiltration must exceed 200 inches per hour, and the compacted thickness may not be more than one‑quarter inch less than the design thickness. [2] Subgrade preparation requires excavation to undisturbed soil, removal of accumulated fines, scarifying a minimum depth of six inches, and avoidance of heavy equipment before placement of geotextile and stone bed; a detention basin is constructed with a minimum of two inches of fine aggregate under coarse aggregate placed in lifts not exceeding eight inches and compacted as specified. [2] The asphalt treated permeable base course follows the requirements of Section 360.3 but compaction is limited to not more than four passes to preserve free drainage. [2]

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–163); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

All Interstate and Expressway rigid pavement designs shall incorporate an Asphalt‑treated or Cement‑treated Permeable Base (ATPB or CTPB) placed over a 2A subbase, the two layers being treated as alternate options in bidding. For the combined base/subbase system the effective modulus of subgrade reaction k is taken as 15 000 psi for spring‑thaw conditions, 30 000 psi for normal conditions, and 50 000 psi for frozen conditions, and the values shall be adjusted so that the ratio of the subbase resilient modulus to the roadbed soil resilient modulus does not exceed 4. Loss‑of‑support factors of 0.5 for a TPBC layer and 1.0 for a 2A subbase are applied, weighted by thickness, to obtain an overall support factor that is used in the k‑value computation. When a treated permeable base (or dense asphalt base) is installed over unbound aggregate together with an underdrain system and positive drainage in new construction or reconstruction, the design calls for an overall drainage coefficient C_d equal to 1.1; if the pavement rests only on unbound aggregate or if a rehabilitation project does not incorporate significant drainage improvements, the prescribed C_d is 1.0. The Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course shall not be placed on unstable or frozen surfaces and shall not be placed when the air temperature is below 35 °F. The mixed material must be produced at a temperature below 320 °F and placed within eight hours of production. Placement shall use steel‑wheel power rollers weighing between eight and ten tons, laid in compacted lifts no thicker than four inches, and the mat must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before any subsequent layer is installed. Compaction requires exactly four roller passes with a static‑mode roller, avoiding over‑compaction that would eliminate free drainage. Surface irregularities greater than one‑half inch must be corrected by adjusting the ATPBC without grinding or milling. Depth control is verified by drilling a six‑inch diameter test hole for each 3 000 square yards of completed base, and any section found deficient in depth by one‑half inch or more must be removed and replaced. Traffic is prohibited on the fresh base except for equipment placing the next course. For the treated permeable base used under a pervious asphalt pavement system, verification shall be performed with two test sections each at least 225 sq ft, and the average infiltration measured according to ASTM C 1701 must exceed 200 in/hr. The compacted thickness cannot be more than one‑quarter inch less than the design thickness. Subgrade preparation shall leave the soil undisturbed and as permeable as possible, avoiding heavy equipment traffic before geotextile and stone bed placement, and any eroded or ponded areas must be scarified to a minimum depth of six inches. Placement of the pervious mixture is prohibited when air or surface temperature is lower than 50 °F, rollers shall be smooth steel‑wheeled static units and compaction is limited to not more than four passes so that drainage is retained. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–162); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

Structural section and dimensions

The Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course specified in Section 360 requires that the mixture be placed in compacted lifts not exceeding 4 inches in thickness; longitudinal joints must be overlapped by 1 inch to 2 inches to achieve a smooth joint after compaction, and depth verification is performed by drilling one 6‑inch diameter test hole for each 3,000 square yards of completed base. Under Section 420.3 the treated permeable base requires that test sections be at least 225 sq ft in area and that their compacted thickness not be more than ¼‑inch less than the design thickness; the subgrade must be scarified to a minimum depth of 6 in before placement, the detention‑basin floor is covered with a minimum of 2 in of fine aggregate and the pervious asphalt mixture itself is laid in compacted lifts of no more than 4 in and rolled no more than four passes to preserve drainage. [2]

[2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

Materials and mixture design

The policy manual requires that a permeable base be specified as either an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base (ATPB) or a Cement Treated Permeable Base (CTPB) placed on a 2A subbase, and ATPB and CTPB must be bid as alternates. The specification shall also provide the base elastic modulus to be used for the effective k‑value, selecting 15,000 psi for spring‑thaw conditions, 30,000 psi for normal summer/fall conditions, or 50,000 psi for frozen winter conditions. Additionally, the design shall ensure that the ratio of subbase to roadbed soil resilient modulus does not exceed 4, with layer thicknesses and loss‑of‑support factors given so the weighted support ratio can be calculated. The ATPBC specification shall identify aggregate and asphalt binder as the constituent materials, requiring that the aggregate be dried as necessary before mixing and that the asphalt material be heated so that, when combined, it coats the aggregate with a film of adequate thickness to provide the required binding properties. The mixed product shall be produced at a temperature below 320 °F, with a viscosity sufficient for adequate coating and without causing segregation of asphalt and aggregate during transport. After production the ATPBC must be placed within 8 hours and compacted only until it remains free‑draining and the aggregate is not crushed. For treated permeable base construction the specified aggregates shall include a minimum of two inches of fine aggregate at the bottom of any detention basin and coarse aggregate placed in lifts not exceeding eight inches, each lift being compacted as required by Section 360.3. The pervious asphalt mixture used for the base must achieve an average infiltration rate greater than 200 inches per hour on two test sections, shall have a compacted thickness that does not fall more than one‑quarter inch below the design thickness, and shall be placed in maximum four‑inch compacted lifts. Installation shall occur only when air or surface temperature is at least 50 °F, and the material shall be allowed to cool to 100 °F before subsequent layers are placed. Compaction shall be performed with a smooth steel‑wheeled static roller limited to no more than four passes in order to preserve free drainage and prevent aggregate crushing. [1][2]

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–162); [2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231), SECTION 400 FLEXIBLE PAVEMENTS (pp. 301–302)

For Interstate and Expressway rigid pavement designs the permeable base should consist of an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base or Cement Treated Permeable Base placed on a 2A Subbase, and the thicknesses of both layers are added when calculating the effective modulus of subgrade reaction; the loss‑of‑support factor for each layer is weighted by its depth to obtain a combined LS value that adjusts the k‑value. The base elastic modulus should be taken as 15 000 psi for spring thaw, 30 000 psi for normal conditions, or 50 000 psi for frozen conditions, and these values should be adjusted if necessary so that the ratio of the subbase to the roadbed soil resilient modulus does not exceed 4. Drainage layers using ATPB or CTPB are only permitted where a positive outlet drainage can be achieved; otherwise a dense base with longitudinal edge drains should be considered. [1]

[1] — Ch. 8 (pp. 161–162)

Structural details and interfaces

For ATPBC placed under Section 360—Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course, longitudinal joints are created by spreading the new mat so it overlaps the edge of the previously placed lane by 1 inch to 2 inches and keeping an uncompacted depth adjacent to the compacted lane to provide a smooth joint after compaction. Transverse construction joints are required at the end of each workday or whenever a pause longer than 30 minutes occurs; a temporary vertical bulkhead must be installed to form a full‑depth, full‑width straight joint, although the contractor may alternatively saw the joint. [2]

[2] — SECTION 300 BASE COURSES (pp. 229–231)

In rigid pavement designs for Interstates and Expressways, the permeable base is required to be an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base or a Cement Treated Permeable Base placed directly over a 2A Subbase, and when both layers are present their combined thickness is used in the effective modulus of subgrade reaction calculation [1]. The effective k‑value computation employs a Base Elastic Modulus of 15,000 psi for spring thaw, 30,000 psi for normal (summer and fall) conditions, and 50,000 psi for frozen conditions, with the additional constraint that the ratio of subbase to roadbed soil resilient modulus shall not exceed four [1]. Loss‑of‑support factors for each layer are weighted by their respective depths according to the prescribed formula so that the interface behavior reflects the contributions of both the permeable base and the subbase [1]. Drainage layers may be specified as ATPB or CTPB only when a positive outlet drainage can be achieved; otherwise a dense base with longitudinal edge drains is required and the use of Open Graded Subbase is prohibited [1]. For an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course, longitudinal joints are formed by spreading the material to overlap the edge of the previously placed lane by one inch to two inches while maintaining an uncompacted depth adjacent to the compacted lane so that a smooth joint results after compaction [2]. If work is interrupted for more than thirty minutes or at the end of a day, a full‑depth transverse construction joint is created by installing a temporary vertical bulkhead or by sawing the joint, ensuring that the joint extends the entire width and depth of the ATPB layer [2]. Surface irregularities greater than one‑half inch are corrected by loosening, adding, or removing ATPB material without grinding or milling in order to preserve free drainage [2]. Traffic is prohibited on the asphalt treated permeable base material except for trucks and equipment required to place the next layer, thereby protecting the interface before subsequent paving operations [2]. When a pervious asphalt pavement system is placed, the existing subgrade is excavated to undisturbed soil and left as permeable as possible, avoiding compaction until the geotextile and stone bed are installed [2]. At detention basins the bottom is covered with a minimum of two inches of fine aggregate and the basin sides are wrapped in non‑woven geotextile to prevent soils from migrating into the storage area [2]. All vertical surfaces that will contact the asphalt mixture—curbs, structures, gutters, and adjacent pavements—are coated with a thin, uniform layer of emulsified asphalt applied in two or more coats; no tack coat is placed between pervious asphalt lifts, each lift is limited to a maximum compacted thickness of four inches, subsequent layers are not placed until the surface has cooled to at least one hundred degrees Fahrenheit, and compaction of the permeable asphalt is restricted to no more than four passes to preserve free drainage [2].

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

In rigid pavement designs the permeable base must be an Asphalt Treated Permeable Base (ATPB) or Cement Treated Permeable Base (CTPB) placed over a 2A subbase, and it may be used as the drainage layer only when a positive outlet drainage can be achieved; otherwise a dense base with longitudinal edge drains should be considered. The design accounts for seasonal moisture and climate by applying a base elastic modulus of 15,000 psi for spring‑thaw conditions, 30,000 psi for normal (summer and fall) conditions, and 50,000 psi for frozen (winter) conditions, with adjustments required so that the ratio of subbase to roadbed soil resilient modulus does not exceed four. Additionally a loss‑of‑support factor is applied to correct the effective k‑value for potential erosion or pumping of the subbase material. When a treated permeable base (or dense asphalt base) is placed over unbound aggregate and an underdrain system providing positive drainage is installed, the overall drainage coefficient C_d should be taken as 1.1; if only unbound aggregate is used or a rehabilitation project does not incorporate significant drainage improvements, the recommended C_d remains at 1.0. In the Asphalt Treated Permeable Base Course, drainage is protected by limiting compaction so that the aggregate remains free‑draining, and the specification forbids compacting the material to a point where it ceases to drain. Climate constraints for this layer require that it not be placed on surfaces that are unstable, frozen, or when air temperature is below 35 °F, and placement must also be avoided during rain. After each lift the mixture must be allowed to cool to 100 °F before subsequent layers are placed, and rolling may occur only after the mat has cooled sufficiently to avoid shoving or lateral movement. The material may not be stockpiled and must be placed within eight hours of mixing to keep moisture conditions under control. The design calls for test sections of the treated permeable base to achieve an average infiltration rate that exceeds 200 inches per hour, thereby confirming adequate drainage. Subgrade preparation requires scarifying the existing soil to a minimum depth of 6 inches and leaving it as undisturbed and permeable as possible; the detention basin is protected with at least 2 inches of fine aggregate covered by non‑woven geotextile to prevent sediment migration. Placement of the pervious mixture shall not occur when air or surface temperature is lower than 50 °F, and compaction is limited to no more than four roller passes and must avoid over‑compacting so that the layer remains free draining. The asphalt treated permeable base must be placed with no traffic on its surface except for the trucks and equipment required to place the next layer, and it must be built to follow the design grade line while being laid in maximum 4‑inch compacted lifts. Longitudinal joints shall overlap the adjacent lane edge by 1 inch to 2 inches, and a temporary vertical bulkhead or saw cut shall form a transverse construction joint whenever more than a 30 minute interruption occurs, extending through the full depth and width of the base. Surface tolerances are verified with a 10‑foot straightedge moved no more than 5 feet per stage, and any irregularity of more than 1/2 inch must be corrected by loosening or adding material without grinding or milling. Verification and expected performance

For the asphalt‑treated permeable base (ATPBC) covered by Section 360, the Representative verifies compliance by conducting field checks that include measuring surface tolerances with a ten‑foot straightedge and correcting any irregularities greater than one‑half inch before final compaction; confirming depth by carefully digging or drilling a six‑inch diameter test hole to the full ATPBC thickness for each three‑thousand square yards of placed base and repairing deficiencies of one‑half inch or more both longitudinally and transversely; limiting compaction to no more than four passes with an eight‑to‑ten ton steel‑wheel roller while ensuring that aggregate is not crushed and drainage is retained; and inspecting joints for the required one‑ to two‑inch longitudinal overlap or a full‑depth vertical bulkhead at transverse locations. For the pervious asphalt pavement system addressed in Section 420, compliance of the completed permeable base is demonstrated by constructing two test sections on the prepared subgrade, each having an area of at least 225 square feet, and then measuring infiltration according to ASTM C‑1701; the average infiltration rate must exceed two hundred inches per hour and the compacted thickness may not be more than one‑quarter inch below the design thickness; compaction is performed with a smooth steel‑wheeled static roller limited to four passes so that porosity is retained, placement is prohibited when air or surface temperature is below fifty degrees Fahrenheit, and subgrade work must be inspected and accepted by the Representative before proceeding. In the context of rigid pavement design, a treated permeable base used in new construction or reconstruction projects together with an underdrain system and positive drainage is expected to improve overall pavement life as reflected by a higher overall drainage coefficient; the specification calls for a drainage coefficient (C d) of 1.1 for such conditions, whereas unbound aggregate only or rehabilitation without significant drainage improvements uses C d = 1.0. The performance effect on service life is therefore measured directly by the assigned C d value in Section 8.10. [1]
