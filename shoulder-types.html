Shoulder Types

Part of: Shoulder is-subtopic-of
Shoulder Types is a topic under Shoulder.

Created 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-29

Shoulder Types are the set of prescribed pavement‑edge structures defined in Chapter 2 §2.6 that provide a functional, structural and serviceable edge to both flexible and rigid roadways [1]. They are employed to furnish a smooth riding surface for bicyclists, relieve serious drainage problems, support truck traffic exceeding a Design Hourly Volume of 150, and accommodate vehicle turning movements at tight‑radius intersections or sharp curves, with Type 1 families (1‑F, 1‑S, 1‑SP) applied adjacent to flexible pavements on Interstates, other limited‑access freeways and arterials, while full‑depth concrete shoulders are required on rigid‑pavement facilities [1]. Design of the shoulder follows traffic‑volume thresholds—extending the pavement structure into the shoulder by one foot when ADT ≤ 5 000 and ADTT ≤ 500, or two feet when those values exceed the limits—and mandates concrete joint spacing not greater than 20 ft, thereby ensuring lateral support and a relatively maintenance‑free edge [1].

Purpose and design objectives

Type 1 shoulders (including variants 1‑F, 1‑S, and 1‑SP) are specified to provide a smooth riding surface for bicyclists where such activity is anticipated; they are also required on roadways where serious drainage problems are anticipated or where two‑lane roads experience truck traffic that exceeds a Design Hourly Volume (DHV) of 150. In addition, Type 1 shoulders are used when traffic is expected to utilize the shoulder for maneuvering at intersections with tight radii or sharp curves and for left‑turn or right‑turn movements that would otherwise impede through traffic. In rehabilitation projects that do not involve pavement widening, shoulder types are specified to supply the needed lateral support and a relatively maintenance‑free edge; concrete shoulders shall have joint spacing that shall not exceed 20 ft, and Type 1 (and its variants) are provided on collectors and local roads adjacent to flexible pavements so that the shoulder has the same surface material when only cleaning or patching is required. When widening is undertaken, the design calls for the widening structure to be extended into the shoulder area by one foot if the current ADT is 5000 or less and/or the ADTT is 500 or less, and by two feet when the ADT is greater than 5000 and/or the ADTT is greater than 500, thereby addressing the need for structural continuity under higher traffic volumes and heavy turning movements. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62)

According to section 2.6 SHOULDERS, Shoulder Types shall satisfy functional objectives such as providing smooth surfaces for bicyclists where such activities are anticipated, addressing serious drainage problems, and accommodating truck traffic that exceeds a Design Hourly Volume (DHV) of 150. Structural objectives are met by requiring full‑depth concrete shoulders on Interstate and Other Limited Access Freeways and Type 2 concrete shoulders on Arterials, Collectors, and Local Roads. Safety objectives include supporting vehicle movements at intersections with tight radii or where turning traffic is expected to use the shoulder, with pavement extensions beginning 250 ft before and ending 150 ft downstream of the intersection. Serviceability objectives are achieved by constructing the shoulder to the effective shoulder width indicated on the design‑criteria charts. In addition, section 2.6 SHOULDERS > B. Resurfacing, Restoration, and Rehabilitation (3R) states that shoulder types must provide lateral support for rigid pavements while remaining relatively maintenance‑free, and the joint spacing of concrete shoulders shall not exceed 20 ft. When only the first few feet adjacent to the pavement are distressed a minimum width of 3 feet of Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S or 6‑SP shoulder may be provided, with the remaining existing shoulder resurfaced in kind. For widening projects the structure shall be extended into the shoulder by 2 feet when the current ADT is greater than 5000 or the ADTT is greater than 500, and by 1 foot otherwise; heavy turning areas are strongly recommended to be paved out‑to‑out. Recycled‑asphalt base courses may be used on shoulders with a minimum depth of 5 inches on Arterials and 4 inches on Collectors and Local Roads. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62)

Scope and applicability

In section 2.6 SHOULDERS, Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S, or 1‑SP shoulders are specified for flexible‑pavement projects on all Interstate and Other Limited Access Freeways and Arterials, on other roadways where serious drainage problems are anticipated, on two‑lane roads whose truck traffic exceeds a Design Hourly Volume of 150, and wherever traffic is anticipated to use the shoulder—such as at tight‑radius ramp intersections or curves sharper than 12°‑30’, at left‑turn approaches requiring right‑side shoulder use with pavement extension or Type 1 shoulder starting 250 ft before and stopping 150 ft downstream of the intersection, and at right‑turn approaches where the shoulder serves as a turning lane. The same types may also be provided elsewhere to give bicyclists a smooth surface when the District Executive deems it appropriate. In section 2.6 SHOULDERS > B. Resurfacing, Restoration, and Rehabilitation (3R), Shoulder Types are used in resurfacing, restoration and rehabilitation projects based on pavement class and extent of work. For flexible pavements on collectors and local roads a same‑material shoulder or a Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S, or 1‑SP shoulder may be provided; on Interstate, limited‑access freeways and arterials where excavation or scarifying of the existing shoulder is required a Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S, or 6‑SP shoulder shall be used, while only cleaning or patching calls for a Type 7 shoulder. When rehabilitating rigid pavements concrete shoulders may be added provided joint spacing shall not exceed 20 ft. During pavement widening the shoulder extension follows traffic thresholds—if ADT is greater than 5000 or ADTT is greater than 500 the structure extends 2 ft into the shoulder, otherwise it extends 1 ft, and the entire shoulder can be paved with the same material as the widened lane. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62)

In new construction or reconstruction projects that employ flexible pavement, a Type 1 shoulder is required only on Interstates and other limited‑access freeways, on arterials where serious drainage problems are anticipated, on two‑lane roadways where the Design Hourly Volume exceeds 150, or where the geometry predicts frequent shoulder usage; when those conditions are not present the standard practice shifts to providing Type 3 shoulders on collectors and local roads (or using the same surface material as the pavement structure) instead of a Type 1. For rigid‑pavement projects the specifications call for full‑depth concrete shoulders on Interstates and other limited‑access freeways and Type 2 concrete shoulders on arterials, collectors, and local roads, making those concrete options preferable to a Type 1 shoulder in those contexts, and the 2‑foot widening into the shoulder area does not apply to inside shoulders of Interstates. When rehabilitating a rigid pavement without widening, concrete shoulders may be provided to give lateral support or a relatively maintenance‑free edge, but the joint spacing shall not exceed 20 ft. On Interstates and other limited‑access freeways where excavation or scarifying of the existing shoulder is necessary, a Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S, or 6‑SP shoulder is specified, and if only cleaning and patching of the existing paved shoulder is required a Type 7 shoulder is preferred. For widening projects, if the current ADT is greater than 5000 and/or the ADTT is greater than 500, the widening structure shall be extended into the shoulder area by 2 ft (or by 1 ft when traffic is lower), allowing the entire shoulder to be paved like the new lane rather than using a Type 1 shoulder; in areas of heavy turning movement paving out‑to‑out is strongly recommended. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62)

Design inputs and requirements

Shoulder design must consider the classification of the roadway—Interstate, other limited‑access freeways, arterials, collectors or local roads—and the existing shoulder condition such as whether it is high‑type paved or only the first few feet adjacent to the pavement are distressed, which determines if excavation or scarifying is needed. Traffic volumes drive the extent of widening: when the current ADT is greater than 5000 and/or the ADTT is greater than 500, the widening structure shall be extended into the shoulder area by 2 feet, while for ADT of 5000 or less and ADTT of 500 or less the extension shall be 1 foot. Material requirements include that concrete shoulder joint spacing shall not exceed 20 ft and reclaimed asphalt concrete may be used in base courses with a minimum depth of 5 inches on arterials and 4 inches on collectors and local roads. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 61–62)

Shoulder design is governed by the project‑specific requirements set out in Chapter 2, Section 2.6 of the Roadway Construction Standards, which reference Publication 13 Design Manual Part 2 for geometric definitions and Publication 72M RC‑25M for the classification of shoulder types such as Types 1‑F, 1‑S, and 1‑SP that are intended to provide smooth surfaces for bicyclists. For any new construction or reconstruction project having a rigid pavement structure, Portland Cement Concrete shoulders shall be provided. When the pavement is flexible, the pavement shall be constructed 2 feet wider into the shoulder area except on inside shoulders of Interstate and Other Limited Access Freeways before applying the designated shoulder type. Full‑depth concrete shoulders are required on Interstates and Other Limited Access Freeways, and Type 2 shoulders shall be used on Arterials, Collectors, and Local Roads. Type 1 family shoulders may be used adjacent to flexible pavements on all Interstates, Other Limited Access Freeways, and Arterials, as well as on roadways with serious drainage problems, on two‑lane roads where the Design Hourly Volume exceeds 150, or where anticipated traffic movements such as tight‑radius ramp intersections, left‑turn conflicts, or right‑turn use of the shoulder dictate pavement extension starting 250 ft before and ending 150 ft downstream of the intersection. Additional performance criteria for bicycle‑compatible rumble strips and edge line rumble strips are contained in Publication 13 Chapter 12 and Publication 72M RC‑22M. Under the resurfacing, restoration, and rehabilitation (3R) provisions, concrete shoulders added to rigid‑pavement rehabilitations shall not exceed a joint spacing of 20 ft. For Interstates and other limited‑access freeways or arterials where excavation or scarifying of the existing shoulder is necessary, a Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S, or 6‑SP shoulder shall be provided with at least a three‑foot minimum width when only the first couple feet adjacent to the pavement are distressed. Type 7 shoulders shall be used where only cleaning and patching of an existing paved shoulder is required. When widening a pavement, if the current ADT is greater than 5 000 or the ADTT is greater than 500, the widening structure shall be extended two feet into the shoulder area; otherwise it shall be extended one foot, and this extension may be eliminated on arterials with less than 5 000 ADT and on collectors and local roads if an optional Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S, or 1‑SP shoulder or a recycled shoulder is provided. Reclaimed asphalt material may be used in the base of recycled shoulders, with a minimum depth of five inches on arterials and four inches on collectors and local roads. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62)

Design methods and criteria

According to the procedures set out in Section 2.6, any new construction or reconstruction project that employs a rigid pavement shall incorporate Portland Cement Concrete full‑depth shoulders, whereas a flexible pavement shall be built with an additional two‑foot widening into the shoulder area before the remaining width is constructed as the designated type. Type 1 shoulders—including the 1‑F, 1‑S and 1‑SP variants—are to be provided adjacent to flexible pavements when the project is located on an Interstate or other limited‑access freeway or arterial, when serious drainage problems are anticipated, when truck traffic on a two‑lane road exceeds a Design Hourly Volume of 150, or when vehicle use of the shoulder is expected at intersections with tight ramp radii or curves sharper than 12°‑30′. In the context of resurfacing, restoration and rehabilitation (the 3R guidelines), concrete shoulders used in rigid‑pavement rehabilitation shall be detailed with joint spacing that does not exceed 20 ft. For collectors and local roads, shoulders adjacent to flexible pavements may be constructed of the same surface material as the pavement structure, or a Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S or 1‑SP shoulder may be used. When widening is required, the extent of the widening into the shoulder depends on traffic volumes: if the current average daily traffic exceeds 5 000 or the product of current ADT and the percentage of trucks, buses and recreational vehicles (ADTT) exceeds 500, the widening structure shall extend two feet into the shoulder; if the current ADT is 5 000 or less and the ADTT is 500 or less, the extension shall be one foot, and the entire shoulder may be paved to the same design as the widened pavement at the District Executive’s discretion. Additionally, reclaimed asphalt concrete may be employed in recycled base courses for Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S shoulders or in surface courses for Type 7 shoulders, with cold‑mixed recycled bases required to have a minimum depth of five inches on arterials and four inches on collectors and local roads. For any new construction or reconstruction project with a rigid pavement structure, Portland Cement Concrete shoulders shall be provided. For flexible pavements, the pavement shall be constructed 2 feet wider into the shoulder area before the designated shoulder type is applied. Full‑depth concrete shoulders are required on Interstate and Other Limited Access Freeways. For widening projects, if the current ADT is greater than 5000 and/or the ADTT is greater than 500, the widening structure shall be extended into the shoulder area by 2 feet; when the current ADT is 5000 or less and the ADTT is 500 or less, the extension shall be 1 ft, with the entire shoulder optionally paved with the same material at the District Executive’s discretion. Recycled asphalt concrete may be used in a cold‑mixed recycled base course on shoulders of arterials, collectors and local roads, but a minimum depth of 5 inches shall be used on arterials and 4 inches on collectors and local roads. Structural section and dimensions

In section 2.6 B of the report, concrete shoulders shall not have joint spacing that exceeds 20 ft. When only the first few feet of an existing high‑type paved shoulder are distressed, a minimum width of 3 ft of Type 1 (or equivalent Type 6, 6‑F, 6‑S, 6‑SP) shoulder may be provided, with the remainder resurfaced to match the new shoulder. For pavement widening, the structure shall extend into the shoulder by 2 ft when the current ADT is greater than 5 000 or the ADTT exceeds 500, and shall extend by 1 ft otherwise; the entire shoulder may be paved to the same design at the district’s discretion. Recycled‑asphalt base courses used on shoulders shall have a minimum depth of 5 in on Arterials and a minimum depth of 4 in on Collectors and Local Roads. In section 3.3 A, a leveling course of at least 1‑inch thickness is required before placing the overlay, and when concrete shoulders or a widened lane are provided they should be between 1 to 2 ft wide together with asphalt shoulders; the specifications also call for Type 6 or Type 7 paved shoulders where applicable. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 61–62), Ch. 3 (pp. 67–69)

Shoulder design follows the limits set in Chapter 2 §2.6 B: concrete shoulders must have joint spacing that does not exceed 20 ft; when a recycled base is used, the base course shall be at least 5 in thick on arterials and at least 4 in thick on collectors and local roads; for widening projects the pavement structure is extended into the shoulder by 2 ft where the existing ADT exceeds 5 000 or the ADTT exceeds 500, otherwise it is extended only 1 ft, and the chosen Type (e.g., Type 6, 1‑F, etc.) depends on road class and whether excavation or resurfacing is required. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 61–62)

Materials and mixture design

For new construction or reconstruction projects that employ a rigid pavement structure, the shoulder shall be provided as Portland Cement Concrete. When the project uses a flexible pavement, the shoulder may be built from any of the concrete types designated in the specification, including Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S, 1‑SP, Full‑Depth, or Type 2, and the source does not prescribe additional material properties or mixture proportions beyond identifying the shoulder as concrete. When a Type 1 shoulder is placed adjacent to flexible pavement, it shall be constructed of the same surface material as the pavement structure. If a concrete shoulder is provided for a rigid pavement, its joints may not be spaced more than 20 ft apart. Asphalt‑based shoulders such as Types 6, 6‑F, 6‑S, 6‑SP or Type 7 may incorporate reclaimed asphalt concrete in either the base or surface course. A cold‑mixed recycled base may be used on any shoulder, provided that a minimum thickness of 5 inches is applied on arterials and a minimum thickness of 4 inches is applied on collectors and local roads. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62)

When rehabilitating a rigid pavement, concrete shoulders may be provided for lateral support but the joint spacing shall not exceed 20 ft; on interstates where excavation or scarifying of an existing high‑type paved shoulder is required, a (3 feet) minimum width of Type 6, 6-F, 6-S, or 6-SP Shoulder may be placed and the remaining shoulder resurfaced with the same surfacing used on the new shoulder. For flexible pavements on collectors and local roads the shoulder should use the same surface material as the pavement structure or a Type 1, 1-F, 1-S, or 1-SP Shoulder. Reclaimed asphalt concrete may be used in an asphalt recycled base course for Type 6, 6-F, or 6-S Shoulders or in the surface course of Type 7 shoulders on any roadway, and cold‑mixed recycled bases may be placed with a minimum depth of (5 inches) on arterials and (4 inches) on collectors and local roads. When widening, if the current ADT is greater than 5000 and/or the ADTT is greater than 500 the widening structure shall extend 2 ft into the shoulder; otherwise it shall extend 1 ft, and the entire shoulder may be paved to the same material and design as the pavement widening. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 61–62)

Structural details and interfaces

In Section 2.6 of Chapter 2 the only structural detail specified for a Type 1 shoulder is a transition length when the shoulder is used near an intersection; the paved portion shall begin 250 ft before the intersection and terminate 150 ft downstream, and concrete shoulders full depth shall be provided adjacent to rigid pavements on Interstates and other limited‑access freeways. No joint, reinforcement, load‑transfer system, or connection requirements are described for that shoulder type in this section. In Section 2.6 > B, which addresses the 3R rehabilitation context, when concrete shoulders are added to a rigid pavement without widening the only explicit structural detail required is control of joint spacing: the joints in those concrete shoulders shall not exceed 20 ft, and the source does not prescribe any reinforcement, load‑transfer devices, transition treatments, or special connections for Type 1 or other shoulder types. Section 3.3, Guidelines for Developing Initial Costs, under Asphalt Rehabilitation Strategies, calls for thorough joint treatment that includes longitudinal and transverse joint cleaning and sealing of concrete pavements and asphalt joint and crack sealing, with saw cutting and sealing of overlays over existing transverse and patch joints where present; heavy‑duty membranes may be used as necessary, a continuous pavement base drain is provided to support the shoulder, and guide rail and drainage structures should be adjusted or replaced as needed. Type 6 or Type 7 paved shoulders are specified in that guidance. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62), Ch. 3 (pp. 67–69)

For new construction or reconstruction projects that use flexible pavement, the design calls for extending the pavement surface an additional two feet into the shoulder zone before constructing the remainder of the shoulder with the specified Type 1 (or 1‑F, 1‑S, 1‑SP) material; when the main carriageway is rigid pavement, a full‑depth concrete shoulder shall be placed directly adjacent to it. Type 1 and its variants are required next to flexible pavements on all Interstate and Other Limited Access Freeways and Arterials, as well as where serious drainage problems are expected, where truck traffic exceeds a Design Hourly Volume of 150, or where geometric conditions such as curves sharper than 12°‑30’ or tight ramp radii anticipate shoulder use; in those cases the pavement structure may be extended through the shoulder area or a Type 1 shoulder installed beginning about 250 ft before an intersection and ending about 150 ft downstream. Concrete shoulders placed during rigid‑pavement rehabilitation must have joints spaced no more than 20 ft, ensuring a controlled interface between the shoulder slab and adjacent pavement. When a widening is performed on a rigid‑pavement facility, the new structural layer shall be extended into the shoulder by two feet if the existing average daily traffic exceeds 5 000 or the ADTT exceeds 500, and shall be extended by one foot when those values are 5 000 or less and ADTT is 500 or less; this extension creates a continuous interface that may be paved with the same material as the widened carriageway. For flexible‑pavement facilities on collectors and local roads, the shoulder shall be constructed of the same surface material as the pavement structure or using Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S, or 1‑SP shoulders, providing a compatible material interface. Reclaimed asphalt concrete can also be used in the base course of Type 6 or Type 7 shoulders, with a minimum depth of five inches on arterials and four inches on collectors and local roads, thereby integrating recycled material into the layer interface. When designing shoulder types for asphalt rehabilitation, the interface treatment follows the overlay steps: a tack coat is applied where bonding between existing and new asphalt is required, all longitudinal and transverse joints are cleaned and sealed (including saw‑cutting and sealing over existing transverse or patch joints) before placement of the overlay, and a continuous pavement base drain is maintained. Full‑depth asphalt shoulders—specified as Type 6 or Type 7 where applicable—are provided with at least a one‑inch leveling course that includes cross‑slope correction to ensure proper transition between shoulder and main carriageway, and guide rail and drainage structures adjacent to the shoulder are adjusted or replaced as necessary to preserve functional continuity. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62), Ch. 3 (pp. 67–69)

Drainage, environment, and accessibility

When a flexible‑pavement project anticipates serious drainage problems, the design shall provide a Type 1 (or 1‑F, 1‑S, or 1‑SP) shoulder adjacent to the pavement; this is one of the specific cases listed for using Type 1 shoulders. The source does not specify additional treatment for moisture, climate, or other environmental conditions beyond that drainage consideration. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–61)

In new construction or reconstruction projects with a rigid pavement structure, Portland Cement Concrete shoulders shall be provided. Where the project uses flexible pavement, the pavement shall be constructed two feet wider into the shoulder area, except that the two‑foot widening does not apply to inside shoulders on Interstate and other limited‑access freeways; the remaining width shall be built to the designated shoulder type. Types 1‑F, 1‑S, and 1‑SP have been developed primarily to provide smooth surfaces for bicyclists where such activity is anticipated, and Type 1, 1‑F, 1‑S, or 1‑SP shoulders shall be provided adjacent to flexible pavements on all Interstates and other limited‑access freeways and arterials, on roadways with serious drainage problems, on two‑lane roads where the Design Hourly Volume of trucks exceeds 150, and where shoulder use is anticipated for turning movements at intersections with tight radii (curves sharper than 12°‑30′) or left‑turn conflicts; in those cases the paved portion shall start 250 ft before the intersection and stop 150 ft downstream. Concrete shoulders under the 3R resurfacing guidelines shall have joint spacing that does not exceed 20 ft to provide lateral support. When only the first few feet of an existing high‑type paved shoulder are distressed, a minimum width of three feet of Type 6 (or its variants) may be provided and the remainder resurfaced with the same material. For pavement widening, the widening structure shall extend two feet into the shoulder where current ADT exceeds 5 000 or ADTT exceeds 500, and one foot where those values are 5 000 or less and ADTT is 500 or less; out‑to‑out paving is strongly recommended in areas of heavy turning movement. Recycled asphalt base courses on shoulders shall be at least five inches deep on arterials and four inches deep on collectors and local roads. [1]

[1] — Ch. 2 (pp. 60–62)
